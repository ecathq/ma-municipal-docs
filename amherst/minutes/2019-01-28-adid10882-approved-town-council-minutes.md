MSBA application process in joint District meetings with a facilitator before Councilors break into
individual District meetings.

 The impact of grades moving to the High School

 Public outreach process and community consensus; listening sessions & public education
 The need to achieve a consensus among the Town Council, so the community does not hear a
mixed message about the process; if a community consensus is not achieved, the project may not
move forward
 Deadline to submit letter of interest to MSBA is April 12; public outreach process must fit within
that timeline
 Need to present information from the Fort River feasibility study to the public to highlight the
value of the information gathered

January 28, 2019 Town Council Meeting
Councilor Hanneke summarized the report of the Governance, Organization and Legislation Committee;
the committee did not address substantive changes to the charge, and voted that the amended charge is
actionable.
Councilors DuMont and Ross presented a statement from the Amherst Regional High School
Environmental Action Club, explained the changes made to the committee charge, and that the changes
were made according to input from the Town Council and the public.
MOTION: Councilor DuMont moved, second by Councilor Swartz that the Town Council order the
establishment of a standing committee [Charter Section 2.5] called the Town of Amherst Energy and
Climate Action Committee (ECAC) to guide the Town in meeting its climate mitigation and resilience
goals. Order that the Energy and Climate Action Committee, within 90 days of its first meeting, submit to
the Town Council for adoption initial recommended target dates, benchmarks and/or annual/interannual
targets to achieve the climate action goals adopted in Article 16 passed by the Fall 2017 Special Town
Meeting [100% renewable energy/electricity] and other emissions reductions goals recommended by the
ECAC and that the Town Council act by voting to adopt the recommendations submitted with or without
amendments within 90 days of receipt from the ECAC.
Discussion: Councilors made informal amendments to remove the reference to Charter section 2.5, as a
reference to the Town Council’s general powers is unnecessary, and to remove the word “standing,” as
the Charter only refers to standing committees of the Town Council.
MOTION: Councilor Steinberg moved, second by Councilor Ryan to remove the 90-day deadlines from
the order.
Discussion: Councilors discussed the 90-day deadline, the committee’s ability to meet the deadline with a
public process, flexibility and scope of the goals as aspirational or practical.
VOTED 3-8-2 (Councilors Brewer, Ryan and Steinberg voted Yes; Councilors DeAngelis, DuMont,
Griesemer, Hanneke, Ross, Schoen, Schreiber, and Swartz voted No; Councilors Bahl-Milne and Pam
Abstained); the motion to remove the 90-day deadlines failed.
VOTED 12-0-1 (Councilors Bahl-Milne, Brewer, DeAngelis, DuMont, Griesemer, Hanneke, Pam, Ross,
Ryan, Schoen, Schreiber, and Swartz voted Yes, Councilor Steinberg Abstained) that the Town Council
order the establishment of a committee called the Town of Amherst Energy and Climate Action
Committee (ECAC) to guide the Town in meeting its climate mitigation and resilience goals. Order that
the Energy and Climate Action Committee, within 90 days of its first meeting, submit to the Town
Council for adoption initial recommended target dates, benchmarks and/or annual/interannual targets to
achieve the climate action goals adopted in Article 16 passed by the Fall 2017 Special Town Meeting
[100% renewable energy/electricity] and other emissions reductions goals recommended by the ECAC
and that the Town Council act by voting to adopt the recommendations submitted with or without
amendments within 90 days of receipt from the ECAC.

Discussion: Councilors discussed the composition of the committee, and the specificity of the
qualifications listed in the charge.
MOTION: Councilor Hanneke moved, second by Councilor Schreiber to remove all the language after
the words “seven (7) residents” in the composition section.
Discussion included:
 Town Manager’s role in seeking committee members with relevant experience per the Charter
 Whether the ECAC should be exclusively experts
 Limiting the number of experts on the charge
 The role of non-experts in writing policy

remove the language after the words “seven (7) residents” in the composition section failed.
MOTION: Councilor DuMont moved, second by Councilor Swartz to change the resident membership
section under Composition to: “Seven (7) residents. Experience with one or more of the following areas:
net zero energy building; energy efficient retrofits; climate change mitigation/advocacy/research; clean
energy practice/policy/infrastructure; community choice energy; green infrastructure for climate
adaptation; sustainable transportation; sustainable farming and forestry; and waste reduction is preferred.”
Discussion: Councilors commented that “preferred” may be a deterrent to interested residents, that the list
may be too specific, discussed adding “some of whom” before the list, and the existing requirement of the
Charter to appoint members with relevant experience.
VOTED 2-11 (Councilors DuMont and Swartz voted Yes; Councilors Bahl-Milne, Brewer, Griesemer,
Hanneke, Pam, Ross, Ryan, Schoen, Schreiber, and Steinberg voted No); the motion to change the
resident composition section failed.
MOTION: President Griesemer moved, second by Councilor Steinberg to change the resident
membership section under Composition to: “Seven (7) residents, some of whom shall have experience
with one or more of the following areas: net zero energy building; energy efficient retrofits; climate
change mitigation/advocacy/research; clean energy practice/policy/infrastructure; community choice
energy; green infrastructure for climate adaptation; sustainable transportation; sustainable farming and
forestry; and waste reduction. From the pool of residents with the required experience, the Town Manager
may, among others, consider inclusion of members of environmental justice communities and other
relevant areas, relevant community organizations, relevant town boards and committees, employees of
relevant offices or departments at the university and colleges, and the local business community.”
Discussion: Councilors discussed Charter section 3.3 (c), to clarify “seek,” the specificity of the list, and
that the charge may need further work before approval.

b) Council Appointments
President Griesemer provided the Town Council with recommendations for appointments to Joint Capital
Planning Committee, Budget Coordinating Group, and Participatory Budgeting Commission, and
explained her process for determining those recommendations.
MOTION: Councilor Schoen moved, second by Councilor Schreiber to amend the process for appointing
Councilors to multiple-member bodies where Town Council is the appointing authority. She referenced
the legal opinion that indicated that the Charter gives the President authority only for Committees of the
Council and that it is up to the Town Council to decide a method for appointing Councilors to town-wide
Committees. The motion would have the full Council decide such appointments. If more were interested
than the slots available, appointments would be by majority vote.
Discussion included:
 Creating a regular procedure for appointing Councilors to multiple-member bodies
 The powers of the Council President and Town Manager in the appointment process
 Option of referring the matter to the Rules of Procedure Committee
 Holding what might be mini-elections for appointments during Town Council Meetings
VOTED 3-9-1 (Councilors DuMont, Schoen, and Schreiber voted Yes; Councilors Brewer, DeAngelis,
Griesemer, Hanneke, Pam, Ross, Ryan, Steinberg, and Swartz voted No; Councilor Bahl-Milne
Abstained). The motion to amend the appointment process failed.
1) Joint Capital Planning Committee
2) Budget Coordinating Group
3) Participatory Budgeting Commission
