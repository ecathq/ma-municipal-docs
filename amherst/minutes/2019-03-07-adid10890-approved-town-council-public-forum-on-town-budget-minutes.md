March 7, 2019 Town Council Public Forum on the Proposed Town Budget
a. Public Forum on Town Budget [Charter Sec. 5.3]
Jeff Lee, of 815 South East Street, commented on bus service, sustainability, economic justice, and urged
the Town Council to support public transportation in the budget.
Claire Bertrand, of 610 Bay Road, expressed hopes that the opportunity for online public comment would
be available, that funding for ranked choice voting implementation and short-term capital expenses would
be included in the budget, and urged the Town to enhance the slide about debt and levy and share that
information.
Amy Gates, of 54 Spaulding Street, offered an idea for proposed revenue for the town: a $10 per bedroom
tax to be paid by landlords; and expressed the feeling that homeowners are bearing the Town’s tax
burden.
Robert Pam, of 229 Amity Street, commented that department budgets are tight, and that last year the
Town decided to fund smaller capital items from the operating budget. Mr. Pam also commented that
setbacks are a concern, and that many residents feel that sidewalks need to be wider.
Barbara Pearson, of 11 Paige Street, asked for clarification about how much revenue new buildings are
bringing to Town. Ms. Pearson also commented about a potential Federal bill that would eliminate
insurance companies, and would like to see historical data and projections about health insurance costs.
Amy Gates asked about the school budget, and how much it can be adjusted by the Town at this point.
Barbara Pearson asked about funding for sidewalks.
Robert Pam commented that there are competing needs between capital and the operating budget; that the
percentage of the tax levy dedicated to capital is commendable, but that restricts the operating budget. Mr.
Pam also expressed concern about the funding of social services; libraries and schools are restricted from
offering additional social services due to budget limitations.
Bill Kaizen, of Goldenrod Circle, commented that the creation of the Participatory Budgeting
Commission feels like it has a lot of potential.
Jeff Lee asked if there will be another public forum when a budget is prepared.
President Griesemer added that the First Congregational Church was successful in its appeal and will
remain a temporary shelter.
