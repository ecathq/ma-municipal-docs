5. Presentations and Discussion
a. MSBA Statement of Interest – Michael Morris, School Superintendent and Anastasia Ordonez,
Chair of the Amherst School Committee
Mr. Morris and Ms. Ordonez spoke about the condition of the schools, and the challenges and barriers
Amherst school educators experience on a day-to-day basis. Mr. Morris stated that the urgency to
address the issues is high. The buildings are approaching 50 years old, and that is the typical lifespan of
school buildings before they need substantial renovation. Schools have issues with their HVAC and
electrical systems, safety issues arising from the layout of the entrance to the schools and sprinkler
systems. In addition, the classroom sizes are significantly smaller than the classroom size suggested by
the state due to the use of dividers used to divide classrooms into quads. An ADA audit showed that all
the buildings have ADA accessibility issues.
Statements of Interest are required by the MSBA to apply to their program. There are two this year, one
for Wildwood and one for Fort River. The School Committee voted unanimously to support the
Statements of Interest.
The agreements in the proposal include pursuing the replacement of Wildwood and Fort River
Elementary Schools with one 600-student school that would serve either grades K-5 or K-6, and
thorough engagement of the community during the feasibility study.
Educational advantages of the proposal include: greater access for entry into the dual-language program,
the resolution of transportation challenges for students in special education programs that are currently
bussed out of their neighborhood zone, the earliest possible date for a new facility for all students in
both buildings, and the ability to retain the current cohort size in each grade level.

March 18, 2019 Town Council Meeting
Ms. Ordonez thanked the Councilors for their participation in listening sessions. In December, the
MSBA advised the district to include a consensus statement; in order to achieve consensus, the School
Committee held three listening sessions for educators and district staff, there were community listening
sessions with a facilitator from which a report was prepared, a feedback forum was posted online,
information was provided online and in the newspaper, and public comment was held during posted
School Committee meetings. An FAQ was prepared in response to public outreach.
Mr. Morris presented concerns expressed during public outreach and listening sessions, and responded
to those concerns.
Mr. Morris explained the cost implications of waiting to address issues at the schools.
Ms. Ordonez stated that the School Committee asked her to convey the committee’s sense of urgency to
address issues at the schools, that the committee believe that they have heard consensus from the
community about moving forward with this proposal, and that from the MSBA’s viewpoint, consensus
means unanimous or close to unanimous support of the proposal.
Councilor Pam asked if cohorts will be the same students in every grade; Mr. Morris responded that
students will move through the grades with the same grade level students, but may be in different classes
year-to-year.
Councilor Schoen suggested edits to the statement of interest.
Councilor Bahl-Milne commented that she has heard from constituents that a negative aspect of the
proposal is the loss of small neighborhood schools, but that in her research, neighborhood schools
typically have a cohort size that is comparable to the proposal; Mr. Morris explained that while
“neighborhood school” can mean different things, yes, the cohort model will remain 2-4 classes per
grade. Councilor Bahl-Milne asked about advantages that we would be losing with a single building;
Mr. Morris responded that the feasibility study will address those concerns.
Councilor De Angelis commented that the diversity of participants in feasibility studies has been good,
and that it is clear that we have been building consensus for a long time.
Councilor DuMont endorsed Councilors Schoen and De Angelis’ comments, and asked that information
from the feasibility study be shared with every household in town.
Councilor Hanneke thanked the presenters for an earlier slide that made it clear that the feasibility study
would be required to investigate options, as one of the biggest concerns she has heard is that the
proposed solution would eliminate options that might be necessary depending on unknown variables.
President Griesemer clarified that the goal is to get funding for the feasibility study, which will also
enter the Town in the queue to receive a grant that would eventually fund the project.
President Griesemer opened the floor for public comment first to students and teachers, and then the
public.

Agenda item 5. b. Presentations and Discussion: East Street School Affordable Housing – John
Hornik, Chair of the Amherst Municipal Affordable Housing Trust and Nathaniel Malloy, Senior Planner
for the Town of Amherst
Mr. Hornik presented the Housing Trust’s proposal, including feasibility of the project, who has been
involved, current status, steps that must be taken if the Town Council wishes to proceed, and pros and
cons of moving forward with the project.
Councilors asked about taxes on the property. Housing Trust member and Executive Director of Home
City Tom Kegelman responded that all affordable housing projects he is aware of pay property taxes. Mr.
Malloy added that affordable units are taxed on a pro-rated basis. Mr. Bockelman added that if the Town
continues to own the property, the income on the rental units would be taxed.
Councilor De Angelis asked about diversity and outreach in offering leases. Mr. Hornik responded that
eligibility rules would be set by the Department of Housing and Community Development. Mr. Malloy
added that affordable housing projects are subject to fair housing regulations.
Councilor DuMont asked about green housing. Mr. Hornik responded that developers are moving in that
direction, and the RFP reflects those commitments.
Councilor Hanneke asked about the Town Meeting vote regarding this property, and how the Town
Council would designate “surplus property” without information about capital inventory or other
information or recommendation from the Finance Committee. Mr. Bockelman responded that the Town
Council is the new legislative body; prior Town Meeting action does not restrict the Town Council’s
actions. It will be up to the Town Council to refer to a committee or to order a study.
Councilor Schoen asked about the value of the land and the community impact costs. Mr. Hornik
responded that he would advise against restricting the developer with requirements in the RFP. It would
be wise to allow the developers to exceed the requirements in their proposals, and allow flexibility for
developers.
Councilor Schreiber asked about a special permit. Mr. Malloy stated that it would depend upon the
developer’s site plan.
Councilor De Angelis stated that there is an urgent need for affordable housing in Amherst, and there is
an urgency to getting the RFP out.
