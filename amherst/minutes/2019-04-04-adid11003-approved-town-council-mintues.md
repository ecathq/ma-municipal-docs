President Griesemer declared the presence of a quorum and called the Special Town Council meeting to
order at 6:33 p.m.

budget to order per Charter Section 5.5(a).
Councilor Steinberg announced audio and video recording by Amherst Media.
Councilor Steinberg announced that, pursuant to Charter section 5.5(c), the Town Council has determined
that it is prudent to separately consider and act on the regional school budget separately, due to legal
requirements, agreements with regional entities or other substantial cause. The Town Council will
consider the proposed regional school budget, the proposed assessment method and proposed capital
expenditure during the month of April. Councilor Steinberg introduced Superintendent of Schools, Dr.
Morris and Regional Schools Finance Director Sean Mangano, who presented the proposed Regional
School Budget to the Town Council. A question and answer period open to councilors and the public
followed the presentation.

assessment method between the four towns. Mr. Mangano explained that the proposed $32,167,342

Dr. Morris stated that there is a proposed statewide change to the charter reimbursement formula, which
will result in less State aid, but there is a pending bill in the House that the MMA has been in support of,

Additions to the budget include a supplemental mathematics program for grades 6-12, restorative
practices that have been successful at the high school and will be implemented at the middle school, and
the addition of special education programs to support the incoming 6th grade class.
Dr. Morris and Mr. Mangano stated that the full budget is available online.
Councilors asked for clarification about the costs of special education programs. Dr. Morris explained that
the incoming 6th grade class contains a higher number of students with IEPs, and that additionally,
families often move to the area seeking special education due to Amherst’s robust special education
programs. Transportation costs for special education continue to increase significantly.
Councilor Bahl-Milne stated that parents and students brought up issues during office hours regarding
incidents at the high school, and asked if the addition of funding for restorative services will address those
issues. Dr. Morris responded that yes, the adjustment counselor, sometimes called a social worker, will
work with students struggling with social/emotion needs.
Councilors asked about the proposed assessment method. Mr. Mangano stated that the proposed method
is better for Amherst compared to the State method. To determine what assessment method is best for
Amherst, the towns consider the percentage of enrollment, property values, income, and the relationship
between those numbers. There have been years that are more or less favorable to Amherst, this year’s
method is more favorable than the previous year’s method. While the District favors a consistent method
because it allows for predictability in Towns’ budgets, this method will be utilized for the next two years
and if no large increases are seen in the Towns, it could last longer.
Councilor Schoen asked about the MMA sponsored bill regarding charter schools, and how to
communicate with residents about advocating for the bill. Dr. Morris responded that the School
Committee has tried to take a more organized stance around advocacy, and will meet with our local
Representatives and Senator to discuss pending legislation. Dr. Morris encouraged councilors to join that
advocacy effort.
President Griesemer asked about revisions to the regional school agreement, and if those changes will be
brought to the Town Council for approval. Dr. Morris responded that attorneys for the Town and Schools
will work on legal clarification, as references to Town Meeting that no longer apply to Amherst; the
revisions will be brought to the Town Council for approval.
Councilor DuMont asked about solar options for the middle school roof, and a proposed solar canopy
over the high school parking lot, advocated by two high school juniors. Mr. Mangano, Dr. Morris, and
Mr. Bockelman spoke about potential solar at the schools. The middle school roof will be studied for
solar readiness and potential skylights, it is on the School Committee agenda, and a solar company is
coming to present a demonstration. An application was submitted to the Ways and Means Committee for
funding to study the feasibility of a parking lot canopy, led by State Representative Domb. If funding was
awarded, it would come through the Regional School Budget.

Councilor Hanneke asked about capital in general, and Mr. Mangano stated that there is a separate section
of the budget regarding capital, and the School Committee authorizes borrowing.
Councilor Steinberg asked about remaining funds if costs are lower than anticipated. Mr. Mangano
responded that there is not typically an excess of revenue, as it is usually offset on the expense side of the
budget. The Regional District can set aside up to 5% of their budget as “free cash.”
Councilor Schoen asked about charter school enrollment. Mr. Mangano responded that as local charter
schools expanded their grades over the last few years, we did lose students, but now we have seen some
students return to Amherst schools from the charter schools, and because local charter schools’ expansion
has ended, numbers should stabilize.
Councilor Bahl-Milne asked about regional school debt. Mr. Mangano explained the relationship between
regional school debt and the JCPC.
Councilor Steinberg announced that the Finance Committee will vote on the proposed budget Tuesday,
and the Town Council will vote on the budget later in April. The Regional School budget must be
approved by 3 out of the 4 towns; the proposed assessment method must be approved by all 4 towns; the
capital project issue must be addressed; and the Town must raise and appropriate funds to meet the
regional school assessment. The Finance Committee will make recommendations to the Town Council
regarding all four of those issues.
Councilor Steinberg adjourned the meeting of the finance committee.
MOVED: Councilor Hanneke moved, second by Councilor Schoen to adjourn the Special Town Council
meeting.
VOTED unanimously to adjourn at 7:13 p.m. on April 4, 2019.
