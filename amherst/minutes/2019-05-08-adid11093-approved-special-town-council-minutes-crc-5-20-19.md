President Griesemer asked about owners’ responsibility for providing parking. Mr. Malloy

Councilor Steinberg asked about conflicting values in the master plan. Ms. Brestrup responded

Mr. Malloy presented an overview of the Master Plan’s Affordable Housing chapter.
Councilor Ross asked about the federal Springfield Metropolitan Statistical Area in which
Amherst is included, and the area median income (AMI) calculation. Mr. Malloy explained that
what is considered affordable in Springfield may not be affordable for most people in Amherst. It
can be difficult for voucher holders to find housing due to those calculations.
Ms. Brestrup presented an overview of the Master Plan’s Transportation and Open Space and
Recreation chapters, and explained the process for Master Plan updates.
Councilor Schreiber asked about the Town Council’s role in Master Plan updates. Councilors
Griesemer and Hanneke provided an overview of the Planning Board’s and the Council’s
respective responsibilities under MGL c.14, §81D and Charter Section 9.8.
Councilor Ryan asked how the development of or updates to the Master Plan translate the
community’s broad vision to a planning tool for specific general areas to address issues affecting
certain neighborhoods. Councilor Schreiber spoke about the master plan for different parts of
Amherst and how they correspond to each other. Ms. Brestrup spoke about the challenges in
planning around what the state proposes for Route 9. The Valley CDC proposal would use a
comprehensive planning process, which would overlay the zoning process. We need to think
about village centers, hold public forums, and distribute information before moving forward.
Councilor Pam expressed an interest in learning more about the comprehensive permitting
process. Ms. Brestrup explained that a comprehensive permit application to waive zoning
requirements, including those under MGL chapter 40B, would be sought in instances where
someone wants to build something not permitted by the Town’s zoning. Mr. Malloy added that
the Town needs to balance the waivers with the Town’s need for affordable housing.
Councilor Hanneke remarked on some of the competing characteristics of Open Space and
Housing objectives and asked if there is a plan for Open Space and Housing thresholds. Ms.
Brestrup explained that the Town established a plan several years ago to focus development in
the downtown and village centers, and noted some of the plan’s features to mitigate development
sprawl. Councilor Hanneke asked how we keep the Town feeling like a small town; when there

Community Resources Committee - Public Comment
Stephen George, 23 Dana Street, Kate Troast, 99 Dana Street, Ann Yee, 29 Dana Place, and Tim
Atteridge, 143 Northampton Road individually expressed concerns with the proposed Valley
CDC low-income housing project on Northampton Road.
John Hornik, Chair of the Amherst Affordable Housing Trust explained that many issues raised
are under the purview of the Zoning Board of Appeals and expressed support for the project.
Councilors discussed the proposed bond issuance for the project in the CPAC proposals.
Councilor Schreiber noted that the Council needs to determine the best way to hear from both
sides. Councilor Steinberg explained the challenges associated with the full Council’s referral of
matters to different committees with different charges, and that he expects the Finance
Committee to discuss the financial aspects of the CPAC proposals.
Councilors continued to discuss the appropriate level for discussion on this matter. Councilor
Schreiber suggested that it should be at the full Council. Councilor Griesemer noted that the
Committee could decide to reconsider its prior vote if it so chose, and provided dates of
upcoming meetings at which the CPAC proposal would be discussed. Councilor Steinberg
explained that statute and the Charter provide a specific date by which budget determinations are
made, and that CPAC is a separate process that isn’t bound by the same deadlines. He noted that
the Council should determine the appropriate time to take this up. Councilor Griesemer noted
the Council could choose to delay action, and further explained that should the Committee not
reconsider its original vote, the Council is not bound to accept the Community Resources
Committee’s recommendation. Councilor Ryan noted his desire for the CRC to reconsider its
recommendation on the bond authorization.
Councilors discussed the nature and short tenure of the Council and its committees, and stressed
that it is learning as it goes. Discussion continued regarding the path this Council matter should
take, and reasons for the CRC’s initial vote to recommend.
Kate Troast, 99 Dana Street emphasized that the proposed Northampton Road development is
significant and it shouldn’t be rushed.
