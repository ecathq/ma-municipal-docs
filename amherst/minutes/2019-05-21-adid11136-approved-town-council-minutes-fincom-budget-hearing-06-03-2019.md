May 21, 2019 Special Town Council Meeting
Katharine Sims of 77 Dana Street offered comments regarding the 132 Northampton Rd project.
Councilor Steinberg explained what will happen at that Finance Committee meeting. CPAC will present
and public comments will be welcome. It is Finance Committee’s opportunity to hear why CPAC is
making their various recommendations. Ms. Sims urged a delayed vote on the Valley CDC funding.
Project seems to potentially impact Town costs. Meaningful input on 2016 Housing Production Plan has
not really happened. Maybe this will be the opportunity to provide input. Cited sections of HPP
regarding public input. Request Council incorporate public input prior to making a decision.
A resident of Northampton Rd asked why the amount for funding the project is not in the budget. Ms.
Aldrich explained that it is a borrowing authorization, but debt service payments are not there yet.
Councilor Steinberg and Mr. Bockelman noted that page 189 of budget book shows the Town’s debt
service. Councilor Steinberg added that the borrowing authorization would affect the budget in the
future.
David Robertson of 39 Northampton Rd, and Physician and Master’s in Public Health, expressed his
supports of affordable housing. One lesson in field of medicine is that doing things with good intentions
doesn’t necessarily have positive outcomes. Attended meeting on April 24. Mr. Robertson expressed
concerns regarding transportation and access to community services, and social services for residents. The
majority of residents will have limited transportation. This population might be homeless or under care of
department of mental health, far from transportation. Safety concern with no sidewalks to shopping. One
possible solution would be for Valley CDC to offer shuttle service. Proposed expansion with sidewalks
on Rt. 9 may alleviate some of the concern. Optimistic timeline for that is 2023. During that construction,
residents will be further interrupted. Social service concerns – often severe mental illness or substance
abuse issues. Twelve out of 28 will be actively homeless or under DMH care. What onsite services will be
available for residents in crisis (relapse)? Valley CDC said a day manager would be available. Nothing
overnight. The Town acknowledges this is an at-risk population. Homeless women are a specific
population at risk. High percentage have been victims of abuse. How will Valley CDC protect women
when 70% of residents will be men? This SRO would be the largest that Valley CDC has ever operated.
President Griesemer responded that at some point a community conversation will have to be held to come
up with resolution on how to move forward from where we are today. Need to look at all the issues from
all perspectives. Before the Council can go forward, this needs to happen. Councilor Steinberg added that
during the Finance Committee’s budget hearing, the role of the Finance Committee is to look at financial
aspects. Councilor Brewer added that one of the reasons for the conversation is that the Council needs to
understand its role. Explained roles of ZBA, Town Manager (CEO), and Council. The only way the
Council can participate is with regard to funding.
Katharine Troast of 99 Dana Street – submitted written concerns, and President Griesemer turned them
over to Clerk of Council for distribution later. Ms. Troast commented that she is concerned that residents
want to be heard.

residents.
