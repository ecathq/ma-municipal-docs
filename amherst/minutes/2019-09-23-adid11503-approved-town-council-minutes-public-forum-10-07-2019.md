September 23, 2019 Town Council Public Forum
Public Comment:
Centennial
Sarah Serwa, of 21 Rosemary Lane, asked about other options before upgrading Centennial, and stated
that residents on fixed incomes will be affected by water rate increases.
G. Mooring spoke about alternatives and the cost of failing to get Centennial up and running, thereby
eliminating one of the water sources in Town. Permitting process for new sources is uncertain, approval
by the Department of Environmental Protection (DEP) is not guaranteed. Water quality is poorest in the
summer, due to an increase in frequency of thunderstorms; water quality improves after the summer.
Lyons Witten, of 120 Pulpit Hill Road and Chair of the Water Supply Protection Committee, spoke about
the benefit of three available water sources in Town versus two, about the uncertainty of certification of
new water sources by DEP in the future, and recommend that the Town continue the process for
upgrading the Centennial water treatment plant.
Chris Hoffman, of 61 Arnold Road, Pelham, commented that it would be a shame to not utilize the
current water resource, asked about upgrades to the water and sewer lines servicing the plant, and asked
about how Pelham residents can provide input.
G. Mooring responded that there is no immediate need to upgrade the two sections referenced, and stated
that there is an operational solution to the handling of the sewer flow. The project must go through the site
plan review and Conservation Commission process in Pelham, where there would be an opportunity for
public comment. Ideally, the new plant would be set back from the road further than the current facility.
S. Serwa spoke in favor of upgrading well 5 and exploring less costly alternatives.
Hickory Ridge
Tom Joyce, of 19 Alyssum Drive, and a previous member of the golf course commented that the cart
paths on the course are not all accessible during the whole year, some of the property floods during part of
the year, raised questions about access to the property by emergency and maintenance vehicles, proposed
that the Town lease the clubhouse to a restaurant, and spoke against acting too quickly on this project.
D. Ziomek stated that there is an understanding that part of the property would be inaccessible during part
of the year, which is what nature does in a flood plain. Some parts of the course could be more accessible
than others. There is a need for an in-depth master plan for the property. The project will come before the
Finance Committee in early October, and the Town can present more specifics on re-use of the property.
The clubhouse may be demolished if there is not a better use for the building.
Bruce Stedman, of 20 Mt Holyoke Drive and Executive Director of the Conway School of Landscape
Design, spoke in favor of the purchase of the property, and urged public engagement in creating the
master plan for the project.
President Griesemer closed the public forum at 7:12 p.m.
