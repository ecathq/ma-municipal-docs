Agenda item 7.a. Action Items: Temporary Public Way Request – 26 Spring Street
Archipelago Investments representative Kyle Wilson spoke about the request for four parking spaces on
Spring Street from October 15, 2019 through January 15, 2021. The request is for use of the sidewalk and
adjacent parking spaces. The Town would be reimbursed for the use of the parking spaces.
Councilor Steinberg asked about disability access and if there was consultation with the Disability Access
Advisory Committee (DAAC). P. Bockelman responded that staff advises referral to DAAC.

amount for street and sidewalk repair is so low, and if the fence will obstruct the street beyond the parking
spaces so that two cars may not pass at the site. K. Wilson responded that the street beyond the parking

Committee.

reservation could be requested to cover the period between referral and Council action.

walking through a construction site. The scaffolding used in major metro areas surrounds a fireproof

barriers.

6. Presentations and Discussion
a. University of Massachusetts Public-Private Partnership (P-3) Project Discussion
University of Massachusetts Staff Present:
Tony Maroulis, Executive Director of External Relations and University Events
Nancy Buffone, Associate Vice Chancellor of University Relations
Shane Conklin, Associate Vice Chancellor of Facilities and Campus Services
Councilors Brewer, DuMont, Griesemer, Ross, and Ryan disclosed their relationships with the University.
Representatives from UMass spoke about the project, which has been in process for six years. The project
would alleviate housing pressure on local neighborhoods. Public private partnership (P-3) is a financing
option to provide housing to students without placing a debt burden on the University. After meeting with
current residents, the University explored ways to keep families with children in their current school
district. Staff are meeting with families individually to ensure they are relocated within the Wildwood
district, and the University will cover moving expenses. Families who are displaced will be offered space
in the new units at their current rates. The University has begun to work on a Request for Proposal, and
the work will begin in earnest in late fall 2019.
Councilor questions:
Councilors expressed concern regarding the closing of the family center.
Councilors asked about the eventual increase in housing capacity. UMass representatives responded that
50% of the current North Village units are offline, there will be an increase in beds at the Lincoln Avenue
Apartments, and the number of units at North Village will remain the same.

Councilor Brewer expressed concern about the lack of play areas at the proposed relocation complexes,
