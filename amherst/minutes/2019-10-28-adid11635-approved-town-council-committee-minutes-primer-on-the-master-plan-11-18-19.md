October 28, 2019 Special Town Council Meeting – Primer on the Master Plan
Councilor Schoen observed that the Master Plan lays out a vision of a walkable downtown and village
centers with public spaces and bike ways, yet zoning provisions allow for narrow sidewalks and buildings
built to the edge and do not address the width of the road. Schoen referred to a sidewalk downtown and a
new building in North Amherst that will prevent later widening to enable buses and bikes. Thus, zoning
can be barriers to achieving the Master plan goals and vision.
Councilor Pam asked about affordable housing and preserving neighborhoods.
Councilor Steinberg asked how the Master Plan works when it can be in conflict with itself.
C. Brestrup responded that the Planning Department has a list of zoning amendments that we are working
on, including buildings set back from the street; the Planning Department and Planning Board are aware
of issues and will work with the Community Resources Committee and the Council to address those
issues. The current developments are located in parts of town that do not have that downtown character.
New developments in areas with deteriorated buildings or parking lots, not tearing down anything
charming. Small affordable developments is a good idea. Developers have trouble securing funding for
less than 40-75 units. Trying to use all the different mechanisms that we can. Have achieved a good
number of units using inclusionary zoning. In terms of the land use policy map, some areas marked for
development and preservation overlap, we will look at those conflicts in the future. Some conflicts may
not be resolved.
Councilor DuMont commented that the Energy and Climate Action Committee will bring forward goals
for the town and they fall within master plan categories. Eventually climate goals should be integrated
into the Master Plan.
Councilor Ryan asked about affordable housing, and how communities determine what percentage of its
housing stock should be affordable housing. What about people who are 80-100% AMI. Senior housing
and aging community. Stabilizing neighborhoods, student rentals have been issues. What can a
community do to stabilize neighborhoods. D. Ziomek responded that in terms of stabilizing
neighborhoods, Amherst has done good work in the last 8-10 years with rental registration and bylaw.
Broke down some of the silos between Inspections, Planning, Zoning, Police, and Fire to work on trouble
spots in town. Neighborhoods have seen improvement in quality of life in last 6-10 years.
