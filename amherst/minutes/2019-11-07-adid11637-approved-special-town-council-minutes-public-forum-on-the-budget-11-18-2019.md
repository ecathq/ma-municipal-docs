2. Presentations and Discussion - Amherst Financial Indicators
President Griesemer explained that this is a preliminary budget hearing, with the goal of sharing early and
obtaining public comment. She referenced the Special Town Council meeting early today at 6 p.m. on the
Preliminary Financial Indicators, held jointly with the Amherst School Committee and the Jones Library
Board of Trustees.
P. Bockelman reviewed the PowerPoint entitled Public Forum on Proposed FY21 Town Budget, which is
a briefer presentation than at the earlier joint meeting. Priorities include financial stability; sustainability
and resiliency (energy efficiency and addressing climate change, for example); planning for the future,
including the downtown, village centers and affordable housing; building community, for example
through age-friendly improvements; the pending infrastructure needs at the school(s), Department of
Public Works headquarters, Fire headquarters, and Library, along with roads, sidewalks and crosswalks.
The model for capital projects is to build reserves, move up the commitment to capital, and maintain a
strong bond rating.

November 7, 2019
Special Town Council Meeting
Public Forum on the Budget
Between now and February, Bockelman will meet with departments to continue to review the budget. The
deadline for the Town Council to adopt the budget is June 30.
President Griesemer opened the floor to public comments, starting with the Fire department.
David Clooney, Fire Department member, asked for an increase in staff funding. The minimum staffing
has remained at 7 since 1992, despite multiple studies done over the years. There have been recent
occurrences when a single firefighter responded to a call due to lack of personnel to respond to the calls.
Denise Barberet of North Whitney Street, asked why if we can find the $100M needed for capital
projects, can we not find the funds needed to provide for the Fire Department staffing.
Bonnie MacCracken of Chadwick Court, and mother of an Amherst firefighter, questioned why additional
staffing is not added when UMass brings more resident students to town. During a recent fire call,
ambulances had to be called from other towns to respond to EMT calls, three of which happened to be at
student residences. Not only should the staffing be increased, but also the town should provide a state-of-
the-art firehouse.
Dawn Schabach of West Street, daughter of a long-time Amherst firefighter, urged for an adequate fire
station and increased staffing.
Liz Welsh of Aubinwood Road, home-care nurse and the parent of a firefighter, expressed concern about
firefighter staffing.
Sarah McKee, and Amherst resident, spoke on the staffing configuration at the Jones Library, many being
kept under 20 hours a week with no benefits. One consequence is that there is high turn-over in these part-
time jobs.
Sharon Povinelli of Montague Road, responded to comments on general town services, suggesting that it
would be useful to have a police officer in the downtown at night.
Don Hafner of Shays Street asked about the impact of the University and Colleges on the budget.
Bockelman responded that these institutions are factored in for water and sewage usage. UMass and
Amherst have their own police force; Hampshire does not have their own force. Town fire responds to all
three institutions; there is only a surcharge for additional services on particular weekends. The Town is
under negotiations with UMass relating to students and staff with children in Amherst schools.
Judith Williams of Shays Street asked about business taxes included in revenues. Bockelman responded
that there is not a separate business property tax, although some do pay additional personal property tax,
as appropriate.
3. Adjourn
