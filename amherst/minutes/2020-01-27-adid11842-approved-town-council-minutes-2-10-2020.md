1. Call to Order
President Griesemer declared the presence of a quorum and called the meeting to order at 6:30 p.m.
Councilors present: Councilors Bahl-Milne, Brewer, De Angelis, DuMont, Griesemer, Hanneke, Pam,
Ross, Ryan, Schoen, Schreiber, Steinberg, and Swartz.
Councilors absent: None.
Councilors participating remotely: None
Others present: Town Manager Paul Bockelman, Assistant Town Manager David Ziomek, Economic
Development Director Geoff Kravitz, Executive Director of the Downtown Amherst Business
Improvement District Gabrielle Gould, and Clerk of the Council Athena O’Keeffe.
President Griesemer announced audio and video recording by Amherst Media.
2. Announcements
President Griesemer announced Councilors and staff attended the Massachusetts Municipal Association
Conference over the weekend. The Four Towns meeting will be Saturday, February 1 at the Amherst
Regional Middle School Library at 9:00 am. On February 1 at 1:00 p.m., there will be a Black History
Month Flag Raising ceremony on the steps of Town Hall. On February 10, the Council will hold two
public hearings: to consider a Verizon petition to install conduit location on Spring Street and on the
installation of a ramp at the Visitor Information Center in the public way.
Valley Community Development Corporation has submitted their eligibility letter for the Chapter 40B
Comprehensive Permit to the Department of Housing and Community Development. The state has not
sent an acknowledgment and 30-day comment period has not begun, but the comment period will be
announced when it begins.

6. Presentations and Discussion
b. Proposed Downtown Investments
Town Manager Paul Bockelman spoke about the need for investment in the downtown, and outlined the
projects that the Town will bring to the Council in the future.
Assistant Town Manager David Ziomek spoke about the status and timeline of the Kendrick Park project.
Economic Development Director Geoff Kravitz and Executive Director of the Downtown Amherst
Business Improvement District Gabrielle Gould spoke about installation of a Performing Arts Shell on the
common.
D. Ziomek spoke about improvements to the North Common, which were presented to the Select Board
in the fall of 2018. The Historical Commission and the Leisure Services and Supplemental Education
commissions lead the effort. A partial funding package was approved by Town Meeting. The preferred
plan seeks to celebrate the Town Hall and resolve the many issues the common currently faces.
G. Kravitz spoke about parking in the downtown, and the potential for a public-private partnership to fund
a parking garage.
P. Bockelman spoke about other improvements in the downtown such as the roundabout on Triangle
Street, walkway on Amity Street, and sidewalks. Future improvements will replace walkways and
crosswalks. The Town is looking outside public funds to improve downtown spaces.

MOTION: Councilor Schoen moved, second by Councilor Hanneke, to refer Section 3.4 Responsible
Employer for Public Construction Projects to the Government, Organization, and Legislation Committee
to make a recommendation whether wording deleted from the bylaw in three instances should be added
back to avoid the risk of weakening the bylaw.. Specifically, add back: 1) the requirement that bidders
"agree in writing that they shall comply" to referenced labor laws; 2) Requirement that
contractors/subcontractors shall "certify under oath and in writing on a weekly basis that they are in
compliance with such obligations"; and 3. The penalty of "liquidated damages payable to the Town in the
amount of 5% of the dollar value of the contract." that was deleted from possible actions in the event of
noncompliance.
Councilor Schoen spoke to the motion, noting that other cities and towns have similar provisions.
Councilor Ross commented that the revision came from the Town’s legal counsel, and asked how the
Governance, Organization, and Legislation Committee address the questions without legal input. G.
Kravitz agreed that the revisions came from the Town’s attorney, and recalled that legal counsel
instructed that state law was sufficient.
VOTED unanimously to refer Section 3.4 Responsible Employer for Public Construction Projects to the
Government, Organization, and Legislation Committee to make a recommendation whether wording
deleted from the bylaw in three instances should be added back to avoid the risk of weakening the bylaw..
Specifically, add back: 1) the requirement that bidders "agree in writing that they shall comply" to
referenced labor laws; 2) Requirement that contractors/subcontractors shall "certify under oath and in
writing on a weekly basis that they are in compliance with such obligations"; and 3. The penalty of

was deleted from possible actions in the event of noncompliance.
