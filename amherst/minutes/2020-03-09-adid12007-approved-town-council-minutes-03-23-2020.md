Agenda item 3. Hearings
a. 6:30 p.m. Public Hearing on Proposed Parking Regulation Changes to Lincoln Avenue
President Griesemer outlined the Council’s authority in regulating the public ways, and the steps taken by
the Town to inform residents and abutters of the public hearing.
Superintendent of Public Works Guilford Mooring outlined the proposed changes to the regulations on
Lincoln Avenue, and the rationale of the proposed changes.
Questions from Councilors:
Councilor Hanneke stated that the Downtown Parking Working Group report disagrees with the proposed
changes. Asked for clarification of if the west side of Amity and Elm is a no parking zone. G. Mooring
responded that it is a no parking zone; there is a high incidence of sign theft.
Councilor Swartz asked about people parking on the west side of Lincoln.
Councilor Schreiber stated that we need more of a guideline of what is an acceptable width of a street
with parking on one or both sides.

Councilor Schoen commented that if we restrict parking on Lincoln, people may park more on Sunset
Avenue. Police Chief Scott Livingstone responded that that could happen. G. Mooring added that Sunset
is not a preferred travel route to campus.
Councilor Hanneke asked how to determine what constitutes a safety issue, as there are other streets that
are impassable with cars parked on both sides. G. Mooring agreed that parking issues change constantly
when students come and go.
Councilor De Angelis took issue with G. Mooring’s statement that high school students parking in
neighborhoods is a “nicer” problem. G. Mooring explained that high school student drivers do not appear
in masse, but become drivers through the year.
President Griesemer asked the Council to restrict the discussion to the proposed changes.
Councilor Steinberg echoed Councilor Schreiber’s comment about the width of streets, and that there are
narrower streets with differing regulations, and spoke in favor of a committee creating a set of broader
based standards to judge this and other streets.
Fire Captain Jeff Olmstead spoke about the width needed to maneuver fire trucks and public safety
vehicles around corners.
Councilor Schreiber asked about restricting parking for a short period, like 8-10 a.m.
Councilor De Angelis commented about special treatment of residents versus working class students and
workers.
Public asking questions:
An Amherst resident who lives at the bottom of Gaylord Street asked about restricting parking on one
side of the street and installing speed bumps.
Jim Barna, Amherst resident of 34 Dana Place and UMass employee, asked if the Council would consider
the rights of people who use this resource, and refrain from discriminating against students and
employees.
Matea Cramer, Amherst resident of East Pleasant Street asked about banning parking in a “hot spot”
between Elm and McClellan Streets.
Anik Pourer, Amherst resident of Gaylord Street, asked if the Council will consider speed bumps and
suggested installing a traffic light at the corner of Lincoln and Amity.
Julian, a young Amherst resident, asked if there is a rule about parking in proximity to an intersection.
Rick Pelletier, Amherst resident of Lincoln Avenue, asked if the Council knows how many children live
on Lincoln Ave.
Public speaking in favor:
David Slovitor, Amherst resident of 194 Lincoln Avenue, spoke in favor of the proposal.
Nancy Gilbert, Amherst resident of 166 Lincoln Avenue, spoke about safety concerns and delivery
vehicles.
Julian, a young Amherst resident, spoke in support of fixing the issue on Lincoln as well as fixing similar
issues on many other streets in Amherst.
Frank Meyers, Amherst resident of Lincoln Avenue, spoke about safety concerns regarding the volume of
traffic, the width of the street, and ambulances maneuvering on the street.

Louise Reilly, Amherst resident of Lincoln Avenue, spoke about her frustration with the speed of cars on
her section of Lincoln Avenue.
Bruce Wilcox, Amherst resident of 191 Lincoln Avenue, spoke in support of the proposed changes.
Anick Pourer, Amherst resident of Gaylord Street, commented about noise.

onto Lincoln.
Dale Peterson, Amherst resident of 234 Lincoln Avenue, spoke about safety concerns on Lincoln between
Route 9 and McClellan Street, and asked that the Council not delay action.
Rick Pelletier, Amherst resident of 160 Lincoln Avenue, spoke about people parking in front of fire
hydrants and who are not ticketed, and stated that the number of accidents per year on Lincoln equal the
number of children living on the street.
Public speaking in opposition:
Matea Cramer, Amherst resident of East Pleasant Street, commented about Amherst being the kind of
Town that give homeowners special treatment.
Jim Barna, Amherst resident of 34 Dana Place, suggested ways of mitigating traffic without losing
parking, such as restricting commercial vehicles on the street, and adding a four-way stop at Amity and
Lincoln. J. Barna spoke in opposition to removing free parking.
Fletcher Clark, Amherst resident of East Pleasant Street, spoke in opposition to the proposed changes.
Sue Crouse, Amherst resident of Lincoln Avenue, spoke about parking as a resource, and displacing the
problem onto neighbors will not solve problems. S. Crouse spoke in opposition to the proposal.
Questions from Councilors:
Councilor Brewer asked how to prevent parking in front of fire hydrants and too close to driveways. Chief
Livingstone responded that outside the Town center, ticketing is by complaint and no parking areas in
front of fire hydrants are not clearly marked.
Councilor Schreiber stated that in order to slow traffic, allow parking on Lincoln. Bicycles are permitted
on the sidewalk outside of the Town center. The speed bumps are a problem for bikes when cars veer
around them. If there is an emergency on Lincoln, cars will find a way of getting out of the way. Public
safety is over-thought.
Councilor Ross asked about alternatives to the proposed changes; suggested making Lincoln and Sunset
opposite one-way streets. The average speed of 19 MPH when the posted limit is 30 MPH appears to be a
direct result of the parked cars on the street. Chief Livingstone responded that he does not believe there is
a speeding problem on Lincoln, eliminating parking may create a speed issue.
G. Mooring spoke about past analysis of the Lincoln-Amity intersection and how it was dealt with in
other neighborhoods near the university. Residents did not want to make the streets one way.
Councilor De Angelis asked about the benefit of making the end of Lincoln a dead end.
Councilor Pam asked why the University does not make cheaper parking further from campus.
Councilor De Angelis stated that it boils down to only residents be accommodated versus people who
come to Town for other reasons.

Councilor Hanneke asked about the purpose of regulating parts of Lincoln differently. G. Mooring spoke
about the establishment of resident and downtown parking permits.
Councilor Schoen suggested making cheaper lots for commuters.
Councilor Pam commented about the age of the data presented, commented about new buildings with no
parking, and spoke in opposition to a Town wide parking plan.
Councilor Schreiber asked about speeding on Lincoln if parking is removed. Chief Livingstone stated that
he expects speeding will increase if parking is eliminated. G. Mooring added that the traffic counter was
placed in a section with no parking on either side of the street, and that speeds would likely be higher at
sections of the street with parked cars.
Councilor Swartz asked about the danger of cars parking near hydrants, and suggested painting curbs to
indicate no parking areas. Captain Olmstead responded that accidents happen when vehicles are
maneuvering close to parked vehicles; painting around hydrants is a good idea.
Councilor Schreiber asked about speeds in zones where cars are parked. G. Mooring responded that when
the Town owns more traffic counters, we will be able to collect more data.
Councilor Brewer stated that if the Council were to accept the changes, when would signage change and
see actual changes. G. Mooring responded that it would be about a month to install signs.
Councilor Hanneke asked if the Fire Dept. and EMS use Lincoln as a cut through. Captain Olmstead
responded that the Fire Dept. does use Lincoln as a cut through.
