(2) Candidate Interviews
President Griesemer summarized the process the Town Council and School Committee developed and
steps taken to fill the school committee vacancy up to this point.
Candidates made brief opening statements, and then answered the following questions posed by President
Griesemer and Chair McDonald.
1. Based upon your opening statement and the description of the responsibilities and
expectations of School Committee members, is there anything else you would like to add that
additionally describes your ability to fulfill these responsibilities and expectations.
2. Similarly, based upon your opening statement, is there anything else you would like to add to
the reasons you are interested in serving on the Amherst and Amherst-Pelham Regional
School Committees and why should we select you?
3. What is your past or present experience or connection with the Amherst Elementary or
Amherst-Pelham Regional Public Schools?
4. What do you think is one strength and one area for improvement in Amherst Elementary and
Amherst-Pelham Regional Public Schools?
5. The public often has a limited understanding of the role the School Committee plays in
school or district decisions such as math curriculum, hiring of principals, or in addressing
parent complaints about an individual teacher. What is your understanding of the role you as
a School Committee member should play in these types of decisions?
6. The committee is expected to collaborate with and hold the Superintendent accountable at the
same time. How would you approach this dual role to achieve the best outcomes for students?
7. How do you deal with differing views when engaging in a committee decision-making
process?
 Extra Question: Is here anything in your work, travel, or other plans that will keep you
away from Amherst and so that you are not able to attend meetings in person regularly and
fulfill other duties of the committee?
8. The school building project that was accepted by the MSBA in December is one-building for
K through 5 or 6 that would replace Fort River and Wildwood schools, with the location of
the project as yet to be determined. As a member of the School Committee, what will you
contribute to the work of this project?
9. What challenges do you foresee in moving the school project forward? What are your ideas to
meet those challenges?
10. What is one important issue you believe the Amherst Elementary School Committee will
need to address over the next 21 months?
11. What is one important issue you believe the Amherst Regional School Committee will need
to address over the next 21 months?
12. In what ways could the School Committee increase engagement and input from more
residents and a more diverse mix of residents with important upcoming School Committee
decisions?
Candidates made brief concluding remarks.

President Griesemer confirmed that Councilors and School Committee members can hear and be heard.
President Griesemer reminded the Council and School Committee members of the process for the
discussion, including that discussion should focus on School Committee responsibilities and expectations
and criteria for selection but not specific candidates or support or non-support for specific candidates.
Peter Demling thanked the candidates, and spoke about matching the candidates to the published
description of the School Committee, and spoke in support of selecting Heather Lord.
Allison McDonald thanked the candidates.
Kerry Spitzer commented that the candidates are well-qualified and expressed hope that they remain
engaged.
Ben Herrington thanked the candidates.
Councilor Pam commented about challenges regarding financial issues the schools will face.
Councilor Swartz commented that all the candidates appear very qualified, and stated that it was not ok
that a school committee member spoke in favor of one candidate given the stated limits of the discussion
period.
Councilor Schoen expressed hope that candidates that are not selected find a role to help in the coming
financial challenges. Need for candidate to understand regional and elementary school budgets.
Councilor Bahl-Milne spoke about the importance of knowledge of the budget, community engagement
skills, and about the importance of lived experience.
