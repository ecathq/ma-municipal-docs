2. Hearings – Public Hearing of the Finance Committee on the Amherst-Pelham Regional
School District Budget for Fiscal Year 2021 - Charter Sec. 5.5(a)
3. Presentations and Discussion – Amherst-Pelham Regional School District Budget
Assessment Method
Superintendent Morris spoke about the proposed Regional School budget, which maintains staff and met
Council budget guidelines. M. Morris spoke about reopening school in the fall; the Department of
Elementary and Secondary Education (DESE) has not released guidelines for reopening yet.
Regional School Finance Director Doug Slaughter spoke about the following:
 The budget and assessment approval process

---

 Process to amend the budget
 Amherst assessments in the past four years
 Budget additions and reductions
 Changes to the Capital Plan
Councilor Steinberg opened the floor for questions.
Councilor Schoen asked if prepayments were due to budget excesses.
M. Morris responded that the budget was frozen as of March 13 to preserve staffing, excesses are not
something you can count on each year.
Councilor Schoen asked about the status of CARES Act funding.
M. Morris spoke about pending legislation on the federal level.
Councilor Schoen asked about state aid.
D. Slaughter responded that the state typically gives about $10M.
Councilor Schoen asked about increases of central and school administrative staffing.
M. Morris responded that the budget is based on level services, no new administrative cuts, not reducing
sections or teacher load, and no program or course cuts.
Councilor Schoen asked about non-union staff cost of living (COLA) increase.
M. Morris responded that non-union staff received a 0% increase last fiscal year, a 2% COLA felt fair for
the FY21.
Councilor Schoen notied that the substitute line was not higher.
M. Morris responded that we did not see a scenario where secondary students are at school each day, so
did not feel the need to increase the substitute line.
Councilor Hanneke asked if the proposed budget is reasonable given known guidelines regarding
COVID-19, and if the schools will need a mid-year increase to the budget.
M. Morris responded that the regional school committee could engage the towns if additional funding is
needed, the regional schools have reserves, and there is also a school choice line. DESE guidance was
regarding summer school. The challenge will be how to meet greater number of students’ needs. No
scenario where 950 students can be in the high schools at one time.
Councilor Pam asked about health coverage if teachers catch COVID-19.
D. Slaughter responded.
Councilor Hanneke asked about scenarios in which state aid decreases.
D. Slaughter responded; M. Morris added that if state aid drops by a significant amount, school would not
be able to run next year.
There were no public comments or questions.
M. Theilman asked if guidelines would apply to the fall or the whole year.
