2. Capital Improvement Program Public Forum – Charter Sec. 5.7(d)
Per Charter Sec. 1.7, a Public Forum shall mean a meeting during which more than one-half of the
meeting time on the agenda is devoted to public comment.
Finance Director Sean Mangano briefly spoke about the recommended projects for FY21, funding sources
and the five-year Capital Improvements plan.
Evan O’Neil of 6 Smith Street spoke about a request for sidewalk funding in June, and was happy to hear
that there is an emphasis on sidewalk funding.
Toni Cunningham thanked the Finance Committee and JCPC for their requests for reports for unspent
capital, glad to see that will part of the process going forward.
Ludmilla Pavlova-Gillham asked about borrowing capacity and how far along we are in the process.
S. Mangano responded that the Town is in the hundred million dollar range.

Comptroller Sonia Aldrich stated that the Town is at about $113M.
Ludmilla Pavlova-Gillham asked about moving forward with Jones Library and Elementary school
projects.
President Griesemer spoke about the four major capital projects the Town had been considering prior to
the COVID-19: a new elementary school building, library building, public safety building and public
works facility.
Councilor Schoen stated that the debt capacity is one number, how much money can be allocated to pay
back loans; if we have enough of an economic recovery after COVID-19 will be able to borrow more.
Town Manager Paul Bockelman stated that it is a challenge to finance those projects to the Town and
taxpayers, and that the timeline is driven by a grant cycle for schools and library.
Councilor Pam asked if the school grant is going forward, will we be able to go forward with library.
Sean Mangano spoke about pre-Covid ability to fund four major projects, the future will depend on
economic recovery.
