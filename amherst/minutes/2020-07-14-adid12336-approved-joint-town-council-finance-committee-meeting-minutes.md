Chief Livingstone spoke briefly about vacancies due to recent retirements and resignations.
Schoen asked about the hiring process for the three current and upcoming vacancies.
Livingstone responded that the recruitment process began in January; it is not unusual for the department
to have one or more vacancies.
Schoen asked about a mental health advocate teaming with an officer.
Livingstone spoke about the benefits and restrictions of a team of mental health professional with an
officer.
Steinberg asked about a domestic violence advocate pairing with an officer.
Livingstone responded that domestic violence calls are more dangerous.
Hanneke asked about the grant funding for a domestic violence specialist, and asked what will happen to
the position if grant funding is not renewed.
Captain Young responded that the department reapplied for grant funding and if approved it will extend
the position for a length of time.
Livingstone added that if funding for the position is not renewed, the department will need to reevaluate
the position.
Griesemer asked about police-initiated calls versus follow-up calls.
Livingstone spoke about police follow-up that are often categorized as “police-initiated” calls.
Captain Ting added that the activity is predicated on problems in each neighborhood in Amherst. The
police do not target a neighborhood, but response is based on calls for service.
Griesemer asked if police are in neighborhoods for calls and follow-up, or if they are patrolling those
neighborhoods.
Captain Ting spoke about officers spending a lot of time in an area due to a call, response, and follow-up.
Amherst Fire Chief Tim Nelson spoke about policing that needs to change, not the police. The Amherst
police department is part of a team that includes the Health and Fire departments. Chief Nelson spoke
about his life experience as a Black man.
Pam commented that there might be some restructuring of follow-up calls; some calls need not be made
by an officer, spoke about residents’ feeling of being policed, and commented that the Town cannot
ignore that the feeling of being policed is real.
Chief Livingstone reiterated that officer-initiated calls are for follow up to calls.
P. Bockelman spoke about engaging in the conversation about how Amherst does “policing” and systemic
change.
DuMont pointed out the attendees that have indicated they wish to make comments, and asked that they
be allowed to speak.

De Angelis thanked the members of the Police department for attending today, and asked if there is public
comment from people who did not speak at the budget hearing last night.
Hanneke stated that out of respect for staff, the committee should get through the general government
portion of the meeting so staff can move on with their work.
2. FY21 budget: General Government Budget, and Debt
General Government
Town Manager Paul Bockelman stated the budget shows an increase of 21.5%; the Clerk of the Council
position shifted departments and had not been budgeted in the Town Manager department previously. The
other change is the reallocation of the Communications officer position. Travel expenses were removed
from the budget.
Griesemer stated there will probably not be a physical Massachusetts Municipal Association meeting this
year, and the Council budget includes funds for minute takers for Council committee meetings.
Schoen asked about legal fees, and asked if there is a team that can come together and do research on
behalf of the Council.
Bockelman responded that the Town Manager is the Council’s staff, and can delegate if needed to
research and report back.
Aldrich added that we can add a separate line for legal fees next year and historic data can be added to the
budget book.
Mangano stated the Finance team is comprised of Accounting, Treasury, and Assessing. Staff worked
through the pandemic and have done great work keeping the Town going. Mangano spoke about the
funding strategy for the department, achievements, and challenges.
Members spoke about financial goals.
Hegner asked about property values.
Assessor Elizabeth Duffy spoke about options to increase the PILOT payments.
Hegner asked about parking trends.
Hanneke asked about the review of evaluations, and paper payroll checks rather than electronic transfers
as a less costly alternative.
Mangano responded that the Town is trying to move away from paper checks but have not made direct
deposit a requirement.
Holly Bowser added that there were some bargaining unit changes regarding direct deposit, there are
some employees who have been grandfathered in and are still receiving paper checks.
Members discussed valuations and negotiations of PILOT payments from UMass and Amherst College.
Pam stated parking permits increased due to new buildings that do not have adequate parking and that the
fees for permits are too low.
Human Resources and Human Rights
Town Manager Paul Bockelman stated the HR Director and HR Manager run the department. The
currently vacant HR Director position has been advertised, and some applications have been received.
Pam stated she hopes to devote more resources to this area in coming years.
Mangano spoke about insurance rates.
Hegner asked about health insurance for regional school employees.

Members spoke about health insurance, liability insurance, and workers compensation insurance
payments.
Members asked about the opt-out health insurance incentive program.
IT
IT Director Sean Hannon stated that a portion of the communication manager position was moved to the
Town Manager department, spoke about a cybersecurity training grant, permitting software, fiber I-Net
new fiber to be installed, new GIS map, and indoor and outdoor WIFI upgrades.
Pam suggested that the Town provide WIFI to apartment complexes.
Griesemer agreed the Town needs to make WIFI more accessible to more areas in Town.
Schoen asked about technology compatibility in a new school building.
Members discussed WIFI in downtown and how WIFI could be provided to a greater portion of Town
residents.
Town Clerk
Town Clerk Shavena Martin spoke about operations through the COVID-19 crisis, department goals, and
accomplishments.
Hanneke asked why issuance of dog license is through the Town Clerk and enforcement is police, and
noted the conflict of interest service levels much higher one year.
Martin responded that per MGL, dog licensing is responsibility of Town Clerk. Conflict of interest
certification must be completed every other year, new employees only would have completed in 2018 and
all employees in 2019.
Theilman asked about students voting from out of town. Martin responded that students registered in
Amherst and unable to come to a polling place on Election Day can vote by mail.
Theilman asked how we can avoid people voting twice. Martin spoke about the statewide voter
registration system (VRIS).
General Services
Town Manager Paul Bockelman stated this includes postage, phones, and the annual audit.
Members discussed the local option for personal exemptions of veteran surviving spouse, veteran disabled
Purple Heart surviving parents, spouse, legally blind and senior.
Griesemer noted that this is not the same program as seniors who can work to offset their taxes.
Theilman asked about the East Street School bond.

5. General public comment
Bryan Monesson-Olson, Amherst resident, spoke about police departments as racist organizations and
spoke in favor of defunding the Amherst Police Department (APD).
Kinga McCraven, an Amherst schools and UMass graduate, commented that the Council was
dispassionate when hearing public comment at the public hearing on the budget, and spoke about the need
for a space for Black community in Amherst, and spoke in favor of cutting the APD budget by 52%.
DW, an Amherst schools graduate, spoke about the lack of representation in Town government, and
spoke about the need for transformational justice practices, solutions outside of policing that work, and
spoke in favor of cutting the APD budget by 52%.

Amilcar Shabazz, Amherst resident and UMass employee, spoke about the need to reassess and rethink
the budget. Need to start the process now of thinking about where we can involve and create program like
Cahoots, putting unarmed trained people.
Rick Last, stated the process seems wrong. Defund campaign was about systematic racism in the Amherst
Police Department. Last asked did you really hear the testimony last night. “We are not clinicians, we
don’t pretend to be.” Resources should be used at the point of the crisis. >1% of calls required police
presence. Last spoke in favor of passing a one month budget and in order to take a deeper look at the
police budget.
Zoe Crabtree, Amherst resident, spoke about officer initiated calls and follow up calls. Being watched and
policed is part of the violence we are speaking about.
Terry Mullen, Amherst resident, spoke about defunding the APD.
NJ Rivilis, Amherst resident, commented that they never noticed a police car in their neighborhood, asked
why drug addiction response by police and not EMS, spoke in favor of funding for community services.
Alexandra Monesson-Olson, Amherst resident, stated that only 12% of calls come from a 911 call, spoke
about patrolling and instigating, different parts of community policed differently, asked why not employ
crisis response teams, and that Amherst needs more social services and less police.
Michele, an Amherst resident, spoke about a personal experience with a mentally ill family member and
experience with the Amherst police department, stating that APD was compassionate and that the police
need more help but need specialized professionals.
Jose Adastra, area resident, commented that they elected not to send their children to the Amherst schools
because there were police in the schools, spoke about the history of the name of the Town, and that Black
people need respect, Reagan and the CIA, indigenous people suffering, double meetings and prioritize
hearing from people.
Gazit Chaya Nkosi, Amherst resident, commented that they are disturbed by limited public comment
when APD was given so much time, demeaning to imply that our sharing is unfounded hysteria, spoke in
favor of a one month budget, and asked the committee to admit they have done something wrong.
Alysha Desharnais, Amherst resident, commented that cutting the APD budget would not create an
imbalance, spoke about social services budget reductions, and that the Town needs to pay attention to
how we are spending money.
Curry Kautz spoke about personal experience with police escalation and spoke in favor of cutting the
APD budget by 52%.
Lauren Reingold, former Amherst resident, spoke about medical code of ethics, stated that pouring money
into police department does not keep the community safe, asked to reinvest 52% of the police budget and
address the mental health crisis in Massachusetts.
Kacey Oen read a statement by a disabled Black resident who faced abuse and harassment from an APD
officer via “wellness checks.” The community member wants safety and accountability, cuts to the APD
budget by 52%, and to use 80k in the budget in a community directed way led by Black-led community
members.
Steinberg and Griesemer thanked those who spoke for their comments, and asked that written comments
be submitted for inclusion in the meeting records.
Members discussed timing of the committee’s recommendation vote.
Members discussed the review, report, and recommendation process.
Members discussed the budget review process.
