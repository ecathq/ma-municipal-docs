1. Call to Order
Steinberg declared the presence of a quorum of the Finance Committee, called the meeting of the Finance
Committee to order at 1:35 pm, and confirmed committee members could hear and be heard, and
announced audio and video recording.
President Griesemer called the meeting of the Council to order at 1:37 pm, and confirmed that Councilors
could hear and be heard.
Steinberg spoke briefly about the agenda and indicated public comment will be taken up later in the
meeting.
2. FY21 budget: Discuss feedback from public hearing
Steinberg spoke briefly about the public comments regarding reducing the Police Department budget
received at the previous meeting.
Schoen spoke about cutting the Police Department (APD) budget by reducing two vacant positions.
Members discussed potential cuts to the APD budget, and that the Council cannot reallocate funds cut
from one department for another use.
Members discussed how the budget drives the tax rate for the year. If later in the year there was a need for
additional funds, those would have to come from reserves.

Town Manager Paul Bockelman, Comptroller Sonia Aldrich and members discussed state law regarding
the budget and funding sources.
Ryan commented that there is a need to hear from the Chief about what cuts would look like for APD.
Ryan spoke about the need for APD in terms of COVID-19 enforcement of guidelines, and with students
returning to the area there will be an increased need for APD support.
De Angelis commented that the UMass police can help with on-campus enforcement, and expressed
discomfort with discrimination between the public health crisis of COVID-19 and the public health crisis
that people of color face in daily life.
Bahl-Milne spoke about a resolution passed in a city in North Carolina regarding reparations as a model
that Amherst could consider, a Community Reparations Commission, and in Amherst, there is a need for
a radical shift of what safe policing looks like.
Griesemer spoke about a meeting with residents, several Councilors, the Police Chief, and local efforts to
defund APD. Griesemer spoke about the need for more time to develop a plan and the constraints of the
budget deadline.
Schoen spoke about concerns from residents about students returning to the area and not abiding by social
distancing and mask guidelines, and asked about how different action can be taken to enforce guidelines.
Bockelman spoke about how enforcement is different during the pandemic.
Ryan stated that we need to know the impact of a hiring freeze on APD.
Griesemer spoke about the upcoming Council meetings, and that policing and public health are related;
the impacts of cuts to the APD budget must be well understood by the Council and the community before
action is taken.
Members discussed contact tracing and other efforts to enforce guidelines locally.
Chief Livingstone spoke about expanding the role of community policing to other officers to compliment
the one officer who serves in that function now.
Members asked about the effect of a hiring freeze on APD.
Livingstone spoke about potential effects of reductions in funding.
Members discussed reducing the APD budget by 52% as many residents have urged.
Members discussed the potential reduction in the size of the police force, and impact on how policing
might change.
Members discussed the committee recommendation and potential vote to recommend the budget to the
Council at the next meeting.
Members discussed a potential hiring freeze, and the timing of potential reduction to the APD budget this
fiscal year or next year.
3. Review draft report
Steinberg spoke about the uncertainty of revenue in the coming year.
Aldrich asked about Council orders for borrowing.
Members reviewed the Council orders that are awaiting Council action.
Members discussed what should be included in the Finance Committee report and recommendation to the
Council.
