a. FY21 Budget – Charter Sec. 5.5(c)
Councilor Steinberg reported that the Finance Committee used the Town Council guidelines to review the
budget, met with heads of every department, and the Finance Committee recommends an additional
motion to address issues regarding policing in Amherst. Councilor Steinberg noted that if the budget is
reduced, funds would not be available for other purposes, as a smaller amount of funds would be raised
via taxation.
Councilor Schoen spoke about substantial discussion during Finance Committee meetings of reducing
two positions to the police department budget, referred to page 52 of the budget that details the types of
police calls received by the department, and spoke about conversations about policing.
Councilor De Angelis spoke about the compromise of freezing two positions, and spoke in favor of
eliminating the positions from the budget, and the symbolic action of not funding the positions.
Councilor Pam spoke about resident committees who are addressing policing in Town and about resident
oversight of policing.
Councilor Ryan asked about the force level of the police department if the two positions are not filled.

Councilor DuMont spoke about public comments regarding the budget, spoke in favor of defunding two
police department positions, establishment of a resident oversight committee, and stated that none of the
$80,000 racial equity funds in the budget be used to fund the Amherst police department.

change in the community, noted the willingness of the police department to have difficult conversations,
and spoke about the need to study the disparities in the community with respect to health, housing, and
other issues. Councilor Bahl-Milne spoke in favor of adopting the budget with a hiring freeze and not
defunding the two police department positions.

hearing from staff and constituents at each Council meeting, and spoke about the need for work to be
done by existing bodies and groups.
Councilor Ross echoed Councilor Brewer’s comments, thanked Councilor Steinberg for the clear and
detailed Finance Committee report, spoke about the limits on Council actions, expressed concern with
cutting the budget, and spoke in support of reallocating funds in the future. Councilor Ross spoke about

Agenda item 8.c. Action Items: Zoning Bylaw 11.250 Amendment – First Reading, Charter 2.10(a)
Planning Board chair Christine Gray-Mullen spoke about the bylaw amendment, the Planning Board
report and joint meetings with the Community Resources Committee (CRC). C. Gray-Mullen spoke about
the site plan review process as a way to improve projects with respect to traffic, parking, and signs to
mitigate any negative impact on the community.
Councilor Hanneke reported that the CRC held a joint hearing with the Planning Board, noting that a Site
Plan Review (SPR) is not a Special Permit and that requiring four votes would make the threshold for a
SPR a higher vote than a Special Permit. The Planning Board size was reduced when the Charter was
adopted. CRC voted against “not less than four” language for the amendment.
Councilor Ryan reported that the Governance, Organization, and Legislation Committee voted the
amendment as clear, consistent, and actionable.
Councilor Schoen spoke in favor of changing the language to “not fewer than four,” stating that would be
a simple majority of 7 members.
Councilor Schreiber spoke in favor of the amendment as presented, that it was supported by the Planning
Board and that it is important for Planning Board members to vote rather than abstaining.
Councilor Pam and Councilor DuMont spoke in support of the “not fewer than four” wording.
Councilor Hanneke spoke about the Zoning Board of Appeals (ZBA) previous requirement of three votes,
which would be a unanimous vote of the ZBA, for Special Permits. Councilor Hanneke spoke about a
past situation in which members were abutters and abutters to abutters, which did not leave enough
members to vote.
Councilor Bahl-Milne stated that the Executive Office of Energy and Environmental Affairs requires a
simple majority vote, and that SPRs in other towns require a simple majority.
Councilor Pam stated a simple majority is at least four.
Councilor Steinberg stated that if there is a vacancy on the Planning Board, putting a minimum of four
votes alters the requirement because it would be greater than a majority.
Councilor Schreiber spoke about the situation Councilor Hanneke mentioned and stated that some SPRs
are not controversial, such as resurfacing athletic fields.
Michael Birtwistle, Amherst resident and Planning Board member, commented that SPRs are often
concerned with large projects with significant impact to the community; a narrow majority would be
problematic, and that anything less than 2/3 vote should be indication that the project lacks community
support. Birtwistle stated the Planning Board has had no difficulty in achieving a quorum, and spoke in
opposition to the amendment, and stated the Planning Board should continue functioning as it has been.
Councilor Brewer spoke in opposition to the inclusion of a written Planning Board minority report in the
Council packet, stating that in the future there will not be a written minority report from the Planning
Board. Councilor Brewer stated that this is not a partisan issue, the Council does struggle to fill Planning
Board vacancies, and spoke in favor of a simple majority vote.

Agenda Item 8.b. Action Items: Valley CDC Project at 132 Northampton Road – Local Option
Planning Director Christine Brestrup explained the Zoning Board of Appeals (ZBA) determines if the
project will accept a first round of tenants, up to 70%, with a local preference. Brestrup stated that there is
concern that this may exclude minorities who are protected by fair housing laws; if the local pool of
applicants does not include a specific demographic then applicants can be pulled from the larger pool of
applicants.
Councilor De Angelis asked about residency requirements for homeless individuals, stating that abutters
supporting the local option may be doing so for the wrong reasons.
C. Brestrup stated homeless residents may have an address at a local shelter. There must be a local and
regional need in order for the project to go through.
Councilor Brewer stated that it is frustrating to agree with something that people are supporting for the
wrong reasons. Spoke in favor of meeting the need in Amherst first, knowing the State will require an
adjustment be made if demographics are not represented in the pool of local applicants.
Councilor Pam spoke in favor of the local option.
Councilor Bahl-Milne stated we need to work regionally to address homelessness, racism, addiction, and
mental health.
Councilor Schoen noted one of the local option criteria is a parent with a child in Amherst schools; the
apartments are studios and would not accept families.
Councilor Ryan spoke in favor of the local option.
Councilor De Angelis left the meeting at 11:00 p.m.
