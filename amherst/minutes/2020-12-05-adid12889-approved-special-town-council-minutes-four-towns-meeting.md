Councilors participating remotely: Brewer, DuMont, Griesemer, Hanneke, Pam, Ryan, Schoen,
Steinberg
Councilors absent: Bahl-Milne, DeAngelis, Ross, Schreiber, Swartz
Others participating remotely: Members of the Amherst School Committee, Town Manager Paul
Bockelman, Finance Director Sean Mangano, Comptroller Sonia Aldrich
The meeting was called to order at 9:08 a.m. by Regional School Committee Chair Alison
McDonald.
Superintendent of Schools Mike Morris stated that this was the first of multiple Four-Town
meetings. Superintendent Morris and School Finance Director Douglas Slaughter presented a
series of slides outlining the Regional School Department’s finances, budget, and projections
under various scenarios.
They noted the many challenges of managing a school district in a COVID-19 pandemic year.
And added that while cost of supplies for schools may be down due to remote learning, costs for
special education services has increased.
One Town Councilor from Amherst asked if the School District was considering the installation
of solar canopies on the Regional School District parking lots. The Superintendent stated that
they had looked into it on a District wide basis but no plans were in place at this time.
At 10:30 a.m., the four towns entered into separate breakout rooms, and the Amherst Town
Council engaged in a discussion.
Councilor Steinberg emphasized the difference between a level-funding and level services
budget. It was noted that there were built in increases – step increases under existing collective
bargaining agreements – that would drive an increase in the School District’s budget. It was also
noted that the reliance on remote teaching may reduce the number of students interested in
attending the Regional School District. This, in turn, will result in a higher per-student cost of
education.

There was discussion of the challenges the School District would face with a level funded
budget. These were put into the context that all Town departments were being asked to develop
level funded budgets and all departments in the Town would have adverse impacts as a result.
At 10:50, the four towns returned to meeting together. Councilor Steinberg reported on the
discussion as did representatives from the other three towns.
Superintendent thanked all for being at the meeting and said the next Four Towns meeting was
likely to be in late January.
The meeting adjourned at 11:02 a.m.
