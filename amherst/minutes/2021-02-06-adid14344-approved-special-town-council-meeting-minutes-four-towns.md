Pursuant to Governor Baker’s March 12, 2020 Order Suspending Certain Provisions of the Open

Presentation on Budget:
Dr. Slaughter of the School Department made a presentation on the FY22 budget. He noted the following:
• Significant drop in student enrollment statewide, however it was unclear how many students will
enroll in ARPS for 21-22.
• Estimated operating budget $32,009,777.
Dr. Morris presented on proposed cuts to reduce the operating budget which included staff reductions,
attempts to not fill vacancies, and non-personnel reductions such as not purchasing Chromebooks.
Mandi Jo Hanneke (Amherst) commented on the high per pupil cost for the district as compared to other
districts. She asked whether there was data to show that ARPS students have stronger outcomes due to the
funding.
• Dr. Morris replied that the district has a higher per pupil cost due to providing services such as
expansive Special Education and English Language Learner programs, the Bright program,
family center, and that the school employs a higher than average number of teachers and staff to
provide smaller class sizes.
Andy Steinberg (Amherst) asked whether there were any lessons to be learned from remote teaching and
COVID that could be carried forward in order to keep students in district schools.
• Dr. Morris responded that the later start time at secondary schools was prompted by Covid but
that the topic had been looked into long before Covid. Dr. Morris concluded that instruction
under Covid costs more money, particularly hybrid instructional models.

Cathy Schoen (Amherst) suggested a collaboration with Amherst College and UMass Amherst was a
possibility (e.g. expansion of dual enrollment programs). She also noted the district could look into more
grant programs to supplement the budget.
Sean Mangano (Amherst) commented that the district commissioned various studies of cost reducing
measures but that there was not much support for those options from the towns or public.
Peter Demling (Amherst) noted the district’s budget is a reflection of the communities’ values and that the
added benefit of the support programs won’t necessarily be reflected in academic metrics (e.g. Special
Education program, co-teaching model, etc.)
Dr. Morris noted that the increase in salary (~4%) also includes a portion of mandatory step increases.
Budget assessments
Bethany Seeger (Leverett) commented that she would be interested to see the effects charters and choice
had on the enrollment and budget for past five years.
• Dr. Morris replied that in recent years there had been general flattening of students leaving the
district due to school choice.
John Trickey (Pelham) commented on the rising costs of budget assessments across the towns and the
effect this would have on EQV. He felt that Pelham could afford 55 – 65% assessment method.
Julia Shively (Pelham) stated that town feels regional method is most fair. She said Leverett was willing
to go to a 45 – 55% assessment method
Lynn Griesemer (Amherst) stated that Amherst was willing to go to a 45% assessment method and would
like a study done on how to reduce expenditure. Andy Steinberg (Amherst) commented he would like to
see this discussion continued throughout the year instead of once a year during the Four Town budget
cycle.
James Walton (Shutesbury) stated his town would like to move to full statutory within reasonable amount
of time, and that Shutesbury was willing to go to a 75% assessment method. He noted that the statutory
method worked in a majority of Massachusetts towns and that he believes it could work for ARPS as
well.
Jed Proujansky (Leverett) said the regional agreement is the town’s end goal. He stated that the statutory
method would sink Amherst unless the overall budget was reduced. He recommended that the District
stay at 45% but would prefer 35%.
George Arvanitis (Shutesbury) commented that various working groups that found the rolling 5-year
statutory would work best for the district. He noted difference in 45% to 100% is relatively minimal and
that it could be achievable post Covid. He was curious to know how Covid would effect EQV and all four
towns’ minimum contributions in the future.
Andy Steinberg (Amherst) commented that the statutory assessment method would not work for Amherst
although it may work for other towns.
Bob Groves (Shutesbury) commented that Amherst also had larger tax base due to its commercial core.
He added that if Shutesbury didn’t approve assessment, it would automatically go to statutory.
