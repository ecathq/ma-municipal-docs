3. Public Comment
a. Jones Library Options
Hank Allan, Jones Library employee and Amherst resident, spoke about his experience working in the
Jones Library over the past 20 years with vendors installing and routing wiring. Allan stated that it will
take thousands of dollars to correct the current issues; there was an inadequate building wiring upgrade in

---

2005, and now the library cannot easily offer additional technology to staff or the public as new, larger,
wiring will not fit.
Lace Stokes, Amherst resident and Jones library employee, spoke about interlibrary loans, poor work
flow in the library, and stated that a new space would be more ergonomic and convenient for employees
serving patrons.
Ken Rosenthal, Amherst resident, stated there was no mention of the $6M fundraising effort, Community
Preservation Act (CPA) money would be available for either solution.
Kent Faerber spoke about fundraising and pledges contingent on the Town’s approval of the renovation
project.
Griesemer stated the CPA funds have been recommended by CPA but on contingent on approval of the
project. It is likely that the library would request CPA funding for a renovation.
Sarat spoke about a potential feasibility study to determine what funds could be raised for renovation.
David Lithgow, Amherst resident, spoke in support of the renovation project, stating that $25,000 was
pledged $25,000 for renovation, not repair, and that it would be irresponsible to turn down $13.8M that is
available if we go forward with renovation.
Hilda Greenbaum, Amherst resident, stated she is not happy with the gambrel roof on the west side, and
asked about an override for the major building projects.
Michael Chernoff, Amherst resident, spoke about programs that are badly served at the moment with the
present building.
Elisa Campbell, Amherst resident, spoke about space for the Burnett Gallery in the proposed expansion.
Sarah McKee, Amherst resident, asked when the Council will decide which projects will be considered
for an override, and asked for a side-by-side comparison of the two options.
Tom Davies, Amherst resident, asked about the value of the library post-construction for each option.
Kitty Axelson-Berry, Amherst resident, spoke critically of the expansion, stating that it appears that the
Town is looking to create a flagship library and make the town an urban metropolis.
Nancy Campbell, Amherst resident, asked about the quality of the proposed construction of the
renovation/expansion plan.
Rita Burke questioned the number of patrons and asked about extrapolation to parking and other needs
that come from higher library usage.
Rudy Perkins commended the Trustees, staff, and Sustainability Committee for elimination of fossil fuels
and electrification of energy system, inclusion of rooftop photovoltaic in the building plan. Perkins asked
about some things that he suggested would not be a good idea because the payback will take hundreds of
years, for example, triple glazing for windows.
Sara Draper spoke about development into the next phase when those details are examined in terms of
cost effectiveness.

Carol Gray, Amherst resident, spoke in opposition to the expansion project, listing the following
concerns: lack of public support for the expansion, loss of the Woodbury Room, the 1996 renovation was
recently paid off, need to determine public support for the project, and desire for an override to determine
public support and funding.
Judith Luddy, Amherst resident, asked about the elementary school building project, and if the school
project can be prioritized without an override.
Anne Herrington, Amherst resident, spoke in support of the expansion project, urging the Council to
support the plan.
Clare Cooke, Amherst resident and youth services coordinator at the Jones, commented that an expanded
library better serving children, the current youth spaces are spread over three floors, with no sightlines
into the various rooms, space for strollers, displays, or kid-level shelving.
Councilor Swartz left the meeting at 8:21 pm
Mia Cabana, head of youth services at the Jones Library, spoke about a lack of designated programming
space.
Marcy Sala, Amherst resident, thanked everyone for their work on this project, and spoke about the
opportunity to use the library as a catalyst for economic revitalization.
