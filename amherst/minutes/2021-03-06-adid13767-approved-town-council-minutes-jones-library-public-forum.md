3. Public Comment
a. Jones Library Options
Sue Hugus, Library Staff member, spoke about safety concerns, the need for flexible spaces, upgrades to
the HVAC system in light of COVID-19.

---

Ellen Brout Lindsey asked why some Councilors do no support the plan.
DuMont stated the Energy and Climate Action Committee is going to be coming out with their climate
action plan in the beginning of May with different proposed actions, and stated she feels hesitation that
would like to see that plan first and balance different capital needs.
Nina Weyl, Amherst resident, asked about library services during construction, and asked how the town
justifies so much library space per capita.
Sharry responded that the library is required to provide services during construction, so services will
continue. Staffing levels will remain, opportunities to increase hours at other branches during
construction. In regards to the space, the Jones Library is the 20th busiest library in the state, and the Town
sees the importance of the library and lifelong learning.
Molly Turner, Amherst resident, asked about the use of the endowment.
Robert Pam responded that the plan is to use the endowment for half of the non-Town costs like books,
heat, and electricity. R. Pam spoke about sources of funding.
Douglas Kelleher spoke about tax credits.
Farah Ameen, Amherst resident, spoke in praise of Library staff, and asked about sustainability and the
rising costs of building.
Lydia Vernon-Jones, Amherst resident, stated we have a climate emergency, need to focus on climate
change in our community, and make ending climate change a top priority.
Lee Jennings, Amherst resident, asked if there is a feasible way to eliminate fossil fuels without
renovation and addition project, and a way to provide a dedicated teen space without renovation/addition.
Ludmilla Pavlova-Gillham, Amherst resident, and asked how to ensure the building becomes a resilient
resource, spoke about renewable energy resources.
Toni Cunningham, Amherst resident, spoke about proposed cost caps, and asked if later project estimates
come out higher, where can cuts be made while complying with the state grant.
John Hoover, Amherst resident, spoke in support of the project.
Laura Draucker, Amherst resident and chair of the ECAC, commented that debt is keeping us with old
buildings that are not meetings needs of community and relying on fossil fuels.
Jeff Lee, Amherst resident, asked about the MBLC grant and library size.
Martha Speigelman, Amherst resident, spoke in opposition to the renovation/expansion project, stating
that it is a waste to tear down functional areas of the library.
Rudy Perkins, Amherst resident, spoke about protecting the net zero bylaw, and the need to scrutinize
energy performance and climate mitigation priorities.
Terry Johnson, Amherst resident, spoke about the library’s tight budget, and asked about the plan to staff
a larger facility and keep branches open, commented that the greenest building is one that is already built,
and the need for long-term maintenance plans.

Heather Sheldon, Amherst resident and co-president of the Special Education Parent Advisory Panel for
Amherst-Pelham Regional School District, spoke about accessibility needs at the Jones library, stating
there are not usable spaces for everyone currently.
Barry O’Connell, Amherst resident, spoke about the need for teen spaces and spoke in strong support of
the project.
Darcy DuMont left the meeting.
Sam MacLeod, Amherst resident, comments about general planning process for capital projects, concern
about three other projects, urged the Council consider relative importance of all projects.
Alice Morse, Amherst resident, spoke in support of Amherst establishing a “five-star library.”
David Lithgow, Amherst resident, impressed by the professionalism of the project planners.
Carol Gray, Amherst resident, spoke about underutilized areas in the Jones library, recommend hiring a
space consultant to see if the space can be reconfigured. In terms of accessibility, asked if it can be
improved without tearing down 40-60% of the building. Spoke in support of an extensive town-wide poll
to prioritize capital projects, with an option for people to say they do not support the project.
Rob Robertson, Amherst resident, commented there are no reasonable reasons to oppose the project and
encouraged the Council to support the project.
Judith Luddy, Amherst resident, spoke in favor of improving and upgrading the library, spoke about the
desire to assure taxpayers the Town can build a school, library, fire safety building, and public works
building.
Elaine Donoghue, Amherst resident, commented that there is not much room to utilize spaces in existing
library, the youth area needs to be expanded, and spoke about funding for previous renovation.
