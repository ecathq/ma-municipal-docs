4. General Public Comment
Alex Kent, Amherst resident, voiced strong support for retaining Footnote “M” in Zoning bylaws and
spoke about the need to preserve downtown neighborhoods by protecting them from being overrun by
non-owner occupied rentals.
Christopher Riddle, Amherst resident, spoke in favor of the Resolution Calling for Swift, Just Building
Decarbonization in the Commonwealth. Riddle spoke about the benefits of the resolution including
encouraging the state take actions that would allow towns and cities to set more rigorous standards for
building.
Gina Etheredge spoke in favor of retaining Footnote “M” in the Zoning bylaws. Etheredge stated she and
her partner fell in love with Amherst when their son was a student here and they would not want to
completely relocate here if the character of the historic district were to change.
Henry Winter, Amherst resident, spoke against Zoning Bylaw changes that would allow individual lots to
be developed in to apartments because it would increase rent and the number of people in the apartments
and therefore number of cars needing to park on the street. They stated the proposed plans will
completely change the neighborhood for parking needs and students will come in because of financial
constraints of the developments.
Anne Burton, Amherst resident, spoke against removing Footnote “M” and about the need to make a
decision about the desired character of the Town. Burton stated allowing condos to be built next to homes
will impact ability to sell the homes. Burton stated the domino effect from removing Footnote “M” will
chart the future of all the blocks that have private homes and if that happens, she will sell her home before
it is too late.

e. Amendments to Town Council Rules of Procedure rule 6.3.d and 6.3.e
Ryan referred to packet item 8.e. Proposed Revisions to ROP - 2021-03-08. Ryan stated Rule 6.3.d was
broken in to two separate items to further clarify when interruptions are allowed and Rule 6.3.e was added
to clarify when a councilor without recognition.
MOTION: President Griesemer moved, second by Councilor Ryan, to amend Town Council Rules of
Procedure 6.3.d. by striking everything after the phrase "point of order" and replacing it with the phrase
"or to raise a question of privilege." and to insert a new Section 6.3.e that reads "Councilors shall not
speak without recognition except as in Subsection d above, or to doubt the presence of a quorum, or to
call the previous question, or to assert the Charter Right to Postpone." and to re-letter all subsequent
sections of Rule 6.3.
Brewer spoke in favor of the changes clarifying the difference between speaking without recognition and
interrupting. Brewer spoke against allowing a councilor to speak without recognition to assert the Charter
Right to Postpone. Brewer stated this nuclear option is undemocratic and questioned why the rules are
privileging it.
Ryan stated the Charter Right to Postpone is a right all councilors have and Governance, Organization
and Legislation Committee (GOL) proposed these changes to clarify that councilors cannot interrupt the
speaker to utilize it.
Steinberg questioned if the proposed changes solve any problems and spoke against the ability for the first
person who speaks to call the question. Steinberg suggested the wording of the rules needs more
clarification and stated making a motion without debate frustrates the motion of the Council and the
Charter’s intent.
Schoen echoed Steinberg and Brewer’s concerns.
