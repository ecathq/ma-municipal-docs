2. Presentation and Discussion Items
a. Massworks/Pomeroy Village Intersection Project
i. Presentation

---

DuMont said the council is faced with the decision of a roundabout versus a signalized
intersection at the Pomeroy Village intersection. She said she hopes to hear residents’ uses of the
village center and what they would like to see in that area.
TM Bockelman introduced the other panelists. He said that this is part of the outreach for this
project and there will be another meeting on Saturday at 2:00 PM for public comment. He said
there will also be a pop-up tent event at some point, socially distanced, to address public
comments. TM Bockelman said EngageAmherst is another tool to interact and post questions for
town staff to answer. He said the grant that the town received will be focused on the intersection,
but he sees this as a catylst for other projects around town.
Brestrup presented the intersection challenges including lack of pedestrian access, no bike lanes,
care-centered designs, and more listed in the powerpoint. She said the town was awarded a $1.5
million grant form a State MassWorks program. Brestrup also presented the history of this
project, dating back to the late 1990s when the design was developed. She said there was a
MassWorks grant that was unsuccessful in 2013 and then were successful in 2020.
Brestrup said they are considering a controlled intersection or a roundabout. She presented the
qualifications of controlled and roundabout intersections. Brestrup also reviewed the questions
that residents can answer in their comments.
ii. Public Comments and Questions on Pomeroy Village Intersection
Project and staff answers
Adrienne Terrizzi – Terrizzi said she is a resident of Orchard Valley. She said she has been
waiting for a long time to get to this point in the process regarding Pomeroy Village. Terrizzi
commented that with the Groft Park rehabilitation and other improvements in town she sees
Pomeroy Village as an asset. She said they are a diverse neighborhood in South Amherst.
Terrizzi said she travels primarily by car and she would like to see more walkways and
bikeways. She said she would also like to see more seating areas and cannot weigh in on the
roundabout vs. controlled intersection debate yet.
Catherine Bell – Bell said she is a resident of South Amherst and is a business owner. She said
she would like to be able to more easily access the businesses at the intersection and primarily
has to travel by car to get to the businesses because there are no sidewalks. She said they have
the opportunity to make a true village center and there is an issue with people driving through the
intersection very fast and a roundabout would speed up traffic even more. Bell also said the area
might be too small to have a roundabout and a controlled intersection would be better for
businesses and pedestrians.
Claire Bertrand – Bertrand said she has lived at this intersection for 10 years and currently lives
elsewhere but she still works in the office buildings near the intersection. She said her concern is

---

safety in that area and she would like to see traffic-calming mechanisms for not just the
intersection but also for the general area. She said this would allow people to see what is going
on in Amherst including all of the schools located in the area and she asked if there would be a
lot of land taken with either of the options for the intersection. Mooring said it depends on the
design. He said if they decide on a roundabout and need turn lanes on West Pomeroy or Pomeroy
that will take away more land and if they just need turn lanes on West Street they could take less
land.
Noah Loving – Loving said he uses the intersection frequently by foot and driving through. He
said he would like to shop there more and the lack of the pedestrian controls in the intersection
prevents him from shopping at the businesses more. Loving said he has an inclination to the
roundabout option, but he would have to see the proposals in more detail. Loving added that he
would be in favor of bike lanes and having areas to congregate.
Ginny Hamilton - Hamilton said they bike through the intersection at least once a week and her
highest priority is pedestrian safety. She said she loves the enhanced signaling idea in the
intersection and she strongly favors this over the roundabout option. She said she hopes to see
more space for biking. Hamilton said she also likes the idea of areas for congregating.
Erika Zekos – Zekos said she comes to the intersection area to eat a lot and she would like to see
increased safety and is leaning towards a roundabout because of the safety factor. She said she
would like to see data about the safety for pedestrians. Zekos said that she would also like to see
sheltered bus stops on both sides of the street and improved sidewalks that extend beyond the
intersection.
Mary Hoyer – Hoyer said she mostly drives through the intersection and also uses the businesses
there. She said she has a preference for a roundabout for the environmental reasons. She said the
crosswalks for the roundabout in the diagram seem to be shorter and the time the pedestrians
would spend on these crosswalks would be shorter. Hoyer said disabled people should be
consulted on this. She added that landscaping can correct almost any bad design, but if you take
current land that is devoted to the parking in front of the buildings and turned it into a plaza, the
parking could be moved to the back of the plaza.
Elizabeth Davis – Davis said she supports the roundabout option because it seems to work well
for the Atkins Farm area, but the sidewalks are also a concern.
Rosemary Kofler – Kofler said she is a resident of South Amherst and primarily uses a car to go
through the intersection. She said the biggest issue is safety for pedestrians and she is concerned
that roundabouts are less safe for pedestrians. Kofler said she would like to see an intersection
where a pedestrian can push a walk button to cross the street and she liked Hoyer’s idea of
parking behind shops. She said she is also in favor of a bike lane and bus stops with shelters.

---

Ziomek said this was a team effort and he acknowledged the town staff at the Planning
Department.
TM Bockelman said this was a great meeting and he would like to hear from more people. He
encouraged participants to share the meeting link for Saturday with others and use the
EngageAmherst site. TM Bockelman also reviewed the process that this project will go through,
starting with a recommendation with the TSO and then having a vote at the Town Council level.
Brewer said she does not believe this is the time for councilors to speak and she clarified that
they do not need a public hearing because they will have these types of meetings and more
outreach. Griesemer said they would only need to have a hearing if they were amending some
sort of bylaw or spending money.
