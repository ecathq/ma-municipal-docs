3. Hearings
a. Public Hearing of the Finance Committee on the FY22 Town Budget – Charter Sec.
5.5(a)
Finance Committee Chair Steinberg called the Finance Committee to order at 6:35 p.m. and confirmed
non-voting Finance committee members can hear and be heard. Steinberg reviewed the applicable section
of the Town Charter that apply to budget process including that this budget does not include the regional
schools budget because it is taken up off-schedule due to the nature of the arrangement with partner
towns. Steinberg stated the Finance Committee will continue meeting with staff from Town departments
and those meetings are posted on the website.
Town Manager Bockelman and Finance Director Sean Mangano gave a brief presentation on the budget:
• The budget and additional info is available on the Town webpage
• The budget is balanced, aligns with goals adopted by the Council, with a modest operating budget
increase of 2.1% that allows for a few new initiatives
• Revenues – brief overview
• Expenditures – brief overview

a. Resolution Opposing the Closure of the Gorse Children’s Center at Mount Holyoke
College

Town Council.
Hanneke commented that it is not appropriate for the Town Council to weigh in on private business
decisions.
Bahl-Milne thanked the people who stayed in the meeting to speak about this issue, and spoke in favor of
the resolution.
Pam spoke in favor of the resolution and about the importance of childcare.
Steinberg stated this is an important discussion for the Council.

6. Presentations and Discussion
a. Reparations for Amherst
Griesemer welcomed guests Dr. Barbara Love, Dr. Amilcar Shabazz, Michele Miller, and Matthew
Andrews.
Barbara Love spoke about proposed actions the group is requesting the Council take, a resolution in
support of H.R. 40/S. 40 and formation of an African Heritage Reparation Coalition.
Dr. Love requested the Council undertake reparative justice, and direct tax revenue into a reparations fund
that would be directed toward racial justice.
Dr. Shabazz spoke about partnering with the Town towards reparations, and work on a Black census.
Michelle Miller read a statement from co-founder of the Reparations for Amherst group Kam Howard.
Matthew Andrews spoke about the trauma of anti-black racism, reparations, and funding for programs
that combat structural racism.

5. Action Items
a. State Reclamation and Mosquito Control Board Spraying
Griesemer stated the proposed plan is for the Town to join the Pioneer Valley Mosquito Control District
as the Town’s alternative management plan as part of the Town’s opt-out application.

Dragon added that the alternative management plan is subject to approval through EEA and would allow
us to opt of spraying by state mosquito control board, spoke about the Pioneer Valley Mosquito Control
Districts services, and the unanimous vote in support by the Board of Health.
Councilors asked about monitoring and evaluation of the program, residents’ concern about spraying, and
making information available to residents.
Public Comment:
Lenore Bryck, Amherst resident, spoke in support of the proposal and spoke about other work to manage
mosquitos in Amherst.
VOTED via consent agenda to suspend Town Council Rules of Procedure rule 8.4 for the current agenda
item.
MOTION: Griesemer moved, second by Ross, in accordance with M.G.L. Chapter 252, Section
2A(b)(2), having held a public meeting on May 17, 2021 at 6:30 pm, during which a period of public
comment was held, and as recommended by the Board of Health, that the Town of Amherst join the
Pioneer Valley Mosquito Control District and use its alternative management plan to opt out of wide area
pesticide application conducted by the State Reclamation and Mosquito Control Board, with the
understanding that the Pioneer Valley Mosquito Control District’s alternative mosquito management plan
has been submitted and approved by the Executive Office of Energy and Environmental Affairs.
VOTED 12-0-1 by roll call (Councilors Bahl-Milne, Brewer, De Angelis, Griesemer, Hanneke, Pam,
Ross, Ryan, Schoen, Schreiber, Steinberg, and Swartz voted Yes; Councilor DuMont was absent) in
accordance with M.G.L. Chapter 252, Section 2A(b)(2), having held a public meeting on May 17, 2021 at
6:30 pm, during which a period of public comment was held, and as recommended by the Board of
Health, that the Town of Amherst join the Pioneer Valley Mosquito Control District and use its
alternative management plan to opt out of wide area pesticide application conducted by the State
Reclamation and Mosquito Control Board, with the understanding that the Pioneer Valley Mosquito
Control District’s alternative mosquito management plan has been submitted and approved by the
Executive Office of Energy and Environmental Affairs.
