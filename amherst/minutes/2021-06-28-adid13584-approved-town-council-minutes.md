4. General Public Comment
Andy Anderson – Anderson spoke about the impending legislation for rank choice voting (RCD)
in Amherst. He gave the history of Easthampton’s process with choice voting dating back to
2019. He said Amherst citizens voted in favor of this bill, and it still hasn’t happened yet.

Attorney General already approved the original language. He urged the Council to amend this
decision and direct the Town Manager to prepare for a ranked choice voting election this

rejecting or accepting the 900 signatures for the moratorium, to consider that nobody but the
Council is deciding to build the future of Amherst. She said they must change one factor at a
time instead of trying to pass multiple bylaws all at once. She urged the Council to take the foot
off the gas, adding that all these bylaws at once are chaotic.

a. Participatory Budgeting Commission
Griesemer introduced the Participatory Budgeting Commission.
Gage said the group convened in October 2019, and this commission was mandated by the
charter to propose a measure adopting participatory budgeting.
Gage presented on the following items:
• What participatory budgeting is – community members decide how to spend parts of the
public budget
• The brief history of the commission’s actions since its creation
• Examples of participatory budgeting
• How participatory budgeting would work
• Requirements for success

• Report on what the Participatory Budgeting Commission did (interviewed other
participatory budgeting projects in the US and researched other participatory budgeting
resources)
Schoen said they started this effort before COVID-19, but COVID-19 changed everything. She

options for existing town programs. She said these existing programs include the Community
