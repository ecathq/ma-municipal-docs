MOTION: Griesemer moved, second by Schoen, that pursuant to Section 8.6 of the Town Charter, that
the Town Council place a question before the voters of the Town at the November 2, 2021 Town Election
the measure seeking affirmation of the Town Council’s April 5, 2021 vote to fund the expansion and
renovation of the Jones Library, and that the question and summary take the following form:
QUESTION: Shall the following measure authorizing a borrowing for the expansion and
renovation of the Jones Library, as voted by the Town Council on April 5, 2021, be affirmed?
BE IT ORDERED by the Town Council of the Town of Amherst that: The Town
appropriate $35,279,700 for the expansion and renovation of the Jones Library, and to
meet this appropriation, authorize the Treasurer, with the approval of the Town Manager,
to borrow said amount, under and pursuant to M.G.L. Chapter 44, Section 7, 8, or
pursuant to any other enabling authority, and to issue bonds or notes of the Town
therefore, which borrowing shall be reduced to the extent of any grants received from the
Massachusetts Board of Library Commissioners, funds received from The Jones Library,
Incorporated in an amount no less than $5,656,576 which, in addition to the $1,000,000
previously appropriated under the Community Preservation Act, represents the Library’s
share of the total project cost, and/or any other source to pay costs of this project; and,
further, any premium received by the Town upon the sale of any bonds or notes approved
by this vote, less any such premium applied to costs of issuance of such bonds or notes,
may be applied to project costs approved by this vote with a reduction of borrowing
authority therefore by a like amount in accordance with M.G.L. Chapter 44, Section 20.
SUMMARY: On April 5, 2021, the Town Council voted with 10 in favor, 2 opposed, and 1
abstention, to authorize a borrowing for the expansion and renovation of the Jones Library. The
Massachusetts Board of Library Commissioners has awarded the Town a grant of approximately
$13.8 million for this project with payments starting this year; approximately $5.7 million will be
provided through private donations as set forth in an agreement with the Jones Library Board of
Trustees; and $1.0 million was appropriated from the Community Preservation Act funds. With
these funds secured, the Town’s share of the total project costs will be approximately $15.8
million. A “yes” vote on this question means that you affirm the Council’s vote to fund the
Library expansion and renovation project and want the project to continue. A “no” vote means
that you reject the Council’s vote to fund the Library expansion and renovation project and do not
want the project to continue.

b. Election Update and Polling Locations
Audette said Precinct 10, formally located on the ground floor of the Bangs Community Center has
accessibility challenges and if the elevator failed it would make this location not ADA compliant. She
said according to MGL Ch. 54 Section 25, she is not confident that they will meet the booth requirement
because of spacing. Audette said Precinct 4 was formally located with Precinct 5 in the activity room at
the Bangs Center and was moved to de-densify the Bangs Center for COVID-19 and to provide for more
parking at the high school location. She added that by moving Precincts 4 and 10 they would be co-
locating to the same polling location which would make sense since both precincts belong to District 4.

small and still an active kitchen to firefighters which cuts off their access to food for that day. She said
they are trying to find a better space and the working fire station presents issues. Audette added that they
must look to additional spacing because of the population growth in Amherst.

locations only.

Brestrup said the town received state funds to help pedestrian safety getting to Kendrick Park from
Triangle Street because currently there are no flashing beacons at the crosswalks.
Ben Breger presented on the following items:
• Amherst given $184K for Shared Streets Grant, the third round is focused on road crossings
• First round of grant focused on outdoor dining, second round still undergoing work for
construction of a ramp outside the Bangs Center, and the third round is focusing on the North part
of the downtown looking at pedestrian safety at 3 road crossings
• Overview of grant: rectangular rapid flashing beacons (RRFB) with audible operation (a
recommendation from the Disability Access Advisory Committee) and repainting of crosswalks
• Overview of targeted pedestrian corridor
• Images of before and after looks of affected crosswalks and slip lane of Triangle Street
roundabout
• Town Council approval needed for 3 pairs of flashing beacons: one at the Triangle Street
roundabout, one at the intersection of Pray Street and East Pleasant Street, and one at the
intersection of Triangle Street, Pray Street, and Cottage Street.
Ryan asked why they are just focusing on the slip lane and not the crosswalk to get to Kendrick Park.
Brestrup said they considered more RRFBs but didn’t want to overdo it because too many would defeat
the purpose. She said she received an email from Tracy Zafian, a member of TAC, who suggested placing
RRFBs in 2 more locations so if the Town Council wants the Planning Department to consider that they
most likely will have the money to do that.
Pam said she appreciated Zafian’s memo and suggested studying this more for the most effective use of
flashing beacons.
Brestrup said the grant requires the Planning Department to finish doing this by December and when they
order the flashing beacons they will take 6 months to arrive, so this is time sensitive. She said if this
should be referred to TSO or another Council body they can work on that.
Schoen asked if the RRFBs require frequent replacement/repair and if there is a possibility to include
illumination to this plan because the streets and crosswalks are very dark at night.
Ross said he is interested in voting on this tonight and not referring to TSO. He suggested creating the
possibility to add to this or make changes by changing the motion to not include the slip lane to give
flexibility to the roundabout plans.
Brewer said it is strange that this didn’t go to TAC and agreed with leaving flexibility in the motion.
MOTION: Griesemer moved, second by Ryan, to approve the installation of Rapid Response Flashing
beacons with audible push-buttons at the crosswalk at each of the following intersections:
• Intersection of Triangle Street, Pray Street, and Cottage Street
• Intersection of East Pleasant and Pray Street
• Roundabout at the intersection of East Pleasant and Triangle Street
DuMont asked if visually impaired people would need to have audible cues on every section, adding that
she is confused as to why they would have an intersection that is not accessible. Brestrup said they have
heard from DAAC that they want to avoid that intersection and routing them in a different direction is a
better solution.

g. Property-Assessed Clean Energy (PACE) Municipal Program
VOTED via consent agenda to authorize participation in the Massachusetts Commercial Property
Assessed Clean Energy Program (PACE) as defined under M.G.L. c 23M, as follows:
Vote Authorizing the City known as the Town of Amherst to Participate in the
Massachusetts Commercial Property Assessed Clean Energy Program
(PACE Massachusetts)
WHEREAS, pursuant to M.G.L. c. 23M, as amended (the “PACE Act”), the Commonwealth has
established a commercial sustainable energy program known as the Massachusetts Property
Assessed Clean Energy Program (“PACE Massachusetts”) to provide a financing mechanism
(“PACE financing”) to private owners of commercial and industrial properties for certain
qualifying commercial energy improvements (“improvements”); and
WHEREAS, pursuant to the PACE Act, PACE Massachusetts is administered by the
Massachusetts Development Finance Agency (“MassDevelopment”), in consultation with the
Massachusetts Department of Energy Resources; and
WHEREAS, under PACE Massachusetts, the owner of the commercial or industrial property
benefitting from the improvements (the “benefitted property”) is required to repay the PACE
financing through the payment of a betterment assessment (a “PACE betterment assessment”)

placed on such benefitted property by the municipality in which the benefitted property is located;
and
WHEREAS, in order for an owner of commercial or industrial property to participate in PACE
Massachusetts, Section 2 of the PACE Act requires that the municipality in which such property is
located must elect to participate in PACE Massachusetts; and
WHEREAS, the City known as the Town of Amherst (the “Municipality”) has determined that it
is in the best interest of the Municipality to participate in PACE Massachusetts as a “participating
municipality,” as provided in the PACE Act, to permit the owners of commercial and industrial
properties located in the Municipality to access PACE financing for qualifying commercial energy
improvements through PACE Massachusetts;
WHEREAS, NOW THEREFORE, BE IT VOTED as follows:
The Town Council of the City known as the Town of Amherst hereby approves the
Municipality participating in PACE Massachusetts pursuant to the PACE Act, and authorizes the
Town Manager to enter into a PACE Massachusetts Municipal Assessment and Assignment
Agreement (the “Agreement”) with MassDevelopment, pursuant to which the Municipality will
agree to (i) levy PACE betterment assessments and impose PACE betterment assessment liens on
benefitted properties located in the Municipality, in the amounts determined by MassDevelopment
to be sufficient to repay the PACE financing, (ii) assign the PACE betterment assessment liens to
MassDevelopment, which MassDevelopment may in turn assign to the providers of the PACE
financing (each a “capital provider”), as collateral for such PACE financing, (iii) include on the
property tax bills for the benefitted properties the installment payments necessary to repay the
PACE betterment assessments, in the amounts and at the times as determined by
MassDevelopment, (iv) collect and pay over to MassDevelopment or its designee, the PACE
betterment assessment installment payments, as and when collected, and (v) enforce, to the extent
required by the Agreement, the PACE betterment assessments and liens; the Agreement to be
substantially in the form presented to this meeting, with such changes, modifications and insertions
as the Town Manager may approve as being in the best interest of the Municipality. The Collector
Treasurer of the Town or such other Town agency as may be designated in the Agreement is
authorized to levy such PACE betterment assessments and impose the PACE betterment
assessment liens on behalf of the Town without further authorization by this legislative body.
Notwithstanding any other provision of law to the contrary, officers and officials of the
Municipality, including, without limitation, municipal tax assessors and tax collectors, are not
personally liable to MassDevelopment or to any other person for claims, of whatever kind or nature,
under or related to PACE Massachusetts, including, without limitation, claims for or related to
uncollected PACE betterment assessments. Other than fulfillment of the obligations specified in
the Agreement, the Municipality has no liability to the owner of the benefitted property or to any
capital provider related to the Municipality’s participation in PACE Massachusetts.

against the changes, including removing Councilors from the body as members. Ryan summarized both

workload, identify the Town Sustainability Coordinator as the non-voting member, and have 1 Councilor
liaison.
