MOTION: Griesemer moved, second by Schoen, to acknowledge receipt of the Participatory Budgeting
Commission Report of June 1, 2021, to thank the members of the Participatory Budgeting Commission
for their thoughtful and thorough work, and to dissolve the Commission.
Councilors discussed the process to approve the commission’s minutes following their dissolution.
VOTED unanimously, 13-0 by roll call, to acknowledge receipt of the Participatory Budgeting
Commission Report of June 1, 2021, to thank the members of the Participatory Budgeting Commission
for their thoughtful and thorough work, and to dissolve the Commission.
MOTION: Griesemer moved, second by De Angelis, Per Charter Section 10.11, to direct the Town
Manager to build on existing budget processes to enhance resident participation in existing funding
programs, including Community Preservation Act, Resident Capital Proposals, Community Development
Block Grant, and others as appropriate.
DuMont asked if this means we are giving up on the idea of a separate participatory budgeting process.
PBC Chair Meg Gage responded that they hope not, the group’s decision made in light of COVID-19 was
that rather than a proposal for $100,000 for participatory budgeting, was to hope that was the Council will
take this up again in the near future. We plan to explore relationships with the university political science
dept. Focus on priorities for the Town given the financial situation the Town is in at the moment. We
need to find meaningful ways people can participate in budgeting process.
Schoen urged people to read the report, stating that almost all the efforts have substantial staff time
devoted to making this work, the idea on the surface seems like it could start right away but it is a staff
intensive processes; the focus can be on programs we have now to make them work better.
Gage added that when the Charter Commission added this section of the Charter, the idea had been that
the Community Participation Officer would help staff this effort; the three people sharing the Community
Participation Officer position do not have the capacity to handle this at the moment.
Bahl-Milne spoke about engagement research at the university.
VOTED unanimously, 13-0, by roll call, per Charter Section 10.11, to direct the Town Manager to build
on existing budget processes to enhance resident participation in existing funding programs, including
Community Preservation Act, Resident Capital Proposals, Community Development Block Grant, and
others as appropriate.
Griesemer spoke about the third motion to refer development of a town-gown partnership to the Town
Services and Outreach Committee.
Brewer and Ryan agreed the referral would be appropriate for TSO to consider.
Hanneke spoke in opposition to the idea, stating that the more the Town asks of the University, the less
likely anything will come of those requests; the Council should prioritize what it requests of the
University.
DuMont spoke in favor of directing the Town Manager without a committee referral.
Ryan spoke in favor of a referral prior to Council action.
Brewer stated the concerns that Hanneke mentioned should be addressed by TSO.
MOTION: Griesemer moved, second by Ryan, Per Charter Section 10.11, to refer to the Town Services

b. Comprehensive Housing Policy – First Discussion
Hanneke spoke about the Community Resources Committee’s work on the policy, thanking John Hornik
and the Housing Trust for beginning the process over two years ago, and thanked Tom Kegelman who
served as a resident expert on housing and whose input was invaluable. Hanneke spoke about
implementation and moving forward with the five housing policy goals as a framework for evaluating
projects, funding, and bylaws. Hanneke reported the Community Resources Committee voted
unanimously to recommend the Council adopt the policy.

Pam praised the CRC for their work, and stated the policy needs more emphasis on UMass creating more
housing units.
Brewer also spoke in praise of the hard work in bringing this policy to the Council, particularly the
effective language used to consider things like an overlay district near UMass, and expressed frustration
with Councilor Schoen sharing her views with residents in writing prior to the meeting, as Councilors
cannot share opinions with each other outside meetings and so were unable to respond to emails from
residents regarding Councilor Schoen’s written comments.
Ryan thanked CRC and asked about next steps and implementation.
Steinberg thanked CRC, spoke about the Finance Committee review of the policy including measurable
goals and setting quotas; we can be lured into more expensive or a less desirable product if we are driven
by goals. The cost of individual units needs to be measured carefully, along with staff time on projects
and recognize that cost as well, and need to consider how much housing Amherst can sustain while
maintaining the community that residents want it to be. Housing is a big problem that cannot be solved on
the back of one town, and service costs rise with a rising population.
Schoen apologized that her comments got out prior to the meeting, and spoke about concerns that she
shared with the idea of improving the policy. Schoen expressed concerns about the following:
• Clarity of the five goals
• Outreach
• UMass student population growth with no increase in University housing
• Businesses buying homes and renting to students
• Finite amount of buildable land in town
• Density tables
DuMont asked that Schoen’s comments be shared with Councilors, and endorsed her statements.
Brewer restated that it is inappropriate for Councilors to lobby support before the Council has an
opportunity to discuss the policy.
Schreiber spoke about UMass requirements for students to live on campus their first year, which used to
be first and second years until 10 years ago.
Hanneke spoke about implementation and next steps, including working with other Town bodies to utilize
as they review proposals and make recommendations to the Council.
Schoen left the meeting at 8:25 pm.
Pam suggested hiring a staff person to work on redevelopment and grant writing.
Bahl-Milne stated the policy sets the priorities for the Town as a starting point, and then focus on which
goals we want to work on. Local developers could be engaged to start working on innovative tax laws to
create affordable home ownership.
De Angelis thanked the committee for their work, and spoke in support many of the ways of increasing
density such as tiny houses, duplexes and triplexes; cannot assume higher density that will create
affordable housing. The question is how we create affordable housing in a system that values money over
people.

d. Town Council and Council Committee Meeting Format – In Person/Hybrid or Fully
Remote
VOTED via consent agenda to suspend Town Council Rules of Procedure rule 8.4 for the current agenda
item.
Hanneke spoke about the importance of meeting in person, that COVID-19 will be around forever and we
will need to learn how to live with it, and asked why Finance is the only committee that is supported with
hybrid meetings.
Bockelman spoke about development of remote work policies, that residents expect staff at Town Hall,
that it is unwise to bring people into rooms together, the Town is taking things one step at a time and
currently looking at mid-October as a next decision time, and that the Town will support Council
committees if they wish to use hybrid meetings.
Councilors discussed meeting in-person with masks, public access to Town Council meetings in different
formats, the need for team-building among Councilors that is better in person, and indoor gathering
regulations in schools and at UMass.
MOTION: Griesemer moved, second by Bahl-Milne to continue all meetings of the Town Council in the
remote meeting format until September 30, 2021.
ROLL CALL VOTE: 6-5-1, with 1 absent (Bahl-Milne, Brewer, DuMont, Pam, Schreiber, and Swartz
voted Yes; De Angelis, Hanneke, Ross, Ryan, and Steinberg voted No; Griesemer Abstained; Schoen was
absent) to continue all meetings of the Town Council in the remote meeting format until September 30,
2021.
