a. 6:30 p.m. Public Hearing: Eversource Petition to install one fully owned
Eversource pole on Pomeroy Lane between two existing poles
Eversource Representative Austin Harpin spoke about the need for the additional pole on Pomeroy Lane.
Harpin stated the existing poles will not accommodate the transformer that is needed for the new home at
157 Pomeroy Lane.
Hanneke asked if it is a new build, and the petition allows underground utilities which would require
cutting the road, and the order does not include a police and fire crossarm allowance.
Harpin stated the lines will be overhead, there will be no need for street cutting; the other issue can be
addressed with a new order.
Bahl-Milne asked about the impact to the trees in the area.
Harpin stated the pole will not require any tree removal.
Hanneke stated the words from page 3 of the petition need added to the order on page 5.
MOTION: Griesemer moved, second by Hanneke, to close the public hearing.
VOTED unanimously, 13-0 by roll call, to close the public hearing.
4. General Public Comment
Bob Pam, Amherst resident and Jones Library Trustee, speaking on his own behalf, spoke in opposition to
continuing the Jones Library building project due to financial concerns. Pam spoke about decisions
regarding the design of the building and other costs.
Ken Rosenthal, Amherst resident, echoed Bob Pam’s comments, stating the project cannot continue as
planned, proposing to take the funds to renovate the library without an expansion. Rosenthal spoke about
costs already incurred and options for fundraising and grant opportunities to offset the cost of moving
forward with a renovation in the future.
Laura Draucker, Amherst resident, responded to Ira Bryck’s comments posted in the Amherst Indy
regarding the townwide vote on the library project and assumed attitudes from voters who supported the
project. Draucker spoke about her support for the project and advocacy at the state level for more
generous grant funding and about inflation of construction costs.

b. Reauthorization for the Town Manager Regarding a Memorandum of
Understanding between the Town of Amherst and the Jones Library
MOTION: Griesemer moved, second by Hanneke, to reauthorize the Town Manager to enter into a
Memorandum of Understanding between the Town of Amherst and the Jones Library, Incorporated, acting
by and for its Board of Trustees, with an addendum/amendment that includes a bridging agreement for the
time period prior to moving to construction.
Steinberg spoke about the principles and process of the Finance Committee discussion. Steinberg stated
the Finance Committee agreed that decisions that require more information should be made after that
information is known. Steinberg stated a Finance Committee motion was made and voted and members
asked if the committee would reconsider the motion in order to arrive at a vote that could be supported by
all members of the committee—this resulted in a unanimous vote at the Finance Committee.
Schoen spoke about concerns that costs were underestimated initially and now are at $10 million higher;
Schoen spoke about pledges and votes on funding that falls short of the revised projected project costs;
Schoen spoke about features that are being cut due to project costs and reflected on cuts made to a
previous renovation project in 1992 that resulted in a flawed addition; Schoen stated continuing the
project would put the Town at financial risk in terms of the other building projects and operating budgets;
loss of time and money and risk to the library with 15 month delay of library repairs such as the failing
HVAC system;, and risk and cost to the Library Trustee’s endowment. Schoen spoke in opposition to the
motion.
Rooney stated she would like a plan B developed and stated she would like to amend the motion to have
an earlier check-in point for the Town Manager at the end of the design development in May.
De Angelis asked about the letter from the MBLC about delaying the second payment to the Town. De
Angelis spoke about the difficulty of the decision on this project.
Finance Director Mangano stated the letter addresses the timing of the MBLC payments; based on the
latest schedules from the OPM, there was hope that the next payment would be in this fiscal year but the
current timeline estimates indicate the next payment will likely be in the following fiscal year. The Town
will know the exact price when bids come in for construction.

Dorothy Pam commented that voters knew costs when there was a townwide vote; comparing the Town’s
continuing the library project to the United States refusal to exit the Vietnam War. Pam spoke about
criticisms of the building design.
Hanneke spoke about the many functions and services the Jones Library provides throughout the year.
Hanneke spoke about the importance of the building for the community, and that repair costs will be
higher than the renovation and expansion costs with the MBLC grant, and the repairs would not include
sustainability improvements that the renovation and expansion include. Hanneke stated the trustees have
supported the continuation of the project. Hanneke spoke in support of the motion and the continuation of
the project.
Taub agreed with Schoen and Hanneke’s comments, stating the library can be everything it needs to be in
its current square footage. Taub stated the proposed building is overly large for the community and that
college students do not utilize the library. Taub stated the trustees were committed in April 2021 to
raising $6M; a $15M fundraising goal does not seem feasible.
Lopes spoke about the Civil War tablets; if included in the library it will be significant to have a
monument in Amherst that is inclusive. Lopes stated fundraising potential due to the significance of the
tablets is unknown but could be significant.
Austin Sarat, Jones Library Trustee Chair, stated all the trustees have a fiduciary role and the group does
not have a chief financial officer. Sarat stated it was clear from the townwide vote that voters endorsed
the vision of the library. The trustees determined, after significant study, that the existing footprint does
not provide adequate space; the needs of the residents cannot be satisfied within the existing building.
Sarat stated the existing building cannot be made environmentally sustainable without tearing down and
rebuilding. Sarat spoke about libraries enlivening towns. Sarat stated the OPM recommended going to bid
and seeing where things stand once the town has received bids.
Devlin Gauthier spoke about her experience borrowing materials and visiting the Jones library, spoke
about the library’s deteriorating condition and that the library is not showing up for every member of the
community as it should. Devlin Gauthier spoke about the trustees’ vote on moving forward with the
project, and spoke in support of the motion and moving the project forward.
Bahl-Milne commented that the risk factor is significant, and by backing away at this point would be a
signal that the Town is unwilling to get to a point to make an informed decision about moving forward.
Bahl-Milne spoke about the risk of postponing in terms of inflation and increasing building costs. Bahl-
Milne spoke about the trustees’ vision based on community needs assessment. Bahl-Milne spoke about
respecting the work of those specialized and experienced people who have been involved in the project so
far.
Griesemer stated the capital process is going to be very difficult, and that the Town must move past
bumps in the road rather than giving up on projects; we must be tough and realistic.
Steinberg stated the voters knew what they were voting for, which was a renovated and expanded state-
of-the-art library; optimistic that the project can succeed; if the project does not move forward there will
be no second chance at the MBLC queue or with their goodwill. Steinberg stated the Council should
respect the voters and move forward.
MOTION: Rooney moved, second by Taub, to amend the motion by replacing it in its entirety with the
following:

That Town Council authorize the Town Manager to amend the Memorandum of Agreement voted
on April 5, 2021, by and between the Town of Amherst and The Jones Library, Incorporated
acting by and through its Trustees, whereby:
The amended MOU serves to address and assign responsibility for the renovation and expansion
project costs through the completion of Design Development documents, at which time an
updated cost estimate and risk analysis is presented to Town Council for consideration; and
In the event a decision is made not to proceed with the project when the Design Development
cost estimate is presented, the Trustees will reimburse the town for its share of the cost incurred
in design development, and pledge to invest whatever funds were raised and pledged for the
renovation/expansion project be assigned to continue the preparation of Construction Documents
and Bidding for a Plan B reuse and repair project; and
In the event the project does not proceed at that time, a Plan B alternative for a design that reuses,
repairs, and renovates to a modest degree the existing library structure to meet ADA requirements
and improved energy efficiencies is initiated at that time to develop a Schematic Design and cost
estimates for Plan B; and
That this agreement will specify the time period for the completion of any payment to the town,
investment by the library in capital improvements, or other means to ensure that the costs are
otherwise covered.
Rooney spoke to her motion, that the checkpoints in her motion allow for more check-in opportunities
and for the Town to proceed with caution.
Hanneke stated the motion is simply a check on the process, because the cost estimates cost to produce,
telling the Trustees that their pledged funds on a plan B project and that individual donors can request a
refund of their gift or allow it to stay with the Trustees and those funds could not be pledged to a repair
project.
Mangano stated there will be a new estimate at the end design and development phase.
Griesemer stated that if the project does not move forward, donors to the project can decide how their
money is used or returned.
Rooney stated the wording regarding using donations for repairs came from the Trustees.
Lopes asked what consequences or expense the Town would incur at that point.
Alex Lefebvre spoke about cost estimates the Town will receive at various points of the project, and
confirmed that pledges are specific to the project and could not be reallocated for repairs; Lefebvre stated
once the project goes to bid, the estimate is locked in and delays in the process increases the cost.
Lefebvre spoke about the expert opinions of library staff who have expressed the need for additional
square footage, and that the decision to expand the library has already been voted by the Town. Lefebvre
spoke about the trustees’ engagement with professional fundraisers who are confident that they can raise
the funds needed.
Miller stated that to stop the process at this point does not feel just or fair to those who have worked hard
to get the Town to this point, and that the Town must let the process play out in order to make a decision
with more information.
Hanneke spoke in opposition to the amendment; stating that she is hesitant to dictate exact terms to the
Town Manager when asking him to carry out a function for the Town, that the amendment would require
additional discussions at meetings and that would potentially negatively impact fundraising efforts.
