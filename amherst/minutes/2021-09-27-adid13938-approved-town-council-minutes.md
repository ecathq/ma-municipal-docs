Agenda item 8.a. Public Way Requests – Placement of Performance Shell on the Town Common
Gabrielle Gould from Amherst BID presented on the following:
• Ray Mann and Naomi Darling as winners of the 2018 BID Band Shell Contest
• History of the Town Common
• The BID’s process with the project
• Details about the architects
• The importance of arts and culture in town
Darling and Mann presented on the following:
• Daytime rendering of the band shell
• The geometry behind the band shell design
• An aerial site plan image
• Views from the side and front of the band shell
• The Structural Concept and Acoustic Study
Gould spoke of the unique piece of art and chance to rebuild. She also spoke of the economic driver that
arts and culture are for the downtown.
Ryan said the band shell has lots of potential and asked how many musicians/performers could
comfortably fit on the stage. Darling said the request was for a stage to accommodate an orchestra. Gould
said the stage will also fit dance performances and plays.
Hanneke asked about the Maintenance Fund, specifically who would be responsible for maintenance with
that fund. She said there is a policy on reservations of the public way regarding the Common so a request
from TSO must come to the Council for a modification of that policy.
Pam asked how they would protect cables and lighting with weather. Mann said the lights are integrated
in the shell and some lights are within the shell. Gould stated that they are working with a lighting
designer that would design an easily removable lighting package, however it is very expensive. Gould

added that the shell is structurally engineered to withstand New England weather and musicians will be

Steinberg said a Boston performance shell included storage space underneath for tech equipment and was

could be possible. Gould stated that the bands from this summer series of concerts are very interested in
the Drake and J Mascis’s wife and manager are on their programming committee for the Drake. Gould

events.
Kuhn said when originally planning the design, the structure could also be considered a piece of
sculpture. He said it has to be beautiful and adding bathrooms and storage space would create an
untenable mass.
Griesemer asked if there was any way to make the back of the stage collapsible without ruining the

consultant they decided to bring the shell down at the back for the musicians to hear themselves and each
other. Mann said once there are moving components, it becomes much more expensive and complex.

commitment and vigilance to monitor this structure for graffiti. Gould said the BID goes around in the
mornings to pick up the trash around the downtown and their mission is to maintain the beauty and
integrity of downtown.
Brewer said TSO will be dealing with whether or not those at Boltwood Inn and Amherst College have
weighed in on this band shell. She suggested that TSO’s Town Common policy should be a separate part
of this and the Finance Committee should weigh in on the Maintenance Fund.

d. Comprehensive Housing Policy
Hanneke said there is an updated report in the packet and all changes are marked in track changes.

policy list should be separated because it implies endorsement.
MOTION: Hanneke moved, second by De Angelis, to adopt the Comprehensive Housing Policy as
revised and recommended by the Community Resources Committee on September 14, 2021 and as
reported in the CRC Report of September 20, 2021 and to refer the Policy to the Town Manager and CRC
for implementation.
Schoen suggested rewording of the phrase “and large portions of land in Amherst” because it could be
interpreted as the town is going too far to preserve farmland. She said the “quality of life” is not being
undermined because of that clause and it sends the wrong message.
Ryan said the quality of life includes an inclusive and diverse community and that is the challenge with
the high cost of land and living. Schoen said this is a housing policy and one thing they value is open
space which is why people want to live in Amherst.
Ryan disagreed with Schoen and said the statement is accurate. Schoen said she would start with the
affordability of housing and would not have even included quality of life.
De Angelis said open space is a challenge, and to pretend it is not there as a part of the problem is naïve.
Pam agreed, adding that no discussions in CRC interpreted it this way. She said this is on the first page
and the paragraph could be edited a bit but does not reflect any ideological conflict from the CRC.
Bahl-Milne suggested starting the paragraph by stating that Amherst has an affordable housing problem
instead of starting it off with the quality of life clause.
Ross said they have to read this paragraph in context because the paragraph before this states that they are
narrowing in on the challenges of housing.
DuMont said there is still a lot of uncertainty about this and they should have another discussion for
another draft.
Members continued to discuss editing the “quality of life” sentence.
MOTION: Schoen moved, second by Bahl-Milne, to amend the policy by changing the first sentence of
the third paragraph so that it reads, “Amherst is facing a housing affordability challenge.”

be conducted that way.

remote. Griesemer said the public can still attend via Zoom with hybrid meetings.

also is concerned about the burden on staff.

DuMont said older Councilors are more at risk.
Ross said some people are really struggling with Zoom and people are getting sick from unmasked
gatherings, not classrooms. Bahl-Milne added that they are creating an unhealthy work environment for
some who are struggling on Zoom.
Ryan said this also impacts staff and they should remain on Zoom until the end of their terms.
Brewer said CRC chose to have a meeting that did not let people have hybrid access and it is still true that
those showing up in person for public comment/those on Zoom will have an impact on staff.

second that amendment.
