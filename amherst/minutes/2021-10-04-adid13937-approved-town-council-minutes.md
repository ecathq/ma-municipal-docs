a. Tax Classification Hearing
Richard Morse called the Board of Assessor meeting to order at 6:36 PM
Griesemer said that Kim Mew will be the new Principal Assessor.
Burgess presented on the following items:
• Tax policy options with in-depth explanation of each option
• Overview of the process and MGL CH.40, §56
• 4 classifications of property and their definitions (residential, commercial, industrial, and personal
property)
• FY22 distribution by property classification
• Whether to split the tax rate or not and consequences of each option
• Residential exemption option
• Small commercial exemption option
• Recommendation: a single tax rate for all properties and no residential, small commercial, or
open space exemptions
Mangano presented the Town of Amherst Residential Exemption Study, including the following items:
• Definition of residential exemption: tax policy that exempts a percentage of owner-occupied
residential property values
• Details of study process: 376 responses to surveys sent out (764 sent in total)
• Communities that have adopted the residential exempt
• The example studied used 15% exemption rate
• Breakeven assessed value - $540,800
• Key points of the policy – detailed in the PowerPoint slides
• Recommendation: Do not adopt the residential exemption
Griesemer said they are not voting tonight, and they will hold a hearing.
Steinberg asked if the residential property increased over $23, does that run into problems reaching the
tax cap. Burgess said the $23.95 tax rate after exemption would not impact the tax rate ceiling. He said
the ceiling is based on what the single rate would be.
Steinberg asked if any other communities that adopted this had rent increases for renters in apartment
complexes. Mangano said they do not have specific experience with that and all other communities that
adopted the exemption are around Eastern Mass/near Boston. He said he can look and see if rent levels
went up. Hargreaves said while taxes would go up, it depends on the demand and supply situation.
Pam suggested that this should have some relationship to age and spoke about New York City’s senior
citizen exemption.
Brewer asked when the was survey sent, were those responses updated on property cards and asked if
there is a penalty for not responding. Burgess said it is updated in the system online and they have not
discussed notifying rental registration about non-responses to the survey.
Brewer said in theory this can push away more families to rent in town and asked if this is the only tool
they thought of. Burgess said no, they are valued as if they are a business, but for 2-3 family homes there
is a market for those and they can use the market approach.
