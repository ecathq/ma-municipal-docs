a. Community Safety Working Group (CSWG) Final Report
Walker called the CSWG to order at 6:34 PM. She said members Darius Cage and Bowman could not be
here. CSWG members shared their thoughts on the second part of the committee’s charge.
Walker spoke about the history of police in America.
Owen presented the following:
• How the CSWG formed
• The Town Council performance goals for the Town Manager
Tennenbaum presented the first CSWG recommendation, which included:
• To revise and adopt a new APD police policy for the use of force policy, consent searches, low
level and pretextual stops, contract issues, and other police policies
Owen shared the second CSWG recommendation, which is to create a Resident Oversight Board (ROB).
She said pages 12 and 13 in the CSWG report list the responsibilities of the group.
Walker shared the third CSWG recommendation, which included:
• The creation of an online dashboard that provides transparent access to data on APD vehicle stops
by race
• Categories of the dashboard
Owen presented the fourth and fifth CSWG recommendations which are the following:
• The creation of traffic control enforcement and alternatives
• Under the CRESS program, a separate traffic control division
• Other traffic control enforcement
Walker shared the sixth CSWG recommendation, which is to engage the town in an extended process of
racial healing and visioning.
Owen presented the seventh CSWG recommendation, which is to develop an anti-racist departmental
culture within the APD.
Walker said the final recommendation from the CSWG is to create a successor group, the Community
Safety and Social Justice Committee (CSSJC). She said the group has already been approved and started
recruiting members.
Owen shared the recommendations that the CSWG were not able to get to because of the lack of
resources.
Walker shared the Town Council’s Resolution Affirming the Town of Amherst Commitment to End
Structural Racism and Achieve Racial Equity for Black Residents.
Pam said she is happy to see other communities do these types of programs so that Amherst is able to see
what works and what does not work. She asked if there would be a separate BIPOC youth center and

BIPOC cultural center or is the CSWG envisioning one center for both. Ononibaku said one building
would be for any youth in town but led by BIPOC residents and another building would be the BIPOC
cultural center. She said they would like to see a commercial kitchen in the cultural center building to be
able to share cultural food.
De Angelis spoke of the difficulties the CSWG faced along the way. She said this work is for BIPOC
residents but connects to all residents.
Ferreira stated that this was a very difficult process and emotionally taxing. She said more lives have been
lost by police along the way and there were many obstacles with a lack of resources. She spoke of the
challenge of dissolving the CSWG instead of making it a standing committee.
Steinberg said there are collective bargaining rights involved in these recommendations and that is a
delicate issue because police departments in this country have misused collective bargaining. He also
asked about the finances and budget of the recommendations.
Ononibaku said when it comes to BIPOC issues, there are budget concerns. She said BIPOC people do
not feel safe in this town and need space in town to be with other BIPOC people. Walker agreed and
referred to the Town Council’s Resolution to End Structural Racism. Owen said in LEAP’s report, they
provided ways to revise policies.
Ross said he appreciated examples from other communities. He asked if the recommendations from ROB
require renegotiation of the collective bargaining agreement. He also asked about racial identification in
reports.
Town Manager Bockelman said the town has a duty to bargain under the collective bargaining laws and
would reach out to the union to discuss the change.
Ferreira said the recommendation regarding the pilot policy of reaffirming someone’s identity is personal
and can be potentially used against someone. She said the group decided to have the policy as a pilot to
see if people see it as helpful or as intimidating.
Schoen asked how age and income bias would be captured on the report. Pam agreed, stating that gender
plays into this as well. Tennenbaum said they recommend lessening preemptive traffic stops for things
like taillights out, or other minor things that would only lead to a “fix-it” ticket.
Ross asked how common the APD policy is of a one-year limit on grievance dismissals. Tennenbaum
said one year is not unheard of and was most likely included in the APD policy as a negotiation contract.
Brewer asked about the charge for ROB and spoke of the subpoena and executive session power. She
asked if KP Law has not weighed in on that, does that need to be resolved before recruiting members.
Bockelman said the Human Rights Commission has some of that power as an individual, not as a
committee and KP Law is looking into subpoena power.
Walker mentioned that the Springfield Resident Oversight Board does have subpoena power. Ferreira said
this shouldn’t delay the Resident Oversight Board, but they do need to be transparent.
Schoen said the Elementary School Building Committee is discussing consolidating 2 schools and leaving
a school empty. She suggested the empty school could be used for an empowerment center and they
should start that discussion soon. She also spoke about adding Daycare to the center if there is the space
for it.

Bahl-Milne asked how they will successfully implement all aspects of this CSWG report. She emphasized
the need to prioritize these recommendations in Amherst’s funding and hiring and asked if there have
been any conversations with UMass to see if they can support the CRESS program. Bahl-Milne also
asked how they can improve public engagement.
Ononibaku asked why UMass cannot pay people for a study. She spoke about recognizing lived
experiences as a skill and a valuable asset.
Owen said part of community engagement should be putting marginalized people in positions of
leadership. She stated that one responsibility of the successor committee is to ensure the programming for
the Youth and Empowerment Center and also asked if UMass has been approached for funding.
Bockelman said funding has not been discussed directly with the University, but they did receive a
significant grant thanks to Rep. Mindy Domb and State Senator Jo Comerford. He said they can approach
the University about this as well.
Walker spoke about the need for translation services for community engagement.
Ferreira said Seventh Generation was the consultant that was great with community engagement. She said
they need to broaden the languages for community engagement and also use the leaders in the
community. Ferreira spoke of the urgent need for a Youth Center and said a lot of the recommendations
can be implemented now.
Ononibaku said the CSWG spoke about the cultural center with other provided social services such as
daycare from local agencies.
Pam stated that filling out a questionnaire takes lots of time and it is a legitimate thing to get paid for.
Public Comment:
Dee Shabazz – Shabazz commented that the CSWG is the most important contribution of a public body in
Amherst thus far and she hopes that Council members that had critiques about the make-up of the
successor body after this report have come to understand how critical it is to maintain a group that
specifically is majority BIPOC. She said the committee also should be compensated with a stipend and
Seventh Generations found that the APD did not have a clear and transparent way to formally complain.
Shabazz said it is important that the DEI Director be hired.
Zoe Crabtree – Crabtree commented that Police Chief Livingstone should be listening and taking these
recommendations to heart. Crabtree agreed with the CSWG recommendations and said the
transformational recommendation is to leave traffic offenses to a separate group and not the APD.
Crabtree spoke of the extensive research she did on calls to APD.
Allegra Clark – Clark commented that the recommendations really come together to reduce the size and
scope of policing. She said CRESS and a traffic control department will address the right response to the
problem at hand. Clark said it is important that all of these recommendations are done with an anti-racist
lens and she spoke of the need for a DEI Director.
Ash Hartwell – Hartwell commented that the amount of detail in this report is amazing. He said this is
just the beginning and the hard part is now ahead. Hartwell emphasized how much work is ahead to make
this successful.

b. Redistricting Plan
Dujovne shared the updated map and said 2 census blocks have changed to address the village centers.
Shannon said making this change is good and the lower boundary is a river and the power cut is the upper
boundary.
Zafian said the map change was based on comments about keeping Pomeroy Village together and East
Village came up last week, but they didn’t change it because they are the same boundaries that are
currently there. She said they wanted to keep residential together and spoke of the disconnect of people
from the west side of North East Street and South East Street to the east side of those streets. Zafian said
there were some concerns raised about students and she sent an email to the Council about those
concerns. She said the DAB found that with the proposed map, only 17% of the student population on
campus is assigned to a new district.
Zafian spoke of the on-campus population and the concern of improving voting power based on race
while disenfranchising the student vote. She said the student population actually makes up most of the
racial diversity in Amherst.
Ryan said he is struggling with the use of Amity Street as a dividing line between Districts 3 and 4
instead of Northampton Road. Zafian said some of it is based on numbers and how UMass dorms are
expanding, and currently the section along Northampton Road near Aspen Heights and Greenleaves were
all in District 5 and those areas did not seem very connected to the downtown either.
Schoen said the DAB has been very responsive to concerns. She said precincts may not matter much
going forward, but people will have to get used to Districts 1A and 1B. She said for the 2023 election,
they must reorient people to this change. Schoen spoke of how many votes are needed to win the local
election and how students are the majority in some districts. Griesemer said the new 1A and 1B Districts
will in effect for the 2022 election.
Hanneke said she is still concerned about the downtown, adding that Lincoln Ave is split into 3 separate
districts. She spoke about voting precincts and urged people to consider the districts when voting.
Pam said she needs an explanation of Lincoln Ave splitting into 3 districts and does not see that as a
problem. She said there are also very few houses on one section of Lincoln Ave.
Bahl-Milne said it is difficult to have Precinct 7 with the downtown but trusts the work of the DAB.
