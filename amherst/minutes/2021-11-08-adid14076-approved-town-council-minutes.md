a. African Heritage Reparation Assembly Progress Report
Jemison called the African Heritage Reparation Assembly (AHRA) meeting to order at 7:35 PM, and
confirmed members could hear and be heard.
Jemison reviewed the AHRA’s mission.
Miller said for funding they have narrowed in on ARPA, the Cannabis Tax Revenue, CPA, free cash,
grants, and private funding options.
Jemison spoke about funding and the importance of community engagement/outreach. She said the
website was the first thing to leverage and they are also planning cultural events to further engage with
the public.
Miller stated they also have started a dialogue with Rep. Mindy Domb. She said they are looking into the
possibility of creating a CPA-like structure for planning and funding municipal reparations.
Jemison said as they build a municipal reparations plan, they need to also plan for bodies in the
community to guide this work in the future.
Miller stated that it is important to understand that reparations for black people is its own path.
Jemison said the assembly’s charge breaks down into financial, eligibility, and community engagement.
She stated that eligibility will come from the work of the black census. Jemison said the community
engagement is being thought of as 3 different groups: residents, the Town, and other groups in the
community.
Griesemer said the Financial Order will come to the Council next week, but it will be referred to the
Finance Committee first and then return to the Council on November 22.
Steinberg clarified that a fund has been created already, it will be decided for the amounts to transfer.
Hanneke asked if the presentation next week will include how the CPA interacts with the Stabilization
Fund.
Schoen asked when AHRA will have a discussion of what the types of applications/uses there will be of
the money. Jemison said the meat of that discussion cannot start until the next meeting and the three-
pronged approach will take up the rest of the 2021 meetings.
Pam asked how the body will conduct the black census. She said she is hoping that they use an outside
group because of confidentiality. Miller said they are still in the process of figuring out the specifics of
the black census and would like to work with a professional body. She added that they have talked with
the Donahue Institute and Rep. Domb about this.

c. Hickory Ridge Update
Ziomek said this is a very complex project and he showed the map that is available on town website. He
stated that the outlined 150 acres is under a purchase and sale agreement and the sale will close by the end
of the year. Ziomek stated that the map shows 2 proposed sites for solar arrays and spoke of the Discover
Hickory events. He said the UMass studio looked at the property and the town has over 90 community
comments on the EngageHickory site. Ziomek said they have submitted a CPA proposal for enhancing
trail access for the future and there are no concerns of contamination by the previous owner. He said they
will be doing more outreach at the Mill Valley apartments soon.
Schoen asked if the map takes into account the fencing around the solar arrays. She also asked if the
current owner will be paying for the ecological restoration areas that the State is requiring.
Ziomek said the maps for permitting came from the Conservation Commission and ZBA. He said they are
currently working with KP Law and the owner’s lawyer on the leased land for solar. Ziomek said those 2
areas of solar will be fenced and screened and there will be a connecting road between the solar arrays.
He also spoke of the solar company’s responsibility of the connector road. He said the solar company
must mitigate their impact on the 17 acres of restoration.
DuMont said a lot of locals use the perimeter of the land and that they should make sure there is still
space to use the perimeter. Ziomek said the key component of this property is the connectivity from north
to south. He said they will be getting a pilot payment and one change regarding solar is that in the long
run it is likely that Amherst will be an offtaker of this.
Brewer spoke about the constraints on the property and suggested the paths be flagged in some fashion.
Ziomek said they want the people who live around Hickory Ridge to connect with the Town about the
project and in the spring, they will have more events on the land.
Bahl-Milne said they could use the land to meet the need for transportation and accessibility for youth and
leisure services.

c. Surveillance Technology Oversight Bylaw – First Reading
Hanneke shared an acquisition flow chart to follow. She said the purpose of this is to protect people’s
privacy. She stated that no one really knows what kinds of surveillance technology is being used in the
town and that is part of the reason why this bylaw is important.
De Angelis shared a personal story about attending a peaceful protest. She said she noticed cops filming
protestors and car license plates. She said when they confronted the police about this, they refused to
discuss it. De Angelis added that now surveillance technology is much more advanced and racially
biased.
Schoen asked about Section I, regarding the committee annual report. She asked if they are worried that if
they didn’t do something, they are exposed to legal actions. Schoen also asked about exemptions,
specifically for red light cameras.
Hanneke said the administrative part of this has been modified extensively and that is why it would be a
committee of the Council. She said she is not sure if red light cameras are allowed in Massachusetts, but
this bylaw would just have the Council approve the use of surveillance technology so that everyone
knows what kind of technology is being used.
Pam agreed with the sponsors and said the Town has to get in front of this and protect residents against
surveillance that is unwarranted.
