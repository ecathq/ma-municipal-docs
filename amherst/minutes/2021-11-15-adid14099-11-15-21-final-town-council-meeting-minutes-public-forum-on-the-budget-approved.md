4. Public Comment
a. Public Forum on the Town Budget – Charter Sec. 5.3
Ash Hartwell, Amherst resident – Hartwell commented that it is important to recognize issues of racial
equity and inclusion which is central to Amherst’s well-being. He said this could be a good example for
other communities. Hartwell spoke of CSWG as just the beginning, not just a budgetary matter. He said
this transitionary period may last years and suggested the town make a strategic plan that looks at the
various initiatives in totality. He said the Government Alliance for Racial Equity gives lots of examples of
this and projects such as CRESS, the Resident Oversight Board, and the DEI department will take lots of
attention in implementation.
Michelle Miller, Amherst resident – Miller commented as Co-Chair of the African Heritage Reparations
Assembly (AHRA) to communicate the group’s financial requests as voted on November 10. She said the
bulk of the AHRA’s work so far has been identifying alternative funding streams. Miller stated that the
AHRA determined these requests will be needed to fully satisfy their charge; future annual transfers to the
designated Reparations Stabilization Fund of 20% of the town’s Free Cash, 100% of the Cannabis Tax
Revenue, $600K of the $1.7 million earmarked in the October spending plan for Racial Equity and
Health, $600K of the $700K earmarked in the October spending plan for Recreation, $6 million total
across 3 rounds of ARPA funds, and $175K of CPA funds.
Jamileh Jemison, Amherst Resident – Jemison commented as Co-Chair of the AHRA, stating that
recognizing the hardships that African residents in Amherst face is a process. She said the funds the
AHRA requested provide opportunities for Amherst to commit to this process. Jemison spoke of the
chance to disrupt the status quo. She commented that now is the time to repair generations of broken
promises.
Marisol Pierce Bonifaz, Amherst resident – Bonifaz commented as a leader of the Sunrise Amherst
movement and spoke of the importance of education and its effect on student’s lives. She said they must
prioritize education and alternatives to policing. Bonifaz said by fully funding the CRESS program and
the other recommendations of the final CSWG would install new hope.

Zoe Crabtree, Amherst resident – Crabtree urged the Council to listen to the recommendations the CSWG
brought forward, adding that implementation needs to be led by BIPOC folks.
Amrita, Amherst resident – Amrita commented that she is a member of the Sunrise Amherst movement
and she spoke of the importance of school to young residents. She asked the Council to remember
students when deciding on the FY23 budget. Amrita stated that racial equity should be a priority and
CRESS should be fully funded. She said climate change should also be prioritized.
Allegra Clark, Amherst resident – Clark commented that she echoes the voices of the youth activists that
just spoke. She said she hopes the FY23 budget does not include more cuts to schools. Clark urged the
Council to fully fund the CSWG recommendations as well as the DEI Department and proposed Cultural
and Youth Centers. Clark added that she has not seen anything about the Cultural Center and hopes that is
not getting dismissed.
Julian, a young Amherst resident– Julian commented as a leader of the Sunrise Amherst movement,
acknowledging that the budget cycle is difficult. He said the residents’ priorities were reflected in who
they elected and current Councilors should slow down and understand the public’s priorities. Julian said
the Council should preserve school funding and spoke of the Police Department’s ability to fund a new
cruiser that is 10 years newer than school buses. He said equitable public safety services must also be
brought into consideration.
Irv Rhodes, Amherst resident – Rhodes commented that he would like to reiterate what the young
speakers have already said, and the school should be getting a 3% increase in the budget. He said in
regard to the request for $6 million for affordable housing by the AHRA, there is no pathway for
affordable homeownership. Rhodes said the lack of the affordable homeownership pathway has created a
permanent underclass. He also asked for staff to specify which funding proposals are for 1 fiscal year and
which are for multiple fiscal years. Rhodes urged the Council not to fund things with ARPA funds that
the town will not be able to afford in 2-3 years.
Nick Chirekos, Amherst resident – Chirekos commented that he is a representative from the firefighters’
group, Local 1764. He said the Fire Department continues to struggle on a daily basis and frequently
relies on neighboring communities. He said AFD’s overutilization of other community resources is now
affecting those other communities. Chirekos stated that AFD staffing needs to increase as they are facing
a significant problem of firefighters burning out and finding work elsewhere. He clarified that the 4
firefighter positions mentioned earlier are not additional, those are just added to help reach the 45-position
mark.
Levi Gibson, Amherst resident – Gibson commented as a member of the Sunrise Amherst movement,
stating that he is concerned about the funding of public schools. Gibson stated that he does not expect to
see further budget cuts to schools, and this will damage student’s mental health and interest in school.
Lydia Irons, Amherst resident – Irons commented that she wants to reiterate the urgency from other
speakers tonight. She asked the Council if they can hear the urgency in everyone’s voices asking for full
funding of CRESS, AHRA requests, etc. Irons said the town can find money by defunding the APD as
they have more money than they need and use.
Isolda Ortega-Bustamante, Amherst resident – Ortega-Bustamante requested looking into how education
most directly impacts long-term health, wellness, and future prospects. She spoke of increasing stress of
students during the pandemic. Ortega-Bustamante commented that in terms of behavioral healthcare there
is a way to expend some of these funds into social workers or clinicians for the emergency mental health

Ellisha Walker, Amherst resident – Walker commented in response to the last discussion of the CSWG
