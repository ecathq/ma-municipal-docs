Temporary Moratorium on the Permitting and Approval of Large-Scale Ground Mounted Solar
Photovoltaic Installations, sponsored by Councilors De Angelis and Griesemer.
MOTION now under reconsideration: To refer Zoning Bylaw Article 16 – Temporary Moratorium on
the Permitting and Approval of Large-Scale Ground Mounted Solar Photovoltaic Installations, sponsored
by Councilors De Angelis and Griesemer, to the Planning Board and the Community Resources
Committee for hearing/s held no later than January 12, 2022, and for a written recommendation and an
explanation as to whether the proposed bylaw is not inconsistent with the Master Plan from the Planning
Board to the Town Council and to the Community Resources Committee no later than 21 days after the
Planning Board hearing, and for the Community Resources Committee to send a written recommendation
to the Town Council and to submit all materials to the Governance, Organization, and Legislation
Committee for review of clarity, consistency, and actionability within 60 days of the hearing held by the
Community Resources Committee.
Hanneke said she supported reconsideration because not many people on the Council knew about the
Chapter 40A Section 3 prohibition on bylaws that prohibit installation of solar mechanisms. She said they
need to consider whether they want to take risk of litigation and also consider what the status of a Solar
Bylaw is at now/when will a draft be ready.
Griesemer said planning staff recommended putting in 18 months on the Solar Bylaw based on
experience with how long it takes bylaws to be developed and passed.
De Angelis said the bylaw would need to fit Amherst and a moratorium would provide that planning time.
She said there is a misconception that they are out to end in ground solar which is not accurate. She said
Spencer, Warren, Athol etc. have all done moratoriums for this reason and the biggest issue is the sense of
having limited time.
Schoen agreed with De Angelis, adding that the Council hasn’t asked to examine the risks of other
decisions they have made. She said everyone’s preference would be to develop a bylaw for Amherst with
enough time and a temporary moratorium makes sense.
Ryan asked the sponsors if and why they feel the ZBA is not up for the task. Bockelman said the
Conservation Commission is restricted to the wetlands only. Griesemer said she has no doubt of the
Conservation Commission’s ability.
Schreiber commented that De Angelis’s concerns are the role of the Planning Board. He said he went
through the zoning priority list and this was not on list. Schreiber said it is time for Amherst to step up to
produce its own clean energy.
Ross said they can move forward with a Solar Bylaw with or without a moratorium. He said staff should
be spending time on the many zoning projects they need to work on and asked Hanneke about the timing
of the public hearing.
Hanneke clarified that when the town passes bylaw, the Attorney General must review it before it goes
into effect.
De Angelis said Chapter 40A Sec. 3 is ambiguous and many local politicians are working to change the
statute language so that communities can reasonably regulate solar. She said that have been following
guidance from the PVPC and looking at the Department of Energy’s resources from the State.
