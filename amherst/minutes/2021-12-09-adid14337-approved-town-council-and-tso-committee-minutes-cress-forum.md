Martha Hanner – Hanner commented that she is representing the Amherst League of Women Voter’s
Racial Justice Committee. She said the body is in support of the formation of the CRESS program. She
said after listening to the forums held by the CSWG, she was sobered to hear from the BIPOC community
about the need for this. Hanner stated that implementation will take time and she hopes there is strong
transparency and communication with the public. She said they also hope to have commitment from the
Town for fully fund the program.
Ash Hartwell – Hartwell commented that the challenge with CRESS will be using the data that comes in
as a way to see the best way to respond. He said there should be a pretty rigorous means of evaluating the
work and use in the larger community for input. Hartwell added that he hopes this will be a learning
process and acknowledged that there will be mistakes made.
Lydia Irons – Irons said Defund413 wants to know how many people are in the meeting today.
Ross said there are currently 8 attendees in the room and originally, they had 23.
Jennifer Page – Page commented that Chief Livingstone said if someone asks for an officer, they are
going to get a police officer. She asked that they push back on the idea that if they ask for a police officer,
they’re going to get a police officer.
Kaylee Brow – Brow commented that she is grateful for the work of the CSWG. She said CRESS will fill
a critical role and she is excited to see how it goes. She echoed Hanner’s comment that she hopes the
Town commits to this program and expands it as time goes on.
Martha Hanner – Hanner said as an individual, the BIPOC community stated their fear of police presence.
She asked if someone called and said they did not want police, would that be honored.
Livingstone said if someone wants a CRESS responder so long as the dispatchers can screen out any
dangerous situation, that would be the response.
