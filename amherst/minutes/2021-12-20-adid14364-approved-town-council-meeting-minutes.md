4. General Public Comment
Ira Bryck, Amherst resident, spoke about the CVS lot rezoning, suggest that other sites deserve
consideration. CVS lot needs resurfacing and charging stations, a solar roof to charge the vehicles and
parking validation with free parking for shoppers. Re: mixed use, there are a lot of businesses downtown,
if we cut of spaces we can’t achieve more retail.
Meg Gage, Amherst resident, thanked the Council for their work and spoke about collaboration across
disagreements. Praised Councilors’ work and the President’s leadership.

---

Ken Rosenthal, Amherst resident, thanked his district Councilor George Ryan for his hard work, and
spoke about the rezoning of the CVS lot. Years to go before we have the ability to build a garage. Plenty
of time to do the parking location. Let the next Council start the planning process for a new garage.
Suzannah Muspratt, Amherst resident, thanked Councilors for their service, and urged the Council to
defer the vote on the two zoning amendments on the agenda. Spoke about comments being posted earlier
before each meeting. Questioned legal advice of the zoning amendment process.
Pam Rooney, Amherst resident, recognized the work the Council has done in the past two years, spoke in
opposition to zoning changes on the agenda. Asked for a minimum of 40% floor area as non-residential
for mixed-use buildings.
John Sheldon, Amherst resident, spoke in support of the zoning overlay regarding the potential parking
garage on the site. A vote for the zoning overlay allows for more discussion, allows for exploring other
sites, and allows the Town to put out an RFP for the site. Take into consideration the loss of parking on
the north common. No reason not to vote on the zoning bylaws this evening. Insulting to outgoing
members not to vote today.
Janet Keller, Amherst resident, thanked the Council for their work. Asked that the Council send the mixed
use zoning amendment to the Planning Board for further study.
Winnifred Manning, Amherst resident, spoke in opposition to the rezoning of the CVS lot. Spoke about
opposition among neighbors.
Local 1764 Ben Graham, president of the Amherst Firefighters Union. Concerns related to firefighters
wellbeing. Spoke about the plan to allow Chief Nelson to extend his employment. Pending arbitration for
overdue contract. Waiting to April for new leadership. Inappropriate actions at the department. Nelson
could be hired as a consultant.

Bockelman spoke about the state law and the special legislation required to allow Chief Nelson to
continue working for the Town past his 65th birthday. Bockelman spoke about Chief Nelson’s leadership
in the Fire Department and, during the COVID-19 pandemic, as a member of the emergency response
team, and other leadership qualities. Bockelman spoke about the importance of Chief Nelson’s role in the
establishment of the CRESS program.
De Angelis asked if the issues that were brought up by the firefighters’ union be addressed appropriately.
Bockelman stated the Town takes complaints of harassment or intimidating behavior very seriously,
allegations will be investigated thoroughly. The Town has a strong retaliation policy that protects those
who come forward with complaints. The firefighters’ union and the Town are actively in negotiations,
moving into arbitration which is a regulated process.
The Chief’s contract would be extended through June 30, 2023.
Councilors discussed hiring procedures for department heads, the Council’s role in the process and non-
involvement in personnel issues under the Town Manager’s purview.
DuMont expressed concern about the statements made by the firefighters’ union and asked if the issue can
be tabled.
MOTION: Griesemer moved, second by Hanneke, to suspend Town Council Rules of Procedure rule 8.4
for the current agenda item.
VOTED 11-1-1 by roll call (Bahl-Milne, Brewer, De Angelis, Griesemer, Hanneke, Ross, Ryan, Schoen,
Schreiber, Steinberg, and Swartz voted Yes; DuMont voted No; Pam Abstained) to suspend Town
Council Rules of Procedure rule 8.4 for the current agenda item.
MOTION: Griesemer moved, second by Hanneke, to support the passage of Special Legislation that
would enable Fire Chief Walter (Tim) Nelson to continue his employment with the Town of Amherst as
Fire Chief beyond his 65th birthday.
VOTED 9-2-2 by roll call (Bahl-Milne, Brewer, De Angelis, Griesemer, Hanneke, Ross, Ryan,
Schreiber, and Steinberg voted Yes; DuMont and Swartz voted No; Pam and Schoen Abstained) to
support the passage of Special Legislation that would enable Fire Chief Walter (Tim) Nelson to continue
his employment with the Town of Amherst as Fire Chief beyond his 65th birthday.
b. Zoning Bylaw Amendments
The following two items were postponed under Charter Sec. 2.10(c) on
December 6, 2021, and further postponed on December 9, 2021
1) Amendments to Article 3, Use Regulations, Section 3.325, Mixed-Use
Buildings and Article 12, Definitions
MOTION CURRENTLY ON THE FLOOR: To adopt revisions to Article 3.325, Mixed-Use Building
and Article 12, Definitions of the Zoning Bylaw, by adding language shown in in bold/italic and deleting
language shown in bold/strikethrough on pages 15-16 of the Motion sheet.
MOTION: Schoen moved, second by Swartz, to amend the revisions to Article 3.325, Mixed-Use
Building and Article 12, Definitions of the Zoning Bylaw by replacing 70% with 65% in the first
paragraph, and replacing 30% with 35% in the second paragraph, so the first two paragraphs read:

No more than 65% of the Gross Floor Area of the first or ground floor shall be residential use,
parking, or common areas shared by multiple uses, unless otherwise permitted below.
At least 35% of the Gross Floor Area of the first or ground floor shall be any permitted non-
residential use, other than parking, including incidental spaces, except that the Permit Granting
Authority may allow the required non-residential use(s) to be distributed on any floor, or in any
building of a multiple building development on the same parcel, provided that the portion of the
first or ground floor of any building facing the Street shall be occupied predominantly by such
non-residential use(s).
Hanneke spoke in opposition to the motion given the reality and a retail study conducted in 2018, the
empty storefronts that occur as a result of requiring a larger percentage of retail without businesses able to
fill that space, currently there is 15,000 sq. ft. of empty retail space in the mill district.
Pam stated if we need a garage it will be purely for tenants for mixed-use buildings downtown.
Bahl-Milne spoke about empty storefronts before the pandemic due to high rents and online shopping, the
desire to attract small businesses.
Brewer asked to hear from supporters of the motion if they intend to vote against the mixed-use revisions
even with the change in percentage.
Schoen spoke in support of the mixed-use amendment, and added that her preference is 40% and this is a
good faith effort to compromise.
Ross stated the proposed percentage might work in the downtown but not in north Amherst or another
village center or retail areas, and expressed concern with setting the percentage too high; Ross stated he
will vote against the mixed-use amendment if the motion passes.
Swartz stated she would support the amendment to try to find some compromise, 35% is a step in the
right direction; mixed-use buildings should have a good amount of retail or it is just an apartment
building.
Schreiber echoed Ross’ comments, stating that a no vote on the mixed-use amendment results in a worse
outcome than a lower percentage, stated that developers are good at gaming the bylaws as leasing offices
count as retail space, and that 30% is a less flawed bylaw than what we have.
Steinberg asked about varying the percentage based on the zoning district.
Brestrup responded she would be reluctant to introduce extra layers at this point since there is already
confusion about the bylaws.
Hanneke stated that if the mixed-use amendment passes, a motion could be made to amend it in the
future, if the mixed-use amendment fails it could come back within 2 years but in the meantime we have
nothing; Hanneke urged the Council to accept 30%/70% and revisit it in the future if needed.
Griesemer noted that at least one applicant is waiting to see what the Council does before submitting their
application.
VOTED 4-9 by roll call (DuMont, Pam, Schoen, and Swartz voted Yes; Bahl-Milne, Brewer, De Angelis,
Griesemer, Hanneke, Ross, Ryan, Schreiber, and Steinberg voted No) to amend the revisions to Article
3.325, Mixed-Use Building and Article 12, Definitions of the Zoning Bylaw by replacing 70% with 65%

2) Official Zoning Map – Map 14A, Parcel 33 Rezoning, North Prospect
Street: Amendments to Article 2, Zoning Districts and Article 3, Use
Regulations
MOTION CURRENTLY ON THE FLOOR: To adopt a new Parking Facility District as part of
Section 2.04, Special Districts, and as Section 3.23, Parking Facility District of the Zoning Bylaw, as
shown on pages 11-14 of the Motions sheet.
Schoen stated there is no need to rush and spoke in opposition to the motion.
DuMont echoed Schoen’s comments and spoke about public comments received in opposition to the
bylaw.
Griesemer spoke in support of the amendment, stating we are not voting a parking garage, we are voting
to zone the parcel.
Brewer spoke in support, stating that the Council should pass the amendment so the location is a
possibility, and other possible sites should be explored and information gathered before the next stage of
the process.
Steinberg stated this is a vote about zoning not a vote to build. The Town will have to come back to the
Council again if there is a sale or long term lease in the future.
Schoen spoke in opposition, stating that the Town does not have to change zoning to build a garage, that
there is no need to rush, and that the parcel is valuable.
ROLL CALL VOTE: 9-4 (Bahl-Milne, Brewer, De Angelis, Griesemer, Hanneke, Ross, Ryan,
Schreiber, and Steinberg voted Yes; DuMont, Pam Schoen, and Swartz voted No) to adopt a new Parking
Facility District as part of Section 2.04, Special Districts, and as Section 3.23, Parking Facility District of
the Zoning Bylaw, as shown on pages 11-14 of the Motions sheet.
