Payroll increase of 1.4% is based on FY22 Salary Schedule

Modest increases to Transportation and Medicaid Reimbursement estimates relative to FY22.
Significant use of E&D to support the budget.
Possible due to being significantly under budget at the end of FY21

Town Budget Comments and Questions
Video timestamp: 41:40
John Trickey (Pelham Finance Committee) noted that the increase in Pelham's portion in the assessment methods is likely due to in
increase in the town's share of students at the regional schools. Trickey asked for data on the overall decline in enrollment at the
regional schools and Pelham's percentage of regional students. He also asked if it was possible to get enrollment forecasts.
Dr. Slaughter replied that they could present enrollment forecasts although gathering this data is a bit harder to predict due
to the change in school choice policies. Dr. Slaughter reviewed the enrollment graph in his presentation (includes all students
in schools: vocational, charter, private schools, not just ARMS & ARHS). Dr. Slaughter confirmed that Pelham's percentage of
regional students has increased which would result in higher cost to Pelham.
Ajay Khashu (Shutesbury Finance Committee) asked for updates on whether the regional agreement would be revisited and the
implication of the review on the budget.
Dr. Morris replied that the Amherst school committee has written a letter to the Amherst Town Council to ask for additional
clarification on the this process.
Rita Farrell (Shutesbury Selectboard) asked how much of the increase in the risks & benefits lines was from increase in enrollees
versus increases in insurance costs.
Dr. Slaughter prefaced that the increase in enrollees is coming off of FY21 which had a lower amount of enrollees than
previous years due to Covid. The overall increase of health insurance cost is approx. 8%; approximately 4% is due to
increase in insurance costs and approximately 4% is due to increase in membership size. Dr. Slaughter added that property
insurance costs have increased as well.
Lynn Griesmer (Amherst Town Council) asked what was driving the increase in legal costs.
Dr. Slaughter replied that primarily due to an increased need for legal advice and that there is generally higher amounts of
negotiations happening during covid.
Griesmer asked what the plan would be in future years if the district could not expect the increases in available school choice and
E&D funds.
Dr. Slaughter replied that the plan is to tapper those sources over the years; school choice funds have been relatively more
predictable than E&D during Covid. Once the disruption of covid lessens and there is greater predictability ideally the district
will use other funds (e.g. Ch 70) that will allow them to lower E&D portion of budget.
Cathy Shoen (Amherst Town Council) expressed that she would like to see how much is spent on charter schools and the charter
formula.
Shalini Bahl (Amherst Town Council) asked for an explanation for the decrease in Special Education services and where the
additional supports for mental health services are reflected in the budget.
Dr. Slaughter replied that special education expenses fluctuate year to year; circuit breaker reimbursement timing makes it
difficult to capture the amount of funding placed into special education as there is a lag between when funds are spent, when
the state sends circuit breaker funds to the district, and how the district places the reimbursement back into budget. Mental
health supports were largely paid for by grants including ESSER funds.
Town Assessment Feedback
Video timestamp: 1:02:51
Trickey (Pelham) noted that while the increase to Pelham is substantial, it reflects the increase in Pelham residents in the regional
schools and the town will pay its portion under the chosen assessment method.
Andy Steinberg (Amherst Town Council) expressed that the assessment method was not sustainable. He noted FY 18 - FY 22 the
region's budget increased over 5%. He noted there has been an increase in Amherst percentage of the regional agreement over past
five years while the other towns' portions decreased. He noted a large portion of the land in Amherst is nontaxable and does not
garner revenue.
Griesmer (Amherst) noted that the Town of Amherst FY23 financial guidelines were a 2.5% increase for the school budget; none of
existing assessment methods have that amount. Griesmer expressed she would like more information about reason for the high per-
pupil expenditure.
Rita Farrel (Shutesbury) expressed that Shutesbury goal is to achieve a 100% statutory agreement with 5-year rolling average.
Julia Shively (Leverett Selectboard) Leverett would like the regional assessment. Shively expressed concern that Amherst would not
be able to afford 100% statutory method.
Amherst-Pelham Regional School Committee Feedback
Video timestamp: 1:13:17
Rhodes commented that budget presentation showed a hard budget line not soft. He noted that the regional budget is to reflect the
community's values and that reducing costs will effect the student experience. Additionally, the regional schools are in a
competitive market.
Demling commented that there is a need for sustained grassroots advocacy on the charter school formula. He noted a 100%
regional agreement method does not consider the Four member Towns' ability to pay and that the per-pupil cost reflects the values
of the community. Additionally, the district has made significant cuts in the past 4-5 years.
