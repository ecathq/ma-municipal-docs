3. Hearings
a. 6:30 p.m. Public Hearing – Eversource Requests to install utility poles at the following
locations:
1. Echo Fill Access Road
Councilors asked if the poles had already been placed; Eversource representative responded that
there are existing poles in the area and new poles have not been planted.
Steinberg asked about the impact on shade trees; Eversource representative responded that
impact will be kept to a minimum to the extent possible.
No public comment.
Councilors agreed that since the placement is on Town property, the Town Manager should
negotiate an easement with Eversource.
2. 46 Rolling Ridge Road
Eversource representative announced the petition has been withdrawn.
Amherst resident Jicheng Tang of Rolling Ridge Road asked questions about installing
underground wires and whether Eversource or the homeowner would be responsible for the cost
digging to install wires.
Eversource representative spoke about the existing pole placement. Councilors agreed to leave
the hearing open to allow time for issues between Eversource and residents to be worked out
outside the meeting.
3. 20 Triangle Street
Eversource representative spoke about the reason for the request, which will serve the Emily
Dickinson Museum for a new HVAC system. Councilors asked about placing a pole so close to
existing poles, and if the function of the new pole can be achieved by using an existing pole.
Eversource representative responded that the needed use will not work with an existing pole.
Councilors asked about impacts to shade trees; Eversource representative responded that it would
be minimal.
No public comment.
4. Woodside Avenue
Eversource representative spoke about the purpose of the new pole, which is to serve Amherst
College and will allow electric lines to be moved underground from the point the new pole
would be installed. Councilors asked about consolidating the poles to avoid crowding the street
with additional poles. Eversource representative stated they will measure and come back with
additional information about minimizing extraneous poles on the street. Councilors agreed that
the hearing should be continued to allow time for additional information.
No public comment.

Effective immediately, to hold all Town Council meetings in the in-person

Pam, Rooney, Schoen, Taub, and Walker voted Yes; Hanneke and Steinberg voted No) to
reconsider the January 3, 2022 Town Council vote:
Effective immediately, to hold all Town Council meetings in the in-person
meeting format until April 1, 2022.
MOTION on the table: effective immediately, to hold all Town Council meetings in the in-
person meeting format until April 1, 2022.
Hanneke spoke in favor of continuing in the current format, with in person access allowed for
Councilors who choose to attend in person and remote access for those who do now.
Pam commented that the Town Room does not allow for enough social distancing and that
Councilors are seated closer than six feet apart.
De Angelis spoke strongly in favor of moving to a remote format.
Griesemer asked that staff weigh in; staff responded that they will support the Council in the
format they choose.
VOTED 7-6 by roll call (Bahl-Milne, Devlin Gauthier, Griesemer, Hanneke, Lopes, Miller, and
Steinberg voted Yes; De Angelis, Pam, Rooney, Schoen, Taub, and Walker voted No) effective
immediately, to hold all Town Council meetings in the in-person meeting format until April 1,
2022.
