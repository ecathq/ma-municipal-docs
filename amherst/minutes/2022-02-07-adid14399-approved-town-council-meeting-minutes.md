4. General Public Comment
Elisa Campbell, Amherst resident, spoke in opposition to the proposed solar moratorium bylaw, stating
that the state is in need of additional solar, and spoke about the urgency of reducing greenhouse gas
emissions.
Ira Bryck, Amherst resident, spoke in support of the solar moratorium bylaw and spoke about the need for
time to develop a permanent solar bylaw.
Sharon McDonald, Amherst resident, spoke in support of the solar moratorium, and spoke about the
urgent need to create a thoughtful solar policy.
Eric Bachrach spoke in support of the solar moratorium bylaw, and about the need to protect the water
supply.
Martha Hanner, Amherst resident, spoke in support of the solar moratorium bylaw so that the Town can
develop a permanent solar bylaw.
Kathleen Bridgewater spoke in support of the solar moratorium bylaw, stating it will give the Town time
to develop a permanent solar bylaw.
Michael Lipinski, Amherst resident, spoke in support of the solar moratorium, and spoke about and
questioned the Energy and Climate Action Committee formula used to determine the Town needs 300
acres of ground-mounted solar to meet its climate goals.

Agenda Item 8.c. Action Items, Proposed Zoning Article 16: Temporary Moratorium on the
Permitting and Approval of Large-Scale Ground Mounted Solar Energy Systems, Including
Photovoltaic Installations – First Reading
Sponsor Devlin Gauthier spoke about the goals of the temporary moratorium bylaw, and what a
temporary moratorium would and would not allow the Town to do.
Doug Marshall, Planning Board Chair, reported on the Planning Board’s review, discussion, and 5-2 vote
to not recommend the Town Council adopt the temporary solar moratorium. Bahl-Milne spoke about
resources available to the Zoning Board of Appeals (ZBA) that have not been taken advantage of.
Hanneke spoke about the Community Resources Committee review, hearing, deliberation, and
recommendation on the bylaw; CRC voted unanimously to recommend the Town Council adopt the
bylaw as amended.
Miller reported that following committee edits for clarity, GOL voted unanimously to declare the bylaw
clear, consistent, and actionable.
Bahl-Milne asked about the unintended consequences of a moratorium, provisions to secure a
decommission fund, and responses to other concerns about the moratorium.

Rooney spoke about a commenter’s concern about wording in the preamble, and suggested adding
“potentially” hundreds of acres to clarify that sentence.
Hanneke stated she is leaning toward not voting for the bylaw, adding that subdivision plans have been
filed which mean that the temporary moratorium and solar siting would not apply to those sites, and it
seems unwise to stop development of solar on preferred areas. Hanneke spoke about the perception of
Amherst as progressive and climate-forward and concern with the appearance that Amherst is not
supportive of solar.
Devlin Gauthier stated Amherst is not a leader on climate, spoke about frustration that the bylaw was not
written five years ago, and the maximum length of the bylaw would be 18 months. Devlin Gauthier spoke
about the resources available to the ZBA and the need for regulation and not reliance on promises by
energy companies.
Lopes asked about what would happen during the moratorium, which towns had successes with solar
moratorium bylaws and what were the successes, and other ways solar projects may be slowed down.
Miller read a statement regarding her consideration of the bylaw. Miller asked about the anticipated
completion date and scope of a solar siting study, FY22 revenue for solar and budgeted revenue for FY23,
and how many acres of solar can be accommodated in town on public and private lands.
Taub noted CRC added a specific expiration date to the bylaw, stated that the moratorium would not send
a negative message about Amherst and that there are many people in the field that view a moratorium as a
responsible action.
Pam spoke in support for the bylaw, and for renewable energy and to preservation of water systems and
safe and clean water.
Schoen spoke in support of the bylaw, and commented about regulations and standards needed, stated a
bylaw can be created more quickly than 15 months, and spoke about the desire to move forward in an
informed and thoughtful way.
De Angelis stated that there are limits to Amherst’s progressiveness, and that it is critical to look at
climate action with a social justice lens. De Angelis spoke about preserving forests and undeveloped
lands. De Angelis spoke about regionalization and the benefit to a greater number of people of
regionalization, using state and federal funding for home retrofits, and space on rooftops for solar panels.
Devlin Gauthier spoke about applying an equity lens to policy.
Hanneke stated an equity lens can be used when developing a permanent bylaw, that we do not need a
moratorium to do that, that anyone can seek an extension on the moratorium, that the solar study is a 6-12
month process and the bylaw cannot be completely drafted during that time.
Walker spoke about the climate crisis as real and urgent, and it is a greater risk if we allow things to move
forward.
De Angelis spoke about the work that would be done during the moratorium on a solar study and bylaw,
as well as conversations with developers and others in the Planning Department and Department of Public
Works to talk and collaborate.
Rooney asked about the subdivision application that was submitted.
