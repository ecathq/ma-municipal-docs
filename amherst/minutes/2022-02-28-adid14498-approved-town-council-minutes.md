7. Presentations and Discussion
a. FEMA Floodplain Study: Update and Next Steps
Planning Director Christine Brestrup told the Council that the Town has been working on updating
floodplain maps since 2012. The maps were last updated in 1983. New methods of updating floodplain
maps were introduced in 2017 and those are now being used. Updated maps are needed to ensure
appropriate insurance liability in the event of flooding. Updated maps are needed before homeowners can
buy flood insurance.
Consultant Jennifer Moss spoke about why the 1983 floodplain maps need updating to satisfy FEMA
requirements. She said the next step for the Town is to draft a letter of determination for FEMA. She said
that 10.9 miles of rivers in the Town have been examined. Many factors have changed since 1983,
including changes in topography in many of these areas. The Town must adopt a completed floodplain
plan and have it approved by the state by October 2022.
Pam asked about interpreting the maps. Brestrup said they are available online. Pam said she still isn’t
sure what the maps represent—flooding or not flooding? Brestrup offered to look over the maps with her
on paper. She said that the maps indicate where floods might take place based on past events. Rooney
asked about what type of zoning bylaw might be needed regarding these maps. Senior Planner Nathaniel
Malloy said that bylaws must conform with the requirements that FEMA lays out. Taub asked why Tan
Brook is not included in the floodplain. Brestrup said that Tan Brook and the downtown area have never
been included in the floodplain.

8. Action Items
a. Water Regulations
Paul Bockelman said this is the first time the Council has dealt with these types of water regulations.
Asst. Superintendent of Public Works Amy Rusiecki took over to discuss the water regulations and the
Town’s four reservoirs. She noted that the Town uses 3 million gallons of water a day on average, but that
it fluctuates considerably based on season. Amherst last approved Water Use Regulations in the early
1970s. An update to these regulations is needed to serve as a set of rules between all users and the Town
and the Water Department in large part because of new state or federal regulations and because of new
technology. She presented a look at the sections that will be needed in new water regulations, including
components of the system, rates charged for water, acquiring or discontinuing service, and liability.
Bockelman noted that a new bylaw would need to be passed so that the Council can adopt new
regulations.
Devlin Gauthier asked about how high fines were that can be levied against people who violate new water
regulations. Also she was concerned that enough time be given to users to mitigate water problems.
Rooney asked about the options on how to proceed. Bockelman said there is no clear path for proceeding
and that’s why the current idea was to pass a bylaw giving the Council authority to proceed. Steinberg
said there was a need to protect the water that goes into the system, particularly based on land use in those
areas. Mooring said that because of zoning regulations the Town is notified when changes are made to
land use in Town. Bockelman said there are state regulations as well. Schoen asked about estimated bills
and long periods of time between meter reading. Mooring said that estimated bills should not happen
often and that people who get an estimated bill should contact the DPW. But he added estimated bills will
still be needed occasionally. He attributed most of that to human error. Rusieki said many people get
letters from the Town asking to replace old meters, but that they often don’t respond.
Rooney asked how people are supposed to know they have a leak in their water system until they get a
very large bill, and noted that homeowners currently have only seven days to mitigate a leak problem.
Mooring said there are often ways to know if you have a leak, and that he is willing to work with people
as long as they contact the DPW and work cooperatively. Rusieki said the seven days requirement is a
state matter. Hanneke asked about the boundary of homeowner responsibility for repair to water service.
Rusieki said everything from the water main to the house is the homeowner’s responsibility. Hanneke
also asked about the leaking issue and said that seven days is almost impossible to detect and repair a
water leak. Mooring said they will work with a homeowner who contacts them.
MOTION: Griesemer moved, second by Hanneke, to refer the proposed Water Regulations and proposed
Bylaw to the Town Services and Outreach Committee, with review by the Finance Committee, and the
Governance, Organization and Legislation Committee, with a report to the Town Council by April 4,
2022.
VOTED unanimously, 13-0 by roll call, to refer the proposed Water Regulations and proposed Bylaw to
the Town Services and Outreach Committee, with review by the Finance Committee, and the
Governance, Organization and Legislation Committee, with a report to the Town Council by April 4,
2022.

b. Proposed Zoning Article 16: Temporary Moratorium on the Permitting and
Approval of Large-Scale Ground Mounted Solar Energy Systems, Including
Photovoltaic Installations – Second Reading
Griesemer said she wanted to approve the temporary moratorium.
Hanneke said she previously supported the Town’s efforts to move toward 100 percent renewable energy,
including votes at previous town meetings. She said the moratorium contravenes that.
Bahl-Milne said that we all care about town forests and about how large-scale solar projects might effect
parts of town, but all might not agree about the effect an 18-month moratorium might have. She added
that there are policies and processes in place to deal with new large-scale solar projects and to safeguard
impacts on the town and neighborhoods.
Miller said that a delay in confronting the climate crisis is not good. She said developers who want to
create new solar projects should be embraced.
Taub said she supported past resolutions to move toward renewable energy. She said the moratorium will
not halt solar installations, it will just mean a pause, adding that having a good bylaw in place is worth the
pause.
Pam said that experts need to be consulted and that at a recent planning board meeting it was clear that
there were a lot of things that the town does not have answers to. She said she supports a brief
moratorium.
Steinberg said he was not at the first reading of this matter, but that he has listened to that meeting and
concluded that no one opposes a strong solar bylaw. But he said he opposes moratoria generally except in
extreme cases and he doesn’t think this is such a case.
De Angelis said she supports the moratorium and is trying to find a balance between renewable energy
and the need to protect forests. She added that labeling the moratorium anti-solar is just a political tactic,
noting that this is a chance to develop a good bylaw.
Lopes said she doesn’t see the moratorium as anti-solar. She thinks that it is important to put people first.
Walker said there are other things that can be done regarding renewable energy and the climate crisis
during a moratorium. And she noted that if this is not done well the first time it will be the most
vulnerable populations who are most adversely affected.
MOTION: Griesemer moved, second by Pam, to adopt Zoning Article 16: Temporary Moratorium on the
Permitting and Approval of Large-Scale Ground Mounted Solar Energy Systems, Including Photovoltaic
Installations, as shown on pages 7-8 of the Motions sheet.
VOTED 8-5 (De Angelis, Devlin Gauthier, Griesemer, Pam, Rooney, Schoen, Taub, and Walker voted
Yes; Bahl-Milne, Hanneke, Lopes, Miller, and Steinberg voted No); to adopt Zoning Article 16:
Temporary Moratorium on the Permitting and Approval of Large-Scale Ground Mounted Solar Energy
Systems, Including Photovoltaic Installations, as shown on pages 7-8 of the Motions sheet.
The motion failed.
Devlin Gauthier said that even though the motion failed she remained optimistic that a new bylaw
governing the creations of large-scale solar projects will be created. She added that large-scale solar
projects will be welcome in Amherst. She made a motion to ask the Town Manager to create a Bylaw
Working Group to draft a new solar bylaw.
MOTION: Devlin Gauthier moved, second by Schoen, to task the Town Manager to create a Solar

this committee should be brought before the Council for comments on March 7, 2022, and should include

Hanneke said that Councilors wanted to make sure this bylaw would happen and be done in a year.
