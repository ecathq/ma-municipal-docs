3. Update on Elementary School Building Project
1. Town Council Discussion with Councilor members of the Elementary School
Building Committee
Schoen spoke about the desire to build an excellent, new elementary school. The new school project must
meet phasing modules set out by the state. Currently the Town is in a preliminary phase. A preliminary
design for the school is expected to be done by the end of March. The new school will replace both the
Wildwood and Fort River schools and will be on one of those two sites. It will serve 575 students in K
through 5th grade.
The School Committee will vote on the education program and space design on March 8. The education
program currently calls for five classrooms per grade and a class size of 19 or 20.
Walker spoke about the use of space in and around the new building. On the Wildwood site, the school
would be two or three stories, or a partial renovation and addition to the existing school. At the Fort River
site a two-story building is more likely than a three-story structure. A partial renovation and addition to
the existing school is possible here as well.

Griesemer said there will be some preliminary costs available by Friday for all the possible options. The
Town still does not know what the state share of the costs will be. A decision on the preferred option for
the new school is expected in June and will be sent to the state. The hope is that the state will report on
their cost share by the end of the year. The goal is to have the new school open in the fall of 2026.
Hanneke said her main concern is cost, and whether a two-story or three-story building is less expensive
to build. She said the Council needed to bring the project in at a cost that the majority of the public and
Council are comfortable with.
Schoen said that the cost of a two-story or three-story school will be assessed but that a three-story
structure is usually cheaper because of the lower cost for the foundation.
Taub said that since 2016 the amount of square feet per student has shifted two or three times.
Schoen responded that in 2016 the discussion included a 6th grade, and that the number of special needs
students has also increased and they require more space in part because of support staff.
Pam said she thinks there is not enough space in the current plan. She specifically would like more gym
space and art space and pointed out health concerns, among others. She also said that a three-story
building would require elevators to make the school handicap accessible, which would increase the cost.
Hanneke also said that more gym and art space would be preferable. Rooney spoke about the importance
of community shared space since it serves every person in the building.
Miller asked whether the Town can receive grants or other gifts for the project.
Bockelman said nothing precludes the Town from seeking outside grants or gifts for the project and that
they will be sought.
Mangano said the Town just needs to be sure that any outside funding will not reduce the state’s
contribution.
Walker asked that there be a cost target so that the entire Council know what the goal is.
Steinberg said many factors go into determining cost.
Schoen said the net cost to taxpayers is something we need to investigate. She noted that the state said
they turn down 85 percent of towns that apply for funding for schools and that Amherst was lucky to be
in the pipeline. She also said there are creative ways to make the most use of space, such as dividers for
large spaces such as gymnasiums
