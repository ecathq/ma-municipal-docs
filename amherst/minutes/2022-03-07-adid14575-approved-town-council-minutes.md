7. Presentations and Discussion
a. Energy and Climate Action Committee Report
Energy and Climate Action Committee Chair Laura Draucker spoke about the committee’s annual report,
and a recent report that found there is a small window to take action before there is irreparable damage
done to the climate and environment.
Schoen thanked Draucker and Sustainability Coordinator Stephanie Ciccarello for their attendance and
participation during Elementary School Building Committee meetings.
Devlin Gauthier spoke about the need for a regional effort and asked Hanneke if there is any climate work
in the Municipal and Regional Administration Committee, where she is a member.
Hanneke spoke briefly about the committee’s work and said she would look into climate efforts from the
committee.
b. Draft Solar Siting and Solar Bylaw Working Group Charge
Bockelman spoke briefly about the draft committee charge.
Devlin Gauthier asked about the working group’s role in the assessment and siting study.
Bockelman spoke about the Sustainability Coordinator’s role as contact with the solar study consultant.
Hanneke asked about the group’s composition, and expressed concern about a lack of industry
representation; Hanneke stated the charge lacks a statement that solar is needed to meet the Town’s
climate action goals.

Pam commented that she would like a tighter timeframe for the group to complete their work.
Lopes asked about the members with solar and or forestry experience, and about authority to extend
deadlines.
Griesemer spoke about the Council’s workload in November and December of last year, and the desire to
prevent the Council from experiencing another intense work period.
Devlin Gauthier commented that she would like to see reference to environmental justice and agricultural
implications in the charge, as well as non-voting members from the Board of Health, Fire Department,
and Wetlands Administrator.
Bockelman spoke about feedback he has already received about the draft charge.
Devlin Gauthier suggested that the Energy and Climate Action Committee more suitably review the
request for proposals.
Pam asked why it is not possible to have a Councilor on the committee.
Steinberg added that it can be awkward to have Councilors as members of committees, as has happened in
the past with Councilors serving on the Energy and Climate Action Committee. Steinberg stated he is
uncomfortable with the idea of a solar industry expert on the committee, similar to having a member of
the Police Department as a member of the Community Safety Working Group.
Devlin Gauthier spoke about her role as liaison to the ECAC and asked about staff support to the working
group.
Bockelman spoke about Sustainability Coordinator Stephanie Ciccarello and Planning Director Christine
Brestrup as staff support, with access to the Town’s attorney for input on the form of the bylaw.

Legislation
Miller thanked each member of the AHRA and spoke about the Resolution Affirming the Town of
Amherst’s Commitment to End Structural Racism and Achieve Racial Equity for Black Resident passed
by Town Council in December or 2020.
AHRA members Hala Lord, Alexis Reed, Amilcar Shabazz, and Jennifer Moyston shared a personal
reflection on “whereas” sections of the resolution.

Following questions, Miller stated the Town Manager would draft the home rule petition in consultation
with the Town Attorney and AHRA, Town could receive support from Representative Domb and House
Counsel, and the petition would define “public purpose” in a broad way in order to allow distribution of
benefits in the future.
MOTION: Griesemer moved, second by Miller, to ask the Town Manager to begin the process of seeking
special legislation to define reparations as a public purpose as described in the K.P. Law memo dated
September 28, 2021 and included in the March 7, 2022 Town Council meeting packet. The Town Manager
will seek guidance, as needed, from the African Heritage Reparation Assembly (AHRA) while drafting the
petition and will provide a copy of the proposed petition to the AHRA for review prior to submitting it to
the Town Council for approval to file with the State Legislature.
VOTED 12-0, with 1 absent (Bahl Milne) by roll call to ask the Town Manager to begin the process of
seeking special legislation to define reparations as a public purpose as described in the K.P. Law memo
dated September 28, 2021 and included in the March 7, 2022 Town Council meeting packet. The Town
Manager will seek guidance, as needed, from the African Heritage Reparation Assembly (AHRA) while
drafting the petition and will provide a copy of the proposed petition to the AHRA for review prior to
submitting it to the Town Council for approval to file with the State Legislature.
d. Proposed Residential Rental Bylaw Registration, Permit, and Inspection Fees
Hanneke stated herself and Councilors, Miller, Schoen, Rooney, and Taub are bringing forward proposed
fees for residential rental bylaw registration; to increase the fee to $250 per parcel and allow a discount
for a parcel that has a maximum of 6 rental units with 1 owner occupied unit and $150 inspection fee. The
proposal does not change the bylaw, only the fees associated with the bylaw. The group of sponsors
believe it belongs with TSO as a review of a fee.
Devlin Gauthier it is more appropriate with TSO, as a review of a fee structure, similar to the parking
permit fee increases.
MOTION: Griesemer moved, second by Devlin Gauthier, to refer the proposed Revision of Fees under
General Bylaw 3.50 – Residential Rental Property to Town Services and Outreach Committee, with input
from Finance Committee, for a report to the Town Council by April 4, 2022.
VOTED 12-0, with 1 absent (Bahl-Milne) by roll call to refer the proposed Revision of Fees under
General Bylaw 3.50 – Residential Rental Property to Town Services and Outreach Committee, with input
from Finance Committee, for a report to the Town Council by April 4, 2022.
e. Town Council Meeting Format
Councilors reviewed the votes taken by the Council on January 3 regarding the Council’s meeting format.
Schoen and Pam spoke in favor of continuing in the current arrangement.
Councilors discussed the various options for in person or virtual Council meetings.
Pam spoke in favor of virtual meetings.
Hanneke spoke in favor of in person meetings.
Lopes spoke about the difficulty of meeting at home for some Councilors.
De Angelis spoke in favor of allowing the President to decide the format in case of inclement weather or
other significant reason.

Taub agreed with De Angelis that she would like not to require staff to come in.
Councilors spoke about the difficulty with meeting at home and the benefit of meeting together in terms
of human contact and relationships.
MOTION: Griesemer moved, second by Devlin Gauthier, to suspend Town Council Rules of Procedure

meetings in the in-person meeting format until July 15, 2022, with the option of virtual meetings if the
President declares 24 hours in advance of a meeting that there will be fewer than 5 Councilors physically
present, and with virtual public access until July 15, 2022.
