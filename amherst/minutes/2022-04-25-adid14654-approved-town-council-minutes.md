3. Hearings
6:30 pm – Town Council and Finance Committee Public Hearing on the Regional
School Budget - Charter Sec. 5.5(a)
Steinberg called the Finance Committee meeting to order at 6:35 pm and confirmed that non-voting
Finance Committee member Hegner can hear and be heard.
Steinberg spoke about the separation of the regional school budget from the Town budget, about the
budget process for the regional school budget, including the vote to consider the regional budget
separately from the rest of the budget per Charter Sec. 5.5

Vince O’Connor, Amherst resident, spoke about housing and properties bought and used by the
University of Massachusetts and Amherst College; O’Connor commented that the CRESS department is
not fiscally rational, and that CRESS employees cutting Amherst police officers due to the hiring of
CRESS employees.

Holloway and Kubiak absent, to close the public hearing.

• The organization’s efforts to find a suitable temporary location
• Lease with Eversource is ending
• Assistance needed to find a temporary location
• Revenue from user fees
• Contractual agreement with the Town

• Communication between the Town and the organization
Griesmer referred to questions sent from the Town to Amherst Media, and questions posed by Councilors
about the contract and services between the Town and Amherst Media.
Bockelman spoke about Amherst Media’s request to use space in the Bangs Community Center, which is
not available now due to the establishment of CRESS services and the installation of the Civil War tablets
in spaces that had previously been vacant; Bockelman spoke about other uses for the building and the
limitations on available space at the Bangs Center. Bockelman spoke about other Town-owned buildings,
which have been determined to be either unsuitable or unavailable to use as a temporary location for
Amherst Media.
Bahl-Milne asked about the potential cost of renovating the Hickory Ridge building for use. Bockelman
responded that the building is in the process of condemnation, and it is in structurally poor condition.
De Angelis asked about the use of the Southeast School building, and if Amherst Media could fund what
is needed to make the space operational for them to use the space. Bockelman responded that he can
investigate costs.
De Angelis spoke about maintenance of Town-owned buildings and asked why the Town is not
maintaining its buildings. Bockelman spoke about the Town using funds to maintain buildings that are in
use and less so for buildings that are vacant. De Angelis asked about grant funding for building repairs so
that the Town can continue using buildings. Bockelman responded that state grant funds are currently
only available for libraries and schools.
Miller asked about Amherst Media’s needs for a temporary facility and asked about the possibility for the
arts coalition and Amherst Media to work together to find a space that is suitable to both groups.
McCollum responded that Amherst Media will need approximately 2,000 square feet of space and an I-
net connection for a temporary space and expressed interest in the idea of funding repairs to a Town
building that Amherst Media could use for a temporary facility.
Rooney asked about use of the middle school, and if there is a point person that Amherst Media can
contact during this process. Bockelman responded that the Amherst Media and Regional School
Superintendent have been in contact regarding potential space at the middle and high schools. McCollum
stated they have explored the option of space at the middle school, and it is not a suitable option; Amherst
Media have also had conversations with Amherst College and UMass about potential spaces on
campuses.
Walker asked about the timeframe that a temporary space will be needed. McCollum responded that they
would need between 1-2 years for a temporary space and spoke about the funding and construction
process.
Griesemer spoke about the agreement stipulation that there be a Town-designated liaison with Amherst
Media. Bockelman stated Communications Manager Brianna Sunryd is interested in serving on the board
on behalf of the Town. Steinberg expressed hesitation about reactivating that role after the previous form
of government appointed a liaison and experienced some tension and discomfort with their role and
position between the two entities. Miller spoke in support of a Town liaison with Amherst Media to
develop a stronger relationship and build trust, not necessarily a board member, to facilitate
communication. Griesemer spoke about her experience serving on both the Town Council and on the
board of the Amherst Survival Center and serving both entities.

responsibility to manage the contract, and that the Council is overstepping its role as a legislative body to
go and advocate for Amherst Media finding a location.

g. Deceptive Advertising Practices of Limited Services Pregnancy Centers
General Bylaw
Devlin Gauthier presented the proposed bylaw and spoke about the following:
• What a limited-service pregnancy center is
• Targeting of low-income and young women
• Misleading advertising with false or misleading information about abortion
• The bylaw’s intent to limit false advertising by limited-service pregnancy centers
• Elements of the bylaw: purpose and authority, definitions, prohibited activities, and enforcement
• Proposed referral of the bylaw to the Governance, Organization, and Legislation Committee for
review, including legal review
De Angelis thanked Devlin Gauthier for her work on the bylaw and spoke about the importance of
women’s rights to abortion access.
Taub asked about legal review.
Rooney asked about enforcement of the bylaw, how is advertising controlled.
Devlin Gauthier stated the bylaw pertains to online and print advertising and is complaint-driven and
targeted to Amherst or printing in a local publication.
Lopes asked about tracking how advertising is targeted to Amherst.
Miller commended Devlin Gauthier for bringing the bylaw forward, clarified that GOL would conduct a
substantive review, and asked about enforcement and enforcement by inspection services.
Devlin Gauthier spoke about how she determined the fine of $300, and the complaint-driven aspect being
enforced by Inspection Services rather than another department.
Schoen left the meeting at 9:00 pm.
Steinberg spoke about referral and review, and that $300 is the statutory limit for bylaws, and if it can be
a fine per day as an option; and questioned if the bylaw should be referred to another committee to
evaluation the assignment of the responsibility to a Town staff department. Steinberg spoke about other
departments that might be the enforcing authority for the bylaw.
Pam spoke in support of the bylaw.
MOTION: Hanneke moved, second by Lopes, to refer the Deceptive Advertising Practices of Limited
Services Pregnancy Centers General Bylaw to the Governance, Organization, and Legislation Committee,
for review of clarity, consistency, actionability, and recommendation on adoption to the Town Council by
June 6, 2022.
MOTION: Steinberg moved, second by Lopes, to amend the motion by adding, and “Town Services and

h. Town Council Motion Development Process: Proposed addition to Town
Council Rules of Procedure
VOTED via consent agenda to amend Rule 10.5j.1. of the Town Council Rules of Procedure by adding
the words “recommended motion language for Town Council action,” after “a record of any votes taken
regarding the proposed action,” so that Rule 10.5j.1. reads:
“Reports shall include the date of the report, list of Committee members, a summary of
the discussion, including pros and cons relating to any proposed action, minority views, a
record of any votes taken regarding the proposed action, recommended motion language
for Town Council action, and dates of meetings held.”
