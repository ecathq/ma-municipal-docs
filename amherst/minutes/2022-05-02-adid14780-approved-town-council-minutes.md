Hanneke asked if the other member towns have supported the debt authorization. Morris responded that in
Leverett the authorization was not placed on the town meeting warrant, so it passed by default.
Pam commented that it is strange that the Council is asked to vote and then vote again if more funds are
needed. Morris spoke about the lack of support from the four towns for the larger project, so a proposal
was not brought that was not expected to receive the needed support from all member towns. Morris
stated the vote assures that the school can move forward with the most critical need, which is the track.
Morris stated that if fundraising goals are achieved the schools can move forward with the larger project,
and if it does not, work will move forward on the track.
Schoen spoke about the Finance Committee discussion regarding the authorization including a stopping
point for fundraising so that the work on the track can begin.
Morris stated no student is turned away from sports due to an inability to pay fees, and the sports
offerings could not be financially supported without the fees.
Hanneke spoke in opposition to the motion, stating that she does not support the way the School
Committee has forced the project, that the School Committee has not been supportive of the larger project
in the community and that a vote for this is a vote solely for the smaller project, which is not in line with
the study to reorient the track to accommodate soccer and football and so that players are not facing the
sunset; a track resurfacing does not correct those issues. Hanneke stated that there is a lack of
conversation about a solution to the problem.
Morris spoke about fundraising and other appropriations funding sources.
Griesemer spoke in support of the motion, stating that the Town can signal its support of the larger project
by a vote on the $1.5M appropriation.
Devlin Gauthier spoke about Community Preservation Act Committee discussion that they would only
support the larger project, and that the smaller option does not feel like a good option.
Morris spoke about the challenge of the situation, and that if there were a four-town traditional funding
mechanism, that would be before the Council rather than the current proposal; a better option was not
available, and the price tag was high.

Pam spoke in opposition to the motion.
Steinberg spoke in support of the motion, CPA money cannot be used for turf, and that nothing will
happen unless we vote yes tonight.
Walker stated she has an issue with the waivers offered for athletes, asked how soon the track would be
unusable, and when would there be an opportunity for the School Committee to revisit the issue.
Morris responded that it has been recommended that the Town not host meets; due to town meetings in
the other towns, it would likely be another six months to a year before the issue could come to the town
meetings again.
Bahl-Milne asked if the track meets could continue if only the smaller project is funded, and suggested
fundraising through alumni directory.
Miller asked about the outcome of a no vote. Steinberg stated the $1.5M funding option would not move
forward, and the Regional School Committee would have to determine how to proceed, and that it would
likely be a year before a new proposal could be brought to the Council.
Hanneke challenged the assertion that a new proposal could not come back for a year; the Regional
School Committee could vote to send another proposal to the towns and the towns could call a special
town meeting if their select boards do not vote to call a special town meeting within 60 days.
Walker asked if there are other fields available to support the activities that could not be played on a
resurfaced field and asked if guidance or direction can be voted to be sent to the Regional School
Committee. Morris stated that a turf field could support many sports.
Morris stated that technically, special town meetings can be called but there was not broad advocacy from
any of the member towns for a larger project and the School Committee created a proposal that they
expected would be supported by the four towns. Morris stated all four towns were giving a similar
message that they would not support a $4M project.
Devlin Gauthier spoke about a potential turf field, that there is an opportunity for the community to bring
more people downtown or could be rented and has a larger use potential than just for student use and if
January is enough time to fundraise for the larger project.
Morris spoke about potential funding options among the towns.
Miller asked about options between the small project and the larger project, and about use of CPA funds.
Steinberg clarified that CPA would be an option up until the purchase and placement of grass seed or
artificial turf.
Walker asked about a fundraising plan. Morris responded that people involved in fundraising are waiting
to see if the towns support the project before beginning fundraising; fundraising would not likely begin
until the remaining two towns hold their town meetings this month.
Schoen spoke about concerns regarding artificial turf. Morris spoke about more recent innovations with
turf that allow more give, and the Athletic Director and boosters are in support of using turf; Morris spoke
about the added costs of maintaining a natural turf field and that fundraising for the cost of a natural field
is unrealistic.

d. Establishment of Rental Registration, Permit, and Inspection Fees in
accordance with General Bylaw 3.50 – Residential Rental Property
Steinberg reported that the Finance Committee voted to recommend adoption of the proposed fees.
Pam reported that the Town Services and Outreach Committee voted to recommend adoption of the
proposed fees.
MOTION: Griesemer moved, second by Devlin Gauthier, in accordance with General Bylaw 3.50, to
adopt the following fees for registration, permit application, and inspections:
A. Fee for Registering and Obtaining a Rental Permit in accordance with the Bylaw:
1. For a parcel with a maximum of six units with at least one of the units Occupied by
the Owner as a Principal Residence: $100
2. For all other parcels: $250
B. Fee for Inspections by the Code Official: $150 per inspection
Miller asked if the fees have already been incorporated into the budget. Bockelman responded that the
Town has factored the fees into the budget but that they would not have a big impact.
Rooney to clarify that the fee is for the processing and it is the same work for 1 unit or 20, and avoiding
the appearance of an inequity.

MOTION: Rooney moved, second by Taub, to amend the motion by striking the words “Registering and
