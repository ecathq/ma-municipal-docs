c. Resolution Calling on President Joe Biden to Immediately Cancel All Student
Loan Debt
Miller reported that Governance, Organization, and Legislation Committee voted unanimously to declare
the resolution clear, consistent, and actionable.
Hanneke spoke in opposition to the resolution, as cancelling all student debt has shown it may make the
problem worse in the future and cost to the government. Would be better for targeted income-based
programs.
Pam spoke in support of the resolution, stating the system is in shambles, and that students did not have to
pay so much to attend a state university in the past.
Schoen agreed with Hanneke’s comments and spoke about new debt stating that it is bad policy to go this
route. Would support a more targeted policy statement.
Bahl-Milne stated cancelling all debt would not fix the problem and direct a more targeted tiered
approach to make sure people who need it are getting support.
Walker spoke as a sponsor, this is not the long-term solution that will solve the educational systems
problems for years to come, this will be a first step. While we cancel debt, we also come up with a long-

Steinberg spoke as a sponsor, stating he had the same concerns initially, but felt that it is the right thing to

d. Resolution Protecting Adult Access to Plant Medicines & Prioritizing Public
Health Responses to Controlled Substance Possession
Miller reported the Governance, Organization, and Legislation Committee voted unanimously to declare
the resolution clear, consistent, and actionable.
Rooney spoke as a sponsor, stating there are many knowns and unknowns regarding information
presented to the public in the past and there needs to be a new mindset and support the opportunity for the
conversation to move forward and decriminalization will free the fear of health-related treatments.
Miller spoke as a sponsor, advocating for the public comment regarding personal positive impacts of the
use of plant medicines. Urged support of the resolution and this is not something that has been treated in
the Amherst community with a high level of priority and send a strong moral statement to our staff and
town leaders and community.

Devlin Gauthier spoke about the cost of drug enforcement laws, for these substances in particular, and the
impact on public health of decriminalization.
Lopes, spoke as a sponsor acknowledged that this is an opportunity to engage in a more robust
conversation around harm reduction.
Hanneke stated the war on drugs has been abominable and that is not acceptable but the way the
resolution is written is that there is no limit on possession of controlled substances and does not specify
plant medicines but would apply to cocaine or heroin; the language regarding policy implications and we
have not heard from public health experts in town or public safety and cannot support the resolution as
written.
Rooney gave a shoutout to the police department and town staff who have placed plant medicine arrests at
a very low priority.
Devlin Gauthier spoke about the difference between decriminalization and legalization.
Steinberg echoed Hanneke’s concerns and that he would support a differently constructed resolution;
question about the resolution establishing policy because the Council does carry weight and puts pressure
on staff, cannot take that lightly and there is a lack of clarity of what exactly is covered by the provisions
of the resolution and the first section reads as an endorsement of use of plant medicine, and that needs to
be considered carefully so that it makes it clear that we are not endorsing it. We don’t have the authority
to decriminalize and there are people who should have been consulted and were not such as the health
director, cress director, fire and police chiefs and legal. Nothing but a vague reference to consulting any
of those people.
Miller stated this is being unfairly targeted and can’t have it both ways that it is non-binding and that
there are things in there about other drugs. Do feel that this is being unfairly targeted that it has to go to
every department when that has not been the practice with other resolutions.
Bahl-Milne spoke in support of the benefits of plant medicine, and the need for research; even though I
would like to see this pass the language we use is a moral statement and sends a message that it is okay to
use plant medicine and there have been other resolutions a list of principles for safe and responsible use
which might offset concerns as it can be unsafe to use for some people.
Community sponsor James Davis spoke about community events to educate people about micro dosing
and safe use, support from Tapestry health and local doctors, stigma and bias against the issue that
prevent conversations from happening. The measure helps groups advocate and reduce stigma.
Schoen expressed concern about the language in the resolution but that she would be supportive of a
resolution that were worded differently.
Walker spoke about criminalization and stigmatization and the need to prioritize and support healthy
communities. To support our community, it needs to be decriminalized so we are not addressing it in
terms of supporting and providing access and resources.
Pam spoke about friends who were harmed by experimenting with psychedelics and that the intent of the
sponsors have been confused with loose wording.
Bahl-Milne asked if it were possible to incorporate comments into a new version.
Steinberg suggested a motion to refer to committee.
