Town of Amherst
Special Joint Meeting of the Town Council and Finance Committee
Public Hearing on the FY23 Budget
Monday, May 16, 2022 5:00 p.m.
Virtual Meeting
Minutes
Note: Pursuant to Chapter 20 of the Acts of 2021, this meeting was conducted via remote means.
Members of the public were able to access the meeting in real time via Zoom, by telephone, via livestream
on Amherst Media, and on TV channel 17.
Councilors participating remotely: Bahl-Milne, De Angelis (joined at 5:30), Devlin Gauthier (joined at
5:10), Griesemer, Lopes (joined at 5:59 pm), Miller, Pam, Rooney, Schoen, Steinberg, Taub, Walker
Councilors Absent: Hanneke
Finance Committee members participating remotely: Hegner, Holloway, and Kubiak
Finance Committee members absent: None
Staff in participating remotely: Town Manager Bockelman, Finance Director Mangano, Comptroller
Aldrich, Clerk of the Council O’Keeffe
1. Call to Order
2. Hearings – Public Hearing of the Finance Committee on the FY23 Town Budget –
Charter Sec. 5.5(a)
Town Manager Bockelman, Finance Director Mangano, and Finance Committee Chair Steinberg spoke
about the following:
• Development of the budget
• Public information opportunities
• Budget development goals of racial equity and sustainability
• Budget development and review process overview
• Strategic thinking for budget development
• Overview of the capital improvement program
Devlin Gauthier joined the meeting at 5:13 pm.
Peter Demling, Amherst resident and school committee member, spoke about the school committee’s
budget which was submitted with a higher total budget than the budget guidance received from the town.
Demling spoke about additional state aid received that totals more than the excess budget proposed by the
School Committee. Demling spoke about School Committee discussions at various meetings about the
budget above guidance.

---

De Angelis joined the meeting at 5:30 pm.
Allegra Clark, Amherst resident, read a haiku about funding for the CRESS and DEI departments, a
BIPOC youth center, tires on the ambulance, funding for the fire department, art, tech and support
services, road improvements, reparations, translation services, and other budget requests.
Kaylee Brow, Amherst resident, spoke about investing in the CRESS department, defunding of the
Amherst police department, and other recommendations from the Community Safety Working Group.
Andrea Munoz Ledo, Amherst resident, urged the Council to meet the demands of the Defund 413 group,
spoke about the need to address public safety in a way that does not include police, and the need for new
strategies for policing. Munoz Ledo urged the Council to consider the recommendations from the CSWG.
Adam Gibbs, Amherst resident, spoke critically about the lack of funds for the CSWG in the budget and
encouraged the Council to fund housing affordability projects.
Birdy Newman, Amherst resident, spoke in support of the budget demands from the Defund 413 and
Sunrise Amherst groups. Newman commented that while people in town have been supportive of the
establishment of the DEI and CRESS departemtns, there has been reluctant to reduce funding for the
police department.
Marisol Pierce Bonifaz, Amherst resident, spoke on behalf of Sunrise Amherst about the group’s
advocacy and engagement efforts, and demands of the Town Council to fund the recommendations from
the CSWG.
Zoë Crabtree, Amherst resident, spoke about the CSWG recommendations for funding for the CRESS
department, expressing disappointment that their budget was not funded at the recommended level.
Crabtree spoke in praise of the CSWG’s work, and expressed disappointment with the budget not
including more funding for the CSWG recommendations.
Demetria Shabazz, Amherst resident, accused the Council of racist practices and of conveying the
message that residents’ input does not matter. Shabazz spoke about CSWG recommendations and
discussions about a study regarding a BIPOC youth center. Shabazz stated underrepresented community
members are being ignored. Shabazz spoke about the budget development process and lack of
communication about a deadline for public input in November.
Jeff Lee, Amherst resident, spoke about impoverished residents and the additional financial pressure on
low income residents due to the funding of the library and other capital projects.
Julian Hynes, Amherst resident, spoke about CSWG recommendations, planting trees and implementing
the sustainability plan, the need for affordable housing, a full time housing coordinator, and fire
department staffing needs.
Lopes joined the meeting at 5:59 pm.
Lauren, Amherst resident, spoke about concerns about youth in the community, social services,
implementation of the CRESS department, and asked how money will be used to support youth in
Amherst.
