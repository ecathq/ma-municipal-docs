First Reading
Planner Ben Breger spoke about the goals, development, and rationale of moving to a general rather than
a zoning bylaw, and changes to the proposed bylaw during the review process.
Hanneke reported that the Community Resources Committee voted unanimously to recommend the Town
Council adopt the Historically Significant Buildings Bylaw.

the bylaw clear, consistent, and actionable.
Pam asked how a court process is more efficient process for appeals.
Steinberg asked what happens if someone disagrees that a building is or is not historically significant.
Devlin Gauthier asked about determination of historically significance.
Brestrup spoke about a previous situation where a building was taken down and neighbors appealed the
decision of the building commissioner after the building had been demolished; the Zoning Board of
Appeals does not have the authority to overturn a decision of the Historical Commission, with the new

is also seeking grant funding to help offset the cost of the project.

funds, it would be funded via water fees.

b. Proposed Housing Task Force
Rooney spoke to her proposal to create a housing task force, with the goal of preserving housing for
families. Rooney stated she proposes a 7-member task force to implement goals one and two of the
comprehensive housing policy.
Bahl-Milne spoke about the importance of the issue and asked about strategies brought forward by the
Planning Director to increase housing, and asked if we need a committee to do more research or to begin
work on implementing the strategies that have been identified.
Pam spoke about making homes more energy efficient and affordable and spoke about the age and
dementia strategies to allow people to stay in their homes as they age. Pam suggested searching for grants
to help retain existing housing stock while creating new housing stock.
Hanneke spoke in support of a referral of a creation of a task force to the Community Resources
Committee, stated that Governance, Organization, and Legislation Committee is the appropriate committee
to review committee charges, the Council has already sent implementation of the Comprehensive Housing
Policy to the Town Manager and Community Resources Committee, and resident committees are not
appropriate for implementation of policies. Hanneke stated that the Council has done a horrible job of
finding applicants to appoint for committees that the Council appoints. Hanneke stated CRC had
difficulties finding a sufficient applicant pool for the Planning Board vacancies; if the Council creates
another committee for appointment by the President or the Town Manager it would be irresponsible to
create another committee. Hanneke stated there is demand for student residents in Town, and if the goal is
to remove student residents from those units we have to find places to house those student
residents—which includes willingness to build new housing, more dense housing.
Taub stated the task force would be similar to the community services working group, and suggested that it
go to the Town Manager as a request to create the committee. Taub stated the CRC has a busy agenda and
the task force would be a dedicated body to look at housing issues in Town in-depth. Taub stated the Town
has been building and building for students and has not been maintaining housing for working people,
spoke about the increase in the Town’s population among students in the new dorms on campus, the Town
is losing its year-round population who sends their children to school.
Rooney stated the university has grown by 10,000 students and it is not the Town’s responsibility to house
the 19,000 students who cannot be housed on campus; there is a national wave of investment in real estate
and the Town has been especially hard-hit due to the pressure of student housing needs. Rooney likened
the task force to the Transportation Advisory Committee to advise committees and the Town Manager to
work toward retaining existing homes for long-term year-round residents.
Pam spoke about faculty members at UMass having a hard time finding housing near campus, and
developers creating housing for students—Pam spoke in support of the creation of the task force and that
developing a task force does not mean that she is opposed to anything.

July 1, 2022, for terms expiring June 30, 2025: Bruce Coldham and Karin Winter.
Hanneke reported that CRC voted unanimously to recommend the appointments.
Hanneke spoke about difficulty to locate applicants for the board, and that an hour and a half before the
interviews began one of the applicants withdrew. Hanneke stated she is uncomfortable with the
committee’s declaration of the sufficiency of the applicant pool. Hanneke spoke about the applicant’s
responses during interviews including misinterpretation of an interview question, and questionable
answers to questions regarding the Planning Board’s scope in implementing the Master Plan and both
touted the Amherst Media issue as good governance, which Hanneke stated it was not.
Rooney spoke in strong support of the candidates and stated she is not concerned with the applicant’s
understanding of the parameters of the process.
Taub stated she believed both candidates are qualified for the position and that the vacancies will be open
at the end of the month.
Pam stated we do have trouble getting people to volunteer for committees but that the Town is lucky to
have two qualified candidates for the committee. Pam spoke about encouraging new committee members
and being grateful for and supportive of candidates.
Bahl-Milne spoke about the level of experience of the current Planning Board, one of the appointees has
experience and the other has a willingness to put in the work to become oriented to the board.

10. Committee and Liaison Reports
a. Community Resources Committee – Hanneke reported that the committee
continues to seek applicants to the Zoning Board of Appeals, there is currently
potentially only one applicant who will submit a statement of interest; Hanneke asked
the Town Manager to assist in recruiting. Hanneke spoke about statutory
requirements for meetings and the intense strain the board will be under if additional
members are not appointed.
b. Elementary School Building Committee – Schoen reported that the report contains
the most recent cost estimates, what the committee has learned about the sites, and
the criteria the committee is using to compare the sites. Next Monday the committee
will narrow the options to one. The committee would submit their report to the
MSBA and they have to approve the next phase of the project. Schoen spoke about
the upcoming community forum and how a new school will be built without
disrupting the ongoing education of current students.
Councilors discussed the various decision points including a number of public
comments regarding the site selection and future use of a current school building that
may be vacant in the future.
c. Finance Committee – Steinberg noted the committee is meeting tomorrow at 3:00 to
discuss the reparations referral, vote on a recommendation on the Capital
Improvement Program, the Centennial Water Treatment Plant funding, and North
Common action as required.
d. Governance, Organization, and Legislation Committee – Miller reported the
committee will interview applicants to the Finance Committee at 9:00 am on
Wednesday, the committee will also review a resolution on the Fair Share
Amendment, as well as bylaws carried forward from the previous session and will
begin work on an equity lens review process.
e. Jones Library Building Committee – Lopes reported that there is a meeting
scheduled tomorrow for discussion of the elevator and gender-neutral bathrooms,
discussion of less expensive material choices for the exterior, and outreach and
community participation.
f. Town Services and Outreach Committee – Pam reported the committee met twice,
the committee heard from the Senior Services Director and Community Participation
Officers. The committee will continue its work on water and sewer bylaws and
regulations.
g. Liaison Reports: Amherst Affordable Housing Trust, Board of Health,
Community Safety and Social Justice Committee, Disability Access Advisory
