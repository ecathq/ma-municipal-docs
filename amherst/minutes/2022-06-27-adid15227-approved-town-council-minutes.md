---

Kamm Howard spoke in support of funding for local reparations as a way to set the state for national
reparations.
Martha Hanner, Amherst resident, spoke in opposition to a Council vote for reparations funding up to
$2M; stating that the new DEI Director has not begun working yet and the new DEI and CRESS
department will need funding.
Darcy DuMont, Amherst resident, spoke in support of a zero-waste proposal, including curbside compost
pick up by the Town and a pay-as-you-throw fee structure for trash, and spoke about supporters of a zero-
waste effort.
Phyllis Labanowski, Amherst resident, spoke in support of the $2M request for reparations funding by the
Amherst Heritage Reparation Assembly, commenting on the potential historic implications of action by
the Town Council.
Irv Rhodes, Amherst resident, spoke in bafflement of the opposition to funding reparations at a $2M
level, stating the AHRA is not making recommendations in competition with CRESS or DEI funding, and
that the AHRA is not asking for cannabis tax income but budget surplus in an amount equal to the
cannabis income.
Robin Rue Simmons, resident of Evanston, IL, spoke in support of $2M funding for reparations, thanked
the AHRA for their leadership and thoughtfulness, and asked the Town Council to support the AHRA’s
recommendation and funding request. Simmons spoke about disbursement of reparations in her
community and a legal framework to distribute reparations in direct relation to the harm.
Kathleen Anderson, Amherst resident and former election official in Town, thanked the Council for their
service and encouraged Councilors to stay energized; Anderson echoed other speakers’ support of $2M
funding for reparations.
Dr. Demetria Rougeaux Shabazz, Amherst resident, echoed earlier comments from Irv Rhodes, and spoke
in support of $2M reparations funding, speaking about the racial wealth gap and equal footing for Black
residents, and asked for an update on connectivity between the Town and Comcast for Amherst Media.
Paige Wilder, Amherst resident, spoke in support of funding for reparations, and spoke about trust of the
AHRA to make decisions.
Pat Ononibaku, Amherst resident, spoke in support of reparations funding at a $2M level, stating she was
distressed at the Council’s last conversation on reparations regarding funding for CRESS and DEI in
terms of competing funding areas.
Amilcar Shabazz, Amherst resident, read a poem by Emily Dickinson, thanked the Council for
consideration of reparations and expressed eagerness for beginning a robust process of consultation with
the African Heritage community in Amherst regarding use of reparations.

b. Community Preservation Act Committee Debt Authorization for ARHS Track
and Fields
Griesemer announced there will be no vote tonight, there will be a public forum and vote on July 18;
Griesemer thanked CPAC Chair Sarah Marshall for her work and leadership while serving on the
committee.
Marshall spoke about the committee’s unanimous recommendation to award $800,000 for the Amherst
Regional High School track and fields.
Hanneke asked about the lack of specificity in the draft order to ensure that authorized borrowing cannot
be used for the rehab of the track in its current location.
Finance Director Mangano responded that the report with the memo was clear enough for the Council to
act on safely, and the report provided the context with the order.
Hanneke expressed discomfort with the order as currently presented.
Comptroller Aldrich stated the report is attached to the order.
Hanneke suggested adding “in accordance with the attached report” to the Council order.
Schoen spoke about CPA funding for the Jones Library and phrasing that made the funding contingent on
other requirements.
Mangano spoke about the total funding for the project, and funding from other towns in the region.
Steinberg spoke about the discussion at Finance Committee, and the key words in the order “track and
field” when there is a stipulation that no CPA funds can be used for artificial turf.
Marshall added that the school wants to install a synthetic turf field when the field is reoriented; CPA
funds cannot be used for the artificial turf, but there are other costs in the project that can be paid for
using the CPA funds.
c. Cannabis Tax Revenue for Reparations
5 | P ag e
Minutes approved by Town Council on September 12, 2022

---

MOTION: Griesemer moved, second by Steinberg, that each year upon certification of free cash by the
Commonwealth of Massachusetts (October), and upon review and recommendation by the Town’s
Finance Committee based on the Town’s overall financial health at that time, that the Town Council add
an amount equal to the certified cannabis tax revenue for the previous year (approximately July) to the
Stabilization fund established for the purpose of reparations until such time as the fund equals $1M.
MOTION: Miller moved, second by De Angelis, to amend the motion by changing $1M to $2M, to add
the phrase "but not to exceed $205,000" after the phrase "that the Town Council add an amount equal to
the certified cannabis tax revenue for the previous year (approximately July)," and change the word
“fund” to “Town’s contribution.”
Miller spoke in support of the motion.
Bahl-Milne encouraged everyone to have safe dialogue, stated that because the funds come from budget
surplus there is no concern that the higher amount is not taking away from other department needs, and
asked what other Councilors’ concerns are.
Steinberg spoke about the Finance Committee’s report, and spoke about the committee’s discussion
regarding inflation and rising interest rates and construction costs and the need to find funding to replace
what is currently funded by ARPA, there is currently a hole in the next few years’ budget.
Pam spoke about the modesty of the AHRA request, and that while the financial future is unknown, the
past harms are known, and she supports the motion.
Schoen spoke about the budget being designed with a cushion when there is budget excess and those
excesses are used as a safeguard in case of emergency or for unmet needs; Schoen stated her impression
was that donations and grants would be sought to supplement the reparations fund. Schoen spoke about
not knowing the criteria for eligibility and eligibility being race based. Schoen spoke about a resident in
her district whose family suffered harms and would not want to accept reparations if they were to become
available to her. Schoen spoke about her personal commitment to get rid of systemic and structural racism
and the Town needs to keep recommitting to the issue.
Mangano asked for clarification about the balance of the fund when reparations begin to be paid and if the
fund should continually be replenished.
Miller spoke about the fund in terms of an endowment, and that contributions from the Town continue in
this manner until contributions reach $2M from the Town.
Hanneke commented that the motion itself does not indicate that it is required to be a budget surplus, and
the AHRA’s charge does not ask them to develop separate plans for funding sources and use for funds—
AHRA keeps asking the Council to vote on funding before knowing what the community-wide healing
process will be. Hanneke stated the AHRA’s current language is too vague and that once the money is in
a reparations fund, it is unlikely that it would be used for anything else. Hanneke stated it is premature to
commit to putting money in. Hanneke stated she would not support the amendment or the motion.
De Angelis spoke about honoring the Black and African Heritage community by funding at up to $2M;
while she does not know how the funds will be used, she trusts that the Black community will decide how
to use the money.
Walker echoed De Angelis’ comments and spoke in support of the AHRA request to increase to $2M and
in support of the amendment. Walker spoke about her experience of racism on the CSWG and intent to
support anti-racist and restorative justice efforts in the community when she ran for a Town Council seat.

Devlin Gauthier spoke about the fund as an endowment, a potential 4% draw on the fund and that the
funding would not be all at once but would reach $2M in about ten years, and spoke about reparative
work in Amherst.
Taub echoed comments from Devlin Gauthier, Walker, and De Angelis, and spoke about the confidence
of the AHRA in the potential uses of the funds in the future.
Lopes spoke about steps to repair past harms, the funds being highly monitored and controlled in terms of
how it can be allotted and stated that it is inappropriate to speak about individual resident experiences or
benefits.
Griesemer spoke about how she developed the motion at Finance Committee, and that it allows the Town
to continue to move forward and build funds so that once AHRA comes forward with a plan and
eligibility process. Griesemer spoke about the sometimes tense discussion between the Finance
Committee and AHRA, and spoke in support of a $1M cap on the fund.
Miller spoke in support of the AHRA’s request for $2M and that the request was met with a motion for
half the amount, and that the amount is inconsequential to the budget; Miller stated that it is critical that
the AHRA knows the Council is setting aside funds so that a plan can be developed.
Walker spoke about the discretion included in the motion that allows the Council to make a decision
about funding each year. Walker spoke about AHRA pivoting their recommendation to accommodate
Councilors’ concerns and include safeguards.
Bahl-Milne spoke about community members’ willingness to extend loans to entrepreneurs but that they
need to see that the Town is willing to put up funds first and that having a strong financial base will
increase confidence in donations and community members.

call to amend the motion by changing $1M to $2M, to add the phrase "but not to exceed $205,000" after
the phrase "that the Town Council add an amount equal to the certified cannabis tax revenue for the
previous year (approximately July)," and change the word “fund” to “Town’s contribution.”
VOTED 10-2, with 1 absent (Bahl-Milne, De Angelis, Devlin Gauthier, Griesemer, Lopes, Miller, Pam,
Steinberg, Taub, and Walker voted Yes; Hanneke and Schoen voted No; Rooney was Absent) by roll call,
that each year upon certification of free cash by the Commonwealth of Massachusetts (October), and
upon review and recommendation by the Town’s Finance Committee based on the Town’s overall
financial health at that time, that the Town Council add an amount equal to the certified cannabis tax
revenue for the previous year (approximately July) but not to exceed $205,000 to the Stabilization fund
established for the purpose of reparations until such time as the Town’s contribution equals $2M.

h. Meeting Format
Griesemer stated the legislature has not extended the special legislation that allows public bodies to
continue to meet virtually, and the Council’s ability to meet in the current format will expire on July 15 if

all Town Council meetings in the in-person meeting format with virtual public access only, with a virtual
meeting option if the President declares there will be fewer than 5 Councilors physically present 24 hours
in advance until September 19, 2022.
