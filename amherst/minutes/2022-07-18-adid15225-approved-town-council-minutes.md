b. Public Way: Hope Church Request Reserved Parking at 16 Gaylord Street
Town Manager Bockelman stated the Church is requesting three parking spaces on the street reserved by
the church. Bockelman stated that he spoke with DPW and Fire department heads and that there was no
concern with the reservation. Bockelman stated that the decision is a policy issue, and he leaves the
decision to the Council.
Deacon Timothy Haley, representing Hope Church, spoke about work being done on the building and the
church’s request for spaces in front of the church for funerals, weddings, and cultural events.
Pam spoke about the width of the street and that it requires cooperation for everything to work and it is
unusual to have such a narrow street with a community building with no public parking.
Rooney spoke in support of a referral to the Town Services and Outreach Committee.
Hanneke spoke in concern with setting a precedent, broadness of the request for three spaces 24/7, and
without an offer of payment for the permanent dedication of spaces in the public way to a private entity.
Hanneke suggested a cost of three parking permits, at a premium for the location.
Devlin Gauthier spoke in support of referral to consider the issues more deeply.
Deacon Haley stated the church could offer to pay for the spaces, spoke about the history of the church,
and stated that he does not believe it is an unreasonable request.
Pastor Anderson of Hope Church asked if there was an option for a non-permanent use of the public way,
such as a reservation for Sundays or for particular hours.

MOTION: Griesemer moved, second by De Angelis, pursuant to chapter 92 of the acts of 2022, to

constables for election sites.

can delegate its authority to the Town Clerk.

1) Zoning Board of Appeals – Charter Sec. 2.9(c)
MOTION ON THE FLOOR POSTPONED ON JUNE 27: To waive Section 9, Interviews, of the
Town Council Policy on Making Recommendations for Town Council Appointments to Multiple-
Member Bodies for John Varner, as he was unable to attend the interview for ZBA applicants that was
scheduled prior to his submission of a Community Activity Form, but did provide to CRC written
answers to the adopted interview questions.
Rooney spoke in support of the motion, stating that the candidate was not given alternatives for an
interview date.
Taub spoke in support of the motion, stating that the candidate was given very little notice for the
interview date.
Hanneke spoke in opposition to the motion, agreeing with Taub that the candidate was not given time to
schedule an interview, adding that the Community Resources Committee will be taking up ZBA
appointments again in order to fill the 2 associate member vacancies. Hanneke stated when she set
interview dates, she contacted every person who was a candidate at the time; Varner’s application came in
after the interviews were scheduled.
Schoen spoke in support of the motion and of Varner’s appointment as an associate member for 1 year.
Pam spoke about the candidate’s name being made public and that a second appointment date should have
been given. Pam spoke about the ZBA chair’s letter in support of Varner’s appointment, and the
importance of giving the ZBA the people the board needs to do the work.
Taub spoke in support of the motion, stating that at a Council meeting in June Hanneke encouraged
Councilors to reach out and encourage potential candidates to submit applications.
Bahl-Milne asked if the candidate can be interviewed during the next round of ZBA interviews.
Hanneke spoke about the Council’s policy on making recommendations, including the various steps of
the process and that Varner could be included in the next round of interviews.
Taub spoke in support of the motion.
Hanneke spoke about her communications with Varner, where she made clear that the policy requires an
interview and that the Council may not waive the policy and not consider his candidacy without an
interview.
De Angelis spoke in opposition to the motion and stated the Council adopted a policy and that is what
should be applied to the situation.
MOTION: De Angelis moved, second by Hanneke, to call the question.
VOTED 11-0 with 2 absent (Lopes and Miller) by roll call, to call the question.

VOTED 6-4, with 2 abstentions and 2 absent (Pam, Rooney, Schoen, Steinberg, Taub, and Walker voted
Yes; De Angelis, Devlin Gauthier, Griesemer, and Hanneke voted No; Bahl-Milne Abstained; Lopes and
Miller were absent) to waive Section 9, Interviews, of the Town Council Policy on Making
Recommendations for Town Council Appointments to Multiple-Member Bodies for John Varner, as he
was unable to attend the interview for ZBA applicants that was scheduled prior to his submission of a
Community Activity Form, but did provide to CRC written answers to the adopted interview questions.
MOTION: Griesemer moved, second by Taub, to appoint John Varner as Associate Member to the
Zoning Board of Appeals, for a term effective immediately and ending June 30, 2023.
Hanneke spoke in opposition to the appointment, stating that the written answers provided do not reflect
an open mind and his answers reflect pre-judgement of potential applications and his statements about
whose opinions matter the most.
Bahl-Milne spoke about Varner’s responses to the interview questions which reflect an opinion that the
most important stakeholders are abutters; Bahl-Milne stated that all stakeholders’ opinions should be
considered.
Schoen spoke about the guidance within the Zoning Bylaw that speak to the need to consider abutters’
opinions when adjudicating applications and land owners’ rights during the process and weighing of
various interests when considering an application.
Rooney spoke about the ZBA considering both perspectives and that there is flexibility within the zoning
bylaw guidance to consider other interests and perspectives.
Pam stated that it appears that Varner is punished for providing his opinion when he was asked for it,
spoke about the deliberative process she has witnessed at ZBA meetings, and spoke in support of the
motion.
Taub echoed Pam’s comments, adding that the other candidate that was appointed as an alternate
responded that the greatest weight should be given to the property owner when asked whose opinions
should be given the greatest weight.
De Angelis stated that following Schoen’s logic, the affordable housing at 192 Northampton Road would
not have been built due to the overwhelming negative response to the project from abutters and neighbors.
De Angelis stated the need of the community can outweigh an abutter’s opinions. De Angelis stated the
Council must weigh and determine the best decision for the Town, not what Councilors want personally.
Schoen stated ZBA is supposed to weigh various factors in reaching conditions, spoke about the
understudy role of an associate member, the various considerations when making decisions, having a
diversity of opinion, and that the Council should value people who are willing to give their time.
Pam spoke about modifications that were imposed on the application for 192 Northampton Road such as
on-site support services and spoke about the need to work toward compromise that works for people.
Hanneke stated Varner was the only candidate who did not speak about weighing differing points of view
and that Varner stated abutters were the only interest group that mattered and that it should be given even
greater consideration if the abutter has lived in Amherst longer—regardless of if the application is
allowed. Hanneke stated that weighing abutters’ opinions regarding land they live next to is not what she
wants to see on the ZBA.
