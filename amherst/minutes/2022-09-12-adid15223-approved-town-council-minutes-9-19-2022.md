Finance Director Mangano spoke about:
 Terms used around the project: “hard and soft costs,” approved budget, and reconciled estimate
 Project costs
 Funding sources
Griesemer spoke about where the project is now and anticipated next steps.
Schoen asked about the decrease in soft costs and what has been removed from the budget for furnishings
and requested a detailed list of what has been removed from the project budget. Schoen stated the MOU
includes a date that the Trustees would give their share of the project from the endowment. Schoen asked
how certain the trustees are about the Historic Tax Credit funding source.
Town Manager Bockelman stated the MOU would need to be updated to reflect the changes to the project
budget.
Pam asked if “fundraising” includes using the endowment.
Griesemer stated that the Trustees agreed to fundraise for their share of the project cost and if their
fundraising efforts fall short, they may tap into the endowment.
Steinberg added that the borrowing authorization for $35,279,700 was not only for the Town’s share, but
it also includes the entire amount of the funding of the project.
Devlin Gauthier asked if the Library Trustees are responsible for either fundraising or utilizing the
endowment and the Council would not tell the Trustees how to use or not use the endowment.
Taub asked if the Council would need to approve another borrowing authorization for the higher project
cost, and at what point do we enter a new MOU.
Bockelman stated the MOU would be agreed soon, once the bid comes in for the project cost, the Town
would return to the Council for an authorization for the higher project costs.
Mangano added that the Town must adhere to what the MBLC requires, they require that the Town
approve all eligible costs. The Town borrows as funds are needed rather than borrowing the full amount at
once. The Town may refinance after 10 years if interest rates are low at that time.
Rooney asked if the Town is required to secure funding prior to going out to bid.
Bockelman spoke about how project costs are estimated, which include projected cost based on
construction start date.
Walker asked about the sequence and order of actions needed.
Griesemer spoke about next steps for the Council.
Schoen spoke about the endowment, and what the Trustees’ treasurer stated about how much the
endowment can be drawn upon and the endowment could tolerate it. Schoen stated the MOU the Council
voted was very specific and the Council would need to be voted again in order to reflect the new project
costs and ensure the Town was reimbursed for the agreed-upon share.
Pam expressed concern about the increased costs of the project and that she does not want to get to a point
in the process where it is too late to stop.

Hanneke commented that 65% of the residents supported the project, and that until we see actual bids, the
risk to the Trustees and risk to the Town remains the same, the Council would not vote the specific
language of the MOU as the executive not the Town Council, and the new MOU would keep the Town

same situation if the project does not move forward. Bahl-Milne stated the cost of repairs is increasing

not giving enough to the DPW and fire station projects.

is an important question. Steinberg stated he will ask the trustees for an update at the Finance Committee
meeting tomorrow. The MBLC takes the stance that increased construction costs are municipalities’ issue
and not the MBLC’s issue; that is a problem that needs to be addressed.
Griesemer spoke a coalition with other towns to encourage the legislature to increase funding for library
building projects.
Walker spoke about concerns with increased costs, and if the project is a sound financial decision
considering the Town’s other capital needs.

b. Acceptance of Public Way: Kestrel Lane and Hop Brook Road
Planning Director Brestrup spoke about the two roadways that the Town has been asked to accept, and the
acceptance process.
Schoen asked about the benefit to the Town in accepting the roads and about the current condition of the
roads.
Brestrup stated the benefit is to ensure the roads are passable to emergency vehicles and school busses; if
a private entity is responsible for maintaining the roads allow them to deteriorate, the roads would
become unsafe for the residents there.
Hanneke asked about sewer and maintenance easements on the subdivision plan, who currently owns the
roads, why it has taken 20-25 years to ask for the acceptance and finish the list that was defined in 2004,
if we do easements or a fee would the Town receive property taxes, and what is the plan to cover the costs
or the acceptance, legal fees, costs of acquiring the roads.
Brestrup responded that it has taken some time because in 2007/2008 the developer did not have enough
money to finish the roads, Tofino has been struggling to meet its responsibilities since around that time.
The HOA has had conversations with various parties and has been working toward bringing this forward.
Town Engineer Skeels stated the mileage would count toward the Town’s chapter 90 allotment, the
conditions were evaluated in 2021 and were in poor and fair conditions and would require minimal repairs
to bring them to better conditions.
Proponent and current president of the Meadows HOA Douglas Donnell spoke about the HOA working
on this for a long time, and have encountered several obstacles; the road was initially expected to be
accepted as roads when the subdivision was created in the late 90s, the subdivision was completed in
2004 and it has taken almost 20 years for the homeowners to prepare the request. The Town voted in 2001
to collect a $100,000 surety from the developer, only $20,000 was collected and the work was not
completed. The homeowners have been actively pursuing this for several years. There have been

Agenda Item 8.a. Proposed Special Act on Residential Property Transfer Fees
Sponsors Devlin Gauthier and Hanneke spoke about the intent of the Special Act that would impose a 2%
fee on property transfers to be allowed by an adopted bylaw and would apply to two types of property
transfers: homes that sell for over 200% the assessed price of median homes, and homes that do not
Page | 6
Minutes approved by Town Council on [date]

---

qualify for a homestead certificate, which are second homes and non-owner-occupied rental homes.
Devlin Gauthier spoke about allocating funds from the fees to the affordable housing trust and a capital
stabilization fund. Hanneke stated the bylaw included with the memo is a sample and is not being
proposed to be adopted by the Town as-is; a bylaw would be developed if and when the special act is
acted on by the legislature. Hanneke added that a bylaw created after a special act is adopted would allow
a bylaw to be created within the constraints of the special act.
Schoen spoke in support and asked about the legislative process.
Devlin Gauthier stated Senator Comerford is working on a bill that would allow cities and towns to adopt
bylaws such as these without special legislation.
Rooney asked if the state accepts the special act as a package with the draft bylaw.
Hanneke stated the special act would be sent to the legislature without the draft bylaw; nine special acts
were transmitted to the legislation in the past session.
Pam asked how the residential property transfer fees have been working in other towns.
De Angelis and Miller spoke in support of the special act.
Schoen asked if the suggestion is to create a capital stabilization fund. Sponsors replied yes that would be
part of the process.

g. Town Council and Council Committee Meeting Format
Griesemer spoke about the expiration of the previous special act and the new special act that suspends the
same provisions to the open meeting Law until March 31, 2023.
Hanneke commented that inclement weather could be added to the motion and that the vote should be
until the end of the Council’s term.
Councilors discussed the meeting format options.
Hanneke spoke in favor of returning to in-person public access to Council meetings.
Miller expressed concern about security at in-person meetings.
Pam shared security concerns and stated that people enjoy attending meetings from home and prefer not
to meet in person.
Miller asked about a pre-registration process.
MOTION: Griesemer moved, second by Hanneke, effective immediately and until and including the first
meeting of the next Council’s term, to hold all Town Council meetings in the in-person meeting format
with remote participation as permitted by law, with in-person public access and the option of virtual
public access; in the event of inclement weather or at the discretion of the President, the President may
announce a virtual meeting will take place.
VOTED unanimously, 13-0, by roll call, effective immediately and until and including the first meeting
of the next Council’s term, to hold all Town Council meetings in the in-person meeting format with
remote participation as permitted by law, with in-person public access and the option of virtual public
access; in the event of inclement weather or at the discretion of the President, the President may announce
a virtual meeting will take place.
Schoen asked about the availability of rooms with hybrid capabilities.
Hanneke spoke about wanting to return to in-person meetings, but that there is a benefit to virtual
committee meetings.
Steinberg expressed concern with staff time and that it is not a good use of our staff time.
MOTION: Griesemer moved, second by Rooney, effective immediately, and until and including the first
meeting of the next Council’s term, unless inconsistent with state law, to hold all Town Council
committee meetings in the virtual meeting format with virtual public access.
Schoen and Hanneke spoke about special committee meetings that they would like to have in person and
open to the public.
VOTED unanimously, 13-0, by roll call, effective immediately, and until and including the first meeting
of the next Council’s term, unless inconsistent with state law, to hold all Town Council committee
meetings in the virtual meeting format with virtual public access.
