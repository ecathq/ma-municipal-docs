7. Presentations and Discussion
a. Tax Classification Presentation
Principal Assessor Mew spoke about the tax classification process, including:
• Process overview
• Required public hearing will be held on November 7, 2022
• Relevant Massachusetts General Laws
• Definitions of residential, commercial, industrial, and personal property classifications
• Current distribution of parcels by property classification
• Single versus split tax rate
• Residential and commercial/industrial impact of a split rate
• Town Manager and Principal Assessor recommendation of a single tax rate
• Other allowed exemptions
Mangano added there was a more in-depth analysis of the impact of a split rate, and the presentation will
be re-shared with Councilors.
Mew added that she anticipated the tax rate will decrease this year and reiterated her recommendation to
adopt a single tax rate for all property types, a factor of 1.00, and no residential, small commercial, or
open space exemptions.
Rooney asked about how residential rental properties are classified.
Mew spoke about how the department of revenue defines commercial parcels, and that the method must
be applied consistently to all residential properties. Mew spoke about restrictions on how homes are
valued.
Mangano added that the income expense method is used to value larger apartment buildings.
Rooney asked if it were possible to take a different look at the issue of residential rental properties.
Mangano stated staff have looked into the issue and will continue to look into it.
Pam asked if Rooney’s idea is not possible if the Town should adopt a residential exemption.
Mangano spoke about the analysis, and that it is not guaranteed that the exemption would give residential
property owners a break and could impact the rental cost for renters. Some developments would see a
$50,000 property tax increase in one year, and that increase would likely affect the cost of rent charged.
Miller asked if there is data from other communities who have adopted a residential exemption.
Mew spoke about feedback she gathered from assessors in communities who have adopted a residential
exemption.
Hanneke spoke about the presentation from the previous year, and examples of tax increases for owner-
occupied and non-owner-occupied properties. Hanneke stated there is a high degree of certainty that an
increase in taxes would be passed on to tenants, and that the result would be higher rental costs in
Amherst.

Agenda item 7.c. Presentations and Discussion: Joint Discussion with the Community Safety and
Social Justice Committee
Co-Chairs Clark and Shabazz called the meeting of the Community Safety and Social Justice Committee
to order at 7:39 p.m. and confirmed members can hear and be heard.
Chief Livingstone spoke briefly about the report submitted to the Human Rights Commission.
DEI Director Young spoke about the addendum to the DEI Department report submitted at the August 15,
2022 Council meeting. Young stated the incident has been challenging and informative, and read her
closing observations written in the addendum to the report:
Some of the youths and families involved in the July 5th Police interaction have expressed feeling
harmed. No one is in a position to deny another’s feelings. We can all agree the patrolman’s
statement to the minors regarding their” individual rights” was incorrect. As the Town moves to
be a safe and welcoming community based on mutual respect for all, harm will occur, but
reconciliation is possible. As a community we must have the courage to admit a mistake and act
to correct it, the strength to do the hard work of reconciliation, and, have the capacity to forgive
and show grace.
Shabazz thanked Young and Livingstone for the reports and stated that both reports are the standard
language used by police to protect officers. Shabazz spoke about the importance of an independent review
board with subpoena power and an independent complaint process. Shabazz stated the committee has
suggested a victim compensation fund. Shabazz stated a review board could determine appropriate actions
after an incident, and that there was harm as a result of the statement by the police during the July 5
incident. Shabazz stated it is not clear what can be done to make the youths and families involved whole
and make amends. Shabazz read statements from the youths about the incident.
Ferreira agreed with Shabazz’s comments, adding that there are holes in the reports, the police report does
not contain information about why the youths were detained, what questions were asked of them, why the
officers were not able to help the youths with the flat tire, and why officers aren’t there to help. Ferreira
stated she needs an apology from the officers and that the report stating there is regret is not an apology or
accountability. Ferreira stated the violation is that the incident constitutes a violation of the youths, even if
there was no contact and no arrests. Ferreira stated the community needs to know if the officers will need
to be held accountable. Ferreira stated she would like to know more about the de-escalation training and
why there were no de-escalation methods used during the incident. Ferreira questioned how the police
determined they were able to communicate well with the Spanish-speaking youths.
Ononibaku thanked Young for her report and agreed with the statements and questions posed by Shabazz
and Ferreira. Ononibaku stated there was no apology from the police officers and called for Chief’s
resignation. Ononibaku stated the meeting tonight is a show, the youths are hurting and need to be made
whole; the reports are mediocre and that a black Police Chief would not behave this way in a
predominately white town.

and there are other interpretive resources available to the department

at the Police Station, Town Manager’s office, and with the POST Commission
• What the POST Commission is, when it was established, and what responsibilities and functions
they provide
• Officers with names A-H have been certified by the POST Commission, including training and
background checks

has access to that has not been released

information about the individuals making noise; the officers can give a verbal warning, a bylaw
violation citation, or in rare instances they can make an arrest; officers identified the youth as
minors and did not have a valid license at that time so could not be allowed to leave at that point.
They were all allowed to call a parent or guardian after the youth were identified. If no parents

responsible for the youth at that point
