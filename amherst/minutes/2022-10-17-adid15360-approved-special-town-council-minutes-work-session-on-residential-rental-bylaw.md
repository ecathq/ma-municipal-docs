1. Call to Order
Griesemer announced the presence of a quorum, called the meeting to order at 5:30 p.m., confirmed
members can hear and be heard, and announced audio and video recording.
a. Work Session: Residential Rental Bylaw
(1) Community Resources Committee Report
Hanneke spoke about the Community Resources Committee’s work on the Residential Rental Bylaw, and
changes to the current working draft of the bylaw.
(2) Council Discussion
Pam asked about when permits are issued in relation to when advertising for rental properties is allowed.
Hanneke spoke about the committee’s discussion on the timing of permitting, when advertising is
allowed, when leases are signed, and when inspections take place.
Miller asked about a database of properties that are used for AirBnBs.
Hanneke stated that not all AirBnBs would be exempted from the bylaw; for short-term rentals, no permit
would be required, but for longer-term rentals a permit would be required.
Miller asked if there is a way to let the building department know that an accessory dwelling is not being
used as a rental.

Griesemer asked about the notification process for tenants.
Hanneke stated tenants would be noticed and would need to give consent for inspection.
Pam asked about the current law regarding inspections.
Morra spoke about the process for conducting inspections by the Town; beginning with a request for the
lease language, arranging access with the occupant, and, if needed, seeking a court order for access to the
property. Typically requests for lease language and access to the property are accommodated.
Devlin Gauthier asked about energy efficiency data gathering.
Hanneke spoke about the questions that the Town will pose to property owners regarding energy
efficiency, including insulation types, fuel types, age, and who pays bills.
Griesemer asked about occupancy.
Hanneke stated the committee has discussed occupancy limits; occupancy limits exist in the zoning
bylaw, the committee’s discussions have been around whether or not an occupancy limit violation should
be considered in violation with the rental bylaw and how and what penalty would be imposed for a
violation.
Councilors discussed occupancy limits, how to enforce limits, ensure safety, that evictions would not be a
preferred resolution method, and how to hold property owners accountable for occupancy limit violations.
Steinberg raised the issue of parking plan requirements in terms of occupancy limits; that properties with
more cars parked than renters allowed, and how a more careful review of parking plans can be integrated
into the bylaw.
Hanneke spoke about conversations with staff about parking plans.
Rooney commented that parking plans and cars are one of the larger issues that cause conflict with rental
properties, and that she would like to see tighter expectations for parking plan reviews and enforcement.
Miller asked if there are legal issues with expanding occupancy limits.
Morra confirmed that there are fire safety requirements that would be associated with higher occupancy
limits, more than 5, such as sprinklers.
Pam expressed concerns that higher occupancy limits will destroy neighborhoods and exacerbate existing
parking issues.
Bahl-Milne spoke about discrimination against renters and that a more detailed discussion about parking
issues is needed.
Taub commented that student renters would have a car per person whereas a family would have only one
or two cars. Taub asked if the Town can make fire regulations stricter.

Morra responded that the Town cannot write a bylaw that lessens or strengthens state fire codes. Morra
added that there was a recent violation of an occupancy limit, and the matter went to court; the ultimate
question was whether the living space was safe. Ultimately, the Town worked with the tenants to reach a
resolution allowing the tenants to leave the property within three months.
Hanneke spoke about the committee’s work on a fee structure, and how fees should be structured that will
allow funding for inspections in the new system.
Steinberg spoke about the characterization of student renters and concern about the potential trickle-down
effect of increased costs that may impact costs of renting in Town.
Taub commented that there were huge parties over the past weekend due to the football game and spoke
critically about the late hours that students are active in Town.
Lopes spoke about the broad range of renters and to be inclusive of renters.
Hanneke stated there will not be a new draft of the bylaw until more feedback is collected at the next
committee meeting, there is feedback from the Town attorney, and the committee has more time to
deliberate on community and Council feedback.
