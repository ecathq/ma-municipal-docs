3. General Public Comment
Zoë Crabtree, Amherst resident, spoke in support of the nine youth and CSSJC members, expressed
disappointment regarding the way the CSSJC has been treated, and stated the police misrepresented the
events in their report according to one parent. Crabtree demanded APD apologize, that the Town conduct
an independent investigation, and the Town should establish a compensation fund.
Rani Parker, Amherst resident, spoke about the need for an apology from the Police Department in order
to reach reconciliation, and thanked Griesemer for her apology.
Birdy Newman, Mount Holyoke student, spoke in support of the nine youths and the need for a
compensation fund, and spoke about following the lead of the CSSJC in finding a path forward.
Kathleen Anderson expressed disappointment in the white members of the Council, and stated the
Council needs to hold the police accountable for their actions on July 5.
John Bonifaz, Amherst resident, spoke about accountability for police misconduct as a critical part of
reconciliation, stated the youths were wrongly detained and told they had no rights. Bonifaz stated the
President should apologize to Ononibaku for stating it was inappropriate for Ononibaku to ask for the
police chief’s resignation. Bonifaz stated the President’s actions were a violation of Ononibaku’s first
amendment rights.
Jim Oldham, Amherst resident, stated the officer’s behavior was shaming and horrifying, and that what
happened on July 5 was an obvious abuse of human rights and Town leadership has failed to
acknowledge and apologize for it. Oldham spoke in support of the CSSJC and what they are asking for.
Anita Sarro, Amherst resident, spoke about her experience working in healthcare and witnessing an
avoidance of responsibility for harm and compared the process of addressing harm in the healthcare
setting to the interaction between the youth and police on July 5.
Gabriel Davila, Amherst resident, spoke about the need for accountability, that the residents have been
ignored and that there needs to be a fundamental change to the Town’s approach to policing.
Bahl-Milne read a statement provided by Patricia Romney, in which Romney stated the outcome of the
July 5 incident was an acceptable outcome, and that the interaction was not an abuse of rights, and asked
what community needs are not being met because Town officials are addressing the incident.
Lauren Mills, Amherst resident, read a selection from the book “Finding Her Voice,” regarding
microaggressions.

4. Presentations and Discussion
a. Joint Discussion with Community Safety and Social Justice (Continued)
Ferreira expressed frustration that so much time has passed, that healing needs to happen once
accountability takes place, and that this has been traumatizing to the young people involved. Ferreira
stated there needs to be action, that the reports presented were full of holes and that the Council needs to
be accountable, and that we need to act quickly.
Clark thanked everyone who spoke during public comment, stated the last meeting ended contentiously,
and expressed hope that there will be a resolution and move forward. Clark spoke about the CSSJC’s
demands regarding the incident of a compensation fund and apology. Clark spoke about a letter regarding
Amherst police on Hampshire College campus detainment of a student, and subsequent letter from the
Hampshire College Dean of Students regarding the police action. Clark stated the remaining asks are to
freeze hiring police offices, engage in visioning sessions, create a resident oversight board, use the
CRESS department, reduce contact between BIPOC and the police, and create a BIPOC youth center.
Shabazz thanked the community and youth showing their support. Shabazz referred to a letter from
William Stewart, who stated he was at the scene and witnessed discrimination during the interaction.
Shabazz relayed Stewart’s comments stating that the police abused their authority and the youth have
been traumatized. Shabazz spoke about the long-term effects of trauma. Shabazz spoke about the harm
done to the community and the lack of an apology from the Police Chief.

a. Motion postponed on October 18, 2022:
MOTION: To refer the matter of the incident occurring on July 5th involving two police
offers and 9 youths to the CSSJC (Community Safety and Social Justice Committee),
HRC (Human Rights Commission), and the AHRA (African Heritage Reparation
Assembly) to collaboratively review the incident with the input of the DEI department
and other appropriate staff, and in consultation with the town attorney, for the purposes of
making a recommendation to the Town Council to repair the harm and reconcile the
incident by November 21, 2022.

substitute the motion on the floor with a new motion that would create an ad hoc committee to develop a
recommendation to create a method of repair and reconciliation.

2. Two (2) Members from the Community Safety and Social Justice Committee (CSSJC);
Two (2) Members of the Human Rights Commission (HRC); Two (2) Members of the
African Heritage Reparations Assembly (AHRA) *
3. One (1) non-voting community liaison member of the APD
The purpose of the committee is to Develop a Proposal for Repair and Reconciliation
with a focus on the incident occurring on July 5, 2022, between two police officers and
nine youths. The proposal shall be in accordance with Massachusetts General Law and
other laws, regulations, and policies. The Committee shall:
1. Clarify definitions and establish a shared language
2. Establish consensus on the factual record of what transpired during the incident on July
5 between the police and 9 youths
3. Establish consensus on the factual record of follow-up regarding the incident on July 5,
through the present
4. Identify and recognize specific harms caused from the incident, through the present
5. Research and explore models for repair including procedural, policy, and
compensatory options
6. Develop a proposed plan for repair, including a concrete process for handling future
similar incidents, for consideration by the Town Council
7. Town Manager, APD, and the DEI and CRESS Director to participate in relevant
discussions as needed
8. If needed, obtain legal review by the Town Attorney
9. Report back to the Town Council no later than the February 6, 2023 Council Meeting
Reports: The Ad Hoc group shall provide a proposed plan including actions, definitions,
a timeline, and item(s) requiring funds to the Town Council. The Chair of the Ad Hoc
group shall be prepared to present the Plan to the Town Council for consideration at the
Council February 6, 2023 meeting.
* CSSJC, HRC, and AHRA Committee Chairs shall recommend representatives to the
Council President
