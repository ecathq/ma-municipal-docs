Item 8.c. Motion Postponed on November 1
MOVED: That the Town Council requests that the Town Manager – working with the DEI
Department and other staff and discuss with and draw upon the ongoing work of the
Community Safety and Social Justice Committee (CSSJC), the Human Rights Commission
(HRC), and African Heritage Reparations Assembly (AHRA) – forthwith identify available
resources both inside and outside of Town government to:
1. Create and implement a reconciliation plan to include a proposal for
community visioning;
2. Create a proposal for a Resident Oversight Board;
3. Undertake a review of public safety protocols for responding to and
handling public safety calls involving minors, in order to recommend
changes to those protocols, if appropriate;
4. Conduct research into the feasibility of a justice compensation fund;
5. Explore options for a Youth Empowerment Center as part of the
Capital Improvement Plan;
6. Develop protocols for active engagement by community responders
(CRESS);
7. Develop and offer racial equity training, know your rights training,
and other options for additional training to support to employees
and members of the public; and
8. Raise awareness in the community about these efforts;
9. And for the Town Manager to report on the progress on this
reconciliation plan to the Council no later than two months from the
date of this vote.
MOTION: Griesemer moved, second by Schoen, to postpone the motion that is on the floor made by
Lynn Griesemer and Seconded by Ana Devlin Gauthier to November 14, 2022 at 6:30 p.m.

4. General Public Comment
Vira Cage, Amherst resident, spoke about Town employee salaries at the Amherst Police department, and
police officers who make six figures, and the concern about the para educators’ salaries. Cage stated it is a
disgrace to know there is a $1.8M renovation project for a park from CPA funds. Cage asked the Council
to be thoughtful about the nine young people that the police confronted and detained and that they also are
high school students trying to survive their junior or senior year, they really want this to be behind them
and that the Council holds the fate of their well-being.
Julian Hynes, Amherst resident, commented that educators asking for a living wage does not seem like
too much to ask. Hynes stated that it is important to equally value the people at the bottom of the pay
scale. The school district must have a mini-survival center for the paraeducators and that is sad for
students to see this happening to staff members. These people need to be paid a living wage and their
would should be valued.
Lamikio Magee, ARPS employee and APA President, spoke about quality of school and quality of
service provided in schools is directly associated with people wanting to live in the community. One of
the biggest decisions a family makes is where to raise and educate their kids. It is the people in the
schools who serve the children that determines the quality of education, not the building itself. There is an
education crisis. Not earning a living wage and not affording to live in the community we serve is what
our employees are experiencing. Educators are asking for fair negotiation and a living wage to maintain
the tax base we want in Amherst.
Gabriel Davila spoke about what the union is asking of the School Committee as reasonable demands and
the ability to directly negotiate is critical to serve its purpose. They want substitute pay, cost of living
adjustments, and a reasonable workload for educators and paraeducators. Davila stated the Town should
value educator pay above improving a park or building a library. Their demands are in line with what is
needed to function. If the school is not educating well, the Town won’t attract families. The Town needs
to show that the community values educators as much as we value the police, and value them at the
foremost.
Alex Lopez, Amherst resident, commented that the school does not have substitutes and he sees
colleagues walking out the door who work in highest needs settings.
Margaret Sawyer spoke about the school budget and the need for the Town to release more money for the
School Committee to fairly negotiate with the school union, with a consequence of a strike if there is not a
solution that is suitable to the union.
Laura Pagliarulo, Amherst resident, spoke about her family’s move to Amherst a few years ago from
Washington DC and selection of Amherst based on the quality of the school system. Pagliarulo spoke in
support of the union educators, spoke about inflation, that a 2% increase with a 8% inflation rate is
essentially a 6% decrease. Spoke about the budget and the cost of educating students for 180 days per
year, that the Town spends $18/hour per child, which is what she pays her babysitter.

Danielle Seltzer, Turners Falls resident and ARPS employee and member of APA negotiating team,
commented the union asked for 8% increase initially but has come down while the School Committee
remained at 2.5%. Seltzer stated the retention rate dropped after the low increase in the last year; retention
is poor everywhere but is particularly poor in Amherst. She spoke about her teaching qualifications and
that she cannot afford to own a home. She stated the union is requesting dignity, the ability to only work
one job, respect from her employer, and in-person negotiations.
Geoff Friedman, ARPS high school teacher and Northampton resident, chose to move to Amherst for the
school system. Friedman stated he believes in public education, tradition of excellence, unwavering
commitment to community, faculty, and staff. The challenge is to do all you can to continue to maintain
the legacy of excellence.
Shawna Towsey Pfarrer spoke about working with kids at the height of their energy and personal
problems, salaries are $17.13/hour less than what Northampton public schools are offering for similar
positions.
Manny Wineman, special education teacher, stated students in the district need more intensive support,
voiced concern about present levels of support for paraeducators and the work paraeducators do is
physically and emotionally challenging while the pay is exceptionally low. The disparity in compensation
raises concerns about justice and all people desire to be valued and respected.
Jeff Kalman, Amherst resident, spoke about adequate pay for Amherst educators. Kalman stated he
moved to Amherst for the education system and the schools are not doing well. He and his wife work in
Amherst schools and their experience has been that salaries are shocking and unfair.
Molly Cooksy, ARPS employee and Greenfield resident, spoke about attending Amherst schools but
cannot afford to live in Amherst now. Cooksy stated many kids have IEPs which are legally binding
contracts and schools put us in a position to be noncompliant with IEPs due to lack of funding. Salaries
are the same as Walmart, Burger King, and Subway. Cooksy spoke about the high level of care children
receive at school and that educators should not have to work second or third jobs.
Remy Fernandez, ARPS employee, Haydenville resident, and performing arts teacher spoke about
teaching performing arts during the pandemic.
Nelli Gonzalez, ARPS educator and Holyoke resident, spoke about her work and that she must work a
second job but that it may not be a sustainable situation for her due to the toll on physical, emotional, and
mental health.
Pam expressed frustration that the Council is unable to do anything about the issues raised during public
comment today.
Rooney stated it was astounding that there was one school board member attending in person, and that she
hopes the School Committee heard the people who spoke.
Bahl-Milne said she heard one of the School Committee members say they have tried to ask for increases
and have not been heard and that maybe it is time to have that conversation at the Budget Coordinating
Group.
Devlin Gauthier stated the Council is here to listen, and that when the Council adopts guidelines, we can
shift the levels we set for each area and could say the school budget should be increased.
Lopes said it is surprising that educators work 2 or 3 jobs in our community.

b. Public Ways
1) Proposed Parking Regulation Changes
• To prohibit parking on the East Side of Lincoln Ave. between McClellan
St. and Amity St. from 8 a.m. to 5 p.m., Monday through Friday from
September 1 to May 30
• To prohibit parking on the east side of Sunset Ave. between Elm St. and
Amity St. at all times
• To prohibit parking on one side of Elm St. at all times
De Angelis expressed concerns about the way the Council has determined how to make regulation
changes on Lincoln Ave., and suggested signage saying it is against the law to park near driveways. De
Angelis stated she could not support the changes without trying other options first.
Hanneke spoke about what should be considered as part of the review criteria and that if the street would
be safe with parking one way, the Council should consider that option.
MOTION: Griesemer moved, second by Schoen, to adopt the following proposed regulations for Lincoln
Ave, Sunset Ave, and Elm St:
• To prohibit parking on the East Side of Lincoln Ave. between McClellan St. and Amity St. from
8 a.m. to 5 p.m., Monday through Friday from September 1 to May 30
• To prohibit parking on the east side of Sunset Ave. between Elm St. and Amity St. at all times
• To prohibit parking on one side of Elm St. at all times
Schoen spoke in favor of the motion, stating it is not only a problem during football games, is directly
attributed to lack of parking at UMass, and that the new regulations would not remove taxpayer access but
would remove non-taxpayer access, referring to UMass students and faculty.
Taub spoke about the following:
• One-way traffic would increase vehicle speeds
• Lincoln Ave as a main north-south access road with more traffic than other residential streets
• The current situation is potentially hazardous during the UMass term
• The street does not meet the recommended width for two-way traffic with parking
• New dorms at the end of the street will add to the problem
• 25-30 parking spaces will be removed with the new regulations
• Parking has been reduced on campus during construction
Lopes reported the TSO voted unanimously to recommend the regulation changes.
Steinberg spoke in favor of the motion, stated the issue predated the Town Council and was discussed
during his terms as a Select Board member, painted lines are not enforceable, and professional width
standards are part of the policy from DPW.

Pam spoke about enforcement and safety in crosswalks on Amity St.
Miller stated it is concerning that Hanneke had a motion in mind before the initial motion had been made

• Speeds will increase because it will be wide enough for cars to go faster

Devlin Gauthier stated there could be a reevaluation period, there is a loss of at least 26 free parking

enforcement.
De Angelis spoke about a comment from a graduate student during the parking hearing who asked what
kind of town Amherst is going to be, and that making these types of changes does not respect UMass
students and staff as members of the community. De Angelis spoke in opposition to the motion.
Lopes stated there were contradictions between residents statements and Fire Chief Nelson’s statements
regarding public safety vehicle access on Lincoln St. and expressed concern about the message the
Council is sending.

concerns.
