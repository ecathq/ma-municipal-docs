Demling spoke in praise of the Finance Department and asked how likely the Town Manager is to raise
the school’s budget by $1M; the School Committee has no power or authority to set the budget amount,
the School Committee can spend what is approved by the Town Council but cannot increase the budget
itself. $1M is not enough to cover what is being requested by the school union.
Bockelman stated the meeting tonight is to present the Town’s financial picture for the Town. The Public
Hearing on November 21 is an opportunity for the public to speak in support of budget priorities to the
Town Council.
Steinberg asked about the amount of spending per capita being skewed by the university, but the revenues
are also skewed due to economic activity due to the university and does not show up in comparisons to
other communities.
Mangano spoke about the costs per capita, the services the Town provides may be different but the Town
is providing services to all 40,000 residents. The Town is one of highest percentage of tax exempt land.
Hanneke commented about the percentage of the budget as personnel costs and asked about cost
increases.
Mangano spoke about employees’ salary steps and cost of living increases.
Rhodes thanked the Finance team for the presentation; Rhodes spoke about new growth as a red flag for
the Town going forward. 20-25,000 residents who carry the burden of paying property taxes; not
projecting an override for FY24, thought the new school building would go for an override in FY24.
Mangano stated there may be a debt exclusion vote, which is different from a tax override and is
anticipated in FY24.
Schoen spoke about the following:
• Explaining to the larger public what it means not to be taxed and debt exclusion divided by
taxpayers

• Examining what other large universities pay in lieu of taxes to their municipality to help frame
Amherst’s situation
• Tax dollars do no stretch as far as they used to
• Spoke in support of the Fair Share Amendment ballot question
• Local receipts are not where they were two or three years ago, there was a surplus last year due to

Mangano spoke about conservative estimates in local receipts due to the volatility in the economy.
D. Pam asked if the Town has done a tax override for regular expenses.
Mangano spoke about an override that occurred in 2011.
Shiao asked about the projections and that it feels like the budget is already decided, and asked when or
how does the district have an opportunity to tell the Town Council what they need.
Bockelman stated the projections show what the Town expects it can afford according to projected
revenue and capital as well as mandated expenses and takes what is left in the budget and divides it
between the Library, Schools, and General Government. There are options available. This is the first
meeting where we are sharing data that we have at this point in the budget year.
Mangano spoke about the departments receiving the same increase, so all departments are not pitted
against each other.
Steinberg spoke about override history, which occurred following the 2009 recession. The override was
implemented over several years rather than all at once. To ask homeowners to approve an override, many
of whom are on limited income, is a difficult decision. The budget forum on November 21 is an
opportunity for the public to come and tell the Council about their desires for the budget; the Council can
suggest a different budget policy. The goal is to give the Town Manager guidance before the budget is
proposed. The Finance Committee will develop a proposed set of guidelines, the committee will provide a
first draft to the Council on December 5.
Rhodes stated there is a difference between a debt exclusion and an override, and spoke about the
difference in how the taxes are applied.
Mangano stated an override is a permanent increase to taxes above 2½%; a debt exclusion is an increase
over 2% on a temporary basis for the life of the debt. Both require passage by voters to go into effect.
Debt exclusions are typically tied to MSBA school projects in the state.
Hanneke stated the BCG, through the Town Manager, can request another meeting of the BCG to discuss
budget issues.
