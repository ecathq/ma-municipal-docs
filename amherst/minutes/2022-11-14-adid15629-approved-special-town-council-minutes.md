b. Motion postponed on November 7, 2022:
MOTION: That the Town Council requests that the Town Manager – working
with the DEI Department and other staff and through discuss with and draw upon
the ongoing 3 Town Council Unofficial Record of Votes 11-01-2022 work of the
Community Safety and Social Justice Committee (CSSJC), the Human Rights
Commission (HRC), and African Heritage Reparations Assembly (AHRA) –
forthwith identify available resources both inside and outside of Town
government to:
1) create and implement a reconciliation plan to include a proposal for
community visioning;
2) create a proposal for a Resident Oversight Board;
3) undertake a review of public safety protocols for responding to and handling
public safety calls involving minors, in order to recommend changes to those
protocols, if appropriate;
4) conduct research into the feasibility of a justice compensation fund;
5) explore options for a Youth Empowerment Center as part of the Capital
Improvement Plan;
6) develop protocols for active engagement by community responders
(CRESS);
7) develop and offer racial equity training, know your rights training, and other
options for additional training to support employees and members of the
public; and
8) raise awareness in the community about these efforts;
and for the Town Manager to report on the progress on this reconciliation plan to
the Council no later than two months from the date of this vote.
Town Manager Bockelman ready a statement regarding the incident on July 5 and the actions taken
following the incident.
Councilors reviewed the motion on the floor and list of motions proposed by Councilors.
Pam commented that she would not like to vote on any of the motions, and that the statement by the
Town Manager was heartfelt and sufficient.
Schoen echoed Pam’s comments regarding the Town Manager’s statement.
Devlin Gauthier commented that she appreciated the Town Manager’s statement, but that there needs to
be additional action taken by the Town Council.
Griesemer spoke about her proposed amendment, explaining that the revisions are based on discussion
and the Town attorney’s opinion and advice on the motions.
Hanneke spoke about her proposed amendments, which were based on the Town Council discussion and
the attorney’s opinion.

MOTION: Griesemer moved, second by Steinberg, to substitute the motion on the floor with the
following:
That the Town Council requests that the Town Manager – working with the DEI Department and other
staff:
1) Propose to the Town Council a plan for community visioning with a focus on public safety and
social justice;
2) Propose to the Town Council a plan for the creation of a Resident Oversight Board, with possible
assistance from and hire, as appropriate, a consultant to help with the development of that plan;
3) Organize a review of public safety protocols for responding to and handling public safety calls
involving all residents including minors, in order to recommend changes to those protocols, if
appropriate;
4) Continue to develop protocols for CRESS regarding active engagement by community responders;
5) Continue the work already begun on exploring options for a youth empowerment center;
6) Provide training regarding racial equity, rights, and other options for training to employees and
members of the public; and
7) Develop a communications plan to raise awareness in the community about these efforts;
and that the Town Manager will report on actions to be taken and/or progress in addressing the above, no
later than four months from the date of this vote. Draft reports to be available to the Council, the
Community Safety and Social Justice Committee (CSSJC), and the Human Rights Commission (HRC), as
well as the public, no less than two weeks prior to the Town Council meeting where the items will be
discussed; that CSSJC and HRC provide written advice to the Town Council 5 days prior to the Town
Council Meeting at which the items will be discussed; that the Town Council will discuss the Manager’s
report and both committee reports, and will hold a discussion that includes public comment.
Councilors discussed the motion.
Lopes spoke in support of the motion, specifically the possible assistance from a consultant.
De Angelis commented that the deadline does not feel realistic.

Walker spoke to the motion.
Devlin Gauthier stated this motion goes beyond the motion passed by the Town Council, and suggested
changing the motion to “update as appropriate.”
Steinberg stated he cannot support the motion, as it interjects the Council into the collective bargaining
process.
MOTION: Steinberg moved, second by Hanneke, to amend the motion by removing the last three
bullets.
Steinberg spoke to the motion, stating there is an implication that the Council is telling the Town
Manager how to conduct negotiations with an implication that the Town Manager report back on those
specific topics and is contrary to what the Charter provides; without an amendment it opens the Town to
legal challenges that are totally unnecessary.
Pam commented that this is about a committee that did a lot of work and paid a consultant to do a lot of
work; the personnel issues are not sacrosanct; when government money is spent, so many reports are
shoved in a box under a bookcase; to say things are totally off the table forever is not acceptable; we paid
for a consultant to do this work and it would be a waste not to do something with that work.
Rooney spoke in opposition to the motion.
Hanneke spoke in support of the motion to amend, stating the motion as made exceeds our authority as a
legislature; the Council has no authority to negotiate the contracts and disciplinary policies, the charter
gives those powers to the Town Manager absolutely.
Steinberg commented that the CSWG is not the Council; the CSWG expressed its opinions on matters
pertaining to collective bargaining, which is very different from the Council expressing opinions on
collective bargaining; the Town Manager can make a decisions on whether to consider those
recommendations during negotiations, and the Council should not interject itself into negotiations.
MOTION: De Angelis moved, second by Hanneke, to call the previous question.

MOTION: Walker moved, second by De Angelis, to recommend the Town Manager assist the APD in
developing a proactive, anti-racist culture in the Amherst Police Department and that it be documented
and regular updates be provided to the Town Council.
Devlin Gauthier recommended changing the words to “Town Manager assist in identifying steps to
develop.”
Hanneke spoke about the Council overstepping its job in speaking directly to a department; the motion
does not say that the rest of the Town should develop an anti-racist culture as well.
Bockelman expressed concern that the motion implies that there is one department that is racist. The APD
has done some of the most anti-racist work in the Town. Bockelman suggested adding anti-racism
training to the Town Manager’s goals.
Steinberg stated he is uncomfortable with a motion that assumes that a particular department needs
attention without a factual basis to make that conclusion. It would be a mistake to pass a motion that has
that implication in it.
Walker spoke about the intention of the motion, the difference between not racist and being anti-racist,
and that the motion was not intended to be negative.
De Angelis spoke about the power of police and about the ongoing work needed to address biases.
Councilors discussed the motion.
