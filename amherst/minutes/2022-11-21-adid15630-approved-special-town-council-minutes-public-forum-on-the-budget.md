4. Public Comment
a. Public Forum on the Town Budget – Charter Sec. 5.3
Residents are welcome to express their views for up to 3 minutes, at the
discretion of the Town Council President, based upon the number of people who
wish to speak. The Council will not engage in a dialogue or comment on a matter
raised during Public Comment.
Jean Fay, Amherst resident and employee of the Amherst schools, shared that she works a second job, is
at the top of her pay scale, and the proposed increase would not make a significant monetary difference.
Fay stated we need to do more for educators and support educators.
Felicia Mednick, Amherst resident, spoke about her work regarding climate action and Amherst’s climate
goals.
Max Page, Amherst resident and President of the Mass Teachers Association, commented about the
Town’s strong financial position; there is an ideology, we do not exist to grow reserves by underpaying
educators. We need to do better; rethink the budget and free up money for the School Committee to pay
fair wages to educators.
Ashwin Ravikumar, Amherst resident, commented that there is one person on staff related to climate,
spoke about underpaying teachers, prioritizing economic growth, revalue things as a society and
community, fund CRESS to have full-time responders, fund teachers, fund other recommendations from
CSWG, reduce police force by half, express values more clearly, create a victims compensation fund,
conduct a community healing process, and consider cutting the police budget.
Anjelica Bernal, parent of Amherst school students, spoke about her son’s special needs and the
paraprofessionals who support his learning. Ashamed of the pay paraprofessionals receive. Spoke about
building an equitable future for school staff.
Lydia Vernon-Jones, Amherst resident & member of the Climate Justice Alliance, spoke about potential
to do our part to reduce and eliminate carbon footprint, achieving climate goals, double climate staff,
extend current policy for purchasing and budgeting, training for boards and committees, work with
CAARP to develop detailed plans to provide timelines for climate action.
Danielle Seltzer, Turners Falls resident, spoke about towns comparable to Amherst, looked up educator
contracts and entry level, master’s degree, and highest step salaries. All of the towns pay teachers more at
beginning, midpoint, and end of the salary scale. Feels like finances happen on the backs of all municipal
employees. The longer this goes on, the higher the tension will be. Getting compensation from higher
education institutions should be a priority.
Zoë Crabtree, Amherst resident, echoed comments from previous speakers. Spoke about a friend who
moved from town because rent became affordable. Spoke in support of additional CRESS responders to
serve 24/7, funds to get CRESS set up with dispatch, funds to launch a BIPOC youth empowerment

Clare Coco, Amherst resident and middle school teacher, spoke about moving from New York to work at
the school and live in a community with great values and with a great school system. After moving was
unable to afford a home and lived in Rolling Green apartments. Hard to live in Town with a teacher’s
salary. Cost of living increases have not been keeping up with inflation for many years.
Birdy Newman, Amherst resident, supported comments calling for climate justice, fair pay for workers,
public safety, and anti-racism. There is a long way to go before everyone experiences fair treatment.
Newman suggested the Town fund BIPOC cultural center and youth empowerment center, reduce size &
budget of police by half, create a victims compensation fund, and fund an extended process of community
healing.
Keith McFarland, educator and Sunderland resident and school committee and regional school committee
member in Sunderland regional school, spoke about the school’s budget development process.
Superintendent states the Town dictates what the budget will be for schools; schools should present the
Town with their needs.
Martha Hanner, Amherst resident and member of the Amherst LWV racial justice committee, spoke in
praise of CRESS and DEI departments. Hanner stated CRESS funding should increase in order to expand
their service to allow 24-hour coverage, DEI funding for programs and activities and administrative
support, and continue to implement CSWG goals.
Sabrina, Amherst resident and student, spoke about policing and safety, racial profiling at the UMass
Amherst police department, spoke in support of funding for 8 more CRESS responders, maintain CRESS
as independent Town body, BIPOC center and youth empowerment center, victims’ compensation fund,
an extended process of community visioning and racial healing.
Cathy Axelson-Berry, Amherst resident, thanked those who spoke before her, reiterated that Town Hall
doesn’t have enough staff to do things, need more Town Hall staff, prioritizing students and educators,
strategic partnership agreements, and sustainable development.
Julian Hynes, Amherst resident, spoke about how critical it is to implement CAARP plan, timelines are
getting closer to goal deadlines. Hynes spoke in support of tree planting, development and renovation of
bike lanes, more Town Hall staff and sustainability staff, more than a 2% raise and respect for work of
school staff.
John Root, Amherst resident, spoke about the Amherst Climate Justice Coalition and solid waste master
plan.
Ya-Ping Douglas, Amherst resident, spoke in support of funding for 8 more responders for CRESS and
maintaining CRESS as independent town body, BIPOC youth center, teachers and climate action.
Rani Parker, Amherst resident, suggested using some capital funds to improve sidewalks, repair
buildings, and adopt an aggressive strategy to maintain buildings as much as possible; spoke about
funding for teachers and paraprofessional staff, planning departments, and tripling CRESS staff. Parker
stated meetings at 10:30 in the morning are not accessible.
Allegra Clark, Amherst resident, spoke in support of the demands from the educators, increase staffing for
CRESS, a freeze on hiring in the police department, youth empowerment center and BIPOC cultural
center.
