7. Presentations and Discussion
a. Elementary School Building Project – Key Dates
Bockelman and Schoen spoke briefly about the estimated project schedule.
Griesemer spoke about the proposed timeline for a debt exclusion vote.
Hanneke thanked everyone for the calendar and for confirming the universities and colleges will still be in
session on the proposed election date; it is important that the vote be held when residents are in town.
b. Surveillance Use Report
Bockelman spoke about the surveillance technology oversight bylaw, and the summary of the report he
has presented as required. The only department with technology that applies to the bylaw is the police
department’s use of dash cameras. There will be a detailed report on in-car cameras in use by the Town,
with notice published on the bulletin board, and a request for approval by the Town Council. Other
surveillance technology may arise in the near future, including body cameras for police officers and
automated library materials handling systems with RFID technology.
Hanneke thanked the Town Manager; there was a lot of concern about what would apply and sponsors of
the bylaw tried to write it to make it about things that are only necessary. The bylaw allows a pocket
approval similar to Town Manager appointments; the Council will have 180 days to approve use of the
technology or it is automatically approved. Hanneke stated she looks forward to seeing the planned use
policy and impact report.

MOTION on the floor, postponed on October 18: To adopt appropriation and transfer order FY23-
05C: An Order Appropriating Funds for a Portion of the Town of Amherst Capital Program – School
Track and Field Rehabilitation, recommended by Finance Committee on October 17, 2022, and shown on
page 6 of the Draft Motions sheet.
Griesemer spoke about the history of the project, and votes taken thus far. The current approval request
for the Town Council is an appropriation of $900,000 from free cash.

Miller asked why the Council cannot specify exactly how a gift of funds to the Regional School is spent.
Mangano spoke about the process, that the Regional School decides what projects to pursue and comes to
the member towns for funds; if the Council were to specify option 2 in its vote, the other two Council
votes would be nullified.
Schoen suggested asking the Regional School Committee to reconsider the options; the Council cannot
vote to appropriate funds without a proposal.
Steinberg spoke about the original request for $1.5M, which included a provision that specified option 3,
option 2 was not included in the vote. If the Council chose not to approve the funding and the funds
needed were not raised by other means, the Regional School would make a new request. Member towns
have town meetings in the spring and need an opportunity to respond; a start-over would delay the project
for at least a year and, due to inflation, the costs would increase.
Pam stated artificial turf is a silly and expensive mistake, and that a complicated process is not a good
reason to move forward with a project Councilors are opposed to; Pam spoke about the Town pledging to
maintain the fields.
Morris thanked the Town’s DPW for taking care of the current fields, stating the Town has some
excellent and dedicated DPW workers maintaining the fields. Work is compromised by the condition of
the fields, and staff are doing the best they can with poorly designed and drained fields.
Councilors discussed the funding approval process among the member towns.
McDonald referred to presentations from School Finance Director Slaughter about the funding process.
Funding from the various sources must fall into place for the project to happen. We are trying to get to a
total number of funds; the plan is with multiple sources, if one town does not approve CPA funds, the
funds need to come from another source and more pressure is placed on the other sources.
Hanneke spoke about a potential motion to amend the appropriation and transfer order to make a
contingency of the RSC creating an advisory group to consider the materials used with public input to
ensure the materials used contain zero or near zero levels of PFAS. The goal is to recognize PFAS is a
concern and desire to make a turf field as safe as possible.
Devlin Gauthier stated she would support an amendment as Hanneke described.
Rooney also spoke in support of Hanneke’s suggestion, adding that it should also be contingent on
consideration of comparison with grass fields.
Schoen expressed concerns with the costs of removing artificial turf, and that the amendment should
include an option for grass.
Mangano stated an appropriation order with a contingency that is not well-defined would be problematic.
Pam stated artificial turf can cause MERSA, there are problems with the temperature of turf, and with
injuries.
Herrington spoke about the factors the Regional School Committee considered, including usability of the
fields and rest time needed for grass fields.
Kenney spoke about usability, life of the turf, maintenance, and expertise needed to maintain a grass field.

 Thanked all of those who came to speak at the meeting: students, coaches, and teammates
 Hoping this is not another time that Amherst drops the ball for their kids

Sara Ross, Amherst resident, thanked the supporters who are in the room. Commented that there is no
such thing as an artificial turf field without PFAS; the industry has shapeshifted to prey on leaders,
parents, and kids. As the field ages, testing will be required to maintain compliance with forever
chemicals. Health Director expressed opposition to artificial turf.
Victoria Dawson, Amherst Regional Middle and High School Athletic Director, spoke in support of
students, coaches, and the DPW staff.
Don Fraser, the girls’ varsity soccer coach, spoke about the crucial need to maximize practice time.
Officials considered stopping games due to field conditions. Poor conditions of the fields put athletes at a
disadvantage without a field to play and practice on.
Vinnie Guiel, the head football coach, commented that students are at a big disadvantage playing on the
field we play on. When it comes to teams playing in Amherst, they are not happy with the field
conditions. Playing on turf is not dangerous; he played football and college, only injury was at Amherst
high school playing on that field. When the field is wet it is impossible to have good footing. The ability
to play on turf when it is raining is safer than on a grass field and all around a safer playing environment.
Never once a bacterial infection; only time was wrestling in college. Schools are moving away from grass
for obvious reasons. More positives with turf field than negatives. Turf is safer and better avenue for kids
looking to get into colleges.
Andrew MacDougall, the lacrosse coach, spoke about access and availability to fields. Our teams need
access to the fields to have full seasons. The first three weeks of each season are in the gym or running on
pavement because we can’t access our fields. Calling grass “natural” is an oxymoron in terms of
irrigation, seeding, and human intervention.
Lyons Witten, Chair of the Amherst Water Supply Protection Committee, spoke in opposition to the
Town purchasing an artificial turf field.
Patrick Schilling, the lacrosse coach, spoke about his three daughters who play on the field. The current
field is unplayable for weeks in the first 3 or 4 weeks of the season, which puts players at a competitive
disadvantage. Turf fields make for a faster play and can’t compete with teams who play on turf fields.
Sam Macleod, Chair of the Community Preservation Act Committee, not speaking on behalf of the
committee, and former coach and player, spoke about access to an all-season field being a significant
difference for kids.

Cedric Gonnet, the Amherst basketball coach and former coach of the youth football team, spoke about
opportunities due to athletics. Bringing the turf field would be an opportunity and advantage for kids;
although there are concerns about the effects of the artificial field, there are many benefits and the

Alberto Morales-Fernandez, the soccer coach, spoke about the experience this season and as a player,

Tim Schmitt, long-time football official and umpire and AHRA alum, spoke about embarrassment going

playing on turf away from home.
Felicia Mednick, Amherst resident, commented that her daughter played varsity sports and disliked turf

the water.
Neil Cunniffe, ARHS student and football player, spoke about the boys’ lacrosse field called the “snake
pit” due to a dip into the woods that is part of the field which causes problems for spectators and safety
problems.
Charlie Edwards, a coach, spoke about the delay of the season due to poor field conditions.
Patrick Staudenmayer, ARHS student and soccer player, experiences injuries. Games have been cancelled
or postponed due to rain. Supposed to host a playoff game that had to be moved to South Hadley due to
poor conditions.
Marcus Smith, Amherst resident and umpire, spoke about danger related to poor field conditions, and that
Amherst is renowned for poor quality fields. Frontier runs their grounds like a golf course, including use
of chemical fertilizers and weed killers.
Skyler Ferro, ARHS athlete, spoke about the poor field conditions, late start to the season, and playing
against teams who have been practicing for weeks longer than Amherst teams.
Elizabeth Haygood, Amherst resident since 1977, spoke about her children who attended Amherst schools
and played multiple sports. Haygood retired from AHRS after 40 years of teaching physical education and
health, and coached track and field for 25 years. Haygood spoke about community members who use the
fields and adaptive PE classes who try to use the fields, classes and teams must wait for fields to dry or
wade through mud when fields are flooded. The poor field conditions hurt the most vulnerable people in
Town, and the Town should invest in kids.
Stephanie Hockman, parent of ARHS athletes and Booster Club treasurer, spoke about low-PFAS options
that are available.

Miller stated she proposed a committee that was strongly opposed and that recommendations from
advisory committees have been rejected.
Morris stated the track resurfacing would move forward if fundraising goals are not met. The cost of
doing multiple grass fields over time is significant, and if the Council wants to pursue that option, it will
be on its own because the price tag will be more significant. Many people will be disappointed and
question the capacity of the community and Town to maintain multiple grass fields when there are other
demands. The grass option is not the preferred option.
Mangano stated a new natural field does not solve the problem, if we don’t change programming on the
field, a new field will be torn up. In 2019 we did a recreational field study and the recommendation
included one synthetic field. Schools need a location so that we can rest the other fields. One rainy
weekend completely devastated a field. If we continue to use fields as we are, we are putting money into
something that will be ruined quickly.
Walker stated she cannot support a motion that is specific to synthetic turf.
Hanneke spoke about this Council’s previous support 11-1 and 11-0 for the fields. If the Council votes no
and the Boosters fail to raise $1.2M in the next 2 months the athletes will be saddled with an inadequate
field, in the wrong orientation or dimensions for athletic events, and unable to host track championships.
There are no options for outdoor practice in wet conditions. Hanneke spoke in support of the motion.
Steinberg stated there is no grass option here, the Town cannot afford to build or maintain grass fields
without the participation of member towns. Steinberg spoke in support of the motion, stating it will be a
move forward to help Boosters to achieve their goals to create the kind of athletic facility that we have
been hearing all night that they want. Steinberg spoke about the reputation of our school and
attractiveness of our community when people know their kids will not be playing on healthy attractive
playing fields.
Miller accused Hanneke of bullying tactics and Steinberg of dishonesty when stating the Town cannot
afford grass before a financial assessment of the costs. Miller stated she wants to meet the needs of
student athletes and give them the best and the Council needs to step up and give them the best by
installing a natural grass field and removing what is harmful.
Morris stated if the vote does not pass, he will be disappointed. The students need an action plan rather
than an exploration plan, and he will be expecting an action plan if this funding is not approved.
Pam stated that students have been deprived for years and it is not the fault of the Town Council. We
were given a motion that is not a good motion, and that she would rather spend money on grass than
artificial turf.
Hanneke spoke about the Weston and Sampson report, which put the cost of grass fields at $1.5M over 12
years, and $11.68M for 8 fields. Hanneke stated it has been a struggle to find a way to pay for DPW, Fire
station, school, and library, and asked with the desire for a youth empowerment center, senior center, and
cultural center, where the Town would find funding for grass fields.
