a. Proposed Amendments to Zoning Bylaw Article 3, Use Regulations, Article 5,
Accessory Uses, Article 11, Administration and Enforcement, and Article 12,
Definitions – First Reading
Senior Planner Malloy spoke about the proposed changes to the zoning bylaw, which were brought to the
Council by the Planning Department, spoke about the purpose and goals of the proposed changes, and
food and drink establishments approved according to the temporary zoning allowed by Article 14 which
is expiring at the end of the year.
Bahl-Milne joined the meeting at 6:45 p.m.
Malloy spoke about the current and proposed use categories, with the corresponding permitting pathway,
examples of new use categories, and summarized the proposed changes. Malloy reported the Planning
Board voted 5 in favor with 1 abstention.
Hanneke reported Community Resources Committee voted unanimously to recommend the changes.
Miller reported Governance, Organization, and Legislation Committee voted unanimously to declare the
changes clear, consistent, and actionable.
Councilors discussed the changes, including:
 Definition of “café”
 Examples of accessory uses
 How conditions in section 3.352.3 would be applied
 Requirements for bars to serve food
 Notifications to abutters of changes to restaurant service
Malloy spoke about proposed uses that would not fit in the use chart, the building commissioner would
find the most closely applicable use, and the restaurant, café, and bar with food are all similar or the same
meaning and the building commissioner would find the best suited category for the use.

Brestrup reported the Planning Board voted 6-0 to recommend adoption of the zoning amendments.

the proposed revisions, to adopt the Flood Insurance Rate Maps, and to adopt the Flood Insurance Rate
Study, dated February 9, 2023. Hanneke spoke about the time of taking effect of the zoning changes, and

clear, consistent, and actionable, and to recommend adoption of the February 9, 2023 date.

Agenda item 6.a. Resolution Concerning the Safety of the Amherst Pelham Regional High School
Athletic Complex
MOTION ON THE FLOOR: To adopt the Resolution Concerning the Safety of the Amherst Pelham
Regional High School Athletic Complex, as presented/amended.
Griesemer spoke about a new draft submitted by sponsors that is available in the meeting packet.
Pam commented that most health organizations agree that PFAS is dangerous, the resolution does not
address how to remediate existing PFAS, and that she cannot support the resolution.
MOTION: Miller moved, second by Hanneke, to replace the previous motion adopting the Resolution
Concerning the Safety of the Amherst Pelham Regional High School Athletic Complex, with the newly-
proposed Resolution Concerning the Safety of the Amherst Pelham Regional High School Athletic
Complex submitted on December 12, 2022.
Miller spoke to the motion, stating that the sponsors, herself, Bahl-Milne, and Hanneke wanted to stick to
facts of record and established timeline, support students and student athletes, express concern about
PFAS, and make a commitment to evaluating those concerns in the new draft of the resolution. Miller
stated the sponsors did not want to get into a debate within the resolution about PFAS, and that if the
Council wishes to give the Regional School Committee direction, it should do that outside this resolution.

c. Proposed Amendment to Bylaw 3.22: Discharging of Firearms – First Reading
Hanneke reported on behalf of the Governance, Organization, and Legislation Committee’s recommended
changes to the bylaw, and the recommendation to modify the bylaw; the changes would not supersede
state and federal gun laws. The change would not allow discharge that is prohibited by state law, and
would allow discharging a firearm in lawful defense, and for the other reasons stated in the bylaw.
Steinberg commented that the numbering for the list of exclusions should not be numbered.
Schoen commented there is other numbering in the bylaw that needs to be corrected when the changes are
adopted.

e. Town Manager Goals – First Discussion
Councilors reviewed the draft goals section by section.
Miller returned to the meeting at 8:10 p.m.
Councilors discussed the following:
 Adding waste hauler as a goal
 Residents spoke in favor of including more details in the goals, particularly climate action
goals
 Flexibility to add or change goals during the year
 Specificity in the goals are more difficult to agree on
 Including a statement that some goals cannot be achieved in a single year and should be
pursued with available resources
 Including maintaining municipal services
 Including operation of a permanent shelter and affordable housing
 New structure of goals
 Including a record of Council votes, such as the vote on the July 5 incident
 Inclusion of ECAC recommendations
 How or if to include specific items the Council has not adopted, such as the waste hauler
bylaw
Councilors reviewed the goals section-by-section, agreeing by consensus on changes suggested by
Councilors.
Councilors discussed at length how to include antiracism efforts and if the Police Department should be
singled out in that section, and if a youth empowerment center should be included.
Councilors discussed the listening sessions Councilors attended at the Police Department.
Bockelman spoke about allocating resources to items the Council has referred to committees and that he
conveys priorities to staff.
