Amherst Town Council (called at 9:22am)
Slaughter reviewed the FY24 expense projections:
 Payroll increases were reflected in payroll accounts and control accounts.
 Special Education expenses have a significant projected 13.4% increase largely due to a projected 14%
increase in out of district placement costs
 8.5% increase projected for transportation expenses.
 7.9% increase projected for risk and benefits: employee and retiree health insurance is projected to
increase 8% and the Hampshire County retirement assessment is expected to increase by 11%
McDonald clarified the presented budget summary reflects the appropriated budget and does not include
federal and state grants. Slaughter confirmed this.
Trickey (Pelham) asked for an enrollment update. Morris replied that at ARHS enrollment had been
unexpected steady with 59 new students. ARMS down 20-25 students as anticipated.
Slaughter reviewed the FY24 projected revenue noting:
 State Chapter 70 aid increased by $30/student. With a new governor it is unclear how much state
support to expect for elementary and secondary education.
 Medicaid reimbursement is projected to return to pre-pandemic levels in FY24
 Funding from the Amherst elementary district to support shared staff and services related to 6th grade
students in ARMS is estimated at $100,000

Slaughter presented two budget projections: one with and one without 4% budget guardrails. If the four towns
agreed to use budget guardrails, Pelham and Leverett would be capped at a 4%. Slaughter noted
adjustments/cuts line could be supplemented with ESSER and possibly through budget adjustments as the
revenue and expenses becomes more clear.
Morris noted that there was not unanimous agreement on the use of budget guardrails from the summer
working group and prefaced that this four towns meeting was not looking for a vote on the use of budget
guardrails but instead meant to be informational.
Mosher (Shutesbury Finance) asked if the state increased special education costs and if it is expected that the
state will increase state funding to help cover the cost increase. Morris replied the increase in special education
cost is a statewide issue and there may be movement around circuit breaker reimbursement. He commented the
commissioner had mentioned that the state had its own ESSER funds to spend. Morris suggested the town
representatives could contact the state legislators to urge for increased state funding to offset the increased
special education expenses.
Griesemer (Amherst Town Council/Amherst Finance Committee) asked if savings and efficiencies would be
realized from the 6th grade move to ARMS. Morris replied that efficiencies would be realized when the new
elementary school building is available and confirmed there could be sharing of building-wide staff between
the Amherst and Regional districts. He commented he would be open to discussing the regional agreement and
efficiencies but added it would be a multiyear process involving legal costs. The district legal counsel has
already been notified that the regional agreement may be possible topic for discussion.
McDonald added that the cost of educating Amherst 6th graders is not included in the regional budget
presented to the four towns today. The cost to educate the Amherst 6th grade students will remain in Amherst
elementary budget. The $100,000 provided to the regional district by the Amherst district is a source of
revenue for the regional agreement due to factors such as space agreements. She noted that ESSER funds are
already supplementing the current budget. She asked for further information on how the district could use
ESSER funds to offset the projected adjustments/cuts line.
Slaughter replied that the district’s first choice would be to leverage funding options to cover the adjustments
line (e.g. ESSER), and to see what efficiencies can be realized. Afterwards they will determine if budget cuts
are needed.
Steinberg (Amherst Town Council, Amherst Finance committee) asked why there were plans to hire a separate
principal for the 6th grade students at ARMS instead of assigning those students to the current ARMS
principal. Morris added that asking one ARMS principal to cover 568 students would spread school leadership
too thin.
Steinberg (Amherst Town Council/Amherst Finance Committee) noted the increase in health insurance cost
was due in part to increases in staffing and asked whether the increase in retirement expenses was also due to
staff increases. Morris replied that additional staff were onboarded due to Covid and with the assistance of
ESSER funds. The budget for staffing will be more refined in January.
Ajay Khashu (Shutesbury finance committee) asked if the funding that Amherst elementary district would be
pay to the regional district for space was included in the $100,000. Morris replied it was included in the
$100,000 and that the funding amount was determined by the regional agreement (charged on a per room
basis).

Mangano (Amherst Finance Director) asked if the personal income surtax and DESE and state updates
included in this projection. Slaughter replied there has not yet been an indication of how the funds will be
allocated. He noted advocacy to state representatives was recommended.
Groves (Shutesbury Finance) asked where the $800,000 in school choice funds was applied in the budget. He
asked if it was being applied to payroll and whether payroll would be higher without that budget support.
Slaughter replied school choice is traditionally applied to educator salaries which in turn reduces the operating
budget line.
Hanneke (Amherst Town Council) said she would like to see the ESSER funds supporting the budget and
asked for the district plan for when those funds are no longer available. Morris replied more information on
this would be available in January. Slaughter noted there are grants that are reoccurring that support the
budget. His office is working to plan and work into a budget without ESSER funds.
Schoen (Amherst Town Council) commented it would be useful for lobbying efforts if the town representatives
could receive information from the district that outlines the ways in which fair share amendment funds could
be used to support the schools.
Capital requests
Trickey noted the girls’ locker room was not on the list of capital requests although it had been highly
demanded to be addressed. Slaughter replied that the project is not completely off the list however it is a big
ticket item and will be considered for the future.
Morris shared that the ARMS roof has be previously discussed by the four towns and that they had already set
aside appropriation for the roof project. During the conversations with the towns, it had been assumed the
district would receive assistance from an MSBA grant. However, the MSBA grant program had been paused.
In light of this, the district is proposing using 10% of the total appropriated fund to fund temporary roof
patching to help the roof for next 4-5 years. Morris noted the state of the roof had led to concerns of damage to
the building.
Slaughter noted that the capital project list would affect future years assessments. With the exception of the
ARMS roof project, the other projects would not be hitting the district’s borrowing in FY24. The numbers in
the projected assessments for capital projects will continue to be refined.
Griesemer commented that she liked the presented list and encouraged the districts to think of the list as a long
range plan for the aging buildings. She commented she would like the list to include making roof surfaces
ready for solar panels in light of the opportunity for solar grants.
Groves asked for the cost to replace the entire roof. Morris replied the last estimate was three million dollars,
however this figure is a few years old and does not include cost escalation. He noted the district would need
additional assessments on the project. He noted that making the roof structurally sound for solar panels would
incur additional costs.
Nancy Grossman (Leverett Finance committee) asked for clarification on the process for which option gets
chosen for the track and field project and the project’s funding sources. Morris replied that the regional school
committee worded its motion that ties it to a specific project option if the target funding goal is met. If the
target funding goal is not met, the committee will explore other options. Morris added that the towns already
voted on the track field assessment in the previous year. Slaughter added that any requests for additional
funding for the project that was not captured in the previous assessment would be presented to the towns in
Spring town meetings.

Schoen suggested the district get a cost estimate of the roof project with and without modifications to make the
roof ready for solar panels. She asked if ESSER funds could be used for the project. She added an upcoming
federal tax credit may be available to help with the cost of the roof. Morris replied the roof project would not
fall within the guidelines for the district’s use of ESSER funds as the funds have to be spent on covid-related
expenses.
Hankinson (Leveret Select Board) said his town will be discussing the roof and he expressed it would make
sense to have temporary repairs until MSBA grants resume.
Carter (Leverett Finances committee) asked what would happen if the regional committee approved artificial
turf and then towns do not approve funding for the project. Morris replied there are already pre-existing capital
funds that had been approved by the member towns. He noted the project was not presenting a new ask for
capital funds as that request had already been approved at a four town meeting in the previous year. Slaughter
added option three could not move forward without the required level of funding due to the language included
in the committee’s motion; the committee would have to follow state guidelines for debt authorizations.
Khashu asked if the capital debt schedule includes track resurfacing. Slaughter replied that this cost was
included in the schedule.
The meeting adjourned at 10:00 a.m.
