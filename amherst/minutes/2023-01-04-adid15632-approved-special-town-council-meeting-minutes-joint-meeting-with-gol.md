After confirming all participants could hear and be heard, Chair Miller declared the presence of a
quorum, called the meeting to order at 9:01 am, and announced audio and video recording.

recording.
2. Action Items
b. Town Manager Goals
Public comment:
Matt Holloway, Amherst resident and non-voting member of the Finance Committee,
spoke about concern regarding the declining birth rate, Town not being child and family
friendly, and expressed that the Town needs to be more accessible to families who wish
to move to Amherst. Holloway noted that the Economic Vitality and Housing
Affordability sections of the Town Manager Goals could be used to make
recommendations for ways that the Town can become more attractive to young families.
Holloway gave an example of creating more avenues to support first-time home buyers.
Darcy DuMont, Amherst resident, suggested a change in the language of objective 3 of
the Climate Action section of the Town Manager Goals. DuMont suggested that the

compost pick-up goal be that the Town Manager “start implementation” instead of the
goal currently being written as “meetings the goal of offering.” DuMont requested that
the hauler bylaw be passed. TI2.4 Lastly, DuMont encouraged the Town to make better

Demetria Shabazz, Amherst resident and member of the Community Safety and Social
Justice Committee, spoke about concern that the Community Safety Working Group
goals set forth by the GOL and the Town Council are not in alignment with the research
and recommendations made to the Council by the Community Safety Working Group.
Specifically, regarding goals involving Community Responders for Equity, Safety, and
Service and Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion. Shabazz suggested that the

the police.
Vira Cage, Amherst resident, spoke regarding objective 5 of the Personnel Goals section
of the Management Goals. Specifically, regarding Community Responders for Equity,
Safety, and Service, the Fire Department, and EMS. Cage commented that they would
like to see a fuller report about meetings that took place at police station with Town
Councilors regarding this issue.
Brianna Owens, Amherst resident, emphasized how important it is for the Amherst police
to create an anti-racist culture to better support the community.
Pat Ononibaku, Amherst resident, expressed concern about the time of this meeting not
being accessible to the public and some Town Councilors. Ononibaku stated that the
Community Safety and Social Justice Committee is aware that the Amherst police need
anti-racist training and that the police need training to properly protect the residents.
Phillip Avila, Amherst resident, Human Rights Commission member, and Committee on
Social Justice member, spoke about being concerned about systemic police biases. Avila
discussed a police interaction last week while on vacation in California. Avila adds that
this happens all the time to people of color. Avila stated that our Town and police
department should look at these goals as an opportunity to be a leader in anti-racist work
and show that they care about this issue. Avila stated being concerned as to why this
matter is being discussed in the GOL meeting when it was voted on in Town Council
meeting previously.
Chair Miller stated that there will be additional time to give public comment during the
Town Council Meeting Monday January 9, 2023.

Miller reminded the Council that items in debate at the last GOL meeting were pulled
out.
Councilors reviewed comments from the Town Manager regarding the goals.

The first comment from the Town Manager that is addressed is the question: “How do we
address the possibility that the funding needed to achieve the goals may not fit within the
budget guidelines?”
Rooney discussed opportunities for the Town Manager to discuss projecting out the cost
of implementing some of the goals. Rooney stated that although the goals are not fully
funded right now, cost estimates by the Town Manager should be presented to the
Council.
Taub added that the goals will help set the budget and stated that the goals make priorities
for the budget to be built around.
Hanneke suggested modifying the language to “including multiple year plans and will
inform the town council as to how the priorities can be implemented into the budget and
if they cannot fit in the guidelines, inform the council why it cannot and how it could be
modified to fit.”
Schoen stated that the language could be changed to having the goals reviewed after the
first year.
Steinberg agreed with the rewording of this section. Steinberg added that time related
priorities are needed so that goals are set appropriately. Steinberg suggested making the
language encourage this process.
Miller asked whether it was ever discussed if the goals should be longer than one year.
Adding that these goals are asking a lot of the Town Manager and that the budget is a
challenge in this timeline.
Hanneke replied that the Town Manager is evaluated on a yearly basis so two-year
performance goals would be a challenge and that two-year performance goals would add
additional complications in an election year. President Griesemer agreed with Hanneke.
Griesemer stated that the multi-year goals can be addressed by using the language of
progress versus achievement in the year.
Climate Action
Schoen commented that the climate action goals be specific goals that the Town Manager
can make progress on this year but are not necessary to achieve in a year. Schoen asked
whether the bike and pedestrian path street and safety belongs in the climate action
section. Schoen commented that the waste-hauler issue needs to be a multiyear process.
Taub requested language changes to Climate Action objective 2, following “complete
joint powers” insert “entity” and following “utilities” to insert “and start implementation
upon approval.” Taub requested language changes to Climate Action objective 3, after
“compost pick-up” to insert “local compost processing.” Taub requested language
changes to Climate Action objective 4, after “take action on the following” to insert “new
initiatives.”
Devlin Gauthier commented that the bike and pedestrian path street and safety objective
is a climate goal to promote bike and pedestrian travel. Devlin Gauthier commented that

the Town Manager may not having control over implementation so language regarding
implementation may not need to be included. That “start implementation” language is

Town Manager work and is covered under rental registration bylaw. Devlin Gauthier

Hanneke and Devlin Gauthier discussed the origin of the language in objective 4 a, b, and
c and whether it should reflect the Climate Action, Adoption, and Resiliency Plan
(CAARP) goals that the Town Manger and staff are currently making progress towards or
the Energy and Climate Action Committee (ECAC) recommendations. Devlin Gauthier
stated that the ECAC specifically researched how these goals will impact the Town of
Amherst 2030 emission goals and that by using the ECAC recommendations the Council
is using ECAC’s expertise and can measure progress towards the 2030 emission goals.
Devlin Gauthier stated that the ECAC goals are phrased differently than CAARP but do
align very well and that ECAC has some additions. Devlin Gauthier stated that the
completion of the solar bylaw and composting are the only things on CAARP that ECAC
does not have. Devlin Gauthier suggested that ECAC goals should be used with the solar
bylaw and composting added.
Steinberg, Taub, and Shoen discussed the feasibility of the goals. Steinberg brought up
concerns that the specifics of this discussion may make the Town Manager job difficult.
Steinberg said that there are various committee recommendations that need to be
discussed, specifically regarding the waste-hauler. Taub disagreed with Steinberg. Taub
clarified that the language of “progress” is used and that although achievement may not
be possible, working towards the goal is important. Steinberg said being too specific may
not be appropriate for the Council at this time with too much unknown. Schoen stated
that this is town manager performance goals, not bylaws so specifics are not necessary.
Shoen stated that Town Manager and staff need the wording to be what works for them
regarding implementation. Chair Miller says that she and Hanneke will check in with the
Town Manager before Monday January 9, 2023.
Devlin Gauthier stated that the language of the climate goals can set a standard of how
the Council writes other goals. Devlin Gauthier discussed that the council agreed to 2030
climate goals and specificity is necessary because that is 7 years from now.
De Angelis questioned if by accepting the ECAC list, does the council accept all the other
goal lists under each section for consistency.
Taub discussed that the waste hauler language is necessary because of the waste hauler
bylaw.

Hanneke discussed that other sections do not get this specific in describing the goals of
the bylaw, so including those goals in the waste hauler bylaw is not consist with all other
bylaws in the document, thus it should be deleted.
Miller discussed whether this language should be voted on. President Griesemer confirms

Griesemer asked whether the vote is made by GOL or the entire Council present. It is
decided that GOL would need to vote on the document.
ii. Community Health and Safety
Hanneke, Griesemer, and Devlin Gauthier discussed objective 1 of the Community
Health and Safety section, “continue to support the revision of the residential rental
bylaws.” Hanneke asked whether this objective, as well as all other mentions of the
bylaws in the goals, should be included.
iii. Economic vitality
Rooney previously emailed Miller, Griesemer, and Hanneke about adding a study and
recommendation to promote the Town becoming more child and family friendly. Miller
discussed putting this language in the Community Development section. Schoen asked
about adding the language to the Finance section.
Schoen said whether language could be added to reflect the grant supported Economic
Development that the Town Manager has accomplished and whether a grant funded
economic development staff would be possible. Devlin Gauthier stated that the Town
Manager uses grants well so additional language regarding grants would be unnecessary.
Devlin Gauthier suggested Economic Vitality objective 4 go in the Community
Engagement section.
Devlin Gauthier brought up whether putting language to make a family friendly town is
adding a value judgment of who should be in the Town.
Rooney stated that enhancing opportunities for children benefits everyone, so it is not
necessarily exclusionary.
iv. Housing Affordability
Schoen asked what is meant by “increase the stock of housing availability” and how the
Town Manager can achieve this since the Town Manager does not have control over
housing diversity and how this relates to affordability.
Miller confirms with Clerk of the Council O’Keeffe that it is okay if the Council does not
take up the sewer regulations and water and sewer bylaws today.

Hanneke stated that the diversity and affordability of housing aligns with the Council
goals and although some of this issue is addressed by the zoning bylaws, the Town staff
in the Planning Department can also work on these goals.
Hanneke discussed deleting the 5th objective because it has already been covered by
previous objectives.
v. Major capital building investments
Schoen commented on objective 2 regarding the Jones Library, stating that there should
language for a plan b, if necessary, because of uncertainty with financing in November.
Griesemer commented that the Town Manager and staff has discussed some plan b plans
so adding this language would be consistent.
vi. Racial Equity and Social Justice
Miller, Hanneke, De Angelis, and Walker discussed the language added in objective 4 of
the Racial Equity and Social Justice section after “and other options to” that reads “the
town council.”
De Angelis discussed that all Town staff should be involved in anti-racist work, including
the Town Council. De Angelis would like to add that starting with the police department
doing this work and include town staff and the Council.
Walker supported adding the Town Council. Walker brought up concern about the
language in objective 4 after “employees” reading “particularly the Public Safety
Departments (CRESS, Fire and EMS, and Police.” Walker brought concern that a motion
on November 14th, 2022, had passed for the police to have anti-racist work monitored and
that this language is a reframing of something that has previously passed. Walker
suggested including the language that has already passed on November 14th.
Miller stated that objective 4 is different than the personal management objectives that
include APD anti-racist trainings.
Walker asked whether the object 4 in racial equity should be consistent with Personnel
Management object 5.
Hanneke discussed that adding the November 14th language would not include the Town
Council in anti-racist training sine that motion was only regarding the APD.
Walker discussed that object 4 is inconsistent with what was passed through the Council
on November 14th and that including additional staff to the training, would not change
what was voted on November 14th. Walker discussed the need for ongoing work, not
adding trainings, to use and implement the trainings. Instead of adding this to all staff,
starting with where the biggest impact can be made, instead of overloading the Town
Manager.

Hanneke discussed that the motion on November 14th is focused only on APD, while
objective 5 is for all Town departments to foster a proactive anti-racist culture. Hanneke

Lopes commented that this is opportunity to expand the impact that the Council can make
in the towns departments. That entire departments to this day do not have people of color,
or directors of color. That if the Council has the opportunity to lead in ant-racist work,
why not expand the potential impact training could have on the entire community.
Miller stated that the rest of the performance goals cannot be addressed in the time
remaining.
Walker discussed that steps to foster the anti-racist environment must be established.
Walker stated that including people of color in a department is not anti-racist work.
Miller supports Walker, stating that using the November 15th motion in objective 5
would be strong.
Lopes stated that they do not believe editing objective 5 takes away from Walker’s
