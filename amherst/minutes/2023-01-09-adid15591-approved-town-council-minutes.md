4. General Public Comment
Edith Allison, Amherst resident, spoke about climate goals, encouraging local leadership to continue to
consider the community and engage with state leaders to enact change.
Allegra Clark, Amherst resident, offered support for the Town Manager goals around racial equity and
personnel management.
Lucy Robinson, Amherst resident, spoke about climate change issues for the sake of everyone, but
especially the youth of the town.
Felicia Mednick, Amherst resident, spoke about the Town Council climate goals and the positive
collaboration with other town committees. They also suggested several additions to Council members.
Darcy DuMont, Amherst resident, spoke about the climate action goals for the town.
Demetria Shabazz, Amherst resident, spoke about the Amherst Police Department making unbiased
choices and being anti-racist. Actions that the Town Council and Town Manager can take were
suggested.
Rani Parker, Amherst resident, spoke about the anti-racism training motion that was passed and support
for change in the world.
Brianna Owen, Amherst resident, spoke in support of the motion that was passed for the Amherst Police
Department to be more actively anti-racist.
John Root, Amherst resident, spoke about the town’s solid waste management plan, encouraging the
Town Council to include the zero waste Amherst waste reduction part of the Town Manager 2023 goals.

8. Action Items
b. Referral of Zoning Revisions Duplex to Community Resources Committee, Planning
Board, and Governance, Organization, and Legislation Committee
De Angelis spoke in favor of zoning revision that could expand housing opportunities for a wider group
of the community.
Pam spoke on the practicality of getting housing that people can afford.
Rooney reviewed the planning guidelines and asked if design guidelines should be addressed before any
zoning restrictions are lifted.
Taub questioned whether permits have not been granted through the special permitting process.
Schoen stated she has written comments she will send to the committee.
MOTION: Griesemer moved, second by Hanneke, to refer the proposed revisions to Zoning Bylaw
Section 3.32, Residential Uses, standards, and conditions for Two Family Detached Dwellings, Town
House, and Subdividable/Converted Dwellings, Zoning Bylaw Article 12, Definitions, and Zoning Bylaw
Sections 4.420, 4.4211, 4.320, 4.326, 4.34, 4.51, and 9.10 to the Planning Board and the Community
Resources Committee for public hearings to be held no later than April 1, 2023, and for a written
recommendation from the Planning Board to the Town Council and Community Resources Committee no
later than 21 days after the Planning Board hearing, and for the Community Resources Committee to send
a written recommendation to the Town Council and to submit all materials to the Governance,
Organization, and Legislation Committee for review of clarity, consistency, and actionability within 60
days of the hearing held by the Community Resources Committee.
VOTED unanimously, 13-0, by roll call, to refer the proposed revisions to Zoning Bylaw Section 3.32,
Residential Uses, standards, and conditions for Two Family Detached Dwellings, Town House, and
Subdividable/Converted Dwellings, Zoning Bylaw Article 12, Definitions, and Zoning Bylaw Sections
4.420, 4.4211, 4.320, 4.326, 4.34, 4.51, and 9.10 to the Planning Board and the Community Resources
Committee for public hearings to be held no later than April 1, 2023, and for a written recommendation
from the Planning Board to the Town Council and Community Resources Committee no later than 21
days after the Planning Board hearing, and for the Community Resources Committee to send a written
recommendation to the Town Council and to submit all materials to the Governance, Organization, and
Legislation Committee for review of clarity, consistency, and actionability within 60 days of the hearing
held by the Community Resources Committee.

MOTION: Griesemer moved, seconded by De Angelis, to authorize KP Law to respond to the Open
Meeting Law complaint filed by Allegra Clark dated December 20, 2022 on behalf of the Town Council,
consistent with the Council’s discussion on January 9, 2023.
The Town Council can determine whether they violated the open meeting law and provide supporting
documentation in support of that determination.
Miller made two corrections to the wording in the memo that will be forwarded for inclusion in the
memo. That request will be sent, along with the meeting recording and minutes to KP Law.
Schoen provided some context for the meeting referenced in the Open Meeting Law Complaint.
Steinberg and Hanneke agreed. Hanneke suggested a clarification be added to the memo regarding the
motion. Pam agreed that, according to the Open Meeting Law, there was not a violation, but there was
something that happened at that meeting based on the reports, that clearly upset town residents and should
be addressed by Town Council.
Walker and Miller both asked for the opportunity to review the public records as they were not present at
either of the meetings in question. Both felt that the Town Council had not been given adequate
opportunity to research the meeting documents to respond to the Open Meeting Law Complaint.
Several Council members commented on the continued need to hold space consistently and that the
Council continue to be transparent in an effort to build trust.
Hanneke offered an amendment to include ‘that the Town Council did not violate the Open Meeting
Law.’ To the end of the motion. De Angelis agreed.
MOTION: Hanneke moved, second by Steinberg, to call the previous question.
VOTED 9-3, with 1 abstention by roll call (De Angelis, Devlin Gauthier, Griesemer, Hanneke, Lopes,
Rooney, Schoen, Steinberg, Taub voted Yea; Bahl-Milne, Miller, and Walker voted Nay; Pam abstained)
to call the previous question.
VOTED 9-0, with 4 abstentions, by roll call (Bahl-Milne, De Angelis, Devlin Gauthier, Griesemer,
Hanneke, Lopes, Schoen, Steinberg, and Taub voted Yea; Miller, Pam, Rooney, and Walker abstained), to
authorize KP Law to respond to the Open Meeting Law complaint filed by Allegra Clark dated December
20, 2022 on behalf of the Town Council, consistent with the Council’s discussion on January 9, 2023, that
the Town Council did not violate Open Meeting Law.
