Rooney asked under part B of the bylaw, Definitions, about what constitutes a group home and
whether fraternities or Mercy House count as group homes.
Jon Thompson responded that neither of those examples counts as a group home and that Mercy
House functions as a regular rental.
Hanneke mentioned that Bahl-Milne had a few points she wanted to share about part E of the
bylaw, Exemptions. Bahl-Milne also mentioned that owner-occupied rental homes should perhaps
be exempt from permitting fees because they tend to be in good condition. Taub concurred with
Bahl-Milne’s suggestion, adding that all rental properties should be required to get a permit but
that owner-occupied rentals should not be charged a fee.
Hanneke noted that many points about inspections were moved from the bylaws to regulations.
Hanneke also mentioned that the Nuisance House bylaw should be to the committee by the end of
the month.
Thompson mentioned that he thinks adding a point system to the nuisance house bylaw is a good
idea.
Hanneke asked the committee to think about other things related to the bylaw they’d like to
discuss at the next meeting.
Rooney recommended that the committee and other meeting participants also revisit the
regulations for future meetings.
Hanneke then redirected the committee’s attention to the draft fee schedule. Rooney commented
that it would be preferable to keep the fees as low as possible for the convenience of rental
properties.
Hanneke mentioned that fee splitting options for particular circumstances, including properties
with multiple units, are still being devised. She then presented several different options for these
circumstances and presented a review of how she arrived at each fee number. The numbers were
informed by inspection and staffing costs.
Rooney asked about the number of applications processed per year and estimated that it would be
well over five thousand. She also asked Morra for a list of the known rental property addresses
broken down by street address so that the committee can better understand the distribution of the
properties.
Taub expressed that she preferred options two and four because she supports an owner-occupied
fee exemption.
Morra stated that owner-occupied rental properties can still have issues and that there therefore
may be issues with fee exemptions for owner-occupied units. Taub clarified that she believes no
one, including owner-occupied rentals, should be exempted from permitting.
Thompson asked about how inspections of owner-occupied units will be paid for if they are
exempt from fees. He also asked about how to robustly verify that units are truly owner-occupied.

De Angelis noted that, considering Thompson’s comments, it might make the most sense to
charge fees in all situations, including when units are owner-occupied. She also mentioned that
making fees universal would make the process more equitable.
Hanneke mentioned that options three and four had the hardest time reaching the minimum
required fee amounts. She previously favored having the permit fee not include the inspection as
a way to reward units that don’t need annual inspections, but she now favors options one and two,
which include the inspection. Hanneke also asked Thompson and Morra which option they think
would be easiest to administer.
Rooney asked how many inspections per year each inspector conducts. She noted that she
supports separating inspections from permitting fees because inspections of multi-unit properties
are much more demanding than inspections of single-family properties. She also asked whether a
complaint inspection should be treated differently from a regular inspection and if there is a cost
difference between normal inspections, code inspections, and complaint inspections.
Thompson answered that complaints are sorted by code violation and that there is no difference in
the inspection or enforcement; it’s more a matter of which “bucket” they end up in.
Morra clarified that the number of complaint inspections does not include minor and quickly
corrected infractions. He also enumerated the many differences between a rental inspection and
other types of inspections, such as roof replacement inspections. He made these comments to
better contextualize the inspection data seen by the committee, especially the total number of
inspections.
Taub asked how many inspectors would be needed ideally. Morra answered that they need a lead
inspector and two additional code enforcement officers.
Taub added that owner-occupied units definitely need a permit and that she agrees with
Thompson that owner occupancy needs to be verified before fee exceptions are considered.

7. Discussion regarding assessment of residential rental properties with Principal
Assessor, Finance Director, and Town Manager (To Begin at 5:30 pm – Joint with
Finance Committee and Town Council)
Steinberg called the Finance Committee meeting to order, confirmed members could hear and be
heard, and announced audio and video recording.
Griesemer called the Town Council meeting to order, confirmed members could hear and be
heard, and announced audio and video recording.
Mew gave a presentation about rental property assessment, how assessment is determined,
assessment methods, and residential parcel data.
Mangano noted that choosing one assessment method over another would not change the overall
tax levy.
De Angelis asked if a building with one residential floor and one commercial floor is considered
residential or commercial. Mew answered that such a building would be designated as mixed-use
and would be assessed with the income and expense method.
Schoen asked if there would be separate tax bills if the ownership of a mixed-use building were
different on different floors. Mew answered that such units would likely be valued using the sales
method. Schoen asked if vacant commercial or residential spaces are valued based on potential
income. Mew answered that vacancy does not necessarily affect assessment unless it is long-term.
Rooney asked about the 5% vacancy rate and estimated that the vacancy rate in Amherst is much
lower. She also asked if the cap rate of 9% is an average industry standard. Mew clarified that the
9% rate is unique to Amherst. Rooney asked how the cap rate is applied. Mew and Mangano
answered that the cap rate helps determine taxable value per property. Mangano also noted that
the cap rate is monitored for continuing accuracy.
Hanneke asked about the different taxation rates for the different floors or units of mixed-used
buildings and whether the different portions of such properties have different cap rates and
assessment methods. Mew answered that the different portions are assessed separately.
Griesemer asked about how individual condos are valued within a condo community or
association. She also asked if the data on apartment buildings includes condos. Mew answered
that condos are in a data category separate from apartment complexes. She also clarified that
condo valuation is based on the interior of each individual condo unless otherwise specified in the
condo’s master deed. Griesemer asked if common features at a condo association like pools or
common rooms influence how condos are taxed. Mew answered that such amenities would
influence the value of each individual unit but are not assessed separately from the condos
themselves.

Taub asked if a rental townhouse development is considered an apartment. Mew answered that it
would depend on how many rental units are within each building. Taub asked about the
significance of a vacancy rate lower than 5% and asked if that rate is monitored. Mew answered
that it is monitored and that 5% was just an example rate for demonstration purposes.
Rooney asked if the assessment methods in a mixed-use building reflect the different vacancy or
occupancy rates in each building portion. Mew answered that the different portions of mixed-use
buildings are assessed separately. Mew also noted that additional research is done in cases of
extended vacancies.
Schoen clarified the relationship between the vacancy rate and the cap rate. She also asked about
housing cooperatives and whether they would be in a different assessment category. Mew
answered that cooperatives would be assessed like apartment complexes because one tax bill is
generated.
Steinberg asked for confirmation that single-family homes rented to multiple tenants are assessed
with the market approach. Mew confirmed this. Steinberg also asked about the relationship
between assessment methods and overall tax revenue. Mangano answered that using the income
assessment method would not necessarily result in better taxation outcomes for renters.
Hanneke asked about income verification for multi-person rental households. Mew answered than
income reporting in such cases is an “honor system” but that outliers are investigated more
thoroughly.
