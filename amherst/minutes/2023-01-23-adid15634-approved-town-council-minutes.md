Rooney asked why the pole was needed and why the loop was created for this building.
Martin confirmed that it has to do with the size of the building being constructed.
Hanneke asked why lines are not being built underground and how far away from the water
main are poles being installed. Martin confirmed that the town has final decision on whether
new lines are above or below ground. The location of poles can be adjusted in consideration
of the water main.
Devlin Gauthier asked who pays for the costs associated with underground line placement.
Pam supported Hanneke’s statement about building the lines underground and the aesthetics
of poles versus underground utility placement.
Bockelman confirmed it’s fine for the lines to go underground as long as the road is re-paved
to DPW specifications. The building customer would pay all associated costs if lines were
built underground. Eversource will be able to determine where water lines are and work
around them if there is underground line placement.
There were no additional questions from the general public.

b. Proposed Special Act on Residential Property Transfer Fees
MOTION Griesemer moved, second by Devlin Gauthier, approve the proposed Special Act set forth
below, “An Act establishing a real estate transfer fee upon the transfer of property in the City Known as
the Town of Amherst,” and to petition the General Court for special legislation, provided, however, that
the General Court may make clerical or editorial changes of form only to the bill unless the Town Council
approves amendments to the bill before enactment by the General Court.
An Act establishing a real estate transfer fee upon the transfer of property in the City
Known as the Town of Amherst
An Act establishing a real estate transfer fee upon the transfer of property in the City Known as
the Town of Amherst.
Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives in General Court assembled, and by the
authority of the same, as follows:
SECTION 1. Except where otherwise exempted pursuant to this act, the City known as the Town
of Amherst may, by bylaw, impose a fee of up to two per cent (2%) of the purchase price upon
the transfer of (i) any real property interest in the City known as the Town of Amherst or (ii) a
controlling interest in a trust, limited liability company, or other entity that directly or indirectly
holds an interest in any class of residential real property situated in the City known as the Town
of Amherst. The City known as the Town of Amherst may define by bylaw what constitutes a
controlling interest and the calculation of the fee.
SECTION 2. The following transfers of real property interests shall be exempt from the
fee established in Section 1: (i) transfers to or from the federal government, the Commonwealth,
the City known as the Town of Amherst, and any of their instrumentalities, agencies, or
subdivisions, including the Amherst Housing Authority; (ii) transfers of real property subject to
an affordable housing restriction; (iii) transfers made without additional consideration to confirm,
correct, modify or supplement a transfer previously made; (iv) transfers with consideration under
$100.00; and (v) transfers between family members, as may be defined by bylaw.

Except as otherwise provided, the purchaser shall have the burden of proof that any transfer is
exempt under this section and any otherwise exempt transfer shall not be exempt in the event that
such transfer (by itself or as part of a series of transfers) was made for the primary purpose of
evading the fee imposed by Section 1.
The Town may not, by bylaw or otherwise, eliminate or reduce any exemption set forth in this
act.
SECTION 3. The fee shall be paid to the Town of Amherst. The Town shall deposit all fees
received hereunder with the Town treasurer/collector. The treasurer/collector shall deposit the
first $250,000 collected in each fiscal year in the Amherst Municipal Affordable Housing Trust
Fund established pursuant to section 55C of chapter 44 of the General Laws. The remaining funds
collected each fiscal year shall be deposited in the Town of Amherst ’s capital stabilization fund,
the Town of Amherst’s general fund, and/or the Amherst Municipal Affordable Housing Trust
Fund, as determined by bylaw.
The Town of Amherst shall have such remedies to collect the fee as provided by law with respect
to the collection of real property taxes. The Town of Amherst may, by bylaw, adopt additional
requirements, exemptions, and regulations to implement or enforce said fee, consistent with this
act.
SECTION 4. The Town of Amherst may, by bylaw, adopt additional requirements, exemptions,
and regulations to implement or enforce said fee, consistent with this act.
SECTION 5. A copy of the deed or other instrument evidencing such transfer shall be provided to
the Town of Amherst and shall be accompanied by: (i) an affidavit signed under oath or under the
pains and penalties of perjury by the purchaser and seller attesting to the purchase price; (ii) the
applicable fee owed or, if applicable, an affidavit of intent to seek one of the permissible
exemptions, as described in section 2, for that property by the purchaser; and (iii) the basis, if
any, upon which the transfer is claimed to be exempt in whole or in part from said fee. Upon
receipt of the transfer fee or satisfactory evidence of exemption, the Town of Amherst or its
designee shall promptly thereafter issue a certificate indicating that the fee has been paid or that
the transfer is exempt from the fee. The Hampshire Registrar of Deeds shall not record or register
a deed unless the deed is accompanied by such certificate.
SECTION 6. The provisions of this act are severable, and if any provision hereof, including
without limitation any exemption from the fee imposed hereby, shall be held invalid in any
circumstances such invalidity shall not affect any other provisions or circumstances. This act shall
be construed in all respects so as to meet all constitutional requirements. In carrying out the
purposes and provisions of this act, all steps shall be taken which are necessary to meet
constitutional requirements whether or not such steps are required by statute.
SECTION 7. This act shall take effect on passage.
VOTED unanimously, 13-0, by roll call, approve the proposed Special Act set forth below, “An Act
establishing a real estate transfer fee upon the transfer of property in the City Known as the Town of
Amherst,” and to petition the General Court for special legislation, provided, however, that the General

e. Authorization if Town Manager to Enter Intergovernmental Agreement with UMass Donahue
Institute for Program Evaluation Services
Griesemer recused herself from the agenda item. Devlin Gauthier stepped in as President for the duration
of the agenda item.
Lopes left the meeting at 7:50 p.m.
MOTION by Devlin Gauthier, seconded by Hanneke, In accordance with MGL C.40 s. 4A which allows
for joint operation of public activities among governmental units, which requires that such inter-

governmental agreements be approved by the Town Council and negotiated and signed by the Town
Manager, the Town Council hereby authorizes the Town Manager to enter into an agreement with the
University of Massachusetts Donahue Institute (UMDI) for consulting services for the Town related to the
evaluation of the Community Responders for Equity, Safety, and Service (CRESS) Department.
Schoen asked about the content of what the Donahue Institute will do as they design their evaluation.
Devlin Gauthier reminded the Councilors that the item is only to authorized the Town Manager to enter
into the agreement. Bockelman confirmed that the Department of Public Health grants require this
evaluation.
Rooney asked if there was a fixed price for the agreement. Mangano said that the details of the payment
structure are still being worked out.
Bahl-Milne asked why the Donahue Institute was chosen over other known organizations that do this
work. Walker asked if other organizations were considered, specifically LEAP. Mangano confirmed that
LEAP voluntarily stepped away at the time CRESS was established, and timing is also a consideration.
Mangano indicated that because LEAP was part of the establishment of CRESS that the Council might
prefer an outside review. Bockelman stated that the Donahue Institute is very good at program evaluation
which is one of the primary reasons they were chosen for this work. Walker restated that her preference
would be to have LEAP, who understand CRESS as developed, would be a better choice.
VOTED 9-1, with 1 abstention (Miller) and 2 absent (Griesemer and Lopes), by roll call, In accordance
with MGL C.40 s. 4A which allows for joint operation of public activities among governmental units,
which requires that such inter-governmental agreements be approved by the Town Council and negotiated
and signed by the Town Manager, the Town Council hereby authorizes the Town Manager to enter into
an agreement with the University of Massachusetts Donahue Institute (UMDI) for consulting services for
the Town related to the evaluation of the Community Responders for Equity, Safety, and Service
(CRESS) Department.
Griesemer returned to the meeting.
