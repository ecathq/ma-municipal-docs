8. Action Items
a. Eversource Petition for Pole Placement at 133 South East Street
Petition withdrawn; no action.
b. Centennial Funding Supplemental Budget Request
Mooring spoke about the cost estimate the Town received for the project, which was higher than the
initial appropriation.
Mangano spoke about the request to rescind the previous $18,000,000 borrowing authorization and
authorize a new borrowing authorization of $21,500,000.
Pam asked if the cost might go up again.
Mooring spoke about the financial contingency of 5%, and stated we do not expect the cost to increase.
Hanneke spoke in appreciation of the Town utilizing the State Revolving Fund; the Town will be able to
obtain a loan at 1.5% interest rate and receive 19.8% in loan forgiveness.
Walker asked about climate impacts the Town has considered when planning the project and delays.
Mooring spoke about energy usage efficiencies and materials delays.
Steinberg asked how much the increase cost has impacted the rate increase predicted in the memo.
Mangano spoke about the increase in debt authorization and the rate increases referenced in the chart
included in the memo.
Councilors discussed the projected rate increases in FY26-28.
Griesemer asked about the impact on the water and sewer rates with the planned water and sewer
regulations.
Mangano spoke about rate increases due to the project cost and due to decreased consumption since 2020
when consumption fell due to the pandemic.

c. Community Preservation Act Committee Recommendation
Mangano spoke about the Community Preservation Act Committee’s recommendations for projects for
FY24.
Hanneke asked about the repurposed debt for Kendrick Park.
Mangano stated the debt can be used for a like purpose.
Miller asked about projects identified for borrowing and how it is determined which projects would be

how the CPA committee manages borrowing, debt, and debt payments in terms of available CPA funds.
The item was automatically referred to Finance Committee.
d. Elementary School Building Project
1) Debt Authorization
2) Debt Exclusion: Ballot Language and Impact on Taxpayers
Bockelman spoke about the project and next steps for the Town Council.
Mangano spoke about the following:
 Current cost estimates
 Elementary School Building Committee approved cost saving measures to reduce the cost
 Town advocated for higher reimbursement levels from MSBA
 Town preparing for three votes: special election for debt exclusion, debt authorization, and
approval of debt exclusion
 Details of the project cost summary
 Funding plan
 Explanation of how debt exclusions work
 Draft language for the debt exclusion ballot
 Draft language for the debt authorization Town Council financial order
 Estimated tax impacts
 Finance Committee review of financing
 Updates to financing plan as market conditions change and information about the project
becomes available
Pam asked about impacts to elderly residents with fixed income.
Mangano spoke about programs to help offset taxes for elderly residents; information is available in the
Assessor’s office.
Miller stated the Town should try to reduce the burden on taxpayers as much as possible by using reserve
funds.
Mangano spoke about managing reserves in terms of all four building projects.
Bahl-Milne commented that the ballot question language is not easily understandable, and asked if more
plain language can be added to the ballot.

e. Non-Citizen Voting Special Legislation
Sponsor De Angelis spoke about the proposed special legislation and about lawful residents as a
disenfranchised group who work, own property, send their children to public schools, and pay taxes in
Amherst.
Sponsor Bahl-Milne spoke about the lengthy and costly process to become a citizen and those who lose
out on the opportunity to vote and achieve a sense of belonging where they live.
Devlin Gauthier asked why the language differs from similar special acts in other municipalities.
Councilors discussed special acts which have been approved by Town Meeting but have failed to be
approved by the legislature.

legislators at monthly meetings with the President and Town Manager.

in the report.
Bahl-Milne commented that the issue of PFAS legislation was not surprising because the Council was

Griesemer’s notes regarding her meeting with Senator Comerford, and that the details in the notes were
not included in the President’s Report.

or not, and that it is vital to trust our leaders to have some discretion about what information is shared in
public.
Miller expressed deep concern, stating the President should not have proprietary information that is not
ready to be public yet.
Walker commented that she feels she has been excluded from information.
