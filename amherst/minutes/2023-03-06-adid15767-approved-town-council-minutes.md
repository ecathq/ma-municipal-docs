Comerford and Domb invited and encouraged the Councilors to communicate their legislative goals and
concerns on behalf of the Town. Domb and Comerford then reviewed the structure and two-year cycle of
the legislative session and the State budget process and its yearlong cycle.
Domb next presented on the schedule and process of Home Rule Petitions and shared that Amherst
currently has two Home Rules filed: HD2929 and HD4164. She also mentioned that she is expecting a
third Home Rule about non-citizen voting in Town elections. Domb emphasized that knowing the Town’s
goals and priorities helps she and Comerford to carry out their roles in the House and Senate.
Domb and Comerford then presented on the following points:
 Amherst’s current and pending Home Rule Petitions
 Their respective committee assignments
 Their respective bills by category
 Their shared advocacy for increased library and school funding
 Regional rail expansion and the upcoming Rail Commission hearing on March 21, 2023
 An upcoming solar siting forum in collaboration with UMass
 The healthcare workforce pipeline
 Local investment updates
Councilor Miller left the meeting at 7:36.
Pam asked for an update on the death with dignity bill. Comerford answered that she has introduced it
again and that it will likely go to the Public Health Committee. She has asked for the bill to be heard early
in the legislative session.
Schoen asked for an update on charter school funding formula and on the Fair Share Amendment’s
impacts on educational and transportation funding. She specifically asked if the Fair Share Amendment
will help to fund the construction of roads. Schoen also asked about incentives for solar power
installation. Domb encouraged the Councilors to enumerate their wishes about the allocation of Fair Share
Amendment funds in a statement at an upcoming hearing. Comerford echoed this suggestion, adding that
Governor Healy has already allocated more funding for rural schools in Massachusetts.
Steinberg thanked Domb and Comerford for their presence and their presentation. He mentioned that the
Town has been significantly impacted by inflation and that inflation will make budgeting difficult. He
advocated the need for a balance between high-need school funding and funding for other Town services.
Steinberg also mentioned that Amherst voters showed great support for the Fair Share Amendment and
echoed Schoen’s questions about how the allocation of Fair Share Amendment funds will benefit the
Town. Comerford answered that there are many funding sources to focus on in addition to the Fair Share
Amendment. Domb thanked Steinberg for his participation in the Massachusetts Municipal Association.
She also encouraged the Councilors to advocate for increased road funding at the upcoming Chapter 90
hearing.
Hanneke echoed Schoen and Steinberg’s concerns about the allocation of Fair Share Amendment funds.
She noted that each year the Town increases its investment in public K-12 education to account for the
relative absence of increased state spending on education. Comerford affirmed and acknowledged
Hanneke’s concerns. Domb mentioned that the Governor’s budgetary allocations for education and
transportation are yet to be seen.

Carol Gray, Amherst resident, concurred with Demling and opined that teachers deserve a pay raise. She

5.2.

amendments to Rule 3.2, 5.1, and 5.2.

be reasonable to ask the Town Clerk to post written public comments by noon on the day of the meeting.
She also expressed discomfort with the thirty-minute cap on public comment, stating that the Council’s
job is to listen to their constituents.
Steinberg also expressed discomfort with the thirty-minute cap on public comment. He asked about the
status of his proposal on the introduction of bylaws and other legislative processes and encouraged the
Council to consider how to use time efficiently.

setting a meeting end time goal at 10 PM.

1) Council Discussion
O’Keeffe shared a presentation about the continuation or cessation of remote participation at public
meetings. She stated that, after March 31, 2023, public bodies should prepare to meet in person but also
voiced a commitment to maintaining hybrid meetings if the Council desires that format.
Griesemer voiced support for continuing to hold hybrid meetings, citing public comments in support of
ongoing Zoom access.
Pam expressed concern about the level of detail visible on the Zoom video feed of the Town Room during
Council meetings.
Bockelman emphasized that, if the State legislature does not act by March 31, 2023, all public meetings
will have to take place in person.
Griesemer asked if virtual public comment can continue to happen if the State legislature does not act on
this issue by March 31, 2023. O’Keeffe confirmed that virtual public access can continue.
Rooney asked what the consequences of continuing to hold virtual committee meetings would be after
March 31, 2023. Bockelman and Griesemer answered that those meetings would be in violation of open
meeting law.
Hanneke voiced support for continuing to hold hybrid Council meetings. She also stated that, as a
committee chair, she would like to be able to access the screen if committee meetings were held in
person. She also asked how each committee members’ remote or in-person participation would be
determined if committee meetings can no longer be held virtually after March 23, 2023.

11. Town Manager Report
Bockelman announced a March 24, 2023 event at which the public can discuss the new elementary school
