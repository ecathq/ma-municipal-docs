8. Action Items
a. Appropriation and Debt Authorization for the Elementary School Building Project
Sean Mangano went over the details of the Elementary School Construction Debt Authorization.
Griesemer mentioned that the Finance Committee will discuss the debt authorization at length during their
meeting tomorrow.
Hanneke expressed concern over not seeing repayment to Capital Stabilization mentioned in the order of
appropriations. Mangano answered that at the end of the year, all surplus is allocated into several buckets,
including Capital Stabilization.
Rooney mentioned that the possibility of using reserve funds was discussed at the last finance committee
meeting.
Pam mentioned comments from residents expressing concern about raised taxes. She advocated for a
solution that will minimize the burden on resident taxpayers.
Schoen said that the Town could make up some of its spending via rebates and grants for renewable
energy.
Walker echoed Rooney’s remarks on using reserves and requested a council conversation on the matter.
Bahl-Milne asked about the average impact on individual homeowners that using $10 million in reserves
would have. She also asked how using $10 million in reserves would impact other building projects and
debt limits.
Taub asked about the individual tax increase that would come with the debt exclusion authorization. She
also asked about the potential process for approving the use of reserves.

Hanneke expressed concerns about using $15 million from the reserves, especially as the capital reserves
were intended to be used to build a new fire station. She also expressed reservations about potentially
having to ask for a second debt exclusion authorization for the fire station.
Lopes asked Mangano about how using reserves would save money for the taxpayers.
Walker clarified that her motion proposed using $10 million in reserves and that Schoen’s motion
proposed using $5 million in reserves. The Finance Committee recommended Schoen’s motion. Walker
asked about making a motion to use an additional $5 million in reserves.
Rooney reminded the committee about the recent establishment of the Capital Stabilization Fund. She
spoke in favor of using some reserves to lessen the burden on taxpayers.
De Angelis expressed concern that there do not seem to be reserves specifically for the Department of
Public Works.
Steinberg reviewed the four funds into which reserves are sorted. He also spoke of the importance of the
general stabilization fund in times of crisis, like during the 2008 recession.
Miller asked how the reserves get replenished and how the funds are built. Mangano explained that the
reserves are replenished by end-of-year budget surpluses. Mangano also explained that 10.5% of the
current budget is allocated for capital.
Miller asked about a timeline for the fire station and DPW projects. Mangano answered that it will likely
be five to seven years until the fire station project begins. He also mentioned that a site for the DPW
project is still being selected.
Devlin Gauthier asked Mangano about how using reserves for the elementary school would impact the
funding needs of the other building projects, such as the fire station and DPW.
Taub expressed surprise at the long timelines for the fire station and DPW projects and asked about how
the reserves could be rebuilt if they were used for the elementary school.
Griesemer clarified that the Town expects to spend $20 million for the new fire station.
Walker explained why she proposes using $10 million in reserves for the elementary school.
Steinberg explained that when the building projects were conceived, the Town planned that they would all
be partially funded by debt. Steinberg also discussed how inflation impacts construction costs and stated
that the building project costs will continue to increase.
Griesemer proposed, based on Schoen’s recommendations, that the Finance Committee should hold a
meeting entirely dedicated to the matter of the elementary school project funding.
Hanneke suggested using some of the upcoming retreat to discuss the elementary school project funding.
Pam asked if this matter should be discussed further at a meeting that is likely to have higher participation
from the public.
De Angelis spoke in support of a Council meeting dedicated to the elementary school project funding on
March 27th.
Mangano stated that the best thing for the other building projects is for the school building project to
move forward.

b. Proposed Water and Sewer Bylaws – First Reading
Devlin Gauthier summarized the proposed water and sewer bylaws.
De Angelis reported on the Governance, Organization, and Legislation Committee review of the bylaws.
Hanneke asked if the Town will have to pay for the publication of the rate sheet each year as the rates are
updated. Rusiecki confirmed that only notice of sewer bylaw changes must be published; water bylaw
changes are not subject to this requirement.
Rooney asked if rates would still need to be published if they were set by the Town Manager instead of
the Council. Griesemer replied that the Council are the Town’s water and sewer commissioners and
therefore set the rates.
c. Proposed Water and Sewer Regulations – First Reading
