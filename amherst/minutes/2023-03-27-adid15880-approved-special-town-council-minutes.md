1. Call to Order
Griesemer declared the presence of a quorum, called the meeting of the Town Council to order at 6:33
and announced audio and video recording. She then verified that all council members present can hear
and be heard and reviewed the meeting agenda.
Steinberg declared the presence of a quorum, called the meeting of the Finance Committee to order at
6:34 and announced audio and video recording.
2. Announcements – None
3. Presentations and Discussion
Griesemer reviewed the meeting agenda and purpose. Walker asked to start with public comment and to
prioritize committee discussion. Griesemer explained that the scheduled presentation would contain new
information that would be important for the Councilors and the public to know to have an informed
discussion. Walker expressed that she would like to retain the ability of Councilors to make a motion at
any time.

2) Council and Finance Committee Discussion
Dorothy Pam mentioned that a financial gift of $20 million from Amherst College to the Town would
help to reduce the impact of the building projects on resident taxpayers.
Hegner asked about the capital stabilization fund replenishment timeline and whether Mangano’s
projections accounted for inflation.
Schoen inquired about the DPW project plan timeline. Mangano answered that the current plan is to begin
the DPW project once the Town has more flexibility in its capital budget. He estimated that the DPW
project would take place within five to ten years.
Rooney asked about the debt amounts that would result from the four planned building projects. Mangano
answered that the current plan is to build up the reserves for the eventual construction of the fire station.
Pam asked if the library building project would be financed with reserves. Mangano answered that the
library project would be financed through the capital budget.
Michele Miller asked where the cost assumptions for the fire station and DPW projects came from.
Mangano answered that the assumptions came from examinations of other similar building projects
throughout Massachusetts and the square footage of those projects.
Miller asked who would be responsible for the cost of the library repairs. Mangano answered that there is
a memorandum of understanding with the library that would split the cost between the trustees and the
capital budget.
Walker asked about the decision to use the reserves for the fire station building project. Mangano
answered that no decision has been made yet and that the decision will ultimately rest with the Council.
Griesemer asked if there are funds set aside for fire station and DPW repairs. Mangano answered that
there are funds set aside for emergency repairs and that planned repairs would have to go through the
capital planning process.
Hanneke asked about the estimated cost of the library repairs and asked Mangano to confirm that the
projected cost in the presentation does not take increased building costs into account. Mangano confirmed
this and mentioned that a more detailed cost estimate for that project is forthcoming.
De Angelis asked Mangano to confirm how much taxpayers would save if $5 million or $10 million in
reserves were applied to the elementary school building project.
Bahl-Milne thanked Mangano for sharing the Town’s efforts to increase affordable housing stock. She
asked if the tax exemptions Mangano shared would apply only to homeowners or if they would also apply
to renters. Mangano answered that the property tax exemptions would apply to homeowners.
Bahl-Milne asked for further information about a Finance Committee member’s statement about the
increased costs of using reserves. Steinberg explained that the statement intended to convey that every
potential financing option would also have consequences. Steinberg also confirmed that the Finance
Committee has not yet taken a position on the financing options for the fire station.
Walker asked if the affordable housing options Mangano shared include any prioritization for Amherst
residents. Griesemer answered that the Council has expressed a preference for the prioritization of
Amherst residents. Mangano added that prioritizing current residents could also limit access for others
who need affordable housing.

Walker expressed that she supports using $10 million in reserves for the elementary school building
project because she wants to reduce the impact on resident taxpayers. She also asked for a clearer
definition of “affordable” as it was used in the presentation. Mangano answered that affordability was
determined based partially on percentages of income used for rent. Walker shared her understanding of
affordable housing versus subsidized housing. Mangano offered to share a more detailed breakdown of
which units are deemed affordable and which units are subsidized.
Walker asked if there are limits to how much aid a family can receive under the Resident Emergency Aid
Limit. Mangano shared that there is a limit of one ARPA application per family. He also shared that this
program would likely expire before the impacts of the debt exclusion would manifest.
Hanneke asked if the savings for low-income residents would be significant enough to justify the use of
$10 million in reserves. She also asked if the impact on taxpayers could be lessened early if another
source of funding, such as a gift from a university, became available. Mangano answered affirmatively.
Steinberg mentioned that the Town’s elder population has urged the Council to consider their situation
when deciding how to finance the building projects, especially considering that many of them live on
fixed incomes. He also mentioned that the elder population relies heavily on the services that the DPW
and fire department provide.
Walker urged the Council to move away from the idea that renters exclusively live in apartment
complexes. She also mentioned that the real impact of tax increases on renters will rely largely on the
discretion of landlords.
Lopes acknowledged the school shooting in Tennessee and those who lost their lives. She also urged the
Council to continually consider how their decisions on this matter will affect the most vulnerable
residents.
3) Public Comment
Carol Gray spoke about Amherst teachers currently in contract negotiations and on work-to-rule; Gray
spoke about Amherst’s tax rates in the state, and advocated for putting the four building projects to the
votes for debt exclusions.
Vince O’Connor stated that he plans to vote for the debt exclusion despite the tax increases. He urged the
Council to follow the example of the mayor of Boston to find ways to reduce rent costs in the Town. He
also advocated for increasing the school budget.
Jeff Lee commented that currently lives on a fixed income and that he is unsure about supporting the debt
exclusion because he fears how property tax increases will affect him. He also urged the Council to
reduce the debt exclusion by choosing not to go forward with the library building project.
Lauren Mills commented that she thinks the cost of building the new elementary school is too high. She
mentioned that she has children who are older and will not attend the new school. She also expressed that
she is generally frustrated with the Town’s school system.
Maria Kopicki urged the Council to focus the rest of their discussion on whether they will use $10 million
in reserves for the elementary school project. She expressed support for using the $10 million in reserves
on the elementary school project. She also urged the Council to remember that their primary duty is to
their constituents.
4) Continued Discussion
Miller asked Griesemer to outline the how the meeting will proceed. Griesemer mentioned that the
Finance Committee will meet tomorrow to discuss their recommendation to the Council and that the
purpose of the current meeting is to provide more information to the Finance Committee. She also
mentioned when early voting and regular polling on this matter will occur.
Bahl-Milne asked about the future financial consequences of using the reserves on the elementary school
project. Griesemer answered that the longer the project is postponed the more it will cost.

Pam expressed that voter turnout will be very important to the matter at hand and that the Council has a
duty to make the elementary school building project as affordable as possible for the Town residents.
Walker shared that the future financial consequences of using the reserves are uncertain. She urged the
Council to consider that a project delay may not produce exclusively negative outcomes.
Taub asked if there is currently a motion on the table regarding the matter at hand. Griesemer answered
that there will be a motion on the table at the April 3rd meeting.
Mangano mentioned that the Finance Committee would need to make a new recommendation for debt
authorization if they decide to do so.
Rooney urged that the Finance Committee to recommend using the $10 million in reserves for the
elementary school building project. She referenced the public comment from a resident on a fixed income
and how much a tax increase would affect him.
Devlin Gauthier expressed concerns about the financial uncertainty that using $10 million in reserves
could create. She advocated for the creation of definite ways to support the most vulnerable residents in
Town. She stated that she can support using $5 million in reserves but not $10 million.
Hanneke echoed Devlin Gauthier’s points, citing the financial uncertainty that could result from using the
reserves. She also mentioned that using large portions of the reserves could reduce the Town’s financial
flexibility in the future.
Taub mentioned that the $5 million already approved by the Finance Committee will be replenished by
energy-saving rebates. She also urged the Council to give the residents any financial relief that they can at
this time.
Walker echoed Taub’s points. She stated that she supports using $10 million in reserves on the
elementary school project because that option will help residents the most in the immediate future. She
also mentioned that the failure of the school building project will threaten the viability of the other three
planned building projects.
Steinberg mentioned that there is currently less than $10 million in the capital stabilization fund. He
stated that living through the 2008/2009 financial crisis highlighted the importance of having robust
reserves.
Walker asked if there was anything preventing her from putting a motion on the table. Griesemer replied
that the present meeting’s posted agenda only included discussion, not action items.
Steinberg stated that he would recognize Walker at the beginning of the March 28th Finance Committee
meeting so she could make a motion before having to leave the Finance Committee meeting.
Griesemer opined that making a motion would be out of order at the present meeting because the posted
agenda included no action items.
Walker inquired if she could make a motion during the “Items Not Anticipated by the Chair 48 Hours in
Advance” portion of the meeting.
Schoen offered to make Walker’s motion at the upcoming Finance Meeting if Walker cannot attend.
Walker expressed that she does not want Schoen to make the motion in the event of her absence at the
upcoming Finance Committee meeting.
Pam asked when the public forum on the matter at hand will take place. Griesemer answered that the
public forum will occur at six o’clock on April 3rd. Pam expressed concern that the Finance Council’s
regular meetings take place at 3 PM, when many people are at work.
Griesemer ruled that Walker’s motion was out of order.
MOTION: Miller moved, second by Devlin Gauthier, to appeal the President’s decision to call the motion
out of order.

Bahl-Milne asked if the Council could informally express how they feel about the content of Walker’s
motion. O’Keeffe reminded the Council that the subject of the current discussion is limited to the appeal
of Griesemer’s ruling.
Walker asked if her motion was ruled out of order as part of the “Items Not Anticipated by the Chair 48
Hours in Advance” portion of the meeting. Griesemer responded that it was ruled out of order because
there were no action items posted on the meeting agenda. Hanneke mentioned that motions have been
made during the “Items Not Anticipated by the Chair 48 Hours in Advance” portion of the meeting before
but that motions during this portion of the meeting must be truly unanticipated.
Rooney asked if Walker’s inability to attend the March 28th Finance Committee meeting constituted an
unanticipated item.
De Angelis urged the Council to recall that rules exist to protect both the minority and majority of the
Council. She concurred with Griesemer’s ruling because there were no action items on the meeting’s
posted agenda.
Taub expressed that she feels uncomfortable that Walker would not be able to vote on her own motion at
the March 28th Finance Committee meeting because Walker is unable to attend the March 28th Finance
Committee meeting.
Walker expressed confusion about the declaration that there would be no votes at the present meeting.
She stated that she saw the meeting agenda that did not include any action items.
Griesemer asked O’Keeffe if she thinks Walker’s motion would be valid during the “Items Not
Anticipated by the Chair 48 Hours in Advance” portion of the meeting. O’Keeffe answered that she is not
sure the content of the motion was truly unanticipated.
Steinberg read a portion of the Attorney General’s webpage on meeting law urging public bodies against
discussing or taking actions on unanticipated items that may be controversial or may be of particular
interest to the public.
Referencing open meeting law, Miller opined that Walker’s motion was within the stated purpose of the
meeting and that her motion was therefore valid although no action items were on the meeting agenda.
Lopes thanked O’Keeffe for her work informing the Council about meeting rules. She expressed concern
about misleading the public by entertaining motions when there were no action items on the meeting
agenda.
VOTED: 6-4, with 3 abstentions, to appeal the President’s decision to call the motion out of order.
