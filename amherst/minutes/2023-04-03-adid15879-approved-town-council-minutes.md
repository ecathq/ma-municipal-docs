4. General Public Comment
Ian Rhodewalt, resident, stated that Amherst teachers deserve a fair contract and a living wage. He noted
that Amherst is known for the quality of its education system and that Amherst voted overwhelmingly in
favor of the Fair Share Amendment in November 2022. He spoke against the cutting of union jobs from
Amherst schools and urged the Council to advocate for more school funding from the State. He also
criticized the Town for having an attorney meet with the teachers’ union.
Allegra Clark, resident, expressed concern about the proposed Amherst Regional Schools budget,
especially about the cutting of several jobs. She stated that students currently need increased mental
health support in schools and that mental health support positions especially should not be cut. She urged
the Council to vote against the proposed budget and also mentioned that some of the regional school
buildings are in poor condition.
Vincent O’Connor, resident, expressed that he was distressed to hear about health and safety issues in the
Town fire station. He emphasized the urgent need for a new fire station and a new DPW building but also
pushed for necessary repairs to be made to the existing buildings. He also emphasized the importance of
the elementary school building project and urged the Council not to place the financial burden of the
project on the taxpayers.
Rani Parker, resident, asked why municipal costs in Amherst seem to be higher than in other surrounding
communities. She also spoke against cutting teacher and paraprofessional jobs, urging the Town Council
to focus on the Town’s people as well as its buildings and infrastructure. She also voiced support for the
Town’s firefighters and advocated for safer working conditions in the fire station.
Carol Gray, resident, echoed previous speakers’ support for the Town’s teachers. She urged the Council
to think more creatively about the budget so that school jobs do not need to be cut. She expressed concern
that the most vulnerable workers in schools are the ones whose jobs are at risk and mentioned temporary
furloughs as a way to save jobs. She urged the Town to accept the teachers’ contract demands before the
vote on the elementary school building project debt exclusion.
Amber Cano-Martin, resident, expressed concern that the proposed budget does not include enough to
accommodate the teachers’ contract demands. She criticized what she sees as the prioritization of
infrastructure projects over the needs of teachers. Emphasizing the central role that teachers play in the
community, she urged the Town not to cut any teaching jobs. She also expressed that she would rather see
an increase in teacher pay than a new library.
Jeff Lee, resident, urged the Town Council not to consider reducing the length of the public comment
periods at their meetings. He also criticized the lack of updates to the public comment section of the
Town website.
Rani Parker, resident, added that she supports the Town taking ownership of the public water and sewer
lines up to residents’ properties.
5. Consent Agenda

8. Action Items
a. Request Town Manager Present $5M Funding Plan to Reduce Financial Impact of
the Elementary School Building Project on Residents
MOTION: Walker moved, seconded by Rooney, that the Town Council request that the Town Manager
amend authorization order FY23 - 06 C to change the amount of funding to be transferred from the
Capital Stabilization Fund from Five Million to Ten Million dollars so that the text is changed as follows:
Strike the text that reads
“Five Million Dollars shall be transferred to pay costs of this project from the Town’s
Capital Stabilization Fund”
and replace it with
“Ten Million Dollars shall be transferred to pay costs of this project from the Town’s
Capital Stabilization Fund”
Walker reviewed the history of her involvement with the elementary school building project. She then
stated that she wants to do whatever she can to alleviate the cost burden on resident taxpayers. She
expressed that she feels she has faced many obstacles to making the above motion. She explained why she
proposed using $10 million in reserves to fund the school building project, expressed frustration with the
process of trying to make her motion, and stated that she does not think the motion in the meeting packet
was strong enough.
Pam thanked Walker for continually advocating for those who cannot afford tax increases. She expressed
that using $10 million in reserves for the elementary school building project would symbolically send a
message to residents that the Town was doing all it could to keep the cost low for taxpayers. She stated
that she intended to vote for the motion because she has heard from residents who are concerned about the
cost of the elementary school building project and because she admires Walker’s persistence.
Hanneke stated that the capital stabilization fund does not currently have $10 million in it. She asked
Walker where the rest of the $10 million in her motion would come from.
Walker answered that the total reserves contain $24 million and that the necessary amount could be
moved to the capital stabilization fund. She also stated that it would be up to the Town Manager to decide
from where the funds the $10 million in reserves would come.
Steinberg expressed that he did not think using $10 million in reserves would be sound financial policy.
He stated that the Town is already in a difficult financial situation because it does not have as much
commercial development and taxable land as other communities. He also noted that Amherst College,
Hampshire College, and UMass are not on the Town’s tax roll. He stated that the reserves exist to protect
the Town’s cash flow and to act as a buffer in the event of a crisis. He also expressed concern about the
potential lowering of the Town’s bond rating.
Walker stated that it has been helpful to hear the concerns on the other side of the debate at hand. She
stated that using $10 million in reserves for the school building project would not deplete the Town’s
reserves. She also stated that none of the other capital building projects are currently relying on the capital
stabilization fund and that, under normal circumstances, the reserves are not regularly used. She proposed
authorizing the use of the additional $5 million in reserves for the elementary school building project and
then having the Town Manager locate an additional $5 million to replenish the reserves.

reduce the financial impact of the debt exclusion for the Elementary School Building Project on residents

presented to the Town Council by November 30, 2023.
Griesemer expressed support for the motion because it gives the Town Manager additional time to locate
more funds and added that the school building project would not appear on tax bills until 2025.
Devlin Gauthier asked why the motion specifically calls for the Town Manager to locate an additional $5
million in funds instead of a different quantity.
Mangano answered that the motion at hand specified $5 million because the Council had been discussing
the use of an additional $5 million for some time.
Steinberg mentioned that the motion was supported by a majority of the voting members of the Finance
Committee and by the three resident members of the Finance Committee.
MOTION: Walker moved, second by Miller, to amend the motion by replacing “November 30, 2023”
with “October 2, 2023,” and to add the following language at the end of the motion:
“AND Should funds comprising that additional $5 million not be secured and guaranteed
by this date, the Town Manager shall be requested to direct an amount equal to the
difference between $5 million and what has been secured, but not to exceed a total of $5
million, to be drawn from Stabilization accounts to fund the Elementary School. Should
any alternative funding opportunities emerge at a later date, those dollar amounts shall, as
possible and appropriate, go back into Stabilization funds to replenish those accounts for
future capital needs.”
Walker spoke to the amendment.
Lopes stated that debt exclusion would impact the Town’s highest earners most because they would pay
more in taxes. She asked how the Council could ensure that the additional $5 million referenced in the
amendment would go towards helping the Town’s most financially vulnerable residents.
Hanneke expressed that the amendment intended to do the same thing as the motion the Council had just
voted against. She therefore stated that she did not support the amendment.
Steinberg concurred, adding that he supported the original motion because of its flexibility.
Pam stated that an October date would be too soon to find additional funds and echoed Steinberg’s point
about the flexibility of the original motion.
Walker stated that her amendment only moved the timeline up by one month and explained that she
wanted to expedite the date because the Town vote on the matter is coming up.
Mangano explained that a November date would allow the Town to close the fiscal year in October as it
usually does.

Slaughter updated the Council on the regional school assessment and stated that four of the six school
union contracts are still under negotiations. He also stated that there has been no change in capital
assessments since the last presentation to the Council.
Griesemer stated that the Finance Committee would have further discussions on this matter and additional
presentations by Slaughter and Morris.
Hanneke asked about Amherst’s share of the guardrails and particularly how Amherst’s contribution has
been communicated to the other three regional towns. Morris answered that the guardrails help to get the
budget passed and help to prevent budget cuts. He also indicated that the guardrails have been discussed
with the other three regional towns.
Schoen asked Morris and Slaughter to bring data about enrollment and staffing trends when they attend an
upcoming Finance Committee meeting.
Bahl-Milne asked why the school budget has decreased by $1 million dollars. Morris responded that
enrollment is down, particularly at the middle school, and that costs are increasing beyond revenues. He
also stated that assistance from the State has not increased by much, mentioning that the Student
Opportunity Act is not benefitting the Town’s school district.
Bahl-Milne asked what kind of advocacy the Council can do to help secure more funding assistance from
the State. Morris answered that the School Committee is already engaging in such advocacy and that the
Council should reach out to the School Committee chairs and to local legislators.
Bahl-Milne asked when the Council and Town Manager should discuss the union contract negotiations.
Morris answered that it would be problematic to discuss it while negotiations are in progress. He
mentioned that the Town Manager is in close connection with the negotiating agents.
Steinberg asked Morris and Slaughter to bring information about charter school attendance when they
come to an upcoming Finance Committee meeting.
Taub asked if the budget helps to address some of the paraeducators’ expressed concerns.
Hanneke asked if the other regional towns have been asked to make supplemental budgets and asked
about the reopening of the regional agreement. Morris answered that more targeted amendments to
regional agreements are now being allowed. He suggested bringing feedback about the regional
agreement to the Regional School Committee.
