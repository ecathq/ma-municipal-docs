3. Public Forum – Charter Sec. 5.6
Tim Neal, resident, asked Mangano to display and explain the funding options for the school building
project. Mangano did so. Neal expressed opposition to using any more than $5 million in reserves for the
school building project. He does support using only $5 million in reserves. He stated that he would like to
see the reserves saved for other building projects.
Vincent O’Connor, resident, stated that he thinks the school project is the most important capital project
before the town. He expressed support for using $10 million in reserves in order to lessen the school
building project’s burden on taxpayers.
Sarah Forsythe, resident, expressed that the Fire Department opposes using $10 million in reserves for the
school building project. She described many issues and safety hazards with the current fire station and
expressed strong support for the building of a new fire station. She also expressed concern that using
additional reserves for the school building project will further delay the building of a new fire station.
Birdie Newman, resident, expressed concern that not enough attention is being paid to the burden the
school building project will place on taxpayers and renters.
Peter Demling, resident, reviewed the history of the school building project and urged the Council to
model unified support for the school building project for Amherst voters.
Ginny Hamilton, resident, expressed support for using $5 million in reserves for the school building
project. She also echoed Demling’s point about showing unified support. She shared that she believes
there is overwhelming support for the school building project among Town residents. She also
commented that there are other ways to reduce taxes besides using an additional $5 million in reserves.
Matt Bloomenfeld, resident, stated that all four capital building projects before the Town are important to
him. He spoke in favor of using only $5 million in reserves for the school building project, citing fears
that using $10 million in reserves would threaten the viability of the DPW and fire department building
projects.
Jeff Lee, resident, spoke in favor of the school building project but criticized its funding by debt
exclusion. He expressed concern about how tax increases will impact low-income residents, citing recent
cost-of-living increases. He also asked about the financial state of the library building project. He
expressed support for using $10 million in reserves for the school building project.
Maria Kopicki, resident, expressed support for using $10 million in reserves for the school building
project. She expressed concern about the pitting of the fire station project against the school building
project.
Clare Bertrand, resident, expressed support for only using only $5 million in reserves for the school
building project, citing concerns for the financial viability of the other four capital building projects. She
emphasized the importance of having an up-to-date fire station for Town safety.

Sarah Marshall, resident, stated that all Town employees need safe buildings in which to work. She spoke
in support of completing all four capital building projects in a timely fashion. She also spoke in favor of
easing taxes by allowing more property development.
Carol Gray, resident, expressed concern that capital building projects are proceeding while Amherst
teachers are on work-to-rule. She criticized the current operating budget and spoke in support of
allocating additional funds for teacher and para-educator pay increases. She also stated that she thinks the
capital building projects should be put on hold until teachers’ demands are met.
Amber Cano-Martin, resident, spoke in favor of the school building project and spoke in favor of using
$10 million in reserves to complete it, citing a desire to lessen the tax burden on residents. She also
echoed Carol Gray’s comments about increasing teacher pay.
Allegra Clark, resident, spoke in favor of using $10 million in reserves to complete the school building
project, citing a desire to lessen the project’s burden on taxpayers. She also cited concerns with the
existing school buildings and echoed Gray’s comments about increasing teacher pay. Clark also
mentioned that she fears the cost of living in Town is prohibitive to low-income families.
