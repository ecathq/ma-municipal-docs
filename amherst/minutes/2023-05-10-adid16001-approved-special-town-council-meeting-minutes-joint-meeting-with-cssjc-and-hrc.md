4. Presentations and Discussion
This meeting is being held in accordance with the Council’s November 14, 2022 vote:
That the Town Council requests that the Town Manager – working with the DEI Department and
other staff:

---

1) Propose to the Town Council a plan for community visioning with a focus on public safety
and social justice;
2) Propose to the Town Council a plan for the creation of a Resident Oversight Board, with
possible assistance from and hire, as appropriate, a consultant to help with the development
of that plan;
3) Organize a review of public safety protocols for responding to and handling public safety
calls involving all residents including minors, in order to recommend changes to those
protocols, if appropriate;
4) Continue to develop protocols for CRESS regarding active engagement by community
responders;
5) Continue the work already begun on exploring options for a youth empowerment center;
6) Provide training regarding racial equity, rights, and other options for training to employees
and members of the public; and
7) Develop a communications plan to raise awareness in the community about these efforts;
and that the Town Manager will report on actions to be taken and/or progress in addressing the
above, no later than four months from the date of this vote. Draft reports to be available to the
Council, the Community Safety and Social Justice Committee (CSSJC), and the Human Rights
Commission (HRC), as well as the public, no less than two weeks prior to the Town Council meeting
where the items will be discussed; that CSSJC and HRC provide written advice to the Town Council 5
days prior to the Town Council Meeting at which the items will be discussed; that the Town Council
will discuss the Manager’s report and both committee reports, and will hold a discussion that
includes public comment.

Ononibaku stated she is disappointed that the Town does not have a safety protocol and does not believe

Avila stated policies need to be reviewed, that black and brown people are policed differently from white

suffered at the hands of the police, and it needs to stop.
Ononibaku asked about the safety protocol, if there are remedies for victims when the protocol is violated,
and if there is the plan for the six young people to be made whole.
Bockelman stated the Town cannot issue checks to individuals, but if there were litigation the Town could
be ordered to make a payment and the Town’s insurance may be involved; it is not within any municipal
official’s authority to write a check to an individual.
Ferreira stated she is sad and disappointed with the Town Manager’s response and asked if he has had a
conversation with the families.
Bockelman stated he has had a conversation with a parent but not with Ononibaku, and that he would
welcome a conversation with her.
Bahl-Milne asked about the LEAP report to see if there is anything that the Town can begin implementing

Rooney asked if CRESS can begin work on reviewing policies and protocols.
E. Miller stated there is conversation about shared training, language, and understanding.
De Angelis asked if the policies are publicly available and Bockelman responded that they are.

Continue to develop protocols for CRESS
Ferreira stated that CRESS needs to be 24/7 and fully staffed so that the police are not responding to any
incidents that are nonviolent. Ferreira asked how that can happen with current staffing and funding.
E. Miller stated there are 20-30 departments in the country doing this work, and none of them have been
24/7 yet. The call volume is low and when talking about adding a second shift the staff would need to be
tripled, including a leadership component. Taking steps before there is capacity is a concern, and adding a
large group of new people before an understanding of the work is concerning. Denver’s program is
beginning overnights this summer, and he is closely watching their progress.
Ferreira spoke about the need for CRESS and that she hears that the department is not asking for more
funds.

E. Miller stated they are working very quickly and building a foundation that is antiracist and will have
community support. There is not evidence around the country that programs can get off the ground so
quickly.
Ferreira spoke about the report regarding the youth empowerment center, and that youth need to be front
and center in the conversations and things need to begin right away.
Young spoke about work planned for the fall to begin working with youth with the help of the
AmeriCorps member.
Ononibaku stated CSWG, through 7Gen, did their research, one of their recommendations is having a
youth center, and asked how that fits into all of this, and that things are being delayed while youth are
suffering.
Young spoke about beginning programming with existing space and that a building cannot be created
quickly.
Ononibaku stated that the Town’s response to anything that benefits the BIPOC community is that there is
not enough money.
Pam asked what the DEI Director needs from the group now.
Young responded that the department needs time to implement programming, that the reports include
good ideas for programming, and there are opportunities to partner with Amherst College and Hampshire
College.
Devlin Gauthier asked about the training sessions, asked if there is a framework or model that is being
used to create an antiracist culture, and how the training is being applied.
Young spoke about the process to develop training sessions for different departments, and that training is
tailored to the department. There are conversations with the department heads about their DEI self-
assessment, with the goal of connecting future training to that.
Devlin Gauthier asked about the communications plan, if there are alternative methods in the plan to
reach communities that are not usually reached with the current modes of communication.
Young stated the Town is in the beginning steps to identify how to meet needs, such as translation
services, without adding additional responsibilities to existing staff. Young mentioned the earmarked
funds that will help with resources.
E. Miller spoke about the essential support he received from Young in bringing CRESS to life.
Parker asked to hear more about CRESS’s approach to look at which police calls the CRESS department
can handle and suggested only an evening shift rather than business hours.
E. Miller said his approach is to address people’s lack of relationships, that we don’t ask people to trust
us, and that trust builds through actions. The department is not working one shift, there are various shifts
to address the different needs at different periods of the day. E. Miller spoke about the education needed
and implementing 911 call response over the summer so the responders are ready when students return in
the fall.
Clark asked about the CRESS budget.

E. Miller stated the department exists in a context and that not everything he asked for is included in the
budget.
Bockelman added that CRESS has been funded entirely with taxpayer dollars, and that it is important not
to make the department reliant on grants. The CRESS responders will be union positions. Bockelman
spoke about the challenges and development of the department.
Clark stated she expected a strategic reduction in the police department once CRESS is fully functional.
E. Miller stated it would be inappropriate for him to discuss another department’s budget.
Bockelman stated that there are a certain number of police officers who are needed to cover all shifts for
the community. CRESS taking calls will be helpful, but officers will still be needed to conduct police
functions. He is not planning a reduction in police staffing.
Clark asked comparing call types between CRESS and the police.
E. Miller stated the call types are listed in his presentation, that he is seeking legal review of what call
types CRESS can respond to, which will be consistent with national standards, and more information is
being gathered about call types. E. Miller stated CRESS has secured new software that is also used by the
fire department, which will help track calls.
Walker asked about how CRESS and the police will function together.
Bockelman stated CRESS has not begun taking 911 calls yet, and it is difficult to project how that will
change before seeing how the department begins taking calls.
Walker asked how the CRESS Director will determine when they are ready to be full time.
E. Miller spoke about gaining trust in the community and training responders, so they are comfortable and
ready to take any call that comes in for them.
Steinberg spoke about the need to evaluate every step of the way to make modifications as needed, and
that it sounds like what the CRESS department is doing.
E. Miller agreed that there are many adjustments made every day, that responders are doing incredibly
hard work, and that he needs to care for his team.
Steinberg expressed appreciation for the CRESS department, and that he wants to be sure we are
protecting their safety and putting them in situations where they have the capacity to solve a problem.
Steinberg stated the police department is not deeply staffed and there is little flexibility to make the
significant cuts that others may have envisioned.
E. Miller agreed with Steinberg’s comments about keeping his staff safe, and that no responder has been
injured or killed in this work in the country. Amherst is a relatively safe community, and responders’
interactions have felt safe. If a call appears violent, CRESS will refer to the police.
Pam spoke about CRESS meeting unmet needs and may involve crime reduction. Pam spoke about
CRESS growing organically and that is good and will improve the quality of life in the Town.
E. Miller and Bockelman spoke about the work CRESS is taking on, the support services that people with
insurance rely on and taking on some of the social service needs that had been handled by the police
because there was no one else to call.
