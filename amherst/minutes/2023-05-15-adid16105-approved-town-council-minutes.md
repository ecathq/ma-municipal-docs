3. Hearings:
6:40 p.m. Public Hearing regarding Eversource petition to install new underground
electrical duct bank from South Pleasant St. to College St.
Eversource representatives Michael Fraga, Mike Kane, Nick Kreigle, and Dylan Sawabi were in
attendance to answer questions about the petition.
Fraga spoke about the petition and stated Eversource has been working closely with Superintendent of
Public Works Guilford Mooring on the project.
Mooring provided additional context for the petition and spoke favorably about the potential project. He
also praised Eversource’s partnership.
Hanneke asked about the dimensions of the manhole covers. She also asked about the status of non-
electrical wires within the project area and if roads beyond College Street would be affected by this
project.
Eversource representatives confirmed the size of the manhole cover and confirmed that the non-electrical
wires would remain above ground for the time being.
Mooring added that the Town would have to consult with Verizon and Comcast in order to move the
additional wires underground.
Councilors asked about the following:
• If all wiring on the existing poles will be moved underground
• The timeframe for completing the project
• Traffic disruptions
• Repaving
• Town costs
Mooring responded that the whole project will take 1-2 years, repaving will be completed by Eversource
and Amherst College and that Verizon, Comcast, Eversource, and Amherst College may contribute funds
to future or related projects.
Eversource representatives responded that efforts will be made to minimize traffic disruptions, only
Eversource wires will be housed in the duct but that Verizon and Comcast wires may be moved
underground in the future, and that the cost of the project will be funded by Eversource and not the Town.

Devlin Gauthier further explained the background and context of the proposed bylaw.
Rooney asked about the gaps in laws related to reproductive and gender affirming healthcare.
Steinberg commented that he is concerned about the number of proposed bylaws that are referred to the
committees, stating that these bylaws can bog down the committees.
Devlin Gauthier answered that this is more an issue of committee processes than of the merits of the

protected by HIPAA or State-level laws related to prosecution of those seeking reproductive care from
other states and others assisting them.
Griesemer asked the proposed bylaw’s sponsors to describe the enforceability of the proposed bylaw.
Hanneke provided example scenarios in which the proposed bylaw would help protect the individual
rights of Town residents.
Pam spoke in favor of the proposed bylaw.
Miller asked if legal review would be sought. Hanneke confirmed that it would be.
De Angelis spoke to the seriousness of the proposed bylaw, citing the life-or-death nature of the
healthcare matters at hand.
