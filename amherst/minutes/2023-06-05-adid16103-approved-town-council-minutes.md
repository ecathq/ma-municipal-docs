pole on Dickinson Street approximately 33 feet southerly of existing pole 35/2 and
approximately 16 feet easterly of the Dickinson Street centerline.

b. FY2024 Budget
Pam asked if the Town is going to lose any ARPA funds due to claw backs. Bockelman responded that
the Town has been close contact with Congressman McGovern’s office about the ARPA funds and that
the funds are not currently at risk of getting clawed back.
Taub asked how the Town would go about pursuing one of the sidewalk and road development plans laid
out in the report. Steinberg responded that road construction costs have been increasing rapidly and that
the plans in the report were a call for creative price reduction measures.
Taub asked if Town staff would be responsible for moving these efforts forward of if the Council should
form a committee to do it, emphasizing that roads and sidewalks are a priority issue for many residents.
Griesemer responded that the Town must first develop its financial guidelines for fiscal year 2025.
Schoen added that the matter has been discussed in the Capital Planning Committee and that there are
only a limited number of pavement suppliers.
Schoen praised the contributions of the Town staff and mentioned that the cost of retaining staff is also
increasing. She informed the Council that there is currently a wage study going on to examine staff
compensation.
Steinberg described how the Town competes with the Commonwealth, Mass DOT, and larger
communities for paving contractors.
Rooney mentioned that there are at least a dozen intergovernmental agreements before the Town Manager
and that the matter at hand would therefore likely have to wait until fiscal year 2025. Bockelman agreed,
stating that it would take time to get partnering municipalities to align their procurement and
appropriations processes.
Pam emphasized how important sidewalks are to her constituents.
Griesemer stated that the Council would vote on the fiscal year 2024 budget at its June 12th, 2023
meeting.

d. Council Order FY24-10 An Order Setting the Water and Sewer Rates to be Effective
July 1, 2023; amendment to Water and Sewer Regulations
MOTION: Griesemer moved, seconded by Hanneke, in accordance with General Bylaw 3.62 Water Use
Regulations, having heard public comment specific to the proposed amendments on June 5, 2023, notice of
which was posted on the Town Bulletin Board for at least 10 days on May 17, and notice of amendments
published in the newspaper on May 22, 2023, to amend the Water Use Regulations Appendix A, as
follows:
By replacing “April 18, 2023” with “July 1, 2023”
By replacing “Domestic Water Rate $4.75 per 100 cubic feet” with “Domestic Water Rate $5.00
per 100 cubic feet”
By replacing “Agricultural Water Rate $4.75 per 100 cubic feet” with Agricultural Water Rate
$5.00 per 100 cubic feet”
Rooney asked why the agricultural water rate is the same as the residential water rate. Mangano answered
that the agricultural water rate is differs from the residential rate because it does not include sewer rate
payments. Steinberg further explained how the water rates and sewer rates were separated out so that
agricultural water users would not have to pay for a service they do not use.
Public Comment:
Vincent O’Connor, resident, urged the Council to post a copy of the meeting agenda in the back of the
Town Room during in-person meetings.
VOTED 9-0, with 4 absent (Bahl-Milne, De Angelis, Lopes, Miller) by roll call, in accordance with
General Bylaw 3.62 Water Use Regulations, having heard public comment specific to the proposed

on May 17, and notice of amendments published in the newspaper on May 22, 2023, to amend the Water
Use Regulations Appendix A, as follows:

per 100 cubic feet”
