7. Presentations and Discussion
a. Town Manager Report on American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA) Funds
Town Manager Bockelman and Finance Director Mangano presented a brief outline of how “round one”
ARPA funds totaling $9.8M have been utilized to date, and ideas for use of the Town’s remaining “round
two” $4.9M ARPA funds, which include $2.8M from round one and $2.1M that was not obligated to
projects.
De Angelis asked about the funds allocated for the youth empowerment center.
Pam asked why the Town received less ARPA funds than Northampton.
Taub asked if the ARPA funds allocated to the Drake was delivered via the Town or the Business
Improvement District.
Walker asked about funding for 3- and 4-year-olds with social/emotional challenges, the subsidies for
transportation, funds for developing the Resident Oversight Board, overall project goals, using funds to
identify Town needs, the distribution of funds for resident assistance, how the Town defined
“marginalized,” and why some businesses were not considered as contributed to the Town’s arts and
culture. Walker asked why the Town wouldn’t target black-owned businesses for ARPA funds and asked
about the application process.
Special Public Comment
Pat Ononibaku, Amherst resident and President of the Black Business Association of the Amherst Area,
thanked the Council and Town Manager for discussing ARPA funds, spoke critically of the Town’s
distribution of ARPA funds to the Drake, stated that funds were discriminated with a racial bias, and
called for an investigation of the distribution of the ARPA funds. Ononibaku asked for $150,000 of
ARPA funds for Hazel and the Black Business Association.

8. Action Items
a. Councilor Compensation – Charter Sec. 2.4: Compensation
MOTION: Griesemer moved, second by Steinberg, in accordance with Charter Section 2.4, to adopt an
increase to Town Council compensation in the amount of $2,500, for a total annual compensation of
$7,500 for Town Council members, and an increase of $2,000 for the Council President, for a total annual
compensation of $9,500, effective January 2, 2024, and to request the Town Manager submit a
supplemental budget appropriation to meet said increase in accordance with Charter Section 5.6.
Steinberg reported on the Finance Committee’s deliberation and recommendation; changes to family care
reimbursements above what has been allocated in the budget and health insurance for Councilors were not
recommended by the committee. The committee requested quarterly report on use of the family care
reimbursement, and recognized the difficulty with the process, which requires a Councilor to pay for care
and then be reimbursed rather than receive funds up front. Steinberg noted compensation for city
councilors in communities not listed in the sponsors’ proposal.
Hanneke asked about the supplemental appropriation portion of the motion, stating that the Council
requesting an appropriation rather than finding funds within their budget.
Bockelman suggested that the Council could request the Town Manager recommend a funding source.
Rooney asked if the increase would apply to the School Committee or Library Trustees.

Griesemer responded that the recommendation covered only Councilors and that the Finance Committee
recommended the Council send a letter to the 2024 Charter Commission requesting changes to the section
regarding compensation.
Steinberg spoke about the Charter language that states the Council can increase their compensation
“subject to appropriation,” and that the Town Manager could not have anticipated an increase when he
was developing the budget. Steinberg added the School Committee and Library Trustees compensation
are in a different Charter section, and do not have the same time limitations.
Pam stated the Council needs to make service on the Council more accessible. Pam spoke in support of
the original proposal.
Schoen spoke about the difficulty with the budget in terms of union negotiations and framed the increase
as equal to a teacher or firefighter’s salary. Schoen spoke in support of an annual compensation increase
similar to staff cost of living increases.
Walker spoke about her initial proposal for compensation and that the intent of the proposal was to
address socioeconomic barriers to service on the Council. Walker noted the increase will not go into
effect until the next Council is seated. Walker stated she did not support the Finance Committee’s
recommendation and that the recommendation is not enough to be an effective Councilor.
Devlin Gauthier spoke about the average cost of childcare, the amount of time and work that serving on
the Council entails, and that the pilot care reimbursement program does not go far enough.
MOTION: Hanneke moved, second by Griesemer, to amend the motion by deleting the phrase "a
supplemental budget appropriation to meet said increase in accordance with Charter Section 5.6." and
replacing it with the phrase "options to the Town Council by October 1, 2023 for appropriating funding
for said compensation."
Hanneke spoke in support of her motion.
VOTED unanimously, 11-0 by roll call with 2 absent (Bahl-Milne and Miller) to amend the motion by
deleting the phrase "a supplemental budget appropriation to meet said increase in accordance with Charter
Section 5.6." and replacing it with the phrase "options to the Town Council by October 1, 2023 for
appropriating funding for said compensation."
MOTION: Walker moved, second by Pam, to amend the motion by increasing Town Councilor
compensation by $5,000 for a total annual compensation of $10,000 and increasing the Council
President’s compensation by $4,500 for a total annual compensation of $12,000.
Walker spoke in support of her motion.
Steinberg spoke in opposition to the motion and referred to the Town’s recent tax increase for the new
school building and the stronger economic situation of cities like Northampton.
Pam spoke in support of the motion.
Schoen spoke about the Council’s financial accountability and spoke in opposition to the motion.
Hanneke stated the Charter Commission added the 18-month requirement in order that the Council would
not vote for an increase to its own compensation, and early enough for those intending to run for a seat
would know the compensation. Hanneke stated the Charter Commission did not set the compensation
high enough.
