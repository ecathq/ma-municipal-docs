4. General Public Comment
Hwei-Ling Greeney requested that the Town distribute American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA) funds to the
organizations who were not selected for Community Development Block Grant funding.
Miranda Balkin urged the Council to vote yes on Hanneke and Devlin Gauthier’s proposed bylaw to
protect abortion rights and rights to gender-affirming care in Town.
Lauren Mills advocated for the creation of a youth empowerment and cultural community center. She also
urged the Department of Health to take mental health and stress-reducing efforts seriously.
Margaret Sawyer asked that the Town Council support Amherst Media by distributing ARPA funding to
them. She also described Amherst Media’s work and role in the community.
Vira Cage requested the Town Council consider the organizations that were left out of the first round of
ARPA funding. She expressed concern that established businesses in Town were overlooked in favor of
newer businesses.
Irv Rhodes urged the Council to distribute ARPA funds on an ethical basis, with organizations prioritized
by need. He also advocated for the creation of a community center in Town.
Vira Cage spoke about equity in the distribution of ARPA funding.

---

Mindy Domb advocated for the use of ARPA funding to repair roads and to enhance the water treatment
plant.
Pat Ononibaku raised concerns about bias in the distribution of ARPA funds. She urged the Council to
use ARPA funds to provide relief to residents and businesses who are recovering from the COVID-19
pandemic. She also requested a public forum on the distribution of ARPA funds.
Talvin Dhingra advocated for the creation of a community center for BIPOC youth. He also stated that
youth should be part of the committee on creating a community center.
Dennis Vandal requested that ARPA funding be distributed to the Amherst Senior Center. He also spoke
about some of the challenges the Senior Center has faced in the wake of the COVID-19 pandemic and
commented on how surrounding communities are caring better for their elders.
Marc Barrette requested that ARPA funding be distributed to the Amherst Senior Center. He also spoke
about how upgrades to the Bangs Center could benefit many groups in the Town.
Amilcar Shabazz spoke about the importance of Black-owned businesses in Town. He also advocated for
greater institutional support for Black-owned businesses.
Isolda Ortega-Bustamante urged the Town to practice greater transparency when allocating ARPA funds
in order to restore the trust of the community. She also spoke about the importance of BIPOC youth
mental health.
Amilcar Shabazz spoke about his experiences as the owner of a transport and bussing company in Town.
He also encouraged greater outreach to businesses who are passed over for ARPA funding.
Lev Ben Ezra urged the Council to distribute ARPA funds to residents who have very limited incomes.
She spoke about increases in poverty and homelessness in the wake of the COVID-19 pandemic.
Amilcar Shabazz Jr. spoke about the importance of transparency in Town affairs. He also compared the
reparations efforts in Northampton to those in Amherst, emphasizing the importance of listening to Black
residents.
Kathleen Traphagen emphasized the importance of distributing ARPA funds to those most impacted by
the COVID-19 pandemic, particularly communities of color.

7. Presentations and Discussion
a. Town Manager Report on American Rescue Plan Act Funds – Follow-up
Bockelman provided an update on ARPA fund allocation and asked for Council feedback.
Pam advocated for the dispersal of ARPA funds to the Senior Center and to Black-owned businesses.
Bahl-Milne emphasized the importance of including youth in the creation of a BIPOC youth
empowerment and community center. She also raised the idea of using ARPA funds to hire an Economic
Development Director to specifically work on affordable workforce housing.
Steinberg emphasized the importance of following the law when allocating ARPA funds. He reminded
the Council that ARPA was intended to directly address harm caused by the COVID-19 pandemic. He
also advocated for keeping the downtown and village centers economically strong.
Rooney echoed Pam’s points, emphasizing the importance of strong entrepreneurship in Town.
Hanneke also advocated for the dispersal of ARPA funds to the Senior Center. She also echoed
Steinberg’s points about the intended purpose of the ARPA funds. She raised concerns about the
installation of solar panels in the high school parking lot because the high school sits on land that belongs
to the Regional Schools, not the Town. Hanneke also echoed Bahl-Milne’s point about using ARPA funds
to help alleviate the housing crisis.
Miller advocated for the creation of a diverse advisory board, similar to the Community Development
Block Grant Committee, to consult on the allocation of ARPA funds.
Taub agreed with Miller’s suggestion of an advisory board. She agreed with Pam and Rooney that Black-
owned businesses should be strongly considered for ARPA fund allocation. Taub also mentioned that
many residents wrote in urging the Town to use some ARPA funding to repair roads and sidewalks.
De Angelis commented that certain groups felt the impacts of the pandemic more keenly due to pre-
existing inequalities and economic disparities. She emphasized the importance of using ARPA funds to
help struggling individuals as well as businesses.

Walker echoed previous points about using the ARPA funds to help those most heavily impacted by the
pandemic. She advocated for the allocation of ARPA funds to the Senior Center, to the creation of a youth
empowerment center, and directly to economically vulnerable residents. She also advocated for more
relaxed standards in ARPA applications for businesses.
Lopes expressed interest in using ARPA funds to enhance the water treatment plant, calling it a public
health and safety issue. She also advocated for the use of ARPA funds on sidewalk and road repairs and
the Senior Center.
Schoen advocated for the sorting of ARPA funds into different “buckets”, i.e., some for community
efforts, some for infrastructure and sidewalk repairs, etc.
Griesemer advocated for the use of ARPA funds to repair roads and sidewalks and to improve the Senior
Center. She echoed Hanneke’s concern about the solar installation at the high school.
Lopes asked for a comprehensive breakdown of the ARPA eligibility criteria.
Miller proposed sorting the ARPA funding into “social service priorities” and “non-social service
priorities”. She also advocated for greater outreach to those organizations and parties who were passed
over for the first round of ARPA funding.
Walker echoed Lopes’ request for the federal and Town ARPA eligibility criteria. She also voiced support
for Miller’s suggested advisory board.
Bahl-Milne spoke about cases in which organizations applied for funds multiple times and consistently
were not chosen to receive them. She emphasized the importance of transparency and communication in
those cases. She asked the Town Manager to communicate his prioritization process for allocating ARPA
funds.
Bockelman invited the Council to continue to communicate with him about ARPA funding priorities.
b. Ranked-Choice Voting Update
Griesemer updated the Council on the recent hearing on ranked-choice voting. Griesemer testified at that
hearing, as did Hanneke. She urged the Council to research the process used by the town of Easthampton
to amend their charter.

h. Transportation and Parking Commission Proposal
Bockelman discussed the role of the Transportation Advisory Committee. He explained that the creation
of a Transportation and Parking Commission could take some of the Council’s responsibilities (as keepers
of the public way) out of their purview. He also noted that many other cities have comparable
commissions that make decisions regarding transportation and parking.
Bahl-Milne spoke in support for the proposal.
Hanneke expressed concern with the Town Council delegating authority to a commission.
Taub echoed Hanneke’s comments.
Steinberg and Rooney commented that the current process needs improvement.
Pam voiced that she is not comfortable with giving up the Council’s authority on political matters related
to parking.
Lopes spoke in agreement with Hanneke and Taub’s comments.
