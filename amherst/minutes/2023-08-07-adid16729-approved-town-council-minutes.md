2. Hearings - None
3. General Public Comment
Peggy Matthews-Nilsen spoke in favor of the dark skies-friendly streetlights policy. She described the
harmful effects of light pollution on both humans and other species.
Eve Vogel spoke in support of reducing excessive nighttime lighting while also strategically using
streetlights to increase pedestrian safety. She described about the importance of a transportation mode
shift away from vehicles. She also requested that the Council postpone the vote on the proposed
streetlight policy until its pedestrian safety provisions are enhanced.
Tracy Zafian noted that she has repeatedly contacted the sponsors of the proposed streetlights policy to
suggest safety-related changes. She encouraged the Council to postpone their vote on the proposed
streetlights policy until its pedestrian safety provisions are enhanced. She also described the Daily
Hampshire Gazette’s coverage of the proposed streetlight policy.
James Lowenthal described light pollution as an environmental crisis. He challenged the idea that more
streetlights would make streets safer for bikers and pedestrians and voiced support for the proposed
streetlight policy.
Rob Kusner spoke in favor of preserving dark skies in Amherst. He stated that the Milky Way is no
longer visible in Town due to light pollution. He spoke in favor of the proposed streetlight policy but
echoed Vogel and Zafian’s points about enhancing its provisions for pedestrian and biker safety.

b. Proposed Streetlights Policy - Sponsor Update

Hanneke summarized the history of the proposed streetlights policy. She added that many of the
comments on the matter focused on locational standards, which were removed from the proposed policy.
She stated that the policy aims to address glare, light color, and over-lighting and that lighting zones are a
“next step” in correcting the Town’s lighting.
Devlin Gauthier thanked everyone who provided feedback on the proposed policy.
Pam inquired about how different lumen levels affect the natural world and pedestrian safety. Hanneke
stated that the proposal originally called for 2200-kelvin lights but now calls for 2700-kelvin lights. She
added that the current streetlights in Town are between 4000- and 6000-kelvin, adding that some studies
suggest that such kelvin levels may be carcinogenic.
MOTION: Steinberg moved, seconded by De Angelis, to postpone the vote on the proposed streetlight
policy until September 11, 2023.
Steinberg explained that two members of the Finance Committee felt very strongly about postponing a
vote on the proposed streetlight policy until the financial implications of the policy can be more deeply
assessed. He also stated that he supported the motion on the floor as a member of the Town Services and
Outreach Committee.
Schoen proposed postponing the vote on the proposed streetlight policy to October 2nd, 2023 so that it can
be financially assessed and so that the wording in some sections could be softened. She also stated that
she wanted more public input on the matter.
Bahl-Milne joined the meeting at 7:25 PM.
Miller stated that, given the substantial revisions to the proposed policy since the Council’s first reading,
a GOL review of the most recent draft would be appropriate. She also voiced support for Steinberg’s
motion.
Lopes thanked the sponsors of the proposed policy. She described how the Town Services and Outreach
Committee agreed on the proposed policy’s light pollution reduction elements but differed on the
pedestrian safety and Town walkability elements.
Taub expressed support for Steinberg’s motion and his suggestion that the Finance Committee review the
proposed policy. She also suggested that the Council assess the implementation of the policy and stated
that she would not feel comfortable with the policy until Tracy Zafian and Eve Vogel were also
comfortable with it.
Rooney voiced support for Schoen’s suggestion that the vote be postponed until October 2nd, 2023. She
also stated that she wants the Transportation Advisory Committee’s review and input on the matter.
De Angelis voiced support for postponing the vote on the policy. She inquired about Miller’s suggestion
that GOL substantively review the most recent draft of the proposed policy, stating that this may not be an
appropriate task for the GOL committee given their charge.
Pam suggested beginning a bicyclist and pedestrian safety education program before changes to the
Town’s streetlight policy are made.
Miller clarified her reasons for suggesting that GOL review the heavily revised draft of the proposed
policy.

c. Revision of Bylaw 3.50 Residential Property and Regulations - First Discussion
Hanneke thanked Jon Thompson and Rob Morra for their input on the bylaw. She then provided
background information on the proposed bylaw and regulations and summarized their main points. She
also stated that the CRC committee proposed sending the fee structure to the Finance Committee to have
a conversation about the fee amounts.
Rooney spoke about CRC’s reasons for updating the Residential Property bylaw, specifically its
relationship to the nuisance property bylaw.
Schoen spoke about rental properties in Town that present perennial problems for Town staff and stated
that she had hoped for a policy that specifically addressed such properties. She then inquired about the
five-year inspection cycle for complaint-free properties and about whether inspection costs could be
billed to the owner in cases involving frequent inspections.
Hanneke stated that Rob Morra had recommended the five-year inspection cycle. She then reviewed the
exceptions to the five-year cycle as enumerated in the regulations. She also stated that CRC tried to tailor
the rental registration permitting to health and safety code enforcement.
Taub confirmed that CRC wrote the bylaw according to the recommendations of the building and safety
departments. She then elaborated on the reasons behind the recommended five-year inspection cycle.
Griesemer thanked the CRC committee, the bylaw’s sponsors, and the Town staff who helped draft the
bylaw. She stated that she would like to refer the bylaw to GOL and that the proposed bylaw seems to
place more responsibility on the Board of License Commissioners. She added that she was glad to see that
subsidized housing was exempt due to federal oversight.
Griesemer asked about the number of occupants per rental property and if the proposed bylaw had gone
out for legal review.
Hanneke answered that the BLC has been reviewing drafts of the bylaw and has given their input along
the way. She also stated that she has been in constant contact with Marian Walker and that Walker had an
attorney review the most recent version.
Hanneke also clarified that subsidized housing is not fully exempt—they will need a rental permit under
the system and Morra will choose whether or not they need inspections.

Regarding the number of occupants per rental, Hanneke mentioned that the CRC discussed the issue at
length and that they decided on no more than four unrelated occupants per rental unit. She also stated that
the issue will continue to be discussed in relation to the draft nuisance property bylaw.
Hanneke stated that the legal review of the bylaw was included in the meeting materials packet.
Steinberg stated that the bylaw represented a significant financial change for the Town. He then provided
some budgetary comparisons to the $475,000 in additional inspector costs. He urged the Council to
seriously consider this additional expense.
MOTION: Griesemer moved, seconded by Hanneke, to refer the proposed revisions to General Bylaw
3.50 Residential Rental Property and the proposed regulations for General Bylaw 3.50 Residential Rental
Property to the Governance, Organization, and Legislation Committee to review for clarity, consistency,
and actionability with a report back to the Town Council within 45 days.
Schoen inquired about whether GOL will need to review the bylaw and regulations again after the
Finance Committee determines the fee amounts. Griesemer stated that this would not be necessary, citing
the fact that GOL and the Finance Committee would be reviewing different documents. She also stated
that the GOL chair could decide to wait until the completion of the Finance Committee review to begin
their review.
Schoen stated that she thinks the Finance committee should review both the fee schedule and the bylaw.
VOTED unanimously 13-0 by roll call to refer the proposed revisions to General Bylaw 3.50 Residential
Rental Property and the proposed regulations for General Bylaw 3.50 Residential Rental Property to the
Governance, Organization, and Legislation Committee to review for clarity, consistency, and actionability
with a report back to the Town Council within 45 days.
MOTION: Griesemer moved, seconded by Hanneke, to refer to the Finance Committee the documents
titled Rental Registration Fee Schedule Proposed Revision 6B2023-08-03 and Fee Schedule Samples
2023-08-03 for a recommendation on the fees to charge under General Bylaw 3.50 Residential Rental
Property with a report to the Town Council by October 16th, 2023.
Schoen opined that the scope of the motion was too narrow because the fee schedule does not include the
frequency of inspections. She requested that the Finance Committee also review the portions of the
regulations that include the frequency of inspections.
Hanneke stated that she would not accept Schoen’s suggestion as a friendly amendment to the current
motion so it would need to be made as a motion to amend.
Rooney clarified that the fee schedule is a formula that could include the timing of fees and the frequency
of fees.
Taub suggested that the Finance Committee could suggest a less frequent inspection schedule as part of
their review of the fee structure.
Griesemer asked Bockelman and Steinberg if they thought the regulations also needed to be referred to
the Finance Committee. Bockelman answered that the regulations would need to be referred to the
Finance Committee in order for the Finance Committee to discuss and recommend changes to the
regulations.

d. Authorization of President to Sign a Letter in Support of An Act to Modernize
Funding for Community Media Programming - Consent Agenda
Hanneke stated that she had a few questions about the bill and that she thought the Council should discuss
the bill before supporting it via consent agenda.
Hanneke discussed how cable subscriptions are decreasing and how cable fees are currently collected and
distributed, stating that these matters are usually negotiated directly between cable companies and
municipalities under a franchise fee. She raised concerns about how the bill would distribute money and
stated that she would likely vote against the bill.
Pam concurred with Hanneke, stating that she was unsure about the letter’s implications and how it would
change the current community media system.
Steinberg stated that he was glad that Hanneke raised these questions. He also mentioned that a
supportive state senator co-sponsored the bill at stake. He proposed amending the letter so that it
expressed supporting legislation that expands the revenue base for cable services without getting specific
about the actual bill.
Griesemer withdrew the item at hand so that she could compare the letter at stake to the Council’s
previous support letter for a similar federal effort.
