4. General Public Comment
James Muspratt asked if there has been any progress in negotiating the usage of underused private parking
lots. He also asked if there is data to document the high demand for on- and off-street downtown parking
and about the parking overlay of zoning which excuses developers from providing parking spaces for
residents.
Ira Bryck urged the Council to implement the recommendations of the last parking study before
expanding the Boltwood parking garage. He also suggested that the developers of large dormitory and
apartment buildings should be responsible for providing parking for residents. Bryck recommended
having a bus dedicated to continuous downtown and village center stops.
Rani Parker expressed that she felt the documents about the parking garage were not put out in sufficient
time for review. She expressed support for Ira Bryck’s comments, particularly those about shuttle use.
Parker also recommended a new study on parking in America and urged the Council to refrain from
making any decisions about the parking garage until there has been more time for the public to review and
comment on the plans.

Rosenthal also urged the Council to take public comment on matters after the presentations on those
matters.

on how the creation of new parking spaces has not kept up with the creation of new apartment complexes
in Town.

b. Final Report – Expansion Facility Study Boltwood Parking Garage
Brestrup provided the history and background information on the study, which was conducted by
Desmond Design Management. She also stated that the Council may want to consider having Desmond
Design Management do a more formal presentation of the study at an upcoming Council meeting.
Nate Malloy presented on the findings of the study.
Griesemer asked when the study was finalized. Bockelman responded that the final study came to the
Town Manager’s office on July 21, 2023.
Pam asked Malloy about usage of the Greenfield garage. Malloy discussed the role of public subsidies
and reserved spaces in the Greenfield garage usage rates.
Taub asked if it would be more costly to add additional spaces to the existing garage or to build a new
structure. Malloy answered that building a new structure would be more costly because of land
acquisition and other factors.
Taub asked how many new potential spaces would be for long-term parking. She also inquired about the
possibility of a developer incurring the cost of building a potential new structure. Malloy responded that
the discussion focused more on the structural feasibility of expanding the garage. Determining
construction costs and other project details would be a next step.
Rooney encouraged the Town staff to update the parking need and capacity numbers.

De Angelis discussed how vertically expanding the Boltwood garage would impact the surrounding
businesses.
Miller asked why the Town is pursuing a site study when they have not yet updated the parking needs
assessment. She also discussed the creative enhancement and utilization of surface parking.
Bockelman stated that the Council requested and funded the parking study.
Griesemer reminded the Council that a parking study was conducted fairly recently. She suggested
determining whether the study needs to be updated before using resources to update it. Griesemer added
that she is not ready to commit to building a new parking garage and that she would rather see greater
utilization of surface parking resources.
Griesemer discussed the history of the parking garage and parking overlay issues and how they were
handled by the previous Amherst Town Meeting. She also raised questions about safety in the lower level
of a potential parking garage.
Schoen elaborated on the history of the parking overlay. Schoen also warned the Council about the cost of
building a small parking garage with less than five hundred spaces.
Devlin Gauthier asked about how often parking studies should be repeated or updated. She also stated that
other infrastructure projects, like road repairs, should be prioritized over a new garage. Malloy answered
that between 2008 and 2018, the ability to handle peak demand stayed approximately the same but also
acknowledged that much changed in Town over the last five years.
Devlin Gauthier asked Malloy how he would qualify significant downtown development in terms of the
numbers of stores and apartments added. Malloy discussed the different parking demands for commercial
and residential units.
Steinberg reminded the Council that the parking study recommended trying to optimize the use of surface
lots that are not used in the evenings.
Bahl-Milne described the 2014 community engagement meetings during which downtown redesign
options were discussed. During these meetings, the two most important things that residents requested
were more parking and a music venue. Bahl-Milne also inquired about the possibility of public-private
partnership parking lots.
Pam questioned the need for an ongoing parking overlay downtown, citing the fact that downtown
development has increased.
Taub provided context and background information on the parking overlay district. She also expressed
concern about a downtown parking garage’s potential impacts on the appearance of Main Street and on
the local downtown shops.
Bockelman challenged the idea that the Town has not implemented any of the parking study’s
recommendations. Mangano then summarized the Town’s recent actions related to parking.
Griesemer stated that before planning a new parking structure, the Town should determine if the parking
study needs to be updated.
Rooney stated that the Council is not yet ready to hear a presentation from the consultant about a potential
Boltwood parking garage. She added that if the Council decides the capacity of a parking garage is
needed, then there should be a complete study on multiple locations and options.

8. Action Items
a. Funding Options for Increase in Councilor Compensation
Miller explained that she requested to remove this item from the consent agenda because she wanted the
option to vote against it being referred to the Finance Committee. She expressed concern that delaying the
matter would violate section 2.4 of the Charter regarding compensation for Councilors.
Griesemer clarified that the Council already voted to implement the increase and that the question at hand
was how to fund the increase.
O’Keeffe stated that she would check on whether delaying the compensation increase would violate the
Charter but expressed that she does not think it would violate the Charter.
In response to a question from Pam about the Finance Committee’s potential authority over the matter at
hand, Griesemer clarified that all committees are recommending bodies to the Town Council. The
Finance Committee would therefore only be able to make recommendations to the Council about how to
fund the increase.
Steinberg expanded on the two clauses of section 2.4 of the Charter.
MOTION: Griesemer moved, seconded by Steinberg, to refer the options to fund the increase in Town
Council compensation to the Finance Committee, with a recommendation and a report to the Town
Council by October 2, 2023.
Mangano presented on the different potential funding options for the Town Councilor compensation
increase.
Hanneke inquired about the plan for distributing the state aid from the Governor’s budget this year. She
also asked if funding the compensation increases through an appropriation of free cash would violate the
budget guidelines.
Mangano responded that there would be a state aid update at tomorrow’s Finance Committee meeting.
Mangano also shared that the state aid increase this year would be relatively small compared to the
overall budget. The Town was therefore not planning for a supplemental increase like the one that
occurred the previous year.
Mangano confirmed that it is the Town’s general practice to increase all operating budgets by the same
amount.
Miller asked if Councilor compensation starting next term would be prorated if the Council selected the
second funding option. Miller then expressed concern about the second proposed funding option and
stated that it may be misleading to those currently running for Town Council. Pam echoed Miller’s
concerns.
Bockelman reminded the Council that they asked for different funding options when they voted on a
compensation increase.

b. Draft Charter Review Committee Charge
VOTED via consent agenda to refer the draft Charter Review Committee Charge to the Governance,
Organization, and Legislation Committee, for substantive review and review of clarity, consistency, and
actionability, with a recommendation and report to the Town Council by October 2, 2023.Griesemer
provided background information about the charter review process and about Michael Ward of the Collins
Institute, who was a consultant to the charter commission. Griesemer also listed the charter provisions
that the charter commission is not able to change.
Griesemer outlined the next steps of the charter commission process, including the referral of the
commission’s charge to the Government, Organization, and Legislation Committee. She urged the
Councilors to send their questions and ideas about the process to the GOL committee.
De Angelis requested that Councilors send their questions and ideas directly to her as the Chair of the
GOL committee.
Hanneke expressed that she does not think the charter commission should be able to request an extension
beyond December 31st. She also expressed concerns about the interim dates listed in the charge and about
the prescriptive nature of the charge. Hanneke requested that O’Keeffe send the Councilors a Word
document version of the charge.
Schoen suggested specifying the scope of the changes the charter commission can make when posting the
call for member applications.
Miller asked about who is eligible to serve on the charter commission. Specifically, she asked if current
Councilors could apply to serve on the commission. Griesemer responded that current Councilors may
apply as long as they are not going to serve on the Council during the term of the charter commission.
Griesemer inquired about when interviews for the charter commission would take place and when the
Council would come forward with an appointment slate of members.
O’Keeffe clarified that members of the charter commission shall be voters not holding elected office
when appointed. Therefore, if appointments occur before the end of the Council’s term a current
Councilor would not be eligible.
Griesemer raised the idea of the current Council appointing some of the charter commission members and
the succeeding Council appointing the rest of the members.
Hanneke clarified that the Council must create the charter commission and appoint its members sometime
during 2024. The charter does not state that the review must be completed by the end of 2024. Hanneke
also asked if the current Council should appoint any of the commission members.

Griesemer introduced the draft proposed timeline and stated that she would like to have a discussion with

proposed meetings.
