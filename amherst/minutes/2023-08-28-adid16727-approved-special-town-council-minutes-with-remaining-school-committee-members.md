1. Call to Order
Griesemer declared the presence of a quorum of the Town Council, called the meeting of the Town
Council to order at 6:32 PM and announced audio and video recording. She then verified that all council
members present can hear and be heard and reviewed the meeting agenda.
Griesemer noted that a quorum of the School Committee was not present due to the resignation of three
School Committee members. A meeting of the School Committee therefore could not be called to order.
2. Announcements
Griesemer announced that the next meeting of the Town Council would be September 11, 2023 at 6:30
PM.
3. Presentation and Discussion – School Committee Vacancies
a. Process
b. Timeline
c. Discussion
Griesemer reviewed the materials in the meeting packet. She then announced that the follow-up to
tonight’s meeting would be to release a vacancy announcement tomorrow.
Griesemer asked for questions and comments on the process section of the memo in the meeting materials
packet.
Shiao inquired about the portion of the memo stating that all candidate materials would be provided at
least three business days before the meeting, noting that the timeline lists two business days instead.

---

Shiao asked how to determine if a second meeting is needed after the initial meeting. Griesemer answered
that it will be determined at the end of the initial meeting, unless there are enough candidates to warrant
two meetings ahead of time.
Shiao asked what other factors will be used to determine whether or not a second meeting is necessary.
Griesemer answered that it will depend on how late the first meeting goes.
Shiao asked for clarification on the voting process. Griesemer answered that voting members will list
their top three candidates by roll call.
Hanneke pointed out that the final bullet on page four of the memo needed to be edited to read that voting
members would list their top three candidates.
Hanneke noted that the third bullet on page three of the memo should state that candidate statements are
to be submitted to the Clerk of Council, not the Town Clerk. She also suggested adding a sentence stating
that the Clerk of Council will forward the statements of interest to the Town Clerk for the purposes of
confirming eligibility.
Hanneke suggested that the Town Clerk’s letters confirming candidate eligibility should be sent to the
Clerk of Council in addition to the members of the Town Council and the School Committee.
Hanneke noted that the interview question development process needs to be clarified now that there is no
Chair of the School Committee. Hanneke also asked for clarification on the voting process.
Schoen suggested using an Excel spreadsheet to track each voting member’s top three choices.
Steinberg stated that he interpreted the proposed voting process differently than Schoen. He also asked if
the Vice Chair of the School Committee remains on the committee. Griesemer answered that Irv Rhodes
is the Vice Chair of the School Committee.
Rhodes clarified that the School Committee policy specifies that the Vice Chair serves in the absence of
the Chair but does not provide insight into how to proceed without a quorum.
Bockelman confirmed that the Vice Chair can serve in the stead of the Chair if the Chair of the School
Committee is not present. He also confirmed that the School Committee cannot be called to order until a
third member is appointed. Bockelman stated that the school attorney would advise on what actions the
Vice Chair can still take in the absence of a quorum.
O’Keeffe stated that the School Committee does still exist despite lacking a third member. She also
confirmed that Rhodes would act as Chair once a quorum was reached.
Pam stated that she opposes the idea of members voting for their top three candidates and that she would
prefer to hold separate votes for each candidate.
Miller asked what outlets the public should use to participate in the process at hand. Griesemer stated that
the public could express their preferences for the School Committee candidates in the upcoming election.
Miller asked the Council their thoughts on creating a method for public participation in the candidate
selection process.

Devlin Gauthier advocated for the creation of a special public comment period prior to the development
of the interview questions. She also advocated for voting members voting for their top three candidates
and stated that the voting process will need to be very clearly spelled out.
Taub expressed support for soliciting public input on the interview questions.
Lopes asked what the Council will need to do to help onboard the incoming School Committee members.
Rhodes answered that there is a required onboarding process undertaken by the School Committee.
Shiao advocated for having a public comment period at the point in the process when the voting members
are deciding which candidates to select. She stated that she is neutral on whether to have public comment
on the interview questions. Shiao also clarified that she is not favor of ranked choice voting but that she
does support having voting members state their top three candidates in no particular order.
Schoen expressed support for soliciting public comment on the interview questions.
Steinberg expressed discomfort with the idea of soliciting public comment because of the exceptional
nature of the current situation. He expressed concern that taking public comment would create a platform
for applicants and candidates in the upcoming election to lobby on their own behalf.
Shiao stated that she thinks taking public comment would be the most democratic thing to do and that she
does not share Steinberg’s concerns about lobbying or campaigning.
Hanneke expressed support for Steinberg’s concerns about potential lobbying or campaigning during
public comment periods, stating that candidates are generally not allowed to use Town resources to
campaign.
Hanneke stated that she thinks the first bullet on page four needs to be reworded in order to conform to
the timeline.
Griesemer stated that, prior to the September 11 meeting, the Town could develop an online mechanism
for people to use to submit interview questions. At the same time, Griesemer stated that she could consult
with the Council and the remaining members of the School Committee to develop a preliminary list of
interview questions.
The Council discussed whether to delete the phrase “and the Chair of the other Board, in consultation
with their respective members” from the first bullet on page four of the memo.
Hanneke asked Bockelman whether he thinks the staff has the capacity to create a question-soliciting
mechanism through Engage Amherst in such a short period of time.
Steinberg proposed an alternate wording for the first bullet on page four of the memo.
Pam opined that an online mechanism would not be a sufficient way to take public input on the matters at
hand.
Bockelman expressed concerns about creating a question-soliciting mechanism through Engage Amherst
in such a short period of time.
De Angelis expressed support for the idea of soliciting potential interview questions from the public, the
Council, and the remaining members of the School Board.

The Council discussed the idea of asking the public to voice their ideas for interview questions during the
general public comment periods of the upcoming Town Council meetings.
Hanneke suggested replacing the word “meeting” with the word “interviews” in the first bullet on page
four of the memo. She also asked who would compile the submitted interview questions into a single
document and whether the Council would vote on the final list of questions.
Griesemer stated that the Council and remaining School Committee members should examine a list of
draft interview questions at the September 11 meeting and should adopt a final list of interview questions
at the September 18 meeting.
Shiao expressed that she does not think the Council needs to prioritize getting public input on the
interview questions. She also stated that the process could be streamlined by empowering the Council
President and Vice Chair to create a draft list of questions. Pam and Miller echoed Schiao’s comments.
Rhodes suggested using lists of past interview questions and perhaps ChatGPT to draft a list of initial
interview questions.
Bahl-Milne expressed support for soliciting public input on the interview questions. She also echoed
Steinberg’s concern about how a public comment period following the interviews could be misused as a
chance to candidates to lobby or campaign.
Griesemer suggested that the first bullet on page four of the memo should read, “Prior to the first
discussion of the interview questions, at a meeting of the Town Council and remaining members of the
Board, the President of the Town Council and the Vice Chair of the other Board, in consultation with
their respective members, and in consideration of the public will jointly develop interview questions. At
the second discussion of the interview questions at a meeting of the Town Council and remaining
members of the Board, the Town Council will adopt a final set of questions”.
Hanneke asked O’Keeffe if Griesemer’s wording effectively created a subcommittee that would be
subject to open meeting law.
O’Keeffe reworded the first bullet on page four of the memo to read, “The Town Council President will
compile interview questions with input from Councilors, remaining members of the Board, and in
consideration of public input, and present them to the Town Council and remaining members of the Board
for a first discussion. At a subsequent meeting, the Town Council will adopt the interview questions”.
Shiao expressed reservations about the new wording of the first bullet on page four of the memo, noting
that it seems to remove the Vice Chair of the School Committee from being involved in the compilation
of interview questions.
Hanneke noted that nothing in the wording of the first bullet on page four of the memo prevents the
Council President from consulting with anyone she chooses before presenting the interview questions to
the Council.
Griesemer stated that taking public comment on candidates during the interview and selection process
could disadvantage some candidates and create an uneven playing field.
Pam expressed concern about not having the Vice Chair of the School Committee in a more official role
during the process at hand.

Shiao advocated for taking public comment at the meeting during which the Town Council will interview
and select candidates.
De Angelis expressed disagreement with Griesemer’s assertion that having a public comment period at
the interview meeting would advantage or disadvantage certain candidates.
Steinberg suggested consulting with an attorney about the question of whether holding public comment at
the interview meeting would violate campaign finance law.
Walker expressed support for taking public comment at the interview meeting.
Devlin Gauthier stated that seeking public input on the interview questions instead of the candidates
themselves would help to minimize politicking during a potential public comment period.
Rhodes echoed Griesemer’s concern that taking public comment on candidates during the interview and
selection process could disadvantage some candidates and create an uneven playing field.
Griesemer stated that the Town would consult with the Town attorney and the Department of Campaign
Finance about the legality of taking public comment on the night of the candidate interviews.
Shiao asked if a decision about whether to take public comment on the night of interviews would be made
at the September 11 Town Council meeting. Griesemer answered affirmatively.
Hanneke pointed out that the timeline bullet for Wednesday, September 20 must indicate that the Clerk of
Council will forward all candidate statements of interest to the Town Clerk for the purposes of confirming
eligibility.
At the bottom of page 8 of the memo, Shiao suggested changing the reference to Budget Coordinating
Group to “on other School Committee subcommittees”. O’Keeffe made this change.
On page nine, Shiao suggested replacing the phrase “the relevant skills and experiences they will bring
the body” with “why they should be selected to fill the vacancy”. O’Keeffe made this change.
Hanneke suggested replacing references to joint meetings with “special meetings”. O’Keeffe made this
change.
Shiao suggested adding that the statements of interest would be available to the public on September 22,
per the timeline. O’Keeffe made this addition to the penultimate paragraph on page nine.
In the Amherst School Committee description, Hanneke suggested adding reference to Union 26
participation. She also inquired about whether the School Committee is currently meeting in person,
virtually, or hybrid.
Rhodes clarified that Union 26 is made up of three members from the Amherst School Committee and
three members of the Pelham School Committee.
Shiao stated that she did not think it was necessary to mention Union 26 in the School Committee
description. She also suggested striking the reference to meeting in the Town Room from the description.

Griesemer reviewed the upcoming Town Council meetings and candidate interview meeting.
