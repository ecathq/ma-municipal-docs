Town of Amherst
Special Meeting of the Town Council and remaining members of the School
Committee
Monday, September 11, 2023, 5:30 p.m.
Town Room, Town Hall
Minutes
Councilors in attendance: Bahl-Milne, De Angelis, Devlin Gauthier, Griesemer, Hanneke, Pam, Rooney
Schoen, Steinberg, Taub, Lopes, Miller, Walker
Councilors absent: none
Others in attendance: Town Manager Bockelman, Clerk of Council O’Keeffe, School Committee
member Irv Rhodes, School Committee member Jennifer Shiao
1. Call to Order
Griesemer declared the presence of a quorum of the Town Council, called the meeting of the Town
Council to order at 5:33 PM and announced audio and video recording. She then verified that all council
members present could hear and be heard and reviewed the meeting agenda.
Griesemer then verified that the two remaining members of the Amherst School Committee could hear
and be heard. Because there is no longer a quorum of the School Committee, a meeting of the School
Committee was not called to order.
2. Announcements
Griesemer announced upcoming meetings including:
 Master Plan Primer, Monday, September 18 at 5:30 PM
 Public forum on the Master Plan, September 18 at 6 PM
 School Committee candidate interviews, September 26 at 6 PM
Griesemer also announced that School Committee candidate statements of interest are due to Clerk of
Council O’Keeffe on Wednesday, September 20 by 4 PM.
3. Public Comment
Vincent O’Connor requested that the Council find ways to better include the public in the School
Committee member appointment process.
Jenna Schwartz raised concerns about how bias in news coverage of the issue at hand may impact the
School Committee candidate interviewing and appointment processes.
Hailey Kelly raised concerns about transphobia in Amherst schools. They also urged the Council to
consider each School Committee candidate’s record on LQBTQ rights during the interviews.

---

Cairo Serna emphasized the importance of considering the needs of students when asking interview
questions of the School Committee candidates. They expressed hope that the new School Committee
members would have a strong commitment to equity.
Richard Roznoy inquired about whether the Council was looking to appoint School Committee members
who also had an interest in running in the November election.
4. Presentation and Discussion – School Committee Vacancies
a. Discussion of Proposed Interview Questions
Griesemer stated that she received potential interview questions from seven Councilors, one School
Committee member, and one member of the public. Griesemer then explained how she used those
suggestions to compile a list of fourteen potential interview questions.
Schoen suggested deleting the sentence “please include any experiences you have with the Amherst
Elementary or Amherst-Pelham Regional Schools” from the questions. She also suggested wordsmithing
the second question to make it shorter.
Shiao suggested omitting the first two potential interview questions. She also stated that she thinks the
Town Council and remaining School Committee members will need two meetings to discuss the
interview process.
Hanneke concurred with Shiao’s points, pointing out that each candidate’s statement of interest would
likely cover the substance of the first two potential interview questions.
Pam expressed opposition to the idea of making the questions available to the candidates ahead of time
and giving each candidate the same questions. She also expressed agreement from the speakers from the
LGBTQ community during the public comment period.
Bahl-Milne raised the idea of developing a rubric for evaluating the School Committee candidates.
Bahl-Milne suggested replacing the first two questions with a single question asking about what the
candidate could bring in terms of specific experience.
Hanneke suggested omitting the potential question about strengths and areas of improvement in Amherst
Public Schools if there are too many other important questions that are higher priority.
Devlin Gauthier concurred with Hanneke and suggested combining the question about strengths and
weaknesses with the first part of question 10. Rooney echoed this idea.
Shiao echoed Hanneke’s point about potentially omitting the strengths and weaknesses question if there
are too many other high-priority questions.
Schoen expressed opposition to combining the strengths and weaknesses question with question 10. She
also suggested emphasizing the actual tasks that School Committee members will undertake during their
eleven weeks of service. Taub echoed this idea.
De Angelis emphasized the importance of addressing the harm that has been done and beginning the
repair process.
Shiao asked why three specific duties of the School Commission were listed in question four at the
exclusion of other duties. She suggested omitting question four and wordsmithing question five to make it
less test-like.

Shiao expressed support for including questions six, seven, and eight.
Rhodes described the onboarding process for new School Committee members and concurred with
Shiao’s suggestion to make the questions less test-like. He also suggested eliminating question four.
Rhodes stated that he would like to see question eight expanded upon because being able to process and
respond to public criticism is a key aspect of School Committee service.
Hanneke expressed willingness to eliminate question four based on Shiao and Rhodes’ comments. She
also suggested eliminating question five and expressed support for including questions six and seven.
Hanneke echoed Rhodes’ suggestion to expand upon question eight. She also suggested eliminating the
last two parts of question nine and keeping only the first part.
Bahl-Milne spoke about the balance between advocacy and process in the roles of elected officials.
Lopes inquired about the time limitations for candidate questions during the interview process.
Specifically, she asked if there would be time for candidates to potentially ask about the substance of the
omitted questions.
Griesemer opined that making time for candidate questions during the interviews might not be a
productive way to assess them.
Schoen agreed with Shiao, Rhodes, and Hanneke that questions four and five should be omitted. She
expressed support for including questions six and seven. She also suggested omitting question nine.
De Angelis suggested amending question eight to ask the candidates about a time they were discriminated
against and how they handled it.
Shiao suggested adding an introductory sentence to question eight about the fact that School Committee
members can receive public feedback that is negative or charged. She also suggested omitting question
nine and making De Angelis’s suggestion its own standalone question.
Taub expressed concern that some of the questions could not be substantively answered in the two
minutes allotted to each candidate per question.
Pam expressed support for De Angelis’s suggested question about a time each candidate was harmed or
discriminated against.
Devlin Gauthier expressed concern about asking candidates to relive or rehash their own traumatic
experiences. She suggested reframing De Angelis’s suggested question to be about how each candidate
has supported individuals who have been harmed or has engaged in repair work.
Rhodes suggested combining questions seven and eight. He also expressed support for Devlin Gauthier’s
proposed question and suggested eliminating question nine.
Shiao stated that De Angelis’s proposed question about experiencing harm could be framed as a
hypothetical. She also stated that she would like to keep questions seven and eight separate.
Griesemer summarized the Council’s discussion on questions four through nine, stating that questions
four, five, and nine would be eliminated and that a new question about dealing with harm would be
formulated.

Steinberg listed the search for a new superintendent and the development of a budget as two of the most
important tasks currently facing the School Committee. He also asked Shiao and Rhodes for their input on
the School Committee’s most pressing issues at hand.
Shiao suggested making the last sentence of question ten its own question. She also suggested reframing
the question about search process involvement to prevent it from being a “yes/no” question.
Regarding question fourteen, Shiao asked if the appointed School Committee members would be
involved in the beginning of the budget process during their eleven weeks of service. Rhodes answered
that they would be involved in the budget process.
Councilor Michelle Miller and Councilor Ellisha Walker joined the meeting at 6:37 PM.
Schoen suggested rewording the question fourteen to be about the candidates’ objectives related to the
budget. She also suggested omitting or rewording the question about ensuring that the School
Committee’s practices, priorities, and decisions are anti-racist.
Hanneke suggested rewording or removing the first part of question twelve. She also suggested
broadening the question about anti-racism to cover other types of discrimination and eliminating question
thirteen.
Hanneke suggested rewording question fourteen to read: “If appointed, you will be part of the start of the
planning for the fiscal year 2025 school budgets. As a School Committee member, how would you
approach those meetings or those conversations?”
Rhodes suggested rewording the question about anti-racism to read: “How will you help to make sure that
the School Committee’s practices, processes, and decisions include the values of DEI (diversity, equity,
and inclusion)?”
Rhodes spoke about the numerous pressures and important deadlines facing the School Committee and
expressed concerns about the newly appointed members having to take part in unfamiliar processes.
Shiao spoke about the importance of keeping the question about anti-racism, but also agreed to add equity
and inclusivity to it.
De Angelis expressed support for Shiao’s comments on anti-racism and stated that she would prefer to
keep the original wording of the question instead of changing it to be about DEI.
Lopes suggested being more specific about what is meant by the term “anti-racist”, pointing to the fact
that there are many different understandings of that term.
Rhodes inquired about how to operationalize the term “anti-racist”.
Walker suggested keeping the term “anti-racist” in the question but also adding language about equity and
inclusion to it.
Shiao stated that each candidate’s answer to the question about anti-racism would reveal their
understanding of the term and would provide useful information about them as candidates.
Bahl-Milne expressed support for keeping the term “anti-racist” in the question but expanding it to
include LGBTQ and gender inclusivity.
Rhodes spoke further on the importance of being able to operationalize the term “anti-racist”.
