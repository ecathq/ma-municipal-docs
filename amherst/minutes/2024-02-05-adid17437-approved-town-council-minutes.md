Agenda item 8.a. Joint Meeting with the Amherst Housing Authority: Election to fill
vacancies on the Housing Authority for terms ending January 5, 2026
Members of the Town Council and remaining members of the Amherst Housing Authority interviewed
applicant Michael Burkart:
Why would you like to join the Housing Authority?
Burkart spoke about his experience with the Housing Authority in Louisville, KY.
How to hold chief executive accountable to the board’s goals?
Burkart spoke about his relationship with the chief executive and not allowing personal relationships to
interfere with duties.
Strengths and weaknesses of the board?
Burkart spoke about bringing the board up to meeting regulations and bringing team on board to create a
solid team and meet requirements. Challenges in retaining good staff.
How to increase affordable housing?
Burkart spoke about the need to have someone build things. The state won’t but there is state money that
is becoming available and the ED is good at applying for grants. Challenges with maintenance staff and
sharing staff with other towns. Other challenge is dealing with aging infrastructure.
What relevant experience do you bring to the role?
Burkart spoke about experience on the board, chairing the board, understand organizational dynamics,
dealing with conflict. Educating people about what the Housing Authority does and how things work with
the state regulations. Need to have a connection with community agencies and letting people know they
are there and there is help for them.
Burkart added that he is looking forward to continuing to work with other Housing Authority members.
MOTION: Devlin Gauthier moved, second by Griesemer, to elect Michael Burkart, registered voter in
the Town of Amherst, to the Amherst Housing Authority, for a term ending January 5, 2026.
The Town Council and remaining members of the Housing Authority
VOTED Unanimously with 1 absent (Lord) by roll call to elect Michael Burkart, registered voter in the
Town of Amherst, to the Amherst Housing Authority, for a term ending January 5, 2026.
The Housing Authority adjourned their meeting at 6:52 p.m.

b. Public Way Requests
1) Other Requests for Permanent Changes to the Public Way: Request to place an
Amherst College Sign at the intersection of College Street and South Pleasant
Street
MOTION: Griesemer moved, second by De Angelism, to refer the request to place an Amherst College
sign in the public way at the corner of College Street and South Pleasant Street to the Town Services and
Outreach Committee, with a report and recommendation to the Town Council by March 18, 2024.
Seth Wilschutz, Assistant Director of Planning, Design, and Construction at Amherst College, spoke
about the request to place an Amherst College sign at the corner of College and South Pleasant Streets.
Rooney spoke in support of the signage and asked about visibility for drivers coming up College Street.
Hanneke expressed concern with the size of the sign, noting the Town’s welcome signs are much smaller,
and asked if the diagrams reflect the current conditions at the corner.
VOTED Unanimously with 2 absent (Lord and Walker) to refer the request to place an Amherst College
sign in the public way at the corner of College Street and South Pleasant Street to the Town Services and
Outreach Committee, with a report and recommendation to the Town Council by March 18, 2024.
2) Acceptance of roads as public ways: Amherst Hills Subdivision off Station Road
MOTION: Griesemer moved, second by Rooney, that the Town Council hereby declares its intention to
lay out Hawthorn Road, Concord Way, and Linden Ridge Road (excluding the cul-de-sac) as public ways
and to refer the layout and plan to the Planning Board for its review in accordance with MGL c.41, s.81G
– 81I; with report back to the Town Council within 45 days from this referral.
Bockelman spoke about the process required for the Town to accept the roads as public ways.
Walker rejoined the meeting at 9:03 p.m.
Ted Parker spoke on behalf of Tofino, the project developer.
James Masteralexis spoke on behalf of the Amherst Hills neighborhood association, urging the Council to
refer the request to the Planning Board and ultimately to accept the roads as public ways without delay.
John Kennedy added that the Amherst Hills roads are in a different situation from the Meadows
subdivision, that the roads in Amherst Hills are ready to be accepted and should not be conflated with the
Meadows request to accept their roads as public ways.
Hanneke asked for a clarification of the part of Linden Ridge Road that is included in the request in terms
of the cul-de-sac and asked about the wastewater pumping station on Station Road as well as the cross-
country sewer and drainage easements associated with this project included in the Manager’s memo.
VOTED Unanimously by roll call with 1 absent (Lord) that the Town Council hereby declares its
intention to lay out Hawthorn Road, Concord Way, and Linden Ridge Road (excluding the cul-de-sac) as
public ways and to refer the layout and plan to the Planning Board for its review in accordance with MGL
c.41, s.81G – 81I; with report back to the Town Council within 45 days from this referral.
3) Acceptance of roads as public ways: The Meadows Subdivision: Kestrel Lane
and Hop Brook Road
Bockelman spoke about the request the Council previously referred to the Planning Board. The Home
Owners Association has agreed to contribute to the cost of bringing the roads to standard as has the Town
but the developer has not agreed to share the cost.

Douglas Dinell, President of the Meadows HOA, spoke about the history of the development, which was
completed in 2004; adding that the home owners have been waiting 20 years to settle this matter, the
developer owns the road but does not want to maintain it, while the Town does not want to accept the
roads until they are improved. The homeowners cannot contract to repair the roads as they do not own
them but offer to contribute funds to improve the roads in order for the Town to accept the roads.
Connie Kruger spoke about her experience as a home owner, stating that Tofino still owes the Town the
uncollected surety funds to complete the work and that this is an unintended problem that has gone on
way too long.
Others from the home owners association spoke in support of the Town accepting the roads as public
ways.
MOTION: Hanneke moved, second by Rooney, to direct the Town Manager to obtain the items in his
memo to the Council prior to considering this item again.
VOTED Unanimously with 1 absent (Lord) to direct the Town Manager to obtain the items in his memo
to the Council prior to considering this item again.
