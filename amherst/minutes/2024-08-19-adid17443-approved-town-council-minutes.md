8. Action Items
a. Proposed Transportation and Parking Commission Charge and Amendments to the
Town Council Policy on the Control and Regulation of the Public Ways
MOTION: Griesemer moved, second by Rooney, to refer the proposed Transportation and Parking
Commission Charge and Amendments to the Town Council Policy on the Control and Regulation of the

---

Public Ways to the Town Services and Outreach Committee, with subsequent review for clarity,
consistency, and actionability by the Governance, Organization, and Legislation Committee, with reports
to the Town Council by October 21, 2024.
Town Manager Bockelman spoke about the purpose of the proposed new Commission and the goal of
making the process more functional, more responsive to resident requests, and to make a clearer process.
The proposal would require the Town Council to delegate its authority as keepers of the public ways to
the new commission in several areas, as outlined in the proposed charge and changes to the Council
Policy on the Control and Regulation of the Public Ways. The proposal echoes similar committees and
processes in other communities. There is also frustration among members of the Transportation Advisory
Committee about their role; establishing a new commission and dissolving the TAC would be amenable
to TAC members.
Councilor Taub joined the meeting at 7:32 pm.
Councilors discussed the proposal:
 Some communities have Councilors serve on a transportation commission; possibility of adding a
Councilor to membership
 Establishing policies for public ways
 Concerns about the membership; lack of a Councilor and specificity of the resident members’
focus and expertise
 Questions regarding the decision-making authority for long-term parking reservation requests
 Permanent or other requests; would the Council retain authority to promulgate policy to consider
requests but delegate to proposed commission
 If permanent closures of sidewalks and streets would be delegated
 How to define “major roadway and sidewalk redesign”
 No equivalent to traffic control that is in the charge in the Council policy
 Large number of municipalities’ commission have recommendation authority to the Council
rather than decision-making power
 Possibility to delegate hearings and recommendations to the commission and Council to retain
final approval and other options for recommendations and approval mechanisms
 Need for clarity for residents, staff, and Town committee members
 Need to align the charge with the policy
 Discomfort with delegating authority to approve permanent changes to the public way
 Requirements for public hearings for permanent changes to the public way
 Preference for the commission making recommendations rather than decisions
 Town Council power to rescind its delegation
 Desire to empower residents to participate in a way that is meaningful
 Council authority to modify the charge and the policy; Council adoption of the charge or Town
Manager
VOTED Unanimously by roll call to refer the proposed Transportation and Parking Commission Charge
and Amendments to the Town Council Policy on the Control and Regulation of the Public Ways to the
Town Services and Outreach Committee, with subsequent review for clarity, consistency, and
actionability by the Governance, Organization, and Legislation Committee, with reports to the Town
Council by October 21, 2024.

2) Motion postponed by Councilor Schoen on July 15, 2024 per Charter Sec.
2.10(c): To appoint Lawrence Kluttz to the Planning Board for a term beginning
immediately and ending June 30, 2027.
Rooney spoke about the qualifications and expertise of the resident who was recommended by the
Community Resource Committee, Melissa Farris.
Schoen stated she will abstain in protest because she is appalled by the appointment process and that a
member of the Community Resources Committee did not disclose that they had recruited Kluttz to apply
for the Planning Board.
Steinberg agreed that the process was troubling, and that the recommendation process should be
reevaluated for a more robust and meaningful selection process that is fair to the candidates and
encourages people to apply for Council-appointed positions.
Taub stated that as per Special Permit article 10, Section 10.30, the Town solicits abutter input and when
abutters avail themselves of the opportunity to offer input at ZBA meetings, that should not be a reason to
deny them a seat on Council-appointed boards and committees. De Angelis agreed that the process needs
reexamining; the Council does not need to “live” by a committee’s recommendation. De Angelis spoke in
support of Kluttz’ appointment due to his comments about diversity and his ability to collaborate with
others. De Angelis stated that Kluttz spoke about needing feedback from abutters but that ultimately the
board is the decision-making body, and that this comment was not well received by some Community
Resources Committee members.
Hanneke spoke about the appointment policy and that if Councilors have proposed changes to the policy,
they can be presented to the Council for consideration. Hanneke spoke about her support for Kluttz;
Kluttz spoke about cautiousness with approving waivers in consideration of the Master Plan and that if
there are consistent waivers that a zoning law should be evaluated to see if an amendment would be
appropriate.
Taub spoke about her perspective of the responses from Kluttz and Farris during the interviews.
Devlin Gauthier spoke critically about abstention votes in protest.
Griesemer spoke in support of Kluttz’ appointment and spoke about the difficulty finding residents to
serve on Town committees.
