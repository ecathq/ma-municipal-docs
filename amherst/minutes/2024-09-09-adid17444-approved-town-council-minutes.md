a. Bylaw 3.33 Refuse Collection and Recyclable Materials; Waste Hauler Program:
Town Services and Outreach Committee Recommendation to advise the Town
Manager to issue a Request for Proposals (RFP) for Town Contracted Waste Hauler
Service
Ryan presented the recommendation of the Town Services and Outreach Committee; a Town contracted
system with a robust pay-as-you-throw fee structure, and curbside composting. TSO recommended the
Council advise the Town Manager to issue a request for proposals to gather information about available
services and costs.
Discussion:
 Cost of consultant
 Inclusion of businesses, apartment complexes, and colleges
 Cost of the potential program
 When will the draft bylaw be presented
 Will Council see the RFP prior to its issuance
 Cost to residents, Town, and potential need for additional staff to administrate the
program
 Timeline of implementation includes space to adjust and negotiate
 Need for more robust outreach
 Coordination with Board of Health to develop and revise regulations
 Commercial food disposal ban already addresses businesses and colleges
 The Town will not be required to commit to a contract following with RFP process
 State laws address food waste from the “top-down;” the Town can address the problem
from the “bottom-up”
 Questions regarding the transfer station; will it remain open with existing services
 Impact to renters, barriers to economic justice
 Consider cost to all residents
 Intent to keep transfer station open with existing services
MOTION: Steinberg moved, second by Rooney, to advise the Town Manager to issue a Request for
Proposals (RFP) on behalf of proposed changes to General Bylaw 3.33, Refuse Collection and Recyclable
Materials.
Discussion:
 All major haulers responded to the request for information, but more specific costs will not be
known until the RFP responses are received
 Need specific language in the motion to include in the RFP
 No staff capacity for development of an RFP
 Need key features of a program in the motion

MOTION: Hanneke moved, second by Devlin Gauthier, to amend the previous motion by replacing the
motion language with the following:
To advise the Town Manager to issue a Request for Proposals (RFP) in accordance with the
below goals, in anticipation of revising General Bylaw 3.33, Refuse Collection and Recyclable
Materials:
1. Town would negotiate and contract with a waste hauler or haulers on behalf of residents
for collection of household trash, unlimited recyclables, and compostables
2. The contract would include a robust pay-as-you-throw (PAYT) fee structure
3. Curbside composting would be made available to all residents
4. Transfer station could remain open but its role in the waste management program has yet
to be determined
5. Program would be phased in beginning with single family and 2-, 3-, and 4-unit
properties, expanding to all residential properties within 3 years
6. Haulers must provide an annual report to the Town on the weight, in tons, of trash,
recyclables, and compostables collected within the Town
7. An advantage in awarding a contract would be given to haulers who dispose of compost
locally
Discussion:
 Key features taken from the TSO report
 Transfer station status with a new program

b. Amendments to Bylaw 3.26 Nuisance Property – First Reading
Rooney reported on the Community Resources Committee review and recommendation of the bylaw, as
presented, including the health and safety goals of the bylaw as well as enjoyment of neighborhoods by
residents.
Devlin Gauthier reported on the Governance, Organization, and Legislation Committee review and
declaration that the bylaw is clear, consistent, and actionable. Rooney also reviewed the restrictions and
enforcement of the bylaw.
Discussion:
 Number of fines per offense
 Who is responsible for fines
 When is rental permit terminated due to violations
 Increase in penalties due to repeat incidents
 Stronger repercussions after multiple violations
 Bylaw applied to all properties, not only rentals
 Questions about general bylaw violations with $50 fines; the proposed bylaw lists $300 fines for
some of the same violations
 The rental bylaw addresses rental properties; nuisance properties could be identified in the rental
bylaw
 The bylaw would likely not be the first tool the Town uses to request residents correct issues such
as obstructions to sidewalks
 Each person could be fined, and fines could be written each day, depending on how the bylaw is
enforced
 The Police Chief stated this would be one tool among many available to address issues
 Bylaw states it is a violation to violate other state and local laws
 Use of the phrase “offensive to community moral standards”
 Public nuisance versus nuisance property bylaw title
 Communication with residents about implementation of the bylaw
 Apparent target of a subset of residents
 Accrual of multiple $300 fines
 Problem that the bylaw addresses
 Intent to address houses with multiple violations with intent for corrective action
 Participation of Building Commissioner and Police Chief during development of the bylaw
 Neighbors to problem properties seeking corrective action
 Intent to hold property owners accountable for tenants’ behavior
 Applies to owner-occupied, long-term, and short-term (AirBnB) rentals
 Need to address problem properties historically
 Utilizing CRESS responders to educate residents of the impact of their behavior on other
residents without adding additional fines and penalties

 Inequity of enforcement
 Enforcement not the only means of altering behavior
 Question if police need the bylaw to address issues; if the current bylaws are not adequate

 Bias in response
 Suggestion for added clarity to eliminate ambiguity in enforcement
 Need for clearer definition of “public nuisance”
