b. Amendments to Bylaw 3.26 Nuisance Property
1) Council Discussion, Motions, and Votes
MOTION: Griesemer moved, seconded by Hegner, to rescind existing General Bylaw 3.26, Nuisance
House and replace it with General Bylaw 3.26, Nuisance Property, as shown on pages 9-13 of the
Motions Sheet.
Griesemer asked Police Chief Ting and Building Inspector Morra how they envision the implementation
of the new bylaw. Chief Ting responded that he does not think it will change much regarding police
operations, unless a property receives four “strikes” or complaints and the police then involve the
property’s landlord in the resolution process. Chief Ting also stated that the new bylaw makes clear which
violations would be handled by the police and which violations would be handled by the Building
Inspector’s department. Morra added that the proposed bylaw allows the police and Inspection Services to
work together to resolve situations involving nuisance properties; the existing bylaw only allowed for
police enforcement.
Hegner asked Chief Ting how many properties in town have been designated nuisance properties and
about the scope of nuisance properties as an issue. Ting responded that the number of nuisance properties
varies from year to year and that most nuisance properties are regular sites of large-scale parties that
produce noise complaints.
Rooney noted that at a recent District 2 meeting, someone asked what would happen after a property’s
second, third, fourth, and fifth violations under the new bylaw. She asked if Ting or Morra could respond
to this question. Ting described how neighborhood liaisons notify landlords after violations and how the
proposed bylaw enhances the police’s ability to address serious cases in which a property gets more than
one violation. Ting also noted that after first violations the recidivism rate is very low. Morra expanded
on Ting’s first point, detailing how the proposed bylaw connects nuisance property violations to rental
inspection schedules and rental permit renewals.
Schoen asked if there is currently a record of nuisance property enforcement actions. Ting responded that
the police do maintain such a record.
Rooney noted that the bylaw requires that property owners or managers be notified each time their
property is issued a violation.
Steinberg inquired about the involvement of the Fire Department and CRESS in the enforcement of the
proposed bylaw. Ting responded that the Fire Department has their own avenues and processes for
addressing violations and that CRESS cannot issue citations.
Walker inquired about the possibility of CRESS fielding noise complaints once they expand and are open
later. Ting responded that he does not think CRESS currently has the capacity to take this on but that it
could be revisited in the future. Town Manager Bockelman added that the proposed bylaw only specifies

---

that two groups—the police and Inspection Services—can enforce it and that it would have to be amended
if the Fire Department or CRESS were given enforcement responsibilities.
MOTION TO AMEND: Schoen moved, seconded by Taub, to amend the proposed bylaw by adding a
clause stating that a rental property that is designated a nuisance property two years in a row shall risk
losing its rental permit as well as more frequent inspections as required under Regulation B2 or
regulations of the General Bylaw 3.5.
Hanneke asked for clarification regarding the Regulation B2 that was mentioned in Schoen’s motion.
Griesemer asked Schoen to correct the reference.
Walker inquired about situations in which a property was designated a nuisance property two years in a
row but had different occupants/tenants each year. She also asked about the possible corrective actions for
landlords who lost rental permits due to repeat violations. Schoen responded that a property with repeat
violations would still be an issue of manager or landlord responsibility, even if the property was occupied
by different tenants at the times of the different violations. Morra added that landlords who lost their
rental permits could regain their ability to obtain future permits by working with Inspection Services or
the Board of License Commissioners to devise and execute a property management plan.
Hanneke noted that a violation or nuisance property violation can remain on record for a property until
the owner requests that it be removed. She also expressed concern about adding penalties for rental
properties that would not apply to non-rental properties, such as the revocation of a rental permit.
Taub stated that because rental properties operate more like businesses, it makes sense to have different
penalties for them than for privately occupied properties. She also pointed out that the proposed bylaw
does not necessitate the revocation of rental permits for violators but simply leaves it as an enforcement
option.
Rooney asked Ting or Morra to clarify what Bylaw 3.50 states regarding the revocation of rental permits.
Morra responded that the rental registration bylaw states that, to receive a rental permit, a property must
be in compliance with all local bylaws and all other local and state health and building codes. Under that
provision the Town has the authority not to issue or renew rental permits for noncompliant properties, but
he does not think the language covers the ability to revoke or suspend existing permits for nuisance
properties.
Hanneke stated that she does not think it is appropriate to create penalties for Bylaw 3.50 that are only
listed within Bylaw 3.26. She then asked Morra if the nuisance property designation under Bylaw 3.26
would be a reason to deny a rental permit under Bylaw 3.50. Morra answered that if a designated nuisance
property has a corrective action plan in place, the designation alone would not prevent the owner from
receiving or renewing their rental permit.
Steinberg suggested that it might be more efficient to refer the proposed bylaw back to the Community
Resources Committee (CRC) and the Governance, Organization, and Legislation (GOL) Committee for
further wordsmithing. Taub responded that CRC had already discussed this particular amendment and that
it did not pass when the Committee voted on it. De Angelis and Devlin Gauthier concurred. Devlin
Gauthier also stated that it would have been helpful to know at the beginning of the discussion on this

---

amendment that CRC had already voted it down. Taub reminded the Council that past recommendations
have sometimes been passed by CRC but voted down by the Council.
VOTED eight to four by roll call with one absent (Ryan) to amend the proposed bylaw by adding a
clause stating that a rental property that is designated a nuisance property two years in a row shall risk
losing its rental permit as well as more frequent inspections as required under Regulation B2 or
regulations of the General Bylaw 3.5.
MOTION TO AMEND: Hanneke moved, seconded by Devlin Gauthier, to amend section B7 to delete
the phrase “offensive to community moral standards”.
VOTED unanimously by roll call with one absent (Ryan) to amend section B7 of the proposed bylaw to
delete the phrase “offensive to community moral standards”.
MOTION TO AMEND: Hanneke moved, seconded by De Angelis, to amend section B3 by deleting the
phrase “or otherwise occupying” and replacing it with the word “at”.
Hanneke explained that she proposed this amendment because “occupying” carries a different meaning
than “residing” and the definition intends to reference the latter.
VOTED unanimously by roll call with one absent (Ryan) to amend section B3 by deleting the phrase “or
otherwise occupying” and replacing it with the word “at”.
MOTION TO AMEND: Hanneke moved, second by Ette, to amend section A by adding the phrase
“repeated or persistent” after “public nuisance by the” and to amend sections B7 and C2 by adding the
phrase “repeated or persistent” after the phrase “but is not limited to”.
Hanneke spoke to the reasons Councilor Ryan proposed this amendment following a CRC discussion
about the meaning of “nuisance”.
VOTED unanimously by roll call with one absent (Ryan) to amend section A by adding the phrase
“repeated or persistent” after “public nuisance by the” and to amend sections B7 and C2 by adding the
phrase “repeated or persistent” after the phrase “but is not limited to”.
VOTED ten to two by roll call with one absent (Ryan) to rescind existing General Bylaw 3.26, Nuisance
House and replace it with General Bylaw 3.26, Nuisance Property, as shown on pages 9-13 of the
Motions Sheet.
c. Amherst Black Reparations Committee Charge and Special Municipal Employee
Status
MOTION: Griesemer moved, seconded by Devlin Gauthier, to adopt the Amherst Black Reparations
Committee (ABRC) Charge, as presented.
Devlin Gauthier, the Chair of the Governance, Organizations, and Legislation (GOL) Committee,
presented on the background and genesis of the proposed charge. She stated that the ABRC’s first task
will be to make feasible recommendations to the Council for adoption. She added that the ABRC has an
ongoing obligation and responsibility to consult regularly with the Black community about the allocation
of funds.

---

Griesemer recognized that Dr. Shabazz and Michelle Miller, who helped to draft the proposed charge,
were in the audience.
MOTION TO AMEND: Lord moved, seconded by Walker, to change the length of term from three
years to two years, with the possibility of renewal.
Schoen expressed concern about two-year terms because she thought that two years may not be enough
time to grapple with the series of complex issues that will be before ABRC members and to devise a
process for collecting and assessing proposals.
Devlin Gauthier asked for clarification that the possibility of renewal is a given for all committee
appointments and therefore does not need to be specified in the charge. Town Manager Bockelman
confirmed that this is true. Devlin Gauthier then proposed a friendly amendment to Lord’s motion to
remove the phrase “with the possibility of renewal”. Lord and Walker accepted this friendly amendment.
Devlin Gauthier also stated that the GOL modeled much of their language in the charge after the
Community Preservation Act (CPA) Committee because their work was similar in nature. The CPA
membership terms are set at three years to account for the learning curve on the allocation and budgeting
process.
Ette asked if ABRC memberships renew automatically after two years and if there is a maximum number
of membership renewals. Bockelman stated that, per the charter, when new committees are formed
members can be appointed for terms of various lengths so they don’t all expire at the same time. He also
stated that term renewals are not automatic and are subject to reappointment by the Town Manager and
confirmation by the Town Council. Most committees try to limit individuals to no more than two three-
year terms, or six years total.
Lord explained that she moved to amend the length of term to two years because she thought that a two-
year time commitment may make participation on the committee more accessible to a wider range of
people, specifically BIPOC people, people with young families, and working people.
Ette raised the idea of staggering terms so that participation could be made more accessible, but
committee members could also gain the skills and experience that come from serving longer terms.
Hegner opined that his previous two-year terms on the Finance Committee gave him plenty of time to
learn about the issues relevant to the committee’s work.
De Angelis stated that she would like to see a mix of two-year and three-year terms for the ABRC.
VOTED nine to three with one absent (Ryan) to change the length of term from three years to two years,
with the possibility of renewal.
Hanneke expressed concern that some points in the charge were essentially asking the ABRC to redo
work that had already been done by the African Heritage Reparation Assembly (AHRA). She asked for
clarification on how the ABRC’s work would be different from the findings in the AHRA’s final report.
She also asked if, per the charge, the Council would be able to allocate funds outside of the ABRC’s
recommendations, about the committee’s process for deliberating on community proposals, and whether
the committee has the ability to establish its own projects and priorities outside of what the Town Council
adopts.

---

Devlin Gauthier responded that the committee would recommend priorities from the AHRA final report
because it would have more specialized knowledge about the scope of its work than the Town Council
would. The ABRC would not redo the work of the AHRA but would assess the feasibility of the AHRA’s
recommendations and use the AHRA’s findings to recommend projects to the Council. She also stated
that she interpreted “a process determined by the body” as including a process for deliberating on
proposals before accepting them. She clarified that the committee would be recommending projects to the
Council instead of establishing its own projects.
Schoen asked if the committee could recommend projects to the Council that were not outlined in the
AHRA’s final report, such as projects working with pre-K children. She also recommended some changes
to the section of the charge about soliciting proposals from the community.
De Angelis reminded the Council that the purpose of the committee was to pursue reparations for the
deep and lasting damage that slavery did to the Black community in the United States.
In response to Schoen’s question, Devlin Gauthier stated that the words “based on the report” were
specifically left out of the charge so that the committee can recommend projects beyond the scope of the
AHRA report.
Hanneke noted that there did not seem to be a connection in the charge between the priority levels
determined by the committee and recommendations for the expenditure of funds. She also asked if the
Council’s role in the committee’s work would go beyond its ability to fund projects.
Hanneke posed a question about when the Town would revise its bylaws, policies, and regulations that
result in structurally racist outcomes. She asked if the ABRC committee should be charged with that
work.
Hanneke, Griesemer, and Devlin Gauthier discussed the possibility of referring the charge back to the
GOL committee.
In response to Hanneke’s point about priority levels, Devlin Gauthier echoed her earlier point about the
ABRC having the specialized knowledge to set priority levels for the recommendations in the AHRA
report. She also stated that the Town Council’s role in the ABRC’s work would be reviewing,
deliberating, and approving funding decisions. She opined that councilors are responsible for bylaw
review and amendment proposals and that she did not believe that extensive bylaw review was an
appropriate charge for the ABRC.
De Angelis put forward the idea of putting together a working committee made up of councilors, DEI
department members, and Human Rights Committee members to review bylaws for structurally racist
outcomes.
Taub expressed that she felt comfortable with the ABRC charge as formulated by GOL and approved by
the chairs of the AHRA.
Hanneke stated that she does not think that “reparations” is specific enough as a word and as a concept to
be delegated to an unelected committee instead of the Town Council. She added that there are many
different opinions on what constitutes reparations and that the AHRA report expresses many different
thoughts on the meaning of reparations.

---

MOTION: Griesemer moved, to refer the ABRC charge back to GOL for further work.
De Angelis noted that the CPA Committee is made up of unelected representatives and that all their
proposals are brought to the Council for approval. She sees little difference between this format and the
proposed format of the ABRC.
Lord stated that the Council already voted with the public’s good faith to allot funding to these efforts.
She also echoed De Angelis’s point that the Council would be able to vote on whether to fund each of the
ABRC’s proposed projects, just as they do with the CPA’s proposals.
Walker echoed Devlin Gauthier, De Angelis, and Lord’s points about the Council’s ability to vote on
funding the ABRC’s proposed projects. She also stated that she does not find this process to be
significantly different from the processes used by any other committees and echoed Devlin Gauthier’s
point that the Council does not have the necessary expertise to make reparations-related funding
recommendations without the input of a committee made up of members of the Black community.
Rooney stated that she does not support send the ABRC charge back to GOL because she thinks the
proposed committee structure that GOL put forth is already appropriate.
VOTED eight to four with one absent (Ryan) NOT to refer the ABRC charge back to GOL for further
work.
Hanneke exercised her right to postpone the vote to adopt the ABRC charge to the next Town Council
meeting so that she could have time to draft further amendments.
