7. Presentations and Discussion
a. Human Rights Commission Report
Human Rights Commission Co-Chair Rani Parker provided an overview of the Human Rights
Commission Report and walked the Town Council through each of its sections. She noted that a major
accomplishment of the Commission this year was the finalization of its bylaws.
Parker noted that the Human Rights Commission received ten complaints in 2024. She explained that
complaints are handled by the DEI Department so that complainant confidentiality can be maintained.
Liz Haygood spoke about the Commission’s goals for events in the coming year.
Deb Kolodny described how the Commission designed a three-year plan that expands their work beyond
receiving complaints. This plan includes community education about what human rights are and the
production of a short informational video on human rights that will be mandatory for all municipal and
school district employees and town committee members to watch. They will also encourage local
business owners to watch the video.
Kolodny stated that in year two of the plan, the Commission will engage in data collection about
residents’ experiences of diversity and inclusivity in different municipal institutions, including businesses,
municipal entities, and schools. Once they have gathered data, the Commissions plans to explore
appropriate avenues for accountability.
Finally, Parker stated that the Commission is keen to work more closely with the Town Council. She also
thanked the Town Manager for helping the Commission fill all but one of its seats.
Hanneke mentioned that she was surprised to see in the report that the Commission expected Town
Council action on its bylaw. She asked the Town Manager and Council President why the Commission’s
draft bylaw had not yet been on the Council’s agenda for review and adoption.
Bockelman explained that the Commission’s draft had gone back and forth between the Town Attorney
and DEI Department. He stated that he still needs to put together a memo for the Town Council that
includes the draft along with the Town Attorney’s opinion. He hopes to have it ready for the Town
Council by the end of the month of October 2024.
Taub asked if the Amherst Human Rights Commission gets word on the resolution of complaints that are
referred up to the State Commission. Haygood responded that the Commission gets a report on such
outcomes from the DEI Department at each of its monthly meetings. Parker then discussed the
Commission’s deliberations on what complaint data should be made available to the public in its reports,
like the one before the Town Council. She also clarified that the Commission is not always informed
about the conclusions of complaints and stated that she is interested in seeing more informal resolution
opportunities that do not involve the law.
Ette inquired about whether the Commission has criteria for the kinds of events it considers cosponsoring.
Kolodny responded that the Commission is quite expansive in its co-sponsoring considerations and that
the bigger issue is the number of events that the Commission and DEI Department must spearhead

---

themselves. She expressed hope that holding a smaller number of events could help bring different
cultural communities together. Haygood added that Commission members try to attend the meetings and
events of other town committees and groups to get an idea of how the Commission can work in tandem
with them and support their missions.
De Angelis asked if the Human Rights Commission has conducted any education for businesses about
wage theft. She also inquired about whether the Commission has drafted any reports related to wage
issues. Parker responded that she was not previously aware that the Commission was expected to be
involved in this issue.

b. Proposed Establishment of School Safety Zones at Amherst Regional Middle School
and Amherst Regional High School

---

1) Council Discussion, Motion, Vote
Devlin Gauthier, one of the main authors of the proposed motion, stated that she and Schoen had drafted
this proposition after certain members of the public brought the issue of school zones to their attention.
Until recently, the Massachusetts Manual on Uniform Traffic Control Devices only allowed for the
establishment of school zones at schools serving first through eighth graders. It has since been updated to
include schools serving kindergarteners through twelfth graders. She also informed the Council that the
look and design of school zone signage is very tightly regulated by the Department of Transportation;
there is therefore very little that the Council needs to decide on beyond the establishment of a school
zone.
Schoen stated that she had met virtually with a representative from Safe Ways to School, a technology
leader, and another advocate to discuss during which hours of the day the signs should flash. She added
that the schools would be involved in this discussion.
MOTION: Devlin Gauthier moved, seconded by Schoen, to establish designated School Safety Zones in
accordance with the Massachusetts Amendments to the Manual on Uniform Traffic Control Devices on
all streets that provide access to the Amherst Regional Middle School and the Amherst Regional High
School with flashing indicator lights to be placed at the discretion of the Superintendent of Public Works
and to be illuminated between the hours of 6:30am and 10:00am, and between the hours of 3:00pm and
6:00pm with a review of this timing by the Town Services and Outreach Committee one year after
implementation.
MOTION: Hanneke moved, seconded by Ryan, to refer this matter to the Town Services and Outreach
(TSO) Committee with a report and recommendation to the Town Council by December 2, 2024.
Hanneke explained that she made the above motion because she stated that the memo on school zone
creation was missing key and required information, such a plan of student routes and limits of the school
zone. She also did not think that the motion to establish school zones had enough detail and expressed
concern that the motion had been made before consulting with the schools about the timing of the flashing
lights on the school zone signs. Ryan added that he seconded Hanneke’s motion because consultations
with the schools, the Department of Public Works, and the Amherst Police Department still need to occur.
Rooney stated that because a lot of students cross Cottage Street by and before 3:00 PM, the school zone
signs should be illuminated in the afternoon starting at least at 2:45 PM. She also stated that she would
not support sending the matter to the TSO committee because there are not many choices to be made
about the school zone and sign placement—most of these details would be dictated by state law. Rooney
added that she is glad that this matter is being discussed and that she supports the creation of an extra-
long pedestrian crosswalk at the top of Mattoon Street.
Steinberg stated that he supports the referral of this matter to the TSO committee because he recalls the
school zone creation process at Fort River Elementary School being quite different and was spearheaded
by staff at the Department of Public Works before it came before the Town Council. Their report included
a comprehensive traffic study of the proposed school zone area. He echoed Hanneke’s point about the
existing motion needing more detail. Steinberg also expressed concern that the proposed school zone
includes Triangle Street because most of Triangle Street does not abut school property and the Uniform
Traffic Manual states that school zones must be on streets abutting school property. Finally, Steinberg

---

expressed concern that the existing motion seems to set a single timeframe for sign illumination for all
schools; the hours of operation are different for the elementary schools and the regional schools.
Devlin Gauthier responded that she does not believe it would be appropriate for the Town Services and
Outreach committee to go back and forth with the Department of Public Works about where the school
zone signs should be placed. She therefore does not support Hanneke’s motion to refer this matter to the
TSO committee. She also explained how she and her co-sponsors arrived at their suggested hours for sign
illumination and suggested that the motion could be changed to say that the hours would be determined
by the school superintendent.
Griesemer stated that she thinks the 6:30 AM start time for sign illumination is appropriate because a
morning movement program occurs at the middle school and high school several times a week and starts
at 7:00 AM. She added that she had previously raised the question of referring this matter to the TSO to
her cosponsors.
Hanneke stated that she would prefer to vote on a motion that included detailed information about where
the school zone signs would be placed. She would also prefer that the school superintendent weigh in on
the sign illumination timeframes before the matter was put to the Council for a vote.
Steinberg noted the importance of also conferring with the Transportation Advisory Committee on the
matter. He also stated that he thinks there should be an opportunity for broad public comment on the
matter before it is voted on.
Ryan echoed previous comments about wanting to vote on a more detailed proposal.
VOTED eight to five with none absent to refer this matter to the Town Services and Outreach (TSO)
Committee with a report and recommendation to the Town Council by December 2, 2024.
c. Adoption of Amherst Black Reparations Committee Charge, as amended –
Postponed action on October 7, 2024
1) Council Discussion and Reading Period
MOTION: Hanneke moved, seconded by Ette, to amend the charge by replacing it with the alternative
presented by Hanneke.
Hanneke explained that she moved to adopt a rewritten substitute of the ABRC charge because the
original charge did not mention slavery or the harms caused by slavery and was instead based in
dismantling racism, which is a forward-looking action rather than an action aiming to repair past harms.
She also explained that her substitute charge aims to connect all funding allocations directly to repairing
past harms in hopes that this may help protect the Town from legal challenges. The substitute charge also
expands the committee membership and makes changes to membership appointment designations and
clarifies the processes for adopting priorities and seeking funding allocations from the Town Council.
Griesemer informed the Council that they would have a fifteen-minute reading period to review the
substitute charge, followed by a special public comment period. Devlin Gauthier requested that Michelle
Miller and Amilcar Shabazz be permitted to participate in the Council’s discussion of the substitute
charge given their instrumentality in the drafting of the initial charge.

---

After the Council’s reading period, Hanneke thanked Cathy Schoen, Dr. Amilcar Shabazz, Michelle
Miller, Deborah Bridges, and Anika Lopes for their comments on and assistance with the crafting of her
substitute charge.
2) Special Public Comment
Alex Cox thanked the Councilors who took the time to revise and specify the scope of the charge. He
asked for clarification on whether implementing reparations or making recommendations regarding the
implementation of reparations was the true charge of the ABRC. He also asked for clarification about
which international human rights standards the charge referred to.
Michelle Miller expressed appreciation for the level of thought that went into the crafting of the substitute
charge and for the fact that more Councilors got involved in its crafting. In terms of which community
members, the ABRC should consult about its actions, Miller advocated strongly for the centering of the
two inner circles outlined in the AHRA report, with some involvement of the third circle as well. She also
shared thoughts about the appropriate composition of the committee’s membership based on lived
experience and invited the Councilors to share their thoughts on this matter.
Dr. Amilcar Shabazz expressed agreement with Miller’s points. He stated that the AHRA was always
very mindful of the model of the Community Preservation Act (CPA) Committee, especially as it relates
to State-level funding. He emphasized the importance of seeing how the State would handle the matter of
reparations policy before deciding that modeling the ABRC on the CPA Committee was the best course
of action. He also referenced the example of reparations work undertaken by Evanston, Illinois and how
the AHRA used Evanston’s work to inform their report. He advised against restricting or limiting the
scope of the ABRC’s work by defining it too narrowly and against expanding the ABRC’s membership to
seven members.
3) Council Discussion, Motion and Vote
Hegner expressed agreement with the points made by Miller and Dr. Shabazz. He also expressed concern
about the proposed expansion of the ABRC’s membership and the restriction of its scope to a few specific
issues because its priorities may shift over time. He also agreed with Alex Cox that the main charge of the
ABRC would be making recommendations rather than binding decisions.
Devlin Gauthier expressed frustration that a Councilors drafted a substitute charge instead of submitting
feedback on the original proposed charge to the Governance, Organizations, and Legislation (GOL)
committee. She also expressed concern that the word “Black” did not appear in the substitute charge and
did not agree with the expansion of the ABRC’s membership as proposed in the substitute charge. She
reasoned that the other committees that the substitute charge included in the ABRC’s membership are
already overburdened and cannot realistically take on additional work. Finally, Devlin Gauthier opined
that consulting only those in groups one and two would not be enforceable due to the laboriousness of
proving ancestry. She stated that such a restriction would also limit the ABRC’s ability to address broader
structural harms brought about by slavery’s long-term effects.
Walker expressed agreement with Devlin Gauthier’s points, including the removal of the words “African
heritage” from the charge. She also expressed opposition to the proposed expansion of the ABRC’s
membership to include members of other committees, stating that the ABRC can consult with those

---

committees without including representatives in their membership. She opined that she did not feel she
had enough time to process the changes made in the substitute charge and agreed with Devlin Gauthier’s
objections to the process by which the substitute charge came to be. Walker also echoed Hegner’s point
about the substitute charge being too restricted to allow for change in the ABRC’s mission over time.
Schoen proposed creating a working group to go over the proposed charge line by line. She stated that by
putting forward some concrete issues on which the ABRC could work, the drafters of the substitute
charge were trying to provide some clarity around the allocation of public funds, not restricting the scope
ABRC’s work. She advocated for clearer and more detailed criteria for soliciting and comparing
proposals.
Ette stated that he thinks the substitute charge is stronger than the original proposed charge because it is
more limited and specific in focus and therefore more actionable. He stated that he had a hard time
imagining what the impacts of the original charge would actually look like. He advocated for sending the
new substitute charge back to GOL for further refining.
De Angelis stated that she liked the substitute charge, but asked its authors why they did not reach out to
GOL with comments about the original proposed charge. She also asked about the chronological extent of
the term “post-Reconstruction” and what years it specifically refers to. Finally, De Angelis advocated for
including healthcare equity as one of the main issues that the ABRC’s work should address and stated that
she does not have a problem with defining certain issues for the ABRC to focus on as long as some
flexibility was possible. She emphasized the paramount importance of continually consulting with the
descendants of enslaved people, as well as all Black Amherst residents who have experienced
discrimination based on race.
Ryan stated that he read the substitute charge as being shaped by the desire to protect the Town from
possible legal challenges and that, for that reason, it may require further discussion. He asked the Council
if the majority felt that narrowing the scope of the charge was useful and helpful; if that was the case, he
suggested that the substitute charge be sent to GOL for further refining. He also added that he thinks the
Council has never had a comprehensive discussion on the AHRA report and that such a discussion should
be included in a future meeting agenda.
Lord expressed agreements with the comments made by Devlin Gauthier and Walker, stating that she
supports sticking with the original five-member structure for the ABRC rather than adding more
representatives from other committees. She also stated that she would like to see the word “Black” appear
in the charge to ensure that the committee’s work benefits those who have directly experienced harm or
those who have descended from those who experienced direct harm. She stated that because Amherst has
passed a Home Rule measure, the Town cannot be penalized for allocating funding to projects based on
race.
Dr. Shabazz clarified that neither the Town of Amherst nor the State of Massachusetts have passed a
Home Rule measure and that the Town could therefore get taken to court if the work of the ABRC was
framed as a reparatory justice measure. He again urged the Council not to restrict the ABRC’s charge to a
small handful of specific issues.
Griesemer stated that footnotes should be added to the charge since several references are made within it
and links are not generally put into committee charges.

---

Devlin Gauthier stated that she is comfortable referring the substitute charge back to GOL for further
discussion because it contains concrete changes to the original charge that they can review and deliberate
on. She also mentioned that GOL could pursue legal input on the charge but was not sure that this was
necessary.
Ette opined that the original charge was so broad that it would be challenging to measure the ABRC’s
progress or success. He also did not feel that the substitute charge was overly restrictive of the ABRC’s
scope.
Walker asked if it is possible to amend a committee’s charge once that committee has already been
established. Griesemer replied that it is possible and reviewed the different ways that a charge could be
amended. Walker then asked if it would be possible for her to engage with GOL on this matter. Devlin
Gauthier, as Chair of GOL, responded that the committee would welcome written comments on the
charge from Walker and that she would be willing to sit down individually with Walker to capture her
comments.
Hanneke affirmed Ryan’s statement about drafting the charge in a way that considered possible legal
challenges.
Michelle Miller stated that the drafters of the original proposed charge were thinking about the feasibility
of the ABRC’s work in the context of reparations not yet being identified as a public purpose. She
encouraged the Council not to overthink the charge and not to confuse theory and practice.
MOTION: Devlin Gauthier, seconded by Ette, moved to refer the proposed amendments to the charge
for the Amherst Black Reparations Committee to GOL.
VOTED unanimously with none absent to refer the proposed amendments to the charge for the Amherst
Black Reparations Committee to GOL.

9. Presentation and Discussion
a. Discussion of 2025 Town Manager Goals
1) Governance, Organization, and Legislation Committee Report
Ryan explained that the purpose of the Town Manager Goals is to help the Council find consensus on its
top priorities for the coming year and to help the Town Manager decide how to allocate Town staff and
other resources. The goals, as a public document, also lay out the Council’s priorities and trajectories to
any interested party and can be used as a historical record documenting the Council’s commitments at a
certain point in time. This can help future iterations of the Town Council to measure or gauge progress on
certain issues.

---

Devlin Gauthier explained that she proposed to GOL that the Town Manager Goals be organized and
presented in a way that would allow for increased clarity and actionability. GOL wanted to seek the
Council’s input on the goals themselves and the general direction in which they point the Town. She then
reviewed the structure of the Goals document, which lists specific action items, bigger-picture objectives,
and the status of each goal in a table. She mentioned that GOL thought it would be most realistic to have
no more than three big-picture objectives for each goal area or issue.
2) Council Discussion
Both Schoen and Rooney expressed satisfaction with the structure of the Town Manager Goals document.
Walker asked how the action items for each objective would be determined and if Councilors would be
restricted to only considering the status of the action items when writing their Town Manager evaluations.
Devlin Gauthier responded that she and GOL would devise and put forward a proposal for how the Town
Council could decide which action items to list in the final draft of the Goals.
Hanneke expressed appreciation for how Devlin Gauthier brought her professional experience into this
project and conversation. She echoed Walker’s question about whether the Council could only evaluate
the Town Manager based on the status of the action items listed in the Town Manager Goals.
Taub stated that she finds this to be a much more workable format than past iterations of the Town
Manager Goals. She also raised the idea of the Council having a special meeting dedicated to discussing
and ranking the Town Manager goals they would like to see in the final document.
