9. Presentations and Discussions
1) Financial Planning for Four Major Building Projects (Elementary
School, Jones Library, Fire/EMS Station, Department of Public
Works Facility)
a) Presentation
Bockelman and Zawadzki presented on financial planning for the building projects listed above,
emphasizing the following points:
 Making progress on major capital building investments consistent with Council votes is a
current Town Manager goal. Such progress shall include supporting the Elementary
School Building Committee, the Jones Library Building Committee, exploring new
locations for the Central Fire/EMS Station and Department of Public Works, and
presenting a financing plan for the replacement Central Fire/EMS Station and
Department of Public Works to the Council
 The elementary school project is currently in the bid stage and is being funded via debt
exclusion, an MSBA grant, and Capital Stabilization.
o Three project bids have been made, all of which were under the target budget
o Two of those three bids have been challenged, and that process is currently with
the Attorney General

---

 The Jones Library project is currently in the bid stage and is being funded via the Town,
an MBLC grant, Community Preservation Act funds, other State and Federal grants, and
private donations
o Two bids have been made, one of which was under the target budget
o Once permitting checks and a review of the under-target bid have been done, the
project will move into the award stage
 The Fire/EMS Station project is in the financial planning stage and next steps will include
a feasibility study and the convening of a Fire/DPW Building Committee
o The preferred location for the station is Hickory Ridge, a piece of land with
buildable space in front of West Pomeroy Street
o The Hickory Ridge location is already owned by the Town so no land
procurement would need to be done
o The Hickory Ridge location would be more accessible for ambulance calls
coming from or going to Cooley Dickinson Hospital
 The Department of Public Works facility project is in the financial planning phase and
next steps will include updating the feasibility study and convening a Fire/DPW Building
Committee
o The preferred location for the new facility is the same site as the old facility on
South Pleasant Street; other options were explored but none of them proved to be
sustainable
o Ruxton, a piece of land off of Pulpit Hill Road, will be used as an auxiliary site
and for equipment storage and overflow
 The expected timings are of the projects are currently:
o 2024 for the elementary school (initial borrowing was secured in May 2024)
o Early 2025 for the Jones Library (the Town’s share of the cost is $15.8 million)
o 2026 for the construction of the DPW facility; 2027 its debt impact
o 2027 for the Fire/EMS station; 2028 for its debt impact
Zawadzki reviewed the balances of the Capital Stabilization Reserve, as well as four different
model financing plans for each building project. Each model plan included different amounts of
Reserves combined with different levy percentages:
 Model 1 would decrease capital allocation (10.0% levy, $15.5M in reserves)
 Model 2 would maintain capital allocation (10.5% levy, $11.2M in reserves)
 Model 3 would delay project start dates (10.5% levy, $10.4M in reserves)
 Model 4 would include high reserves use (10.5% levy, $23.1M in reserves)
Zawadzki then compared the models based on urgency, how they would affect the Town’s bond
rating, overall cost, and flexibility. She recommended at the Council go with Model 2 because it
allows for the most flexibility and has the smallest impact on the Town’s reserves. She requested
that the Council commit to project locations by December 2, 2024 and commit to a financing
plan by December 16, 2024.

2) Council Order FY25-05B: An Order Appropriating Funds for a
Portion of the Town of Amherst Capital Program – Road and
Sidewalk Repairs
a) Council Discussion, Motion and Vote
MOTION: Griesemer moved, seconded by Ette, having held a public forum on November 18,
with a recommendation by the Finance Committee, to adopt Appropriation and Transfer Order
FY25-05B: An Order Appropriating Funds for a Portion of the Town of Amherst Capital
Program – Road and Sidewalk Repairs, as shown on page 15 of the Motions Sheet.
The Council discussed the following points related to Appropriation and Transfer Order FY25-
05B:
 Devlin Gauthier stated that she was struggling with these allocations after knowing that
some of the reallocated funds were returned from the schools. She stated that she would
like to see the entire sum at stake in this motion reallocated to the schools and otherwise
cannot support the motion.
 Hanneke echoed Devlin Gauthier’s concerns, stating that she has never been comfortable
with the prioritization of roads and sidewalks over all other matters. She also stated that
she found it striking how little of the FY2025 capital budget was allocated to the
elementary schools.
 Hanneke explained that the Regional School Committee must vote on its own capital
allocations before the four towns vote on them and that, with one exception, the Council
has always approved the Region’s capital requests. However, she stated that the Council
should currently wait to make this allocation to see if the Regional School Committee or
Amherst School Committee have any capital needs more pressing than roads and
sidewalks.
 As a member of JCPC, Schoen stated that there were no unapproved capital requests
from the Regional School Committee and that in order to allocate additional capital
funding to the Regional Schools, the Committee would have to submit proposals.
 Steinberg stated that he would not assume that the Town would end up with a large
surplus at the end of the year because of many uncertainties at the federal level. Steinberg
also emphasized that road and sidewalk improvements were a high priority for
constituents and that the building of the new elementary school would put increased
stress on the roads.

---

 Ryan stated that there is a clear need for sidewalk and road improvements, but that he
would also be sympathetic to using some of the surplus of the schools’ capital needs if
they made tangible capital requests.
 Devlin Gauthier pointed out that JCPC has seen capital requests from the elementary
schools, primarily Crocker. In response to Steinberg’s comment, she stated that she has
not asked constituents about their thoughts on roads and sidewalks but that she has heard
from many constituents about prioritizing the needs of the schools.
 Griesemer stated that she thought it was unfortunate to set up the schools against the
roads and sidewalks because they are prioritized by different groups of constituents and
because she has heard many promising suggestions about how additional money for the
schools could be obtained. She suggested continuing a conversation that treats both the
roads and the schools as priorities.
 Ette stated that many constituents have come to him with concerns about the roads and
that road and sidewalk improvements are therefore very worthy of prioritization.
 In response to Griesemer’s comments, Devlin Gauthier argued that the roads and the
schools are not important to distinct groups of constituents because nearly all constituents
use the roads. She clarified that she was not advocating for disinvesting in roads, but that
a great deal of parents explicitly asked the Council to prioritize the schools in this
moment. She suggested looking for innovative alternate ways to fund road and sidewalk
repairs as well as school needs.
 Ryan stated that he is hesitant to use a one-time allocation to try to address the systemic
problems the schools are facing.
 Rooney stated that she supported holding off on this allocation for roads and sidewalks to
give the Regional School Committee time to put together specific capital allocation
requests.
 Taub suggested authorizing $500,000 from free cash for road and sidewalk repairs and
gifting an additional $500,000 to the Regional Schools for a capital project of their
choice.
 Walker expressed appreciation for Devlin Gauthier’s desire to respond to the many parent
requests to prioritize the schools during the public forum on the FY2026 budget. She
suggested taking more time to consider the Town’s most pressing capital needs and how
funds could be allocated to best meet those needs.
MOTION: Ette, seconded by Hanneke, moved to call the question on the motion on the table.
VOTED nine to three by roll call with one absent (De Angelis) to call the question on the
motion on the table.
VOTED six to six by roll call with one absent (De Angelis), having held a public forum on
November 18, with a recommendation by the Finance Committee, not to adopt Appropriation
and Transfer Order FY25-05B: An Order Appropriating Funds for a Portion of the Town of
Amherst Capital Program – Road and Sidewalk Repairs, as shown on page 15 of the Motions
Sheet.
