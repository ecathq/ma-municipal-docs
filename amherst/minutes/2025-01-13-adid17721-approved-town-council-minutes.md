1. Call to Order
President Griesemer declared the presence of a quorum, called the meeting to order at 6:30 p.m.,
confirmed Councilors could hear and be heard, and announced audio and video recording.
Agenda item 8.a. Election of Officers – Charter Sec. 2.2(b)
a. President
O’Keeffe reviewed election procedures and opened the floor for nominations.
De Angelis nominated Griesemer; Griesemer accepted the nomination.
Griesemer made a statement on behalf of her candidacy.
Councilor Hanneke spoke about the need for change in Council leadership; Griesemer has made promises
to make changes that she has not followed through, Griesemer has not informed Councilors adequately
about meetings she attends, and Hanneke will not vote for a candidate.
Schoen spoke in support of Griesemer; there have been positive changes, including shortening meetings,
Griesemer gave up her place on the Finance Committee and continued to attend meetings in the audience,
and that there are other challenges the Council faces.
Taub spoke in praise of Griesemer’s dedication of time and effort in service to the Council as President.
Lord spoke in praise of Griesemer’s time in assisting Lord as a Councilor and thanked her for her
willingness to serve as President.
After written votes, O’Keeffe announced the results.
VOTED 10 for Griesemer, 1 for no candidate, 2 abstentions.
Votes for Griesemer: De Angelis, Ette, Griesemer, Hegner, Lord, Rooney, Ryan, Schoen, Steinberg, Taub
Abstentions: Devlin Gauthier, Walker
Vote for no nominee: Hanneke
Absent: None
O’Keeffe administered the oath of office to President Griesemer.

---

b. Vice President
Griesemer opened the floor for nominations.
Devlin Gauthier nominated Ryan; Ryan accepted the nomination.
Rooney nominated Schoen; Schoen accepted the nomination.
Ryan and Schoen made statements on behalf of their candidacy.
De Angelis, Devlin Gauthier and Steinberg spoke in support of Ryan’s candidacy.
Rooney and Taub spoke in support of Schoen’s candidacy.
After written votes, O’Keeffe announced the results.
VOTED 6 for Ryan, 5 for Schoen, 2 abstentions.
Votes for Ryan: De Angelis, Devlin Gauthier, Ette, Hanneke, Ryan, Steinberg
Votes for Schoen: Hegner, Rooney, Schoen, Taub, Walker
Abstentions: Griesemer, Lord
Absent: None
The vote to elect a Vice President failed.
Griesemer opened the floor for nominations.
Rooney nominated Schoen; Schoen accepted the nomination.
Devlin Gauthier nominated Ryan; Ryan accepted the nomination.
After written votes, O’Keeffe announced the results.
VOTED 7 for Ryan, 5 for Schoen, 1 abstention.
Votes for Ryan: De Angelis, Devlin Gauthier, Ette, Griesemer, Hanneke, Ryan, Steinberg
Votes for Schoen: Hegner, Rooney, Schoen, Taub, Walker
Abstentions: Lord
Absent: None
O’Keeffe administered the oath of office to Vice President Ryan.
At 7:10 p.m., the Council recessed the regular meeting to open the Public Forum on Appropriations
Outside the Budget.
At 7:21 p.m., the Council returned from recess.

h. Eversource Easement at 70 South East Street
MOTION: Griesemer moved, second by Schoen, to approve AN ORDER AUTHORIZING THE
CONVEYANCE OF A PERMANENT UTILITY EASEMENT ON TOWN PROPERTY, as shown on
page 18-19 of the Motions Sheet.
Perient explained the request for the easement; the utility company is installing an underground service
line and is requesting an easement in order to maintain the service line and prevent building above the
underground utility line.
Council discussion:
 The easement would allow a driveway and would be appropriate for maintenance
 The easement does not allow rights to unnamed parties, such as other utility companies
 Power lines will be underground at the new school
 Ownership of the transformers remains with the utility company
 Other work is within the Town right of way
VOTED Unanimously by roll call to approve AN ORDER AUTHORIZING THE CONVEYANCE OF
A PERMANENT UTILITY EASEMENT ON TOWN PROPERTY, as shown on page 18-19 of the
Motions Sheet.
i. Department of Public Works and South Fire Station Locations and Financing
Model
Hegner reported the Finance Committee discussed the financing models and leaned toward two but did
not make a recommendation on a specific model. The committee agreed it was not necessary to choose a
model at this point, but that the Committee liked elements of two different models.
Schoen added that the Committee assumed the location of the two sites would be the sites; the model will
need to be adjusted at the close of the fiscal year; the Joint Capital Planning Committee will need to
consider the building plans during their process this year, with attention to out-years.
