a. Percent for Art Bylaw: Elementary School Building Project
MOTION: Schoen moved, second by Walker, to set a limit of $100,000 for the Percent for Art budget for
the Fort River Elementary School. This budget would need to cover commissioning the art works,
installation, insurance (if needed), and other Town taxpayer costs related to soliciting proposals, public
engagement, and contracting for the art Page 2 of 3 03-10-2025 projects.
Schoen spoke to her motion, noting the group that developed the bylaw was not envisioning such a
large funding amount and options for locating art in, on, or outside the new building.
Hanneke and Ryan spoke in opposition to the motion; art can be expensive and it is not known how
much it will cost, and there are other variables and unknowns.
Devlin Gauthier suggested hearing from the Public Art Commission.
MOTION: Steinberg moved, second by De Angelis, to postpone action on the motion until the May 19,
2025 Town Council meeting.
Steinberg spoke to the motion; a postponement would allow the Council to hear from the Public Art
Commission.
VOTED 12-1 by roll call to postpone action on the motion until the May 19, 2025 Town Council meeting.
Aye: De Angelis, Ette, Griesemer, Hanneke, Hegner, Lord, Rooney, Ryan, Schoen, Steinberg, Taub,
Walker

c. Proposed Acceptance of Statute enabling Amherst to install and operate school bus
violation detection monitoring systems
MOTION: Hanneke moved, second by Griesemer, to refer the proposed acceptance of MGL Chapter 40
sec. 71 to TSO with a report to the Town Council by April 28, 2025.
 This law, recently passed in Massachusetts, would permit municipalities to opt into camera-
based traffic violation enforcement for vehicles passing stopped school buses.
 Referral proposed to the Town Services and Outreach Committee (TSO) for review and public
input.
 Councilors raised concerns about privacy, surveillance, enforcement feasibility, and potential
legal challenges (drawing comparisons to red-light camera issues in other states).
 TSO Chair noted current workload and requested a later reporting deadline (after May 1).
 Several councilors expressed discomfort with advancing this proposal at all, citing civil liberties,
cost, and unclear implementation responsibilities.
 Councilor Hanneke emphasized the need for a public discussion before making a decision, even
if implementation remains unlikely.
VOTED 5-8 by roll call to refer the proposed acceptance of MGL Chapter 40 sec. 71 to TSO with a report
to the Town Council by April 28, 2025.
Aye: Devlin Gauthier, Griesemer, Hanneke, Ryan, Steinberg
