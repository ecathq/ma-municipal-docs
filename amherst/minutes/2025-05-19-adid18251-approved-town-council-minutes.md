a. Eversource Petition to install one solely owned underground duct bank at 70 South
East Street
DPW Superintendent Mooring spoke about the need for the underground duct bank, which will serve a
transformer that was moved closer to the new school building.
David Meagher, Eversource Representative, added additional information; the duct bank will not serve
the planned solar arrays.
Public comment:
Jessica J., abutter, asked about the structure and location of the proposed duct bank.
Meagher responded that no new poles are being requested.
MOTION: Griesemer moved, second by Hanneke, to close the hearing.
VOTED Unanimously by roll call, 9-0, with 4 absent (Devlin Gauthier, Ette, Taub, Walker) to close the
hearing.

c. Percent for Art – Elementary School Building Project
Public Art Commission members presented information about the Percent for Art for the Fort River
Elementary School Building Project.
MOTION ON THE FLOOR (postponed at a previous meeting): Schoen moved, second by Walker, to set a
limit of $100,000 for the Percent for Art budget for the Fort River Elementary School. This budget would
need to cover commissioning the art works, installation, insurance (if needed), and other Town taxpayer
costs related to soliciting proposals, public engagement, and contracting for the art projects.
Schoen spoke to her motion, stating that the building committee worked hard to manage project costs
and spoke in favor of reduced funding for public art.
Discussion:
 Commitment to the bylaw
 Amherst values
 Placement of the art

 Decision-making process
 Public input
 Accessibility of the art
 Funding amount

d. Amherst Pelham Regional School District Budget
MOTION: Griesemer moved, second by Hanneke, in accordance with Section 5.5(c) of the Amherst
Home Rule Charter and in compliance with Sections 5.5(a) and 5.5(b) of the Amherst Home Rule
Charter, to separately consider and act on the Amherst-Pelham Regional School District budget and
assessment method for Fiscal Year 2026 because it would be prudent to do so due to the regional
agreement with the three other towns in the Regional School District.
No Councilor comments or questions.

VOTED Unanimously by roll call, 10-0, with 3 absent (Devlin Gauthier, Ette, Taub) in accordance with
Section 5.5(c) of the Amherst Home Rule Charter and in compliance with Sections 5.5(a) and 5.5(b) of
the Amherst Home Rule Charter, to separately consider and act on the Amherst-Pelham Regional School
District budget and assessment method for Fiscal Year 2026 because it would be prudent to do so due to
the regional agreement with the three other towns in the Regional School District.
MOTION: Griesemer moved, second by Schoen, in accordance with Charter Sec. 5.5, having been
referred to the Finance Committee, having been recommended by the Finance Committee report of
May 19, 2025, a Public Hearing held on May 12, 2025, notice of which was posted for a minimum of 10
days on May 1, 2025, to adopt Council Order FY26-02: An Order Approving the Amherst-Pelham
Regional School District FY2026 Budget and Appropriating the Town of Amherst Share of the Budget
Assessment, as shown on page 11 of the Motions Sheet.
Schoen reported on the Finance Committee’s unanimous recommendation to adopt the Council Order;
the committee submitted a written report.
Council discussion:
 Increases are not sustainable
 How schools are managing escalating costs
 No staff cuts have occurred
VOTED Unanimously by roll call, 10-0, with 3 absent (Devlin Gauthier, Ette, Taub) in accordance with
Charter Sec. 5.5, having been referred to the Finance Committee, having been recommended by the
Finance Committee report of May 19, 2025, a Public Hearing held on May 12, 2025, notice of which was
posted for a minimum of 10 days on May 1, 2025, to adopt Council Order FY26-02: An Order Approving
the Amherst-Pelham Regional School District FY2026 Budget and Appropriating the Town of Amherst
Share of the Budget Assessment, as shown on page 11 of the Motions Sheet.
e. Referral of Proposed Amendment to Zoning Bylaws: Footnote M – Increase of
additional lot area in the RG Zoning District
MOTION: Griesemer moved, second by Rooney, in accordance with MGL Ch. 40A §5, to refer the
proposed Zoning Bylaw amendment to Footnote M to the Planning Board, for public hearing within 65
days and report to the Town Council; to Community Resources Committee, for public hearing and
recommendation to the Town Council; and to Governance, Organization, and Legislation for a final
review of clarity, consistency, and actionability.
Planning Board Chair D. Marshall spoke briefly about the Planning Board’s proposed zoning amendment,
which would change the minimum square footage for additional apartments or condominiums in the RG
Zoning District from 4,000 to 6,000 square feet. Marshall spoke about the Board’s discussions of density
in different zoning districts.
Council discussion:
 Hanneke – housing crisis, exclusionary zoning; does not support the proposal
 The proposal was not initiated by staff
 State law requirement of referral to the Planning Board
 Ryan – not a compelling argument for the proposal from the Planning Board or staff
 Rooney – considering allowing greater density in other areas of Town; in favor of referral
