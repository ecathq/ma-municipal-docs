TOWN OF AMHERST STREETLIGHTS POLICY
Adopted by Amherst Town Council, Keeper of the Public Way, ______________
Pursuant to Charter Section 2.14, the Town Council is designated the Keeper of the Public Way, and
therefore maintains authority to adopt this Policy. The Town Council reserves the right to modify this
Policy at any time.
Public roads and sidewalks are illuminated to achieve a number of different objectives that include:
providing for clear and comfortable visibility at night; promoting business use of public facilities by
making streets and sidewalks more inviting during hours of darkness; facilitating nighttime vehicular and
pedestrian circulation; and improving nighttime safety for all modes of travel. This Policy is enacted to
ensure streetlights are appropriately scaled to meet the range of the community’s goals, which compete
simultaneously for both more and less artificial nighttime illumination and provide for traveler safety and
comfort as well as enhance nighttime business and social activity while reducing the degradation of the
nighttime visual environment. Additionally, the accompanying performance standards aim to minimize
light pollution, glare, and light trespass. The Town Council recognizes that achieving the right balance is
a difficult task and adopts this Policy in order to set forth the means for achieving that balance.
Streetlights will generally be provided by the Town as follows:
• at intersections;
• at “dead ends” and ends of cul-de-sacs;
• for road conditions deemed potentially hazardous, such as severe curves or topographical changes
or areas with a high accident history;
• on all downtown streets (generally, the Central Business District);
• on other streets with heavy pedestrian traffic, such as in the vicinity of schools and in other
commercial areas.
The Town Council shall interpret the application of the above criteria to specific streetlights, and
the Town will provide streetlights at other locations deemed appropriate by the Town Council.
However, streetlights will not be provided by the Town as security lighting for private property or
for pedestrians in residential neighborhoods unless at least one of the above criteria is met or the
Town Council otherwise deems the situation to require a streetlight, in order to maintain equity of
determinations for all residents.
Streetlight spacing will be at the discretion of the Town Council.
The Town will resume paying for streetlights that were eliminated in 1991 or that have been paid for by
residents since 1991, upon the request of residents, if the streetlights meet the above criteria.
Requests for additional streetlights will be considered according to the above criteria. Residents are also
encouraged to notify the Town of existing streetlights that they consider unnecessary.
The elimination of existing streetlights that do not meet the above criteria will be considered during the
annual budget process.
The Town Manager may request the Town Council waive this Policy in order to install or remove
Streetlighting where such installation or removal would not comply with this Policy. The request shall
include the following:
1. Recommendation from the Department of Public Works or Public Safety officials that such
installation or removal would enhance public safety;
2. Reasons for the request;
3. Explanation of the adverse impacts to the public including vehicle and pedestrian traffic and
associated impacts from said installation or removal.
Streetlight Performance Standards are set forth in Appendix A. These standards are designed to minimize
light pollution, glare, uplight, and light trespass, as well as to regulate illumination levels.
This policy shall be made available to the Planning Board for its use in requiring streetlights in new
subdivisions or other developments.

TOWN OF AMHERST STREET LIGHTING POLICY
APPENDIX A – STREETLIGHT PERFORMANCE STANDARDS
A. Definitions.
1. BUG Rating System: A simple lighting classification system (IES TM-15-20 standard or its
successor) by the Illuminating Engineering Society (IES) and the International Dark-Sky
Association (IDA) to describe the Light Pollution contributions from outdoor Luminaires,
including for Streetlighting. Each letter is attached to a number ranging from 0 (ideal) to 5
(unacceptable). “B” describes backlight that often contributes to light trespass. “U” describes up
light, which contributes to Skyglow, which limits a person’s ability to see the stars while
providing little or no light towards the ground. “G” represents Glare or “forward light,” which
makes it difficult for people to see and thus makes roadways more dangerous.
2. Correlated Color Temperature (CCT): A specification of the color appearance of the light emitted
by a Lamp, measured in Kelvin (K). CCT values are typically provided in lighting manufacturer
data sheets and serve as a general “warmth” or “coolness” measure of its appearance.
3. Fully Shielded: A Luminaire constructed and installed in such a manner that all light emitted by
the Luminaire, either directly from the Lamp or a diffusing element, or indirectly by reflection or
refraction from any part of the Luminaire, is projected below the horizontal plane through the
Luminaire's lowest light-emitting part.
4. Glare: Intense and blinding light emitted by a Lamp that reduces visibility and causes visual
discomfort and/or momentary blindness.
5. Illuminance: The luminous flux incident per unit area of surface, often measured in foot-candle
(fc or lumens/ft2). It is a measure of how much the incidence light perceptibly illuminates the
surface.
6. Illuminating Engineering Society (IES): A recognized authoritative body on the science and
application of lighting that publishes and promotes recommended practices for a variety of
specific lighting applications.
7. Lamp: The bulb or other light-emitting portion of a Luminaire, not inclusive of any reflective or
refractive optics used to direct light.
8. Light Trespass: Any light emitted by a Luminaire that shines directly or indirectly beyond the
Public Right-of-Way on which the Luminaire is installed and increases the Illuminance above the
original (natural) illumination level as measured at the lot line in foot-candles.
9. Light Pole: A pole located in the Public Right-of-Way used to mount ornamental lighting or an
overhead Streetlight.
10. Lumen: A standard unit of measurement of the quantity of light emitted from a source of light.
11. Luminaire: A complete lighting assembly, consisting of a Lamp, housing, optic(s), and other
structural elements, but not including any mounting pole or surface.
12. Municipal Parking District: The Municipal Parking Overlay District on the Town Zoning Map.
13. Public Right-of-Way: A strip of land acquired by deed, reservation, dedication, forced dedication,
prescription, or condemnation and intended to be occupied or occupied by a Roadway, crosswalk,
Sidewalk, railroad, electric transmission line, oil or gas pipeline, water line, sanitary or storm
sewer, or other similar use.
14. Roadway: For the purposes of this Policy, any portion of a Public Right-of-Way that is paved or
graveled and used for vehicle travel.
15. Sidewalk: For the purposes of this Policy, any portion of a Public Right-of-Way that is paved or
graveled and used for pedestrian or bike travel, but not vehicle travel.
16. Skyglow: A glow in the night sky deriving from an artificial light source or sources.

17. Streetlight: Any Lamp or Luminaire within a Public Right-of-Way intended to Illuminate a
Roadway, Sidewalk, or Multiuse Path mounted on a Utility Pole or Light Pole that directs light to
the Roadway or Sidewalk.
18. Streetscape Lighting: Lighting mounted on stand-alone Light Poles, within the Public Right-of-
Way, typically no more than 10 to 15 feet in height, serving a decorative function in addition to
serving to illuminate Sidewalks, building facades, and in some instances the Roadway.
19. Uplight: Direct light emitted above a horizontal plane through the Luminaire’s lowest light-
emitting part in its mounted position.
20. Utility Pole: A pole owned and maintained by a utility company used to support power lines,
telephone lines, cable lines, etc.
21. Village Centers: The areas of Amherst generally referred to as the North Amherst Village Center,
Cushman, Pomeroy Village Center, and East Amherst Village Center.
B. Streetlight Performance Standards.
1. Shielding.
a. All Streetlight installations, except Streetscape Lighting, shall be Fully Shielded. To meet this
cutoff goal, the Department of Public Works will specify Fully Shielded fixtures as part of
any fixture procurement process.
b. Streetscape Lighting shall emit no more than 100 lumens of Uplight (BUG rating of no higher
than U2). All other Streetlights shall emit 0 lumens of Uplight.
2. Nuisance. No Streetlight Luminaire shall create a lighting nuisance in the form of Light Trespass,
Glare, or Skyglow.
a. Light Trespass. The maximum illumination level at a Public Right-of-Way line abutting
residential or sensitive uses, except in the Municipal Parking District and the Village Centers,
shall not exceed 0.01‐foot-candle, as measured at grade in either the horizontal or vertical
plane. The maximum illumination level at a Public Right-of-Way line abutting non-
residential or other non-sensitive uses or in the Municipal Parking District and the Village
Centers, shall not exceed 0.10 foot-candles, as measured at grade in either the horizontal or
vertical plane. Sensitive uses include but are not limited to land subject to a conservation
restriction under Massachusetts law. The measurement for compliance may be made by the
Superintendent of Public Works or their designee.
b. Glare. Glare is declared to be a lighting nuisance when it constitutes a hazard to public safety
along a Public Right-of-Way or causes intense discomfort to occupants of adjacent properties.
This determination will be made by the Superintendent of Public Works or their designee
through a site visit and a visual inspection, and can include considering the BUG rating for
Glare, which should be no higher than G1.
c. Skyglow. No Streetlight shall change the overall brightness or color of the night sky in the
Town of Amherst. The effect of the lighting in both on and off states may be verified with
photographs or by use of a sky quality meter. On the Bortle scale, the goal is to achieve a
class 3 or lower sky for areas outside the Municipal Parking District and Village Centers.
3. Spectrum management. The Correlated Color Temperature (CCT), measured in Kelvin (K), of all
Luminaires, Lamps and bulbs shall not exceed 2700 K, as specified on the product or its
packaging. The warmest possible color temperature (lowest Kelvin value) should be chosen.
4. Illumination Levels.
a. The Illuminance of all Luminaires, Lamps, and bulbs in Streetlights shall not exceed the
values contained in the current edition of the IES RP-8 handbook on best practices for
roadway, walkway, and parking lot lighting. The measurement shall be made with a
photometer placed horizontally on the ground at points within the area of illumination.

b. If possible and not cost prohibitive as determined by the Town Manager or their designee,

intended to light only Sidewalks or Multiuse Paths shall be fifteen (15) feet above the Sidewalk
surface.
C. Types of Lighting Fixtures. Amherst shall use the most efficient and effective type of light fixture
when replacing or installing Streetlights. Factors that shall be used to determine efficiency and
effectiveness include: fixture cost, installation and maintenance labor, manufacturer warranty, energy
use and cost, light quality, the fixture’s life cycle cost, and ability to allow future programmable
(wireless or wired) dimming control without the need to replace the full fixture or luminaire.
D. Maintenance Standards
1. The Town shall maintain on its website an official map of the location of the Town’s Streetlights
and a corresponding list of the fixtures, and, if available, the wattage, Illuminance, and spectrum
of those Streetlights. This official map shall be updated regularly.
2. The Town shall log citizen reports of Streetlight outages, register those outages on the official
streetlight map, taking note of critical locations where traffic and pedestrian safety is at risk,
evaluate if those Streetlights need to be replaced ahead of normally scheduled replacement, and
then replace the “burnt-out” Streetlight if justified according to the evaluation process.

3.40 OBSTRUCTION OF PUBLIC WAYS AND SNOW AND ICE REMOVAL
PENALTIES FOR VIOLATION OF THE OBSTRUCTION OF PUBLIC WAYS AND SNOW AND
ICE REMOVAL BYLAW
Noncriminal Disposition: $50.00 penalty per 24-hour period in which sidewalk snow, ice, or obstruction
is not removed or treated and each instance that snow or obstruction is deposited in the public way
(enforcement by: Inspectional Services)
A. Purpose.
It is the express intent of this bylaw to assure for all travelers, including those with disabilities or mobility
impairments, safe and convenient access to sidewalks, including abutting curb cuts/curb ramps, public
ways, and public parking places.
B. Removal of Snow, Ice, and Other Obstructions from Sidewalks.
1. The owner of real property that abuts a sidewalk shall remove snow or ice, whether by natural
accumulation or otherwise, no later than 24 hours after the end of the precipitation which preceded
the accumulation. If the snow or ice has accumulated other than by precipitation, it shall be removed
no later than 24 hours after its accumulation on the sidewalk. For purposes of this bylaw, removal of
snow and ice shall mean clearing sidewalks to the surface, or, where it is impracticable to do so,
treating sidewalks with sand or other abrasive materials appropriate for such purposes.
2. The owner of real property that abuts a sidewalk shall remove any debris or other obstruction that
impedes the travel of a person, whether by natural occurrence or otherwise, no later than 24 hours
after the obstruction occurring on or above the sidewalk.
3. The owner of real property that abuts a sidewalk shall remove any vegetative overgrowth that
impedes the travel of a person, no later than 10 days after receiving a notice to remedy. For the
purposes of this bylaw, vegetative overgrowth extending into the sidewalk zone or overhanging the
sidewalk 10 feet or less above the sidewalk shall be considered an obstruction.”. If that overgrowth is
from a public shade tree, the Tree Warden shall be contacted by Inspectional Services for evaluation.
C. Depositing Obstructions on Public Ways.
No person shall deposit snow, ice, leaves, debris, or other obstructions on any public way, sidewalk, curb
cuts/curb ramps, or public parking place. Containers for waste hauling collection must be removed from
any public way, sidewalk, or public parking place on the day of pick up.
D. Remedies
In addition to the remedies provided above, the Town Manager may, after due notice to, and an
opportunity for the owner of the real property to be heard, perform, or otherwise cause the clearing or
treating of snow, ice, or other obstructions as described in this bylaw, and may recover from the owner
the expense therefore which shall not exceed $500.00. This expense shall constitute a municipal charge
lien against the real property as provided in M.G.L. c. 40, § 58.
