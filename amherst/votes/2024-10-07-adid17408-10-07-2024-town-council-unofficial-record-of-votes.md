3.26 – NUISANCE PROPERTY
PENALTIES FOR VIOLATION OF THE NUISANCE PROPERTY BYLAW
Criminal Enforcement: $300.00 fine
Noncriminal Disposition: $300.00 penalty (enforcement by: Police Officers, Inspection Services)
A. Purpose
The purpose of this Bylaw is to protect the quiet enjoyment of residents in Town and to
empower the Town to take corrective action on properties that have become a Public Nuisance
by the unreasonable repeated or persistent interference with or disturbance of the general
welfare of a neighborhood or the Town through documented pervasive criminal activity, code
violations, or other causes precipitating the deployment of any Town resource. This applies to all
Properties in Town, whether they are owner-occupied or leased.
B. Definitions
1. Gathering: A party, crowd, or event, where a group of persons assemble for a social occasion or
activity.
2. Owner: The legal owner(s) of record for a particular parcel of land or a building(s) located
thereon, as listed in the tax assessors’ records, or, if known, the owner(s) of record as identified
in the deed for the particular parcel of land or building(s) located thereon, as recorded at the
Registry of Deeds, provided, however, that the legal owner(s) may be a natural person,
executor, corporation, trust, or any other entity recognized by law.
3. Occupant: The person or persons with the legal right to reside, or who is residing at, a
particular parcel of land or building(s) thereon.
4. Person: An individual, executor, corporation, trust, partnership (including general partnership,
limited partnership, and limited liability partnership), and a limited liability company. In
addition, any similar entity permitted by law to hold title to real estate shall be deemed a
Person.
5. Person-In-Charge: Any Person having charge, care, control, or management of a Property, the
Owner of the Property, and/or a Person who is authorized to act on the Owner’s behalf with
regard to the Property.
6. Property: All lands, including all structures, improvements, and fixtures thereon, and Property
of any nature appurtenant thereto, or used in connection therewith.
7. Public Nuisance: An unreasonable interference with a right common to the general public, such
as a condition dangerous to health, or that otherwise threatens the general welfare of a
neighborhood or the Town in general and includes, but is not limited to repeated or persistent,
activities that could be deemed violations of State and local laws and local regulations and as
further defined in Section C.2 of this Bylaw.
8. Underage Person: Any person under 21 years of age.
C. Public Nuisance Violations.
1. It shall be a violation of this Bylaw to create, host, allow, attend, or engage in a Public Nuisance.
2. A Public Nuisance may include but is not limited to repeated or persistent:
a. Activities that could be deemed a violation of federal or state law, or local bylaw or
regulation, including but not limited to the following Laws, General Bylaws, Zoning Bylaws,

and Town Regulations, whether or not an enforcement action is taken under the specific
law, bylaw, or regulation:
1. Massachusetts General Laws relating to:
(a) Disorderly conduct, M.G.L. Ch. 272, Sec. 53 (e.g., Penalty for Certain Offenses).
(b) Possession or consumption of Alcohol by an Underage Person or furnishing Alcohol
to an Underage Person, M.G.L. Ch. 138, Secs. 34 and 34c.
(i) This section shall not apply to conduct involving the use of Alcoholic Beverages
that occurs exclusively between an Underage Person and a parent or legal
guardian as set forth in M.G.L. Ch. 138, Sec. 34.
(ii) This section shall not apply to any Massachusetts Alcoholic Beverages Control
Commission licensee at any Property regulated by the Massachusetts Alcoholic
Beverage Control Commission.
2. Zoning Bylaws relating to:
(a) Outdoor Lighting, Secs. 7.105, 10.393, or 11.2417.
(b) Maintenance of Vegetation, Secs. 3.2041(5), 10.392, or 11.3.
(c) Parking, Secs. 7.000 or 7.104.
3. General Bylaws relating to:
(a) Obstruction of Public Ways and Snow and Ice Removal, Sec. 3.40.
(b) Regulations Relating to Animals, Sec. 3.21.
(c) Junked Vehicles, Sec. 3.42.
(d) Unlawful Noise, Sec. 3.24 or M.G.L. Ch. 272, Sec. 53.
(e) Littering and Illegal Dumping, Sec. 3.16.
(f) Refuse Collection and Recyclable Materials, Sec. 3.33.
b. Gatherings that cause unreasonable disturbances.
D. Nuisance Property Designation. Upon provision of notice of a third violation of this Bylaw in a 1-
year period measured from the date of the first violation in connection with a specific Property, the
Property shall be deemed, by operation of law, to be a Nuisance Property.
E. Persons Liable. The following persons shall be jointly and severally liable for penalties when a
violation of this Bylaw occurs in connection with a Property:
1. For a Property receiving the first or second notice of violation in a 1-year period:
a. The Person(s) who organized or sponsored an activity that resulted in a Public Nuisance;
and/or,
b. Persons engaging in activity resulting in a Public Nuisance.
2. For a Property receiving the third or subsequent notice of violation in a 1-year period and having
been notified of the first and second violation:
a. The Person(s) who organized or sponsored an activity that resulted in a Public Nuisance;
and/or,
b. Persons engaging in activity resulting in a Public Nuisance; and/or,
c. Owners and Person(s)-In-Charge of the Nuisance Property; provided, however, that the
Owner of the Property shall not be held responsible for a violation and penalties if the
Owner is actively trying to evict an occupant from the Property and that the occupant was
the cause of the Public Nuisance.
F. Enforcement and Penalties.

1. Monetary Penalty. Monetary penalties may be imposed as specified in the penalty section
above.
2. Abatement. Action to abate a Public Nuisance may be taken by all reasonable means available in
law and in equity including but not limited to the issuance of an order by the enforcing authority
requiring the Public Nuisance to be corrected, issuance of a citation by the enforcing authority,
or through an order from a court of competent jurisdiction.
3. Each day or portion thereof that a violation of this Bylaw exists shall constitute a separate
violation. If more than 1 provision of this Bylaw is violated, each provision violated shall
constitute a separate violation.
4. The Town shall maintain on its website a list of nuisance property complaints including dates
and addresses, violations, and Town responses.
G. Notification and Nuisance Property Correction Process.
1. First and Second violations. On the first and second violation within a 1-year period, Notice of
Violation of a Public Nuisance shall be delivered to all Occupants, Property Owners, and
Persons-in-Charge, advising them that a third violation on the same Property within a 1-year
period, as measured from the date of the first violation, will result in liability of the Owner and
Persons-In-Charge for all penalties associated with the violation as more particularly described
below. Compliance with this Section may be attained by email, Certified Mail, and/or posting
the Notice on the Property.
2. Third and all Subsequent Violations. On the third violation and all subsequent violations within a
1-year period measured from the date of the first violation, Notice of a Public Nuisance Violation
and Designation as a Nuisance Property shall be delivered to all Occupants, Owners, and
Persons-In-Charge. Compliance with this Section may be attained by email, Certified Mail,
and/or posting the Notice on the Property. The Notice shall include the following:
a. The Property has been designated a Nuisance Property.
b. The Owners and Persons-in-Charge are liable for all subsequent violations of the Nuisance
Property bylaw at the Property if, within the required timeframe, a meeting with the
enforcing authority is not scheduled, a Corrective Action Plan is not submitted, or the
Corrective Action Plan is not implemented.
c. Each day or portion thereof that a violation of this Bylaw exists beyond the Corrective Action
Plan’s timeframe shall constitute a separate offense. If more than one provision of this
Bylaw is violated, each provision violated shall constitute a separate offense.
d. Informing the Owner of the requirement for the Owner or designee to contact the enforcing
authority within five days to schedule a meeting, and to submit a Corrective Action Plan
within 10 days.
3. Nuisance Property Correction Process.
a. Upon receipt of the Notice of a Public Nuisance Violation and Designation as a Nuisance
Property, the following shall occur:
a. Within five days of the date of the Notice, the Owner(s) or designee shall contact the
enforcing authority to schedule a meeting to discuss the Property and discuss a
Corrective Action Plan to address the issues at the Property.
b. Within 10 days of the date of the Notice, the Owner or designee shall submit a
Corrective Action Plan to the enforcing authority, which shall indicate the ways the

Owner intends to take control of the Property so that it does not accumulate more
violations.
c. Within 14 days of the date of the Notice, the enforcing authority and Owner shall meet
to review the Corrective Action Plan.
d. At that meeting, the enforcing authority will provide the Property Owner either:
(a) A written approval of the Corrective Action Plan and timeframe for implementation;
or
(b) A written list of deficiencies in the Corrective Action Plan that need to be addressed
in the next submission, a date by which a revised Corrective Action Plan must be
submitted to the Town, and a date for the next meeting. All subsequent reviews will
follow this procedure.
b. Once the Corrective Action Plan is approved by the enforcing authority, the Corrective
Action Plan is binding.
c. An Owner may request the enforcing authority remove the Nuisance Property designation if
the Property has not received a violation in the six months following the
approval by the Town of the Corrective Action Plan. Removal of the designation
is at the discretion of the enforcing authority.
H. Failure to Correct
1. Failure to implement the Corrective Action Plan within the timeframe is a separate violation and
may result in additional penalties.
2. Failure to contact the enforcing authority, schedule a meeting, or submit a Corrective Action
Plan within the required timeframes of this Bylaw is a separate violation and may result in
additional penalties.
3. A rental property that is designated a nuisance property two years in a row shall be at risk of
losing the rental permit as well as more frequent inspections as required under General Bylaw
3.50 regulations.
I. State Law Not Preempted.
1. This Bylaw shall not limit the authority of police officers to make arrests for any criminal offense
arising out of conduct regulated by this Bylaw, nor shall this Bylaw limit the Town’s or the
Commonwealth’s authority to initiate and prosecute a criminal offense arising out of the same
circumstances necessitating the application of this Bylaw.
2. No provision of this Bylaw shall apply where prohibited or preempted by the law of the
Commonwealth or federal law.
